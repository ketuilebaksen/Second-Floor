Steelers Nation, the news is coming in waves this week, and almost all of it is breaking Pittsburgh’s way. A star running back is back at full speed. The hottest player on the roster just picked up a league award. The injury report is shrinking. An insider says the front office is getting ready to strike before the deadline. A former Steelers fan favorite’s name is in the headlines. A veteran receiver is suddenly on the trade block. And the team Pittsburgh plays on Sunday is probably going to be without its starting quarterback. That’s a lot to cover, so today we’re doing it rapid-fire: every major update, what it really means, and what’s just noise. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Update number one: Rico Dowdle is a full go in practice.

This is the big one. Dowdle hasn’t played since he dislocated his toe in Week 2 against New England. On Wednesday, he was limited. On Thursday, he was a full participant. Steelers Depot summed it up: “Odds are looking strong he’ll play.”

Why does that matter so much? Because Dowdle isn’t a depth back. He’s run for 1,000 yards in back-to-back seasons, 1,079 with the Cowboys in 2024 under Mike McCarthy, and 1,076 with the Panthers in 2025. Pittsburgh signed him in March for two years and 12.25 million dollars. So far, Steelers fans have seen 15 carries for 37 yards. On Sunday, they might finally see the real thing.

And here’s the part people forget. Dowdle can catch. In both of those 1,000-yard seasons, he caught 39 passes, and three of his five touchdowns in 2024 came as a receiver. With the Steelers this thin at wide receiver, that’s not a small detail. A back who can run a route out of the backfield gives Rodgers another safe target on third down. And McCarthy knows exactly how to use him, because he was Dowdle’s head coach in Dallas when he broke out. That’s not a learning curve. That’s a reunion.

Update number two: Jaylen Warren is the AFC Offensive Player of the Week.

And Dowdle is joining a backfield that’s already on fire. Warren was named AFC Offensive Player of the Week after the 34-22 win over the Colts. His line: 15 carries for 101 yards and a touchdown, six catches for 87 yards and another touchdown, 188 yards from scrimmage. He scored on an 80-yard catch-and-run and a 50-yard run, and no NFL player had done both in the same game since Herschel Walker in 1986.

McCarthy called him “our featured player right now,” and said there’s “a number you’d like to continue to give him” even with Dowdle back. So don’t worry about Warren losing his role. Think about what happens when a defense has to deal with both of them.

And look at what’s happening around them. Over the last three games, Rodgers has completed 63 of 105 passes for 826 yards and nine touchdowns. He’s thrown three touchdowns in each of those games. Pat Freiermuth just caught two touchdowns against the Colts. Germie Bernard caught six passes for 80 yards in his first career start. The offense is humming, even without its top receivers. Adding a healthy Dowdle to that mix is like adding fuel to a fire that’s already burning.

Update number three: the injury report is getting a lot shorter.

Here’s the full picture from Thursday. T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward were both full participants after scheduled rest days on Wednesday. Keeanu Benton, the defensive tackle who missed Wednesday with an ankle injury, came back as a limited participant. Jalen Ramsey was limited with his wrist, the same injury he played through against the Colts when he made nine tackles. Jamel Dean was limited with his ankle, which is encouraging after he missed the Colts game. And DeShon Elliott was limited, practicing on his 21-day window off injured reserve. He could be activated this weekend, but Steelers Depot thinks Week 7 is more likely.

The only two Steelers who didn’t practice were Roman Wilson, with a dislocated finger, and Ben Skowronek, with a calf injury. McCarthy already ruled both of them out for Tampa. The final injury report with game statuses comes out Friday, so keep an eye on Dean.

Update number four: “strong indications” the Steelers will be active.

This is the update that should get every Steelers fan’s attention. Longtime Steelers insider Mark Kaboly posted on Wednesday: “Getting strong indications that the Steelers are going to be active heading into the NFL trade deadline set for Nov. 10.”

He didn’t say which position. But let’s be honest about where the need is. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot. Wilson and Skowronek are out. The healthy receivers on the 53-man roster are DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen, with Brandon Smith, Isaiah Hodgins, and Brandon Johnson on the practice squad. Even Aaron Rodgers’ old Green Bay teammate James Jones said this week, “I do think that they need to make a move.”

And this front office has done this before. Last season, Pittsburgh made a midseason trade for safety Kyle Dugger from New England. It wasn’t a blockbuster, but it filled a need right when the team needed it. This year, Khan has already shown he moves fast, claiming two cornerbacks off other teams’ practice squads, bringing back KJ Henry and Elandon Roberts, and building the right side of the offensive line with Brock Hoffman and rookie Max Iheanachor. So when Kaboly says “active,” I take him seriously. The deadline falls on the Tuesday right after Pittsburgh’s Week 9 bye, which gives Khan three more weeks to find the right deal at the right price.

Update number five: the JuJu Smith-Schuster story.

On Thursday, Noah Strackbein of Sports Illustrated reported, citing a team source, that the Steelers were set to host free agent JuJu Smith-Schuster for a visit on Friday. That’s Pittsburgh’s 2017 second-round pick, a Pro Bowler here in 2018, and a player who caught 26 touchdowns in five seasons in black and gold.

But Behind the Steel Curtain later posted an update saying the Steelers are not hosting him. So the reports conflict, and the team hasn’t confirmed anything. If he does sign, he’s a depth piece: last year with the Chiefs, he had 33 catches for 345 yards and a touchdown. He’d bring toughness and familiarity, but he’s not the answer by himself. And even the original report said he’s unlikely to be ready for Tampa.

Update number six: Jauan Jennings is on the trade block, and this is the name I want you to know.

Now here’s the trade target that actually makes a lot of sense, and it’s backed by real reporting. Jauan Jennings, the former 49ers receiver who signed with the Minnesota Vikings this offseason, is suddenly a trade candidate.

Here’s what’s happening. On October 14th, Pro Football Talk reported that with the Vikings signing Odell Beckham Jr. to their practice squad last week, Jennings could be traded. Ian Rapoport floated the possibility that the 49ers “make sense” as a suitor, especially with Deebo Samuel dealing with a hamstring injury in San Francisco. And Jennings’ numbers in Minnesota explain why: in his first four appearances as a Viking, he has just two catches for nine yards. PFT wrote that the Vikings seem perplexed by his lack of impact.

But look at what he did before this year. In 2024 with San Francisco, he caught 77 passes for 975 yards and six touchdowns, and he only missed 1,000 yards because of an ejection in the final week. In 2025, he caught 55 passes for 643 yards and nine touchdowns. That’s a player who was a legitimate starting receiver just last season. He’s 29 years old, he’s physical, and he’s known as a tough, willing blocker, which is exactly what McCarthy’s run-heavy offense needs from a receiver.

And the contract makes this interesting. According to PFT, Jennings is on a one-year deal with a 4.5 million dollar signing bonus that’s already been paid, a base salary of about 1.63 million, and close to 100,000 dollars per game in active-roster bonuses and incentives. Wikipedia lists the deal as worth up to 13 million. For a team acquiring him now, a lot of that money is already spent. That makes him a cheap rental for the rest of the season.

And Jennings has big-game experience. He’s played in 11 career playoff games, catching 23 passes for 289 yards and a touchdown. Back in 2024, he had one of the best single games any receiver had that season: 11 catches for 175 yards and three touchdowns in Week 3. Over his career, he has 2,590 receiving yards and 22 touchdowns. That’s not a project. That’s a proven, physical, chain-moving receiver who just landed in the wrong situation in Minnesota.

So what would a deal look like? The video going around suggested a conditional fifth-round pick, and honestly, that’s in the right range for my opinion too. Something like a late-round pick, maybe with a condition tied to his playing time or the Steelers making the playoffs. Pittsburgh would not need to touch the 2028 second-round pick it got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. That pick can stay in the vault for a bigger target. The one complication is the roster: the 53 is full, so Khan would have to make a corresponding move. But with three receivers hurt, that’s a problem a lot of teams would love to have.

Now, let me be crystal clear. There is no report connecting Jennings to the Steelers. The team named in the reports is San Francisco. And some videos out there are getting the details very wrong. One of them claimed Jennings would give “Kenny Pickett” a better target, talked about “George Pickens,” and called this the 2023 season. None of that is right. Pickett and Pickens are long gone. Aaron Rodgers is the quarterback. And it’s 2026. It also claimed Pittman has been on injured reserve since August. He went on IR on October 10th.

So here’s my opinion, not a report. If Minnesota is ready to move on from Jennings, Khan should at least make the call. A late-round pick for a 29-year-old receiver who caught nine touchdowns last season, at a salary that’s mostly already been paid, is exactly the kind of low-risk move Khan makes. He wouldn’t be a superstar. But he’d be a reliable, physical third-down target for Rodgers while Pittman, Wilson, and Skowronek get healthy. And when everybody’s back, he becomes outstanding depth for the playoff push.

The risk? His four games in Minnesota were ugly. Two catches is two catches. And if the 49ers want him back badly, they know him better than anyone and could outbid Pittsburgh. But this is a name worth watching.

Update number seven: the Jerry Jeudy idea.

There’s one more receiver name making the rounds, and it’s a spicy one. NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala discussed the idea of the Steelers trading for Browns receiver Jerry Jeudy. She called him “just an odd man out” in Cleveland, and noted that Browns GM Andrew Berry “has obviously made trades within the division when he feels strongly about somebody.”

Jeudy was a Pro Bowler in 2024 with 1,229 yards. This year, he has eight catches for 81 yards in five games. He’s under contract beyond 2026, so he’d cost more than Jennings and come with a longer commitment. It’s an idea, not a report. But between Jennings and Jeudy, there are two former high-level receivers sitting on benches right now. One of them could end up in Pittsburgh.

And one quick note on Calvin Ridley: I’ve mentioned him as an option before, but this week Kinkhabwala warned against that kind of move, pointing out he’s had “plenty and plenty and plenty and plenty of opportunity” in Tennessee. His last 12 regular-season games have produced zero touchdowns. Jennings, who scored nine times last year, is a much more interesting veteran target.

Update number eight: Tampa Bay is probably without its quarterback.

And now the update that could decide Sunday’s game. Baker Mayfield dislocated his right thumb in Week 3 against Minnesota. He’s missed the last two games, and he didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday this week. Head coach Todd Bowles said he hasn’t resumed throwing. Covers lists him as highly likely to miss the Steelers game.

That means Tampa will probably start Jalon Daniels, an undrafted rookie. And here’s the warning: don’t sleep on him. In his first start, a 17-14 loss to Green Bay, he threw for 148 yards with a touchdown and two interceptions, and ran for 55 yards. Then last week, with Tampa as a 9.5-point underdog in Dallas, he completed 76 percent of his passes for 189 yards and a touchdown, and the Bucs got their first win of the season. Bucky Irving had a huge day on the ground in that game too.

The Bucs also had safety Antoine Winfield Jr. miss Wednesday and Thursday with a rib injury. On the other hand, Chris Godwin and Tristan Wirfs were full participants.

And the Mayfield situation is worth watching right up until kickoff. According to reports, he’s been pushing to play, and he had stitches removed on Monday. When the injury first happened, the expectation was that he’d miss at least three weeks, and possibly up to six. Oddsmakers clearly don’t expect him to play, since the line didn’t move after the latest update. But if he somehow gets cleared, Tampa becomes a much tougher opponent. That’s why Friday’s final injury report matters on both sides.

Tampa is 1-4. The Steelers are 3-2, and DraftKings opened Pittsburgh as a three-point road favorite. On paper, this is a game the Steelers should win. But Pittsburgh has lost five straight on the road, including that 27-24 loss in Cleveland, and a young quarterback playing at home with nothing to lose can be dangerous.

Update number nine: the bigger picture after Tampa.

Here’s one more thing to keep in mind as all these updates pile up. After Tampa, the Steelers play the Saints in Paris in Week 7. Then there’s one more game before the Week 9 bye, and then the trade deadline hits on November 10th. That stretch will decide what kind of season this is. If Pittsburgh wins in Tampa, rides that into Paris, and gets healthy at receiver, Khan can shop from a position of strength. If the road struggles continue, the pressure to make a bigger move will grow fast. Either way, these next three weeks are going to be some of the most important of the McCarthy era so far.

What all these updates add up to.

So let’s put it all together. Pittsburgh’s backfield is about to be whole for the first time since Week 2. The defense is getting healthier, with Watt and Heyward full, Benton back on the field, and Dean and Elliott trending in the right direction. The front office is reportedly preparing to be active before the deadline. And the opponent will likely be without its starting quarterback and starting safety.

Here’s how I’d attack Tampa. Feed Warren early and let him set the tone. Bring Dowdle in to wear the defense down, especially in the second half. Use Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington in two-tight-end sets to make up for the missing receivers. Let Rodgers work off play action and get the ball to Metcalf and Bernard on time. And on defense, make the rookie quarterback uncomfortable. T.J. Watt against an undrafted rookie quarterback is a matchup the Steelers have to win.

And remember the inside knowledge Pittsburgh brings. Sean Murphy-Bunting was on Tampa’s practice squad until early October, practicing against this offense every day. Jamel Dean, if he plays, spent the first part of his career in Tampa. And on the other side is Kenneth Gainwell, who left Pittsburgh for the Bucs in the offseason.

A win would make Pittsburgh 4-2 and end the road losing streak heading into Week 7, when the Steelers face the New Orleans Saints in Paris. That’s a big stage, and it would be a lot more fun to get there on a two-game winning streak. Picture the black and gold heading to Paris with a healthy backfield, a confident quarterback, and real momentum. That’s the kind of trip that can bring a locker room even closer together.

The bottom line.

So that’s the wave: Dowdle back, Warren honored, a healthier injury report, “strong indications” of a deadline move, a JuJu story with conflicting reports, a real trade candidate in Jauan Jennings, a spicy idea in Jerry Jeudy, and a Tampa team that’s likely missing its quarterback. Not every one of these is a done deal, and I’ll always tell you which ones are real. But put them together, and this is the most positive week Pittsburgh has had all season. Two weeks ago, people were asking whether this team was falling apart after a Thursday night loss and a sideline argument on national TV. Now the questions are about which receiver Khan adds and how many touches Dowdle gets. That’s a completely different conversation, and the Steelers earned it by winning, healing, and staying patient.
