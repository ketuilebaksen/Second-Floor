One position group on this team got an A-plus from me. Another one got a D. And they played in the same game, on the same afternoon, in a win over the Cincinnati Bengals. That's how strange this Steelers team is right now. So today we're doing something different. Report card day. Every position group, every grade, no sugar-coating. And at the end, I'm grading the one decision that could matter more than all of them, because the Joey Porter Jr. trade talk took another turn this week, and the Browns are waiting for us tomorrow night.

What's up, Steelers Nation, welcome back to Second Floor. We've been living inside this Bengals game for a few days, so today I'm not re-telling the story. You know the story. 30-27, Boswell for the lead, Harmon for the finish. Today is about accountability. Who earned their grade, who didn't, and what has to carry over to Cleveland. If you're new here, hit subscribe, turn on notifications, and grab a pen, because class is in session.

Let's start at quarterback. Aaron Rodgers. 19 of 34, 292 yards, three touchdowns, one interception. Now, the completion percentage isn't pretty. Fifty-six percent is not a number you frame and put on the wall. But I don't grade quarterbacks on completion percentage alone, and here's why. This offense asked him to push the ball down the field. For two weeks we complained that everything was a quick checkdown, that nobody was even trying to attack a defense vertically. On Sunday, they tried. And when you throw deeper, you're going to miss some. That's the trade-off, and I'll take it every time.

What really stood out to me was how he moved inside the pocket. Rodgers is 42. He's not scrambling around like the guy from ten years ago. But you don't need to run forty yards to buy time. Sometimes it's one step up. Sometimes it's a little slide to the left to find a throwing lane. He did that all afternoon. And that's the difference a functional offensive line makes. When the pocket holds, he has somewhere to move. When it collapses, he has nowhere to go. His accuracy did drop off a bit late as Cincinnati's rush got more involved, and there were a few pre-snap messes that I'll get to under coaching. But overall, this was the first game of the season where Rodgers looked like the guy Pittsburgh brought in. Grade: B-plus.

Running back. Jaylen Warren. This is the A-plus. There is no debate. Seventeen carries, 127 yards. Three catches, 49 yards. Career-high 176 scrimmage yards. PFF gave him a 91.2 grade, the best on the offense. And remember, he played through a shoulder injury that was so sore they didn't even let Rodgers throw to him during the walkthrough. What's the first thing that happens on Sunday? He catches the ball out of the backfield and takes it for thirty yards. That's how you answer a question about your health.

But here's what I want people to notice, because it gets lost when a running back puts up a big number. His pass protection. If you want your quarterback taking deep shots, somebody has to pick up the blitz long enough for those routes to develop. Warren did that. He was running through contact, making guys miss, catching outlets, and blocking. With Rico Dowdle out, Travis Homer and Lew Nichols chipped in a little, but this was Warren's afternoon from start to finish. And he even had another big run erased by a holding call. You can't add those yards back, but you could see how much damage he was doing. Grade: A-plus. And with Dowdle still not practicing this week, he's going to carry the load again on Thursday.

Offensive line. This is the group everybody was screaming about, so let's be fair. Max Iheanachor, in his first NFL start, earned an 81.3 overall grade from PFF, and a 91.4 run-blocking grade, the best on the team. Brock Hoffman posted an 88.6 pass-blocking grade and, according to PFF, allowed zero pressures and zero sacks. Zero. Against Dexter Lawrence and Jonathan Allen. That is a heck of a debut for the new right side.

Was it perfect? No. Iheanachor gave up a couple of pressures, and there were reps where the first contact didn't go his way. But what I liked is that he kept his feet moving. He didn't quit on the play when it started badly. In the run game, you could see the power. Zach Frazier and Mason McCormick had tough assignments inside and kept battling. Troy Fautanu allowed some pressure on the left side too, so I'm not calling this flawless. But this line gave Pittsburgh 138 rushing yards and gave Rodgers time to throw deep. That's progress. Grade: B-plus.

Tight end. Darnell Washington. Three catches, 67 yards, and he led the team in receiving. And that 47-yard catch-and-run down the sideline late in the game was the longest play of the day for Pittsburgh. It wasn't a deep ball. It was a short throw, and then he just kept going. Cincinnati couldn't get him down. That is what happens when you have a tight end that size who can actually move. Pat Freiermuth chipped in too, and he helped spring Warren on that first-drive gain. Grade: A-minus.

Wide receivers. This is where I have to be tougher. Roman Wilson had three catches for 60 yards and the 38-yard touchdown on the opening drive. Great. But that drop that turned into an interception right before halftime? The ball was right there. You have to finish that play. That mistake turned into a Bengals touchdown and a tie game. I'm not going to let one play erase everything he did, but I'm also not going to pretend it didn't happen.

DK Metcalf had three catches for 31 yards and the touchdown on five targets. Some people want him to get the ball fifteen times a game. I actually liked that Pittsburgh didn't force it. He can matter without being the focus of every snap. But the two penalties hurt. One of them was a hold where he just needed to let the defender go. And that flag wiped out a Warren run. At the same time, I saw him hustling downfield and throwing blocks for his teammates, including on Washington's big play. So give him credit for the effort, and still expect more discipline. Ben Skowronek gets a gold star for the touchdown and the muffed-punt recovery. As a group? Grade: C-plus.

Offense overall: A-minus. Thirty points. 411 yards. Seven yards a play. Seven of twelve on third down. And I love the intent. Mike McCarthy attacked the middle of the field. Third-and-long didn't automatically mean bring out the punt team. They were trying to pick it up. That's a mindset change, and it matters.

Now flip the page. Because the defense's report card is going to hurt.

Defensive line and edge rushers. Cincinnati scored 27 points and put up 352 yards. And the front that is supposed to be the identity of this franchise was quiet for most of the day. T.J. Watt finished with zero pressures, according to PFF. Amarius Mims handled him one-on-one for much of the game, and I have to give Mims credit. On the other side, the Bengals gave Orlando Brown more help, which changed Alex Highsmith's assignment. Nick Herbig graded out at 46.1 on PFF, with a team-low tackling grade. Now, in fairness, Burrow was getting the ball out incredibly fast, and PFF itself noted there's not much a rusher can do against that kind of quick release. But you can't only look at sacks. Cincinnati was also winning up front in the run game, and that let them stay comfortable all afternoon.

And yet, the biggest play of the entire game came from this unit. Derrick Harmon, quiet for fifty-nine minutes, beat his man, stripped Burrow, and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered. Cameron Heyward had two quarterback hits. So they get credit for the finish. But one play doesn't erase fifty-nine minutes. Grade: C-minus, and that's with the Harmon bonus.

Linebackers. Patrick Queen had a game-high 13 tackles. Payton Wilson was the best-graded defender on the team at 79.9, and a lot of that came from strong coverage work against Ja'Marr Chase. That's not easy. The problem is that too many of those tackles happened after the catch or after the runner was already a few yards downfield. Grade: B-minus.

And now, the secondary. This is the D. Pittsburgh played a lot of zone. I understand the idea. Keep everything in front, don't give up the explosive play, make Cincinnati earn every yard. The problem is the Bengals were perfectly happy to earn every yard. Burrow went 28 for 37 for 282 yards and three touchdowns. Take the underneath throw, pick up six, do it again. At some point you have to make that uncomfortable, and Pittsburgh never did.

Asante Samuel Jr. allowed both the Chase touchdown and the Higgins touchdown and graded out at 45.5 on PFF. He eventually got benched for rookie Daylen Everette. That's disappointing, because a lot of us felt good about him coming into the season. Brandin Echols graded out at 40.6, the lowest on the defense, and he left the game with a concussion. Jalen Ramsey had some good moments tackling against the run, but there were communication breakdowns in coverage. And here's the most frustrating part. You play soft to prevent the big play, you give up the underneath stuff anyway, and then Higgins still beats you for a touchdown. You're paying the price of a conservative plan without getting the benefit. Grade: D.

Special teams. Chris Boswell was perfect. Three for three on field goals, from 44, 36, and 33 yards, plus three extra points. And that 33-yarder with just over two minutes left was the game-winner. That matters after he missed a kick in each of the first two weeks. The punt unit pinned Cincinnati inside the 20, and Skowronek jumped on that muffed punt to set up the tying field goal. The kickoff coverage, though, still gave the Bengals too much return yardage and too many short fields. Grade: B-plus.

Coaching. This is where I split the grade. Offensive game plan? A. The lineup change on the right side was the right call, the aggression was the right call, attacking the middle was the right call. But the pre-snap operation was a mess. Pittsburgh burned all three first-half timeouts with more than seven minutes still left before halftime, and they had to burn more after the break, a lot of it because they couldn't get the play in or the right guys on the field. And defensively, when the short throws keep working and the rush isn't getting there, I need to see an adjustment. A blitz. Some press coverage. Something that makes Burrow think twice. I didn't see enough of it. Overall coaching grade: B-minus.

So the report card reads like two different students. The offense made the honor roll. The defense is getting a phone call home.

And that brings us to the question hanging over this entire secondary. Joey Porter Jr.

Here's what's new since we last talked. Porter practiced fully again on Tuesday, listed with a conditioning designation. That's now two full days in a row, even if both were walkthroughs on a short week. When Mike McCarthy was asked if Porter will be available Thursday, his answer was, "We hope so." He said Porter's work will determine his status leading up to kickoff. So there's a real chance we see him on the field in Cleveland.

At the same time, the trade talk keeps building. We already knew from Adam Schefter that Dallas and Detroit had called. Now Josina Anderson is reporting that the Cowboys and Steelers have their own thresholds on compensation, and right now, in her words, "it doesn't match." Meaning Pittsburgh is asking for more than Dallas wants to give. Jerry Jones has openly acknowledged the chatter. And there were some rumblings about the Giants as well, but the beat reporter who posted about it later clarified it wasn't an official report, so I'm not treating that as news.

Let me break down what this means. Pittsburgh is not giving him away. If the Steelers were desperate to dump him, a deal would already be done. The fact that the price "doesn't match" tells you Omar Khan is holding firm. And every day Porter practices fully, his value goes up, both on the market and on this roster.

Now look at the secondary report card I just gave you. A D. A starting corner benched. Echols with a concussion and the lowest grade on the defense. Ramsey's wrist went from a sling on Monday to a cast on Tuesday, and he was limited. And this is the week Porter finally looks ready to play.

My opinion hasn't changed, and I want to be clear this is my take, not a report. If Porter is healthy, you play him. You let him press. You let this defense get out of that soft zone shell. If someone blows you away with an offer before the deadline, fine, you listen, because that's the job. But you don't sell your best press corner at a discount the same week your secondary graded out as the weakest unit on the team. The best-case scenario for Pittsburgh is Porter plays Thursday, plays well, and either raises his trade price or makes the front office realize they can't afford to lose him.

Quick injury rundown for Thursday. Rico Dowdle, toe, still hasn't practiced. Expect Warren to be the lead back again. Echols, limited with the concussion. Ramsey, limited, wrist in a cast. Porter, full. And the good news continues. Aaron Rodgers and Jaylen Warren are nowhere on the injury report.

Kickoff is tomorrow night at 8:15 Eastern in Cleveland. Every team in the AFC North is 2-1, so the winner walks out of that stadium in sole possession of first place, at least until Sunday. The Browns have a defense that gets after the quarterback, and they'll go right at Iheanachor to see if his first start was real. But their offensive line has struggled to protect Deshaun Watson, so this is the perfect chance for T.J. Watt and this pass rush to erase that zero-pressure grade.

So here's my homework assignment for Thursday. Offense: same effort, cleaner operation. No wasted timeouts. Keep feeding Warren. Keep attacking the middle. Defense: stop playing scared. Make Watson uncomfortable early. If Porter is out there, let him be physical at the line. And special teams: tighten up that kickoff coverage, because on the road, short fields kill you.

Final grade for the Bengals game? B. The offense earned an A-minus. The defense earned a C-minus. Special teams bailed them out with a B-plus, and Derrick Harmon handed in the extra credit that decided the whole thing. That's a winning report card, but it's not a championship report card. Not yet.
