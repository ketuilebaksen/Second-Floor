THE DECISION EVERYONE IS ARGUING ABOUT

Steelers Nation, DK Metcalf has become the most talked-about Steeler of the bye week, and not for the reasons anyone wanted.

Let me set the record straight first. Metcalf hasn’t made a bombshell decision. He hasn’t demanded a trade. He hasn’t made a public statement. And the Steelers haven’t benched him, cut him, or shopped him, as far as any reporting shows.

But there is a decision on the table. It’s just not Metcalf’s to make. It’s Pittsburgh’s. After one catch for four yards against Cleveland, a drop that turned into an interception, and a week of national voices calling him finished, the Steelers have to decide what DK Metcalf is to this team for the rest of 2026. And maybe beyond.

So today we’re going to treat it like a front office would. What happened. What the numbers say. What the critics say. What the coaches say. What the contract says. And then the three paths Omar Khan can take, plus my pick. I’m James, this is Second Floor. Let’s get into it.

WHAT HAPPENED ON SUNDAY

Start with the play that lit the fuse.

Third quarter against Cleveland, Pittsburgh already comfortably ahead. Aaron Rodgers found Metcalf open down the middle of the field. And this matters, because it cuts against one of the criticisms: he was open. But the ball went through his hands and right to Browns cornerback Denzel Ward for an interception, according to Heavy and PennLive.

Metcalf finished with one catch for four yards on six targets. Tight end Darnell Washington led the team with three catches for 94 yards. And the Steelers won 27-6 anyway, on the strength of 200 rushing yards and eight sacks.

So the drop didn’t cost a game. But it cost something else: patience. Because it wasn’t an isolated bad day.

THE NUMBERS THAT STARTED THE FIRE

Here’s the full-season picture, according to Pittsburgh Steelers On SI and Heavy.

Through eight games, Metcalf has 33 catches on 64 targets for 378 yards and one touchdown. That touchdown came back in Week 3 against Cincinnati. He’s had exactly one 100-yard game, the five-catch, 115-yard night in the first Cleveland game. He also had 11 catches for 93 yards in Tampa. But in five of eight games, he’s been held under 50 yards.

His averages are career lows: 11.5 yards per catch and 40.6 yards per game, per Heavy. He’s on pace for roughly 59 catches and about 803 yards, which would be the worst full season of his career. Before 2025, he had never finished below 900 yards.

PennLive’s Nick Farabaugh added two numbers that explain a lot. Metcalf has a drop rate around 15 percent. And he’s seen 56 more snaps in press coverage than any other receiver in the league. In other words, defenses are lining up right on him, daring him to beat them physically off the line, and it isn’t working the way it used to.

THE CRITICS: SMITH, COWHERD, AND THE NOISE

The national reaction has been brutal.

It started before the game, on ESPN’s NFL Countdown, where former quarterback Alex Smith said, “The problem is who is he throwing the ball to?” He called Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle the two best players on Pittsburgh’s offense, and then turned to Metcalf: “DK Metcalf, I’m sorry, he looks like a shell of himself. This guy cannot separate anymore. He can’t pull away on a go route. Can’t pull away on a slant.” And the line that stung most: “At some point, I think Aaron’s going to stop looking that way.”

Then, on Monday, Colin Cowherd went further on FS1’s The Herd. “He’s washed, it’s over, he can’t play,” Cowherd said. “And he’s driving Aaron Rodgers nuts.” He added, “Now he’s getting in the way, can’t catch,” and called for action: “Omar Khan needs to cut bait with DK Metcalf.” He also said, “The DK Metcalf thing, it’s not working,” and that it’s “time to move off him now.”

Even former Steelers took shots on social media. I’m not going to repeat some of what was said, because it crossed the line. But the point is clear: the noise around Metcalf is the loudest it’s been since he arrived.

It’s worth noting that not every national voice is down on Pittsburgh’s offense. On Wake Up Barstool on Monday, Jon Gruden defended the Steelers against the “fraud” label, saying, “You don’t win four straight games unless you’re playing good football,” and, “I think Pittsburgh is a little better than people realize.” Gruden’s focus was the team, not Metcalf specifically. But it’s a reminder that a 6-2 team in first place isn’t broken, even if one piece is struggling.

THE OTHER SIDE: WHAT THE COACHES ARE SAYING

Now let’s hear from the people who actually make decisions.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio addressed it on Monday. He was honest about the drop: the Ward interception “is one he’d want back,” as Heavy reported, or as PennLive put it, a play Metcalf “would love to have back.” But then he backed his receiver firmly. “We have a ton of confidence in DK,” Angelichio said. “We haven’t lost confidence in DK Metcalf whatsoever.” And: “we’ve seen him do it and he will do it.”

Angelichio also pointed to Metcalf’s catch in New Orleans as a huge play. He’s right about that one. In Paris, on third-and-14, Metcalf hauled in a 51-yard catch that set up Pittsburgh’s only touchdown in a 16-10 win.

And Steelers Depot ran a headline this week saying a beat writer reports the Steelers remain “committed” to Metcalf “being here again next season.” I couldn’t open the full article, so I’m going only on the headline. But if that’s accurate, the organization isn’t wavering, at least publicly.

THE SEPARATION ARGUMENT: SMITH HALF-RIGHT?

Here’s where I want to be fair to both sides.

Smith’s main claim is that Metcalf can’t separate anymore. But on the biggest play of his day, he did separate. He was open down the middle. The failure was the catch, not the route. So Sunday’s tape doesn’t fully support Smith’s point.

But the season-long data does lean his way. If Metcalf is seeing more press coverage than anyone in the league, and he’s not winning consistently against it, that’s a separation problem at the line of scrimmage. And a 15 percent drop rate compounds it. When a receiver struggles to get open and also drops some of the balls he does get, quarterbacks stop throwing his way. That’s what Smith meant.

So my verdict: Smith overstated it with “cannot separate anymore,” but he identified a real trend. And Cowherd’s “washed” is the kind of take that sells TV, not the kind that holds up after one bad game in a 27-6 win.

THE CONTRACT: WHY “CUT BAIT” ISN’T REALISTIC

Now let’s talk about the part Cowherd skipped: the money.

According to Pittsburgh Steelers On SI, the Steelers acquired Metcalf from Seattle on March 9, 2025, for their 2025 second-round pick, 52nd overall. He then signed a five-year, $150 million deal, with four years and $132 million of new money covering 2026 through 2029. And this offseason, the Steelers restructured his contract, converting his $23.6 million base salary into a signing bonus.

Here’s why that matters. His 2026 cap hit is about $13.2 million. But his dead money in 2026 is about $49 million. In 2027, his cap hit jumps to about $38.4 million, with roughly $35.8 million in dead money. In 2028, about $39.9 million in cap with about $23.9 million dead.

Translation: releasing or trading Metcalf right now would be a massive cap hit. That’s why SI said a move is unlikely unless his play declines even further. “Cut bait” sounds great on a talk show. On a salary cap sheet, it’s one of the most expensive moves the Steelers could make.

THE PRICE PITTSBURGH ALREADY PAID

It’s worth remembering how big a bet the Steelers made on Metcalf in the first place. They didn’t just sign him. They traded a second-round pick to get him, and they reorganized their receiver plans around him. George Pickens eventually ended up in Dallas. The idea was simple: Metcalf would be the alpha outside receiver this offense hadn’t had in years.

That’s part of why the criticism is so loud now. When you invest a second-round pick and top-of-market money, the bar is a Pro Bowl-caliber season, not five games under 50 yards. Metcalf is the seventh-highest-paid receiver in the league by average annual value, according to Heavy. Fans expect seventh-best production.

But sunk cost cuts both ways. The pick is gone. The money is committed. The only question that matters now is how to get the most out of the player Pittsburgh has.

THE THREE PATHS FOR THE STEELERS

So here are the realistic options, as I see them.

Path one: stay the course. Keep Metcalf in his role as the top outside receiver, trust the coaches, and let him play through it. This is what the Steelers are signaling publicly. The upside is he regains confidence and becomes the guy who had 115 yards against Cleveland in Week 4. The downside is more weeks like Sunday.

Path two: adjust the role. Keep Metcalf, but stop asking him to be the number one option against the best press corner every week. Add a receiver who can win quickly, move Metcalf into a more vertical role, and use the tight ends and backs more in the passing game. Jason La Canfora floated a version of this, with Metcalf working vertically on the outside while another receiver handles the intermediate work.

Path three: move on. Explore a trade or release. Given the contract, this is not a 2026 option. It could become an offseason conversation if the second half looks like the first.

WHY THE TRADE MARKET JUST GOT MORE COMPLICATED

Path two depends on adding a receiver. And the market got a little trickier this week.

The top target has been Colts receiver Josh Downs. But Steeler Nation pointed out a problem: Pittsburgh no longer owns its 2027 fourth-round pick. According to that report, it went to Dallas in the Broderick Jones trade in August. Jordan Schultz said on Ryan Clark’s The Pivot that the Colts want a third-round pick, but “ultimately, RC, they’ll probably get a fourth.” If the Steelers don’t have a 2027 fourth to send, they’d have to get creative, maybe with 2028 picks.

There’s also new competition. ESPN’s Ben Solak predicted in his Week 9 column that the Colts would send Downs and a 2027 fifth-round pick to the Rams for a 2028 third-round pick. That’s just a prediction, not a report. But Solak also raised a fit concern: Downs needs to be protected from blocking duties, and Pittsburgh’s receivers are asked to block a lot in this run-heavy offense.

And NFL Network’s Daniel Jeremiah, according to a Steelers Depot headline, said Downs “feels redundant” for the Steelers and that Brian Thomas Jr. makes the “most sense” as a target. I couldn’t read the full piece, so I’m going off the headline. But it’s notable, because Thomas is a vertical outside receiver, which is closer to what Metcalf was supposed to be.

Meanwhile, The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones said Thomas, who caught just two passes in October, would likely cost a Day 2 pick. And Jets receiver Garrett Wilson got a “no way” from ESPN’s Adam Schefter’s source.

THE ROOM AROUND METCALF

Part of the Metcalf conversation is really a conversation about everyone else. Because if Metcalf isn’t producing, someone else has to.

Germie Bernard has stepped up. The rookie had six catches for 80 yards in his first start against the Colts, 11 catches for 87 yards and his first NFL touchdown in overtime in Tampa, and a key 15-yard catch before halftime against Cleveland. He’s earned Rodgers’ trust fast. Heyward said on The Rich Eisen Show that Bernard is “so mature for his age.”

At tight end, Darnell Washington just had a career day with 94 yards, and Pat Freiermuth has been reliable near the goal line. Jaylen Warren is a real receiving threat out of the backfield too, with that 80-yard catch-and-run touchdown against the Colts still fresh in everyone’s mind.

Behind them, Brandon Smith earned a spot on the 53-man roster before the Browns game. Marquez Valdes-Scantling is on the practice squad, with Rodgers lobbying to get him on the field. Ben Skowronek is working back from a calf injury. Roman Wilson was in the rehab group last week, according to Ray Fittipaldo. And Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with plantar fasciitis, with Mark Kaboly saying the team is “not ruling out, I guess, surgery at this point.”

So the pieces exist. What’s missing is a consistent second outside threat, and right now, that’s supposed to be Metcalf.

HOW THE COACHES CAN HELP HIM

Here’s my opinion on what the staff can do during the bye, without any trade.

Get him off the line of scrimmage. If defenses are pressing Metcalf more than any receiver in the league, use motion, stacks, and bunch formations to give him free releases. It’s harder to jam a receiver who’s moving or lined up behind a teammate.

Use the run game as his weapon. Pittsburgh is running the ball as well as anyone. When safeties creep up to stop Warren, that’s when the play-action shot to Metcalf should come. Washington’s 58-yard catch-and-run against Cleveland showed how wide open those shots can be.

Simplify his reads. A receiver who’s thinking too much plays slow. Give him a smaller menu of routes he wins on, and let him play fast.

And rebuild confidence with easy completions early in games. One quick slant on the first drive can change how a receiver plays for the next three hours.

THE PERFECT CHANCE TO ANSWER: SUNDAY NIGHT IN CINCINNATI

If Metcalf wants to change the conversation, the schedule just handed him the biggest stage possible.

After the bye, the Steelers visit Cincinnati on Sunday Night Football on November 15. It’s first place against second place in the AFC North, with Pittsburgh at 6-2 and the Bengals at 5-2. And Cincinnati’s defense just gave up a season-high 36 points to Tennessee, according to Yahoo Sports.

His only touchdown this season came against the Bengals in Week 3. A big night in prime time against the same opponent would do more to quiet Cowherd and Smith than any quote from a coach.

THE QUESTION BEHIND THE QUESTION: WHAT DOES RODGERS THINK?

All of this comes back to the quarterback.

Rodgers hasn’t publicly criticized Metcalf. After the win, he praised his line and said, “the receivers blocked well on the edge.” And back in Tampa, he credited Metcalf directly: “Once we got DK involved, the offense just shifted.”

But this is Rodgers’ final season. He’s 42. He doesn’t have time for a long rebuild of trust with any receiver. If Smith is right that Rodgers will “stop looking that way,” the Steelers need to give him someone else to look at. And if Angelichio is right that Metcalf “will do it,” the bye is the perfect time for him to prove it in practice.

THE HISTORY LESSON

One more thing worth remembering. Metcalf’s first season in Pittsburgh, 2025, ended with 850 yards, his lowest total to that point, after he missed the final two regular-season games. Before that, he’d been a model of consistency in Seattle, with 900-plus yards every season.

So this isn’t a guy who was never good. It’s a guy whose production has slipped two years in a row. That’s the trend the Steelers have to evaluate. Is it scheme? Quarterback fit? Age? Confidence? The answer probably involves a little of each.

WHAT I’D WATCH AFTER THE BYE

Here’s how I’ll judge Metcalf over the next month.

One. Catch rate on contested and catchable balls. The drops have to stop. That’s non-negotiable.

Two. Production against press. If he’s going to see more press than anyone, he has to win some of those reps, especially in the red zone.

Three. Rodgers’ trust. Watch where the ball goes on third down in Cincinnati on November 15. That will tell you more than any press conference.

Four. Role changes. If the Steelers add a receiver, does Metcalf’s target share drop, and does his efficiency go up? That’s the best-case scenario.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

On Metcalf: keep him. The contract makes moving him in 2026 almost impossible, and he’s still capable of the kind of plays he made in Paris and in Week 4. But stop forcing him into a role he’s struggling in. Give him help.

On help: I still want a receiver before the deadline. Downs remains my first choice if the price is right, but the missing 2027 fourth and the blocking concern Solak raised make it more complicated. If the Colts insist on a third, I’d walk away. If Pittsburgh can make it work with a 2028 pick, fine.

On Thomas: Jeremiah’s reasoning makes sense for fit, but a Day 2 pick for a player with two catches in October is steep. I’d only do it at the bottom of that range, and only if Jacksonville moves him before the end of the bye.

And whatever happens: don’t let one talk-show cycle make the decision. Make it based on the film, the money, and Rodgers’ window.
