Steelers Nation, after two losses in a row, I know a lot of you are tired of bad news. So today, let's start with the good stuff, because there's actually a real triple dose of it heading into the Black Friday game against Denver.

One: DK Metcalf is available. The NFL decided not to suspend him for Sunday's ejection in Philadelphia.

Two: Michael Pittman Jr. is coming back. Mike McCarthy said Tuesday he's "very confident" Pittman should be out there this week.

Three: Nick Herbig is back on the practice field. After missing the Eagles game, he was limited on Tuesday, which is real progress.

That's the triple dose. And it's genuine. But every one of those three comes with fine print, and there's some bad news on the injury report too, starting with Alex Highsmith. So we're going to go through all of it honestly. Then we'll look at what the Philadelphia film says about how this offense should use all these receivers.

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's get into it.

DOSE ONE: METCALF CAN PLAY

Let's start with the biggest one.

On Monday, NFL Network and ESPN both reported that the league won't suspend Metcalf for his run-in with Cooper DeJean. That makes him eligible for Friday. He could still be fined, but a fine doesn't take him off the field.

For a short week against an 8-2 team, that matters. The Steelers don't have to rebuild the receiver room in four days.

THE FINE PRINT ON METCALF

But I'll be honest, and so is the video I'm working from today: this one comes with mixed feelings.

On Monday, Metcalf denied intentionally trying to poke DeJean in the eye. He said he regretted not being out there for his teammates. And McCarthy said he believes in second chances, and indicated the team wouldn't suspend him internally either.

The video's take is that it's not saying the Steelers should give up on Metcalf, but that a football team needs accountability. And it adds a line I really like: it wants to see a coaching staff that makes personnel decisions based on performance, not reputation.

That's the key phrase for this week. Performance, not reputation. And on Sunday, the performance gap in the receiver room was hard to ignore.

THE NUMBER THAT STARTED THE CONVERSATION

Here's the comparison everyone's making.

Metcalf finished Sunday with one catch for two yards on four targets before he was ejected, according to the AP. Max Hurleman, elevated from the practice squad, finished with six catches for 61 yards on seven targets, including a 20-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter. That was the Steelers' only touchdown of the day.

The video I'm working from is fair about it. Their situations were different. Hurleman got a bigger role because Metcalf was gone. And one game doesn't make Hurleman the best receiver on the roster. But when your offense is struggling this much, a player who steps in and produces immediately should get the coaching staff's attention.

And he has. Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio said this week, according to Steelers.com: "Max was awesome."

WHAT ABOUT THE FINE?

One more Metcalf note. According to Schefter's report, Metcalf's conduct is still being reviewed for a fine. No amount had been announced when I recorded this. For context, according to the AP, he was fined $12,172 for a similar incident with Packers linebacker Quay Walker last November. I'd expect something in that neighborhood, but we'll see.

THE HURLEMAN ROSTER QUESTION

There's a practical issue here that the video points out.

Hurleman went back to the practice squad after the game. That's how practice squad elevations work. So if the Steelers want him on the field Friday, they need to make a roster move. Either another elevation, or signing him to the 53-man roster.

I think he's earned it. And honestly, the question isn't whether he's active on Friday. It's whether he's active every week from here on.

DOSE TWO: PITTMAN IS COMING BACK

Now, the second piece of good news.

Michael Pittman Jr. missed the Philadelphia game with his foot injury. But on Tuesday, according to Steelers.com, he was estimated as a full participant. And McCarthy said: "I'm very confident that Michael Pittman should be out there this week."

That's the most definitive thing McCarthy has said about Pittman in weeks.

THE FINE PRINT ON PITTMAN

Here's the catch. Pittman is still technically on injured reserve. According to Pro Football Talk, the Steelers have to activate him before he can play Denver.

And Tuesday's report was an estimate. Steelers.com notes the team held a walkthrough, not a full practice, which is normal on a short week. So "full participant" on a walkthrough day is good, but it isn't the same as a full-speed practice.

Still, combine McCarthy's confidence with a full designation, and I'd be surprised if Pittman isn't activated before Friday.

Angelichio summed up what he brings: Pittman "gives us another option," according to Steelers.com. That's an understatement for a receiver with two 1,000-yard seasons in his career.

DOSE THREE: HERBIG IS BACK ON THE FIELD

The third piece of good news is on defense.

Nick Herbig was inactive against Philadelphia with his hip and rib injuries. On Tuesday, according to Steelers.com, he was limited. McCarthy said that as of Monday, Herbig was in the rehab group, so getting him onto the practice report as limited is a real step forward.

Steelers Depot's headline captured it well: Herbig is taking his first steps back.

THE FINE PRINT ON HERBIG

The catch here, according to Sports Illustrated's Steelers site, is that even if he plays on Friday, he probably won't be at full health. Hip and rib injuries affect how an edge rusher bends, gets off the line and absorbs contact.

So if he's active, he might be on a snap count. But even 60 or 70 percent of Herbig is a lot better than none, especially with what's happening with Highsmith.

THE BAD NEWS: HIGHSMITH

And that brings us to the bad news.

Alex Highsmith was having a great afternoon in Philadelphia. The video I'm working from says he had two sacks and a forced fumble on just six defensive snaps before he hurt his ankle in the first quarter. Sports Illustrated also says he left early in the first quarter. And the video adds that he was giving Eagles left tackle Jordan Mailata real problems.

According to Pro Football Talk, Highsmith now has 5.5 sacks this season. And he did not practice on Tuesday.

McCarthy's words: "It's going to be tough for him to make it this week."

The video says ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported the injury isn't believed to be serious, but that Highsmith wore a protective boot on Monday. I couldn't find Fowler's report directly, so I'll treat that as the video's account. But "not serious" and "tough to make it this week" can both be true. A short week doesn't give an ankle much time.

WHO STEPS UP ON THE EDGE

So if Highsmith is out and Herbig is limited, who carries the load opposite T.J. Watt?

According to Steelers.com, Jack Sawyer will be called on. His words: "It's the next man up mentality." The video also mentions KJ Henry, who was signed to the 53-man roster from the practice squad last week, according to Steelers.com.

Here's an encouraging stat from the video: the Steelers had three sacks in Philadelphia, two from Highsmith and one from Sawyer. So Sawyer has already shown he can get home.

And defensive coordinator Patrick Graham, talking about the players who stepped up on Sunday, said: "I applaud them."

WHY HIGHSMITH'S ABSENCE HURTS MORE THAN USUAL

Let me explain why losing Highsmith this week stings a little extra.

It's not just the sacks. Highsmith has been one of this team's most reliable edge defenders against the run for years. When he's on the field, offenses have a harder time getting outside. And against Philadelphia, outside of Barkley's 38-yard touchdown, the run defense held up really well.

Without Highsmith, and with Herbig possibly limited, the Steelers lose some of that edge discipline. Watt is still Watt. But a young player like Sawyer is going to see a lot of snaps against an 8-2 offense, and Denver will test him.

So the defensive game plan probably has to change. More stunts and twists to create pressure, instead of relying on pure one-on-one wins. And more help from the linebackers on the edge in run support. That's where Patrick Queen and Payton Wilson become even more important.

WHAT A SHORT WEEK REALLY MEANS

And there's a scheduling detail that explains a lot of this week's injury report.

According to Steelers.com, Tuesday's report was an estimate, because the Steelers held a walkthrough instead of a full practice. That's standard on a short week. When you play Sunday and Friday, there's no time for full-speed, padded practices. Bodies need to recover.

That's also why you'll see a lot of full-participation designations that look better on paper than they might in reality. A full participant in a walkthrough isn't the same as a full participant in a Wednesday practice during a normal week. Keep that in mind when you read the reports this week, especially for Pittman and Dunker.

ONE MORE INJURY: SKOWRONEK

There's one more name to watch. Receiver Ben Skowronek did not practice on Tuesday with his calf injury, according to Steelers.com.

According to Sports Illustrated, he missed three games before returning in Week 10, and the calf seems to be an ongoing issue. But he's played only 66 offensive snaps this season, so his absence would mostly affect special teams. With Pittman coming back and Hurleman emerging, the Steelers have options.

THE DUNKER UPDATE

And one quick offensive line note. Rookie guard Gennings Dunker was also a full participant on Tuesday, but he's still on injured reserve, according to Steelers.com.

McCarthy said Dunker still needs work, particularly in pass protection. So I wouldn't expect him to be activated this week. Spencer Anderson stays at left guard.

NOW, THE BIG QUESTION: HOW DO YOU USE ALL THESE RECEIVERS?

Here's where the video I'm working from gets really interesting, and where I want to spend some time.

If Pittman is back, Metcalf is available and Hurleman is elevated again, the Steelers suddenly have more receivers than they've had in weeks. The video asks the right question: what's the point of having all these receivers if you're not getting the right guys involved?

And it lays out a plan I actually like.

THE VIDEO'S ROTATION PLAN

Here's the idea, as the video describes it.

Put Hurleman on the field in obvious passing situations. Keep Metcalf and Pittman out there when you want size, especially when you're establishing the run. And on third and long, when somebody has to get open near the first-down marker, give Hurleman the chance, because he can work underneath and give Aaron Rodgers another way to move the chains.

The video also wants designed opportunities for Roman Wilson downfield, instead of asking every receiver to do the same job.

That's role-based football. Not "who's the WR1." It's "who's the best receiver for this down and distance."

WHY WILSON FITS THAT PLAN

The video points out that Wilson caught three passes for 43 yards on Sunday, including a 33-yarder that it says was Pittsburgh's longest offensive play of the game. I couldn't confirm that exact play, but his 43-yard total is in the Bleacher Report box score, and the rest fits.

That's exactly what Wilson is for. He stretches the field. And as the video says, this offense needs more plays like that down the field.

Germie Bernard, meanwhile, had six catches for 40 yards on 10 targets. He's more of an intermediate, possession-type option right now. That fits a rotation too.

THE OFFENSIVE PROBLEM IN NUMBERS

But the receivers aren't the whole story. The video spends real time on why the offense stalled in Philadelphia, and the numbers are rough.

According to the video, the Steelers didn't pick up their first first down until the final play of the first quarter. Think about that. An entire quarter without moving the chains.

The video says Pittsburgh finished with 255 total yards. The CBS Sports box score I looked at earlier had 233, so the exact number depends on the source. Either way, it's not enough. And the time of possession makes it even more frustrating. The Steelers held the ball for about 35 minutes and still scored only 16 points.

As the video puts it, it's not just about sustaining drives. It's about doing something with them.

THE RUN GAME NUMBERS

Here's the stat that tells the whole story.

According to the video, Aaron Rodgers, at 42 years old, led the Steelers in rushing with 29 yards on five attempts. Jaylen Warren had 28 yards on nine carries. Rico Dowdle had 15 yards on five carries. The team finished with 73 rushing yards on 20 carries.

When your 42-year-old quarterback out-rushes both of your running backs, something's wrong.

The video's suggestion is to build the run game around what Spencer Anderson and Brock Hoffman do best, with power and duo concepts, so linemen aren't constantly asked to make blocks that don't suit them. That fits what McCarthy said after the game, when he admitted the team didn't react fast enough to how Philadelphia played its linebackers behind the double teams.

THIRD DOWN

And that brings us to third down.

The video has the Steelers at 5-of-17 on third down. The CBS Sports box score I saw had 4-of-16. Either way, that's under 30 percent.

The video makes a great point here. Fixing third down isn't just about calling better plays on third down. It's about getting into manageable situations before you get there. If first and second down don't produce anything, you're facing third and long every time, and defenses can pin their ears back. That's when the sacks pile up.

RODGERS AND THE PROTECTION

Speaking of sacks, here's where the video defends Rodgers, and I think it's right to.

Rodgers completed 30 of 44 passes for 227 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions, according to Bleacher Report. The video says he was sacked five times for a loss of 45 yards.

The video's view is that the numbers don't excuse every mistake, but that at this stage of his career, expecting Rodgers to carry an offense without reliable protection isn't realistic. It wants more screens and shorter concepts for Bernard and Wilson, to create easier throws.

That's especially important on Friday. According to FOX Sports, Denver leads the NFL with 17 fourth-quarter sacks. Quick throws aren't optional against that defense. They're necessary.

THE DEFENSE: BETTER THAN 19 POINTS SUGGESTS

Now the defense, and here the video gives some credit.

According to the AP, Joe Milton completed 11 of 21 passes for 141 yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions. DeVonta Smith had five catches for 41 yards on 12 targets, according to Bleacher Report. Philadelphia missed some chances in the passing game.

Against the run, the video says the Eagles finished with 91 rushing yards on 26 attempts, about 3.5 yards per carry. Saquon Barkley had 75 yards on 17 carries, but 38 of those came on his touchdown run. Outside of that one play, the video says the Steelers held him to 37 yards on 16 carries.

That's a defense that played well enough to win, outside of one big play and a rough first quarter. According to the video, Philadelphia scored 13 points in the first quarter and only six the rest of the game.

THE SECONDARY QUESTION

The video raises one personnel question in the secondary that I want to address.

It notes that rookie cornerback Daylen Everette, the third-round pick out of Georgia, started in Philadelphia and finished with six combined tackles and a pass defended. Its view is that it understands the value of giving a rookie experience, but it'd like to see what Asante Samuel Jr. could provide in a bigger role, and let Everette learn at his own pace.

That's a fair debate. Everette's numbers on Sunday were solid, though. And against Denver, physicality on the outside matters. My lean is a rotation, not a benching. Let the matchups decide.

CREDIT TO SPEARS-JENNINGS

The video also gives credit to another rookie, and I want to echo it.

Robert Spears-Jennings, the seventh-round pick, forced the fumble on the opening kickoff of the second half, according to the video. Carson Bruener recovered it, and the AP confirms the recovery. The Steelers turned it into a field goal.

The video also says Spears-Jennings started at safety in Philadelphia. A seventh-round rookie who starts on defense and makes game-changing plays on special teams is exactly the kind of young player a team like this needs.

THE THIRD-DOWN CALL THAT ENDED IT

One last defensive note from the video.

With just over a minute left, Philadelphia faced third-and-6, and Joe Milton found DeVonta Smith for seven yards to pick up the first down and let the Eagles run out the clock. The video says Jamel Dean made the tackle, but the first down was already secured.

Its criticism of Graham is that it wanted a more aggressive call there. And its broader point is fair: it's not asking Graham to abandon what he's doing. It just wants more aggression when the moment demands it.

WHAT THIS ALL MEANS FOR FRIDAY

So here's how the triple dose and the fine print come together for Denver.

According to FOX Sports, Denver is 8-2 and leads the AFC West. The Broncos are favored by 2.5 points at Acrisure Stadium. Kickoff is 3 p.m. Eastern on Prime Video, according to Yahoo Sports.

The good news gives the Steelers more weapons on offense than they've had in weeks: Metcalf, Pittman, Wilson, Bernard and Hurleman. And Herbig could give the pass rush a boost, even at less than full strength.

The bad news is that Highsmith probably won't play, and that's a real loss after the way he started in Philadelphia.

The video I'm working from closes with a line I agree with: you can't always control which players are healthy, but you can control how effectively you use the ones who are available. That's the test for McCarthy and his staff on Friday.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for the Broncos game. Opinion, not a report.

Metcalf: plays, but in a defined role. First and second down, size packages, run game. Earn the third-down targets back.

Pittman: activate him. Snap count around 30 on his first game back.

Hurleman: elevate or sign him to the 53. Third-down slot receiver.

Wilson: at least two designed deep shots.

Bernard: screens and quick game against the Denver pass rush.

Run game: power and duo built around Anderson and Hoffman. Warren needs 18 to 20 carries.

Edge rush: Sawyer starts if Highsmith is out. Herbig on a snap count if active. KJ Henry rotates.

Secondary: rotate Everette and Samuel based on matchups.

Defensive play-calling: be aggressive on third down late. Don't let Denver's quarterback get comfortable when the game's on the line.

FINAL WORD

Here's my bottom line. This really is a triple dose of good news. Metcalf is available. Pittman is coming back. Herbig is practicing again.

But good news only matters if you use it well. The Steelers had the ball for 35 minutes in Philadelphia and scored 16 points. Their quarterback led them in rushing. And they converted fewer than a third of their third downs.

More receivers won't fix that by themselves. A smarter plan will. Use the right receivers in the right situations, fix the run game up front, protect Rodgers with quick throws, and the Steelers can absolutely beat Denver on Friday.
