Steelers Nation, Omar Khan has added a weapon for the Saints game, and before you get too excited, let me tell you exactly who it is and who it isn’t. The weapon Khan actually added this week is Marquez Valdes-Scantling, a speedy veteran who is now teaming up with Aaron Rodgers for the third time in his career. The weapon so many of you keep asking about is Tyreek Hill. You’ve told me again and again that you want him in black and gold, and that you believe he’s the piece that finally brings a seventh Lombardi Trophy to Pittsburgh. So today, we’re going to do both. We’ll look at what MVS brings to this offense in Paris, and then I’m going to lay out the championship road map with Hill, plus the latest on where his free agency really stands, because some new developments this week matter a lot. And I’ll be upfront, like always: Hill hasn’t signed anywhere, and there’s no report connecting him to Pittsburgh. Everything about Hill in a Steelers uniform is my opinion, not a report. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The weapon Khan actually added.

Let’s start with the real move. On Tuesday, the Steelers signed Marquez Valdes-Scantling to their practice squad after the Chargers let him go. This season, he’d been with the Chargers and spent time with the Cowboys too, catching two passes for 22 yards in three games for Los Angeles.

But this isn’t about what he did in Los Angeles. It’s about what he can do with Rodgers. They were together for four seasons in Green Bay, and Steelers fans saw a taste of it late last year, when MVS was elevated in December and finished with 10 catches for 80 yards and a touchdown in five games. Now he’s back for round three with the same quarterback.

And the early reviews are good. Mike McCarthy said MVS “looked good in practice,” adding, “It’s been awhile, but yeah, we’ll see.” He also said the veteran “looked like he stepped right in, like he never left.” Earlier in the week, when he was asked whether MVS might dress against the Saints, McCarthy didn’t rule it out: “We got an abbreviated practice here today and a full one before we go tomorrow. So yeah, we’ll see what happens.”

Why this matters in Paris.

Here’s why that signing matters more than it looks on paper. Ben Skowronek has been ruled out for the Saints game with his calf injury. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are on injured reserve. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen on the active roster, with MVS, Brandon Smith, and Brandon Johnson on the practice squad.

When Smith and Johnson were elevated for the Tampa game, neither of them was targeted. MVS is different. He’s a vertical receiver, a two-time Super Bowl champion, and a player Rodgers already trusts. Even if he only plays 20 snaps, his speed forces a safety to respect the deep ball, and that helps Metcalf and Bernard underneath.

So yes, Khan added a weapon. It’s not a blockbuster. But on a week when the receiver room is thin and the trip is long, a player who already knows the quarterback’s timing is exactly the kind of low-risk move this front office has made all season.

Big-game experience matters.

There’s another reason I like this move. MVS has been on the biggest stages in the sport. In the AFC Championship Game after the 2022 season, he caught six passes for 116 yards and a touchdown against the Bengals, and the Chiefs went on to win the Super Bowl. He won another ring the following year. When the Steelers are thinking about December and January, having a receiver in the room who has made plays in those moments is a quiet but real advantage.

He also brings something younger players can learn from. Bernard is a rookie, and Wetjen is still developing. A veteran who knows how Rodgers wants routes run, how deep to take a stem, and when to break off a route against pressure can help that whole room, even on the days he doesn’t catch a pass.

And now, the weapon everyone is dreaming about.

Now let’s talk about the name that fills my comment section every day. Tyreek Hill. And I want to approach this one a little differently than we have before. Instead of asking whether Hill would be good in Pittsburgh, because obviously he would, let’s ask a tougher question: what would actually have to happen for Hill to bring a championship to this team?

Step one: the knee has to be ready.

The first thing is health. Hill turned 32 this year, and he’s coming off a dislocated left knee with multiple torn ligaments, including his ACL, from late September of 2025. He played just four games last season. Miami released him in February.

His rehab has had ups and downs. In the summer, he admitted he still had “no power” in his left leg. Later videos showed him looking much more explosive. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said a return could come “perhaps some time in October.” Well, we’re near the end of October, and he still hasn’t signed. So for Hill to help any team win a title, that knee has to hold up through December and January, against the best defenses in the league.

Step two: he has to be available.

The second step is availability, and this is where things got more complicated this week. Hill is dealing with an off-field legal matter, including a civil verdict earlier this month, and the NFL has said it’s reviewing the situation under its personal conduct policy. That’s all I’ll say about the details. What matters for football is this: NFL insider Jordan Schultz said on the Ross Tucker Podcast that it’s “more likely” Hill gets suspended before he signs anywhere, and that “I don’t know if anything with Tyreek Hill is necessarily imminent.”

And here’s an important detail. Hill doesn’t have to be on a team for the league to discipline him. Suspensions have been handed down to unsigned players before. So any team signing him would have to plan around a possible absence, which makes a playoff push with Hill even harder to map out.

Step three: the price has to be right.

The third step is the contract. According to The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones, Hill doesn’t want to sign for the veteran minimum. Rosenhaus has said his client is in discussions with multiple teams, but also that “nothing is imminent at this time.”

For Pittsburgh, I think the only deal that would ever make sense is a short-term, incentive-based contract. A modest base salary, with bonuses tied to games played and production. That protects the team if the knee or the suspension takes him off the field, and it rewards Hill if he’s the player he used to be. Whether Hill would accept that is a different question.

What the timeline would look like.

Let’s say, hypothetically, that everything broke the right way. What would a Hill timeline in Pittsburgh look like? The trade deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday after the Steelers’ Week 9 bye. Free agents can be signed at any time, but any new receiver would need a few weeks to get into game shape and learn the offense. Realistically, that points to the second half of November at the earliest.

And the schedule after that is loaded with big stages. The Steelers host Denver on Black Friday, and they have a Sunday Night Football game at home against Houston in Week 13. Picture Hill streaking down the sideline under the lights at Acrisure Stadium, with Rodgers dropping one in over his shoulder. That’s the picture fans are holding on to. And by then, Pittman and Wilson could be back from injured reserve too, giving Rodgers the deepest receiver room he’s had all season.

Why the dream won’t go away.

I also understand why this idea keeps coming back, no matter how many obstacles there are. Steelers fans have watched this defense win games almost by itself for two straight weeks. They’ve watched the offense go three quarters without a touchdown in Tampa. They see a 42-year-old quarterback who might be chasing his last ring. And they see one of the fastest receivers in NFL history sitting on the market. Of course people connect those dots.

But wanting something and being able to make it happen are two very different things. The best front offices don’t chase every big name. They figure out what’s realistic, what fits, and what it costs. And Khan has shown all season that he’s willing to say no when the price or the risk is too high.

Step four: Pittsburgh has to beat the competition.

The fourth step is the market. Kansas City has been the team most often linked to Hill, and for good reason. He spent his first six seasons there and won a Super Bowl. But Jones reported he doesn’t “get the sense” the Chiefs are “in a rush,” and Kansas City has looked at other receivers. Then this week, Bleacher Report’s Alex Kay floated the Rams as a fit, and that idea got more interesting after the Rams learned Davante Adams will miss six to 10 weeks with a ruptured hamstring.

And where are the Steelers in all of this? Back in early October, one prediction market had Pittsburgh at about one percent to be Hill’s next team. That’s not zero, but it tells you the Steelers aren’t considered a serious contender for his services right now.

Step five: he has to fit what already works.

And the fifth step is fit. This is the part where I actually get excited, because if steps one through four ever fell into place, the football fit is beautiful.

The Steelers’ defense is already playing at a championship level. Over the last two games, opponents converted just 10 of 34 third downs. According to Heavy, Pittsburgh is now in the top half of the league in total yards allowed, at 307.5 per game, and in passing yards allowed, at 204.3. Jalen Ramsey was just named AFC Defensive Player of the Week. T.J. Watt was cleared after a calf scare. That’s a defense that can carry a team through January.

What the offense lacks is a game-breaker. In Tampa, the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters and didn’t score a touchdown until early in the fourth. When they finally got going, it was because Metcalf and Bernard took over. Rodgers said it himself: “Once we got DK involved, the offense just shifted.”

Now picture Hill on the other side. Defenses can’t roll coverage toward Metcalf anymore. Safeties have to stay deep. Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle see lighter boxes. Bernard finds open space underneath. And Rodgers, in what may be his final season, gets the kind of speed he’s always made the most of. That’s how a great defense and a future Hall of Fame quarterback turn into a champion.

Hill’s résumé, one more time.

And let’s remember why people are still dreaming about him at 32. With Kansas City, Hill made six Pro Bowls, three first-team All-Pro teams, and won a Super Bowl. Then in Miami, he was a Pro Bowler and first-team All-Pro in each of his first two seasons. In 2023, he caught 119 passes for 1,799 yards and 13 touchdowns, leading the entire NFL in receiving yards. Players like that change the math for an entire defense. Even a version of Hill that’s 80 percent of his peak would be the fastest weapon Pittsburgh has had in years.

The championship formula without Hill.

But here’s what I want every Steelers fan to remember. Even if Hill never comes, this team has a real formula. The defense is elite. Rodgers just tied Peyton Manning for third all-time in regular-season touchdown passes. Warren was the AFC Offensive Player of the Week in Week 5. Bernard has caught 17 passes in his first two starts. MVS is back. DeShon Elliott is close to returning. And Pittman and Wilson can come back later in the season.

Add one more piece at the trade deadline, and this roster can compete with anybody in the AFC. Championships are usually built by teams that keep getting healthier and better in November, not by one splashy signing in October.

What Khan’s pattern tells us.

Think about every move Khan has made this month. He claimed Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith off other teams’ practice squads. He brought back MVS. He released Rayshawn Jenkins to make room for DeShon Elliott. None of those moves came with a big contract or a big risk. Each one solved a specific problem at a reasonable cost.

Hill would be the opposite of that pattern. He’d come with a big name, big expectations, a recovering knee, and an uncertain availability. That doesn’t mean Khan would never do it. But it does mean the conditions would have to be just right, and right now, they aren’t.

What to watch from the receivers on Sunday.

When the Steelers take the field in Paris, here’s what I’ll be watching from this receiver group. First, does Rodgers get Metcalf involved early, like he said he wanted to after Tampa? Second, does Bernard keep building on his first two starts, maybe with his first 100-yard game? And third, if MVS is elevated, does McCarthy use him as a deep threat right away, even on just a handful of snaps, to loosen up the Saints’ secondary?

New Orleans has some talent on the back end, including cornerback Kool-Aid McKinstry, who was listed with a head injury this week but practiced fully. So the Steelers’ receivers will have to win their matchups. That’s where Rodgers’ timing and decision-making become so important. He doesn’t need a superstar on every route. He needs receivers who are where they’re supposed to be when the ball comes out.

Rodgers and the deep ball.

And that brings us back to why speed matters so much in this offense. Rodgers has spent his whole career punishing defenses that leave a receiver one-on-one deep. In Green Bay, that’s exactly the role MVS played for him, the receiver who could run past a cornerback and make the safety pick a side. Hill would be that role at an even higher level. But even without Hill, giving Rodgers one player the defense has to respect deep makes the short throws, the play action, and the run game all work better.

Trade block: the realistic upgrade.

So let’s talk about that piece, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

If Khan wants to add a receiver before the November 10th deadline, my first call is still to Indianapolis about Josh Downs. The Colts fired their general manager, rival teams expect them to sell, and Downs is a young route runner on an expiring deal. That’s the realistic version of adding firepower, without the knee questions, the suspension questions, or the bidding war with Kansas City.

And Khan has the ammunition. The Steelers own an extra 2028 second-round pick from the Joey Porter Jr. trade with Dallas. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the deadline. If Khan does make a move, I’d expect it to be targeted, reasonably priced, and focused on helping Rodgers right now.

Why Elliott is part of the weapons story too.

And don’t forget the other “addition” that could happen this weekend. DeShon Elliott isn’t a receiver, but getting him back would be like adding a veteran starter to the defense without giving up a single pick. McCarthy said, “It’s been a long road back, but he’s a big part of what we’re about in Pittsburgh.” He set a career high with 108 combined tackles in 2024, his first season here. If he’s activated on Saturday, the Steelers’ biggest addition of the week may end up being on defense, not offense.

Sunday’s test.

But first, there’s a game in Paris. The Steelers face the Saints on Sunday at the Stade de France, with kickoff at 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh time. Watt, Highsmith, Ramsey, and Heyward are all expected to play. Rico Dowdle and DeShon Elliott are questionable, and Elliott has to be activated from injured reserve by Saturday afternoon if he’s going to suit up. Reporters including Kaboly have indicated the Steelers plan to have him on the field.

The Steelers are 4-2, tied with the Browns and Ravens, and Cleveland comes to Pittsburgh next week. A win in Paris sets up a game for first place in the AFC North.

The bottom line.

So did Omar Khan add a huge weapon before the Saints game? He added a smart one. Marquez Valdes-Scantling is back, he already knows Rodgers, and he gives this short-handed receiver room some much-needed speed.

And could Tyreek Hill come to Pittsburgh and help bring home a championship? On the football field, absolutely, the fit would be incredible. But for that to happen, his knee has to be ready, the league’s review has to play out, the price has to make sense, and the Steelers would have to beat Kansas City and others to him. Right now, none of that is in motion. So keep the dream alive, but keep your eyes on the trade deadline, because that’s where Khan’s next real weapon is most likely to come from.
