Steelers Nation, the Tampa Bay game is in the books, and the attention immediately shifts to the man upstairs. Omar Khan has spent this entire season building a roster that can survive injuries, and now the most important three weeks of his year begin. The trade deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday right after the bye. Steelers insider Mark Kaboly says there are “strong indications” Pittsburgh will be active. And one quiet move Khan made last week may have already set up his next big decision. So today, we’re going position by position through Khan’s board: where he’s likely to strike, where he’s likely to stand pat, what the Elandon Roberts signing might mean for Patrick Queen, and the long-term question that could define Khan’s next offseason, Jaylen Warren’s next contract. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

First, what “active” really means.

Let’s start with the tweet everyone has been talking about. Kaboly posted: “Getting strong indications that the Steelers are going to be active heading into the NFL trade deadline set for Nov. 10.” That’s a real signal from someone with sources inside the building.

But I want to be careful about how we read it. “Active” doesn’t automatically mean a blockbuster. Under Khan, active has usually meant smart, targeted moves. Last season, his midseason trade was for safety Kyle Dugger. This season, he’s already claimed cornerbacks Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith off other teams’ practice squads, brought back pass rusher KJ Henry, and re-signed linebacker Elandon Roberts. That’s the Khan pattern: fix problems quickly, protect the draft capital, and swing big only when the price is right.

And he’s holding real ammunition for a bigger swing: the 2028 second-round pick he got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. That pick isn’t even one of Pittsburgh’s own. If Khan wants to make a statement move, that’s the chip.

So let’s go through the roster and figure out where that chip might be spent.

What the Tampa tape tells Khan.

Before we go position by position, think about what Khan will be studying from this game. Whatever the scoreboard said, the film from Tampa is his first real look at three things. First, how this offense functions with Metcalf battling an illness and two practice squad receivers in the lineup. Second, whether Dowdle looks like the back Khan paid for in March, in his first game back since Week 2. And third, how the secondary held up without DeShon Elliott, against a mobile rookie quarterback in Jalon Daniels and a talented running back in Bucky Irving. Every one of those answers points toward a different move. That’s why this week, more than any other this season, the front office will be watching the tape just as closely as the coaching staff does.

And there’s another factor. The Steelers came into Tampa having lost five straight road games. Khan knows that a team that can’t win on the road can’t win in January. If he sees a roster that’s one or two pieces away from being a real contender, he’ll be more willing to spend. If he sees bigger problems, he’ll be more careful. Either way, this was the most important game tape of his season so far.

Position one: wide receiver, the obvious need, with a twist.

Everyone assumes receiver is the top priority, and on the surface, it is. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot. Roman Wilson went on injured reserve on Saturday with his finger injury, and his earliest return is Week 11 against the Eagles. Ben Skowronek has been dealing with a calf injury. Before Tampa, Pittsburgh had to elevate Brandon Smith and Brandon Johnson from the practice squad just to have enough bodies.

But here’s the twist, and it’s a point worth taking seriously. Adding a starting-level receiver now would have consequences when everyone gets healthy. Pittman is a big part of the long-term plan, and Khan will want to see what he looks like after the bye when he’s eligible to come off injured reserve. Wilson was playing well before he got hurt. And rookie Germie Bernard has been taking real steps, including six catches for 80 yards in his first career start against the Colts. If Khan brings in a high-priced starter, somebody in that group loses snaps when the room is full again.

So in my opinion, the smart move at receiver is a bridge, not a blockbuster. A veteran who can help for the next month and then become depth. The names that fit that profile right now: Jauan Jennings of the Vikings, who Pro Football Talk reported could be traded after Minnesota signed Odell Beckham Jr. to its practice squad, and Darius Slayton, who the Colts released on Friday just 24 days after signing him. Jennings caught nine touchdowns in San Francisco last season, and much of his one-year deal has already been paid through his signing bonus. Slayton is a free agent with 4,435 career receiving yards, though his knee is a question mark. There’s no report connecting either player to Pittsburgh. But both fit the “bridge” idea.

There’s also a more aggressive idea floating around. NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala talked about the possibility of the Steelers trading for Browns receiver Jerry Jeudy, calling him “just an odd man out” in Cleveland. Jeudy had 1,229 yards and a Pro Bowl in 2024, but only eight catches for 81 yards through five games this season. He’s under contract beyond 2026, so he’d be more than a bridge. And a trade inside the AFC North is rare. But Kinkhabwala pointed out that Browns GM Andrew Berry “has obviously made trades within the division when he feels strongly about somebody.” It’s not a report. It’s an idea worth watching.

And one name I’d take off the board: Calvin Ridley. Kinkhabwala warned that Ridley has had “plenty and plenty and plenty and plenty of opportunity” in Tennessee, and he has zero touchdowns in his last 12 regular-season games. Khan doesn’t need a project. He needs production.

And if Khan does decide to go bigger, the dream is still Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, a 24-year-old with 1,282 receiving yards as a rookie. That’s the kind of player you add for the next five years, not just the next five weeks. That’s where the Dallas pick would make sense.

Position two: cornerback, probably not a priority.

A lot of fans want a cornerback, and I understand why. The secondary has been patched together for weeks. But when you look closer, I don’t think this is where Khan spends.

Jamel Dean, when healthy, is a legitimate number one corner. He was questionable for Tampa with his ankle, but he said he was “very optimistic,” and he’s trending toward being fully back well before the deadline. Asante Samuel Jr. had a team-high four pass breakups against the Colts, three of them on third down and one on fourth down. Rookie Daylen Everette had two passes defended in his first NFL start. And Khan already added Murphy-Bunting and Smith as depth.

Remember, against Indianapolis, this secondary didn’t allow a single passing touchdown. Once Dean is fully healthy, this room should be fine. I’d be surprised if Khan uses real draft capital here.

Position three: safety, the sneaky one.

Safety is more interesting. DeShon Elliott is working back from injured reserve, with his 21-day practice window open. He wasn’t activated for Tampa, but he could be ready for Week 7 against the Saints in Paris, and his return would be a huge boost for communication on the back end.

Jaquan Brisker, the Pittsburgh native the Steelers signed in March after four seasons in Chicago, hasn’t had the start to the season that fans hoped for. Robert Spears-Jennings is young, and the coaching staff likes him. And in a pinch, Jalen Ramsey, who’s been playing through a broken wrist, can always slide back to safety.

So would Khan add a safety? Only if the right player becomes available at the right price. If Elliott returns healthy, I think the Steelers ride with what they have. But if Elliott has a setback, this could quietly become the position where Khan makes his move, just like he did with Dugger last year.

Position four: running back, the dark horse.

Now here’s one most people aren’t talking about. Running back.

Rico Dowdle, the two-time 1,000-yard rusher Khan signed in March, was a full participant in practice on Thursday and Friday and was set to return in Tampa after missing three games with a dislocated toe. But before the injury, his start in Pittsburgh was slow: 15 carries for 37 yards in his first two games. Meanwhile, Jaylen Warren took over and became the AFC Offensive Player of the Week.

So the question for Khan is simple: does Dowdle look like the player who ran for over 1,000 yards in both Dallas and Carolina? If he does, the backfield is set. If he doesn’t, and especially if pass protection is a problem, Khan might look for another back before the deadline. And here’s a fun name: Kenneth Gainwell, the former Steeler who signed with Tampa this offseason and was on the other sideline this week. Would the Bucs move him for a late pick? That’s my speculation, not a report.

One name I’d cross off the list: Tony Pollard. His name has come up, but Pro Football Rumors reported on October 11th that the Titans have no plans to trade him. GM Mike Borgonzi has turned down teams that called, and Tom Pelissero said Tennessee would only consider a “ridiculous offer.” That’s not a deal Khan makes.

The move that may already be in motion: Patrick Queen.

Now let’s talk about the most interesting part of Khan’s recent work, because I think his last move might be setting up his next one.

On Monday, the Steelers brought back Elandon Roberts on the practice squad. On the surface, it was a depth signing. McCarthy called Roberts a veteran who “just wants to win,” and said, “Can’t have enough people like that.” But look a little deeper.

Last season in Las Vegas, Roberts played for defensive coordinator Patrick Graham, the same Patrick Graham who now runs Pittsburgh’s defense. And the Raiders’ own website described Roberts as the leader of that linebacker group “as the Green Dot,” meaning the defender wearing the helmet radio and getting the calls from the sideline. He started 16 games and made 90 tackles in that role.

Why does that matter? Because Patrick Queen has been the man in the middle of Pittsburgh’s defense. Queen is in the final year of his contract, with a reported cap hit of around 17 million dollars this season, and he’s set to become a free agent in 2027. He had a great game in Week 3 against Cincinnati, when he earned a game ball, but the Post-Gazette’s Gerry Dulac said after the Cleveland loss, “I see a lot of criticism out there about Patrick Queen and it’s deserved.” Steelers Wire suggested this week that the Roberts signing could mean the team “could be nearing a trade decision.”

So here’s my opinion, and I want to be clear that there is no report the Steelers are shopping Queen. If a team offers a reasonable pick for Queen before the deadline, Khan now has a veteran who already knows how to run Graham’s defense from the middle. That’s what makes the Roberts move so smart. It doesn’t force a Queen trade. It makes one possible. And if Khan pulls that off, getting value for a player who’s likely leaving in free agency anyway, that’s exactly the kind of move that earns the phrase “he’s done it again.”

The other side? The Steelers are in a playoff race. Payton Wilson has been excellent, with 10 tackles, two for a loss, and a fourth-down pass breakup against the Colts. But trading a starter in the middle of a push sends a message to the locker room. Khan will only do it if the return is clearly worth it.

The long-term question: what is Jaylen Warren worth?

Now let’s zoom out to the decision Khan will face next offseason, because Warren’s play is forcing it into the spotlight.

Warren signed a two-year extension before the 2025 season. According to NFL Network, the deal was worth 17.5 million dollars over three years, with 12 million guaranteed, and it runs through the 2027 season. So next offseason, he’ll be heading into the final year of his contract and eligible for another extension.

And look at what he’s doing. Against the Colts, 188 yards from scrimmage, an 80-yard touchdown catch, and a 50-yard touchdown run, the first player to do both in the same game since Herschel Walker in 1986. Three straight games with at least 125 scrimmage yards. Coming into Week 5, his 5.6 yards per carry ranked behind only Kenneth Walker III, James Cook, and Bijan Robinson among NFL running backs. McCarthy called him “our featured player right now.”

And here’s what made that 2025 extension so smart. Before the deal, Warren was set to play on a one-year restricted free agent tender worth 5.35 million dollars. Instead of letting him play out that tender and hit the market, Khan locked him up at a price that now looks like a bargain for a player producing at this level. That’s front office foresight. It’s also why Warren’s next contract will be a much bigger conversation. When you get a player at a discount once, the next deal usually costs more.

So should the Steelers pay him like a top running back? Here’s my opinion. Warren has been one of the best values in the NFL on his current deal, and that’s a credit to Khan. But paying top-of-market money to a running back is always a risk, because the position takes a beating and production can drop quickly. If Warren keeps this up all season, he’ll deserve a raise, and the Steelers should want him back. But the smart deal is one that rewards his production without locking the team into huge guarantees for a running back who’ll be approaching 30 by the end of a new deal. That’s the balance Khan has to find. And honestly, how Dowdle looks over the next month could influence that decision too. If Dowdle proves he can carry a big load, the Steelers have leverage. If not, Warren’s value goes even higher.

And think about the timing on the Queen question. If Khan were to move him, it would most likely be before the November 10th deadline, while teams that need linebacker help are still willing to pay. After the deadline, Queen plays out the season and walks in free agency, and Pittsburgh might only get a compensatory pick down the line. Khan has shown with the Joey Porter Jr. trade that he’s willing to make tough calls on players who won’t fit his long-term plan. Porter brought back a 2028 second-round pick. Queen wouldn’t bring that much, but the principle is the same: don’t let value walk out the door for nothing.

Why this all adds up to “he’s done it again.”

So let’s put the pieces together. Khan extended Warren at a reasonable price before he broke out. He signed Dowdle in March for about six million dollars a year. He built the right side of the offensive line with Brock Hoffman, a one-year signing, and first-round rookie Max Iheanachor. He patched the secondary with practice squad claims while Dean and Elliott healed. And he re-signed a linebacker who knows Graham’s system from the inside, which could open the door to a Queen decision.

Every one of those moves looks small on its own. Put them together, and you have a roster that can absorb injuries, compete in the AFC North race, and still give Khan the flexibility to strike before November 10th.

What happens next.

The schedule doesn’t wait. Next up is the trip to Paris to face the Saints in Week 7, then one more game before the bye, and then the deadline. That’s three weeks for Khan to watch the tape, measure the market, and decide.

Here’s what I’ll be watching. At receiver, whether Bernard and the young players keep producing, or whether the passing game needs a bridge veteran. At safety, whether Elliott comes back healthy. At running back, whether Dowdle looks like the 1,000-yard back Khan paid for. And at linebacker, whether Roberts gets elevated and how much he plays, because that will tell us a lot about Queen’s future.

The bottom line.

Omar Khan’s best moves this season haven’t come with press conferences. They’ve come quietly, one at a time, and they’ve kept this team competitive through a wave of injuries. Now, with Kaboly reporting “strong indications” of activity before the deadline, the quiet phase may be ending. Whether it’s a bridge receiver, a safety, a running back, or a Patrick Queen trade, I expect Khan to make at least one move that matters before November 10th. And if history this season is any guide, it’ll be a move that looks small on the transaction wire and big on the field.
