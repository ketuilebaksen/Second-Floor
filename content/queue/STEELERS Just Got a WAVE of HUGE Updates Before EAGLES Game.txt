Steelers Nation, after the worst week of the season, Wednesday finally brought some momentum the other way. Four injured players walked back onto the practice field. The left guard everyone feared was lost for the year got a diagnosis that’s a lot better than it looked on the cart. And Aaron Rodgers dropped a hint about what he thinks can fix this passing game.

But it’s not all good news. Two key defenders popped up on the injury report with new issues, and the team is kicking the tires on outside help for a secondary that just got torched by Ja’Marr Chase.

So today, we’re going through the whole wave: who’s back, who’s hurt, who’s visiting, and what Rodgers’ no-huddle comments could mean for Sunday in Philadelphia. And at the end, my board, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

FOUR PLAYERS BACK ON THE FIELD

Let’s start with the headline from Wednesday.

According to Steeler Nation, Mike McCarthy confirmed the team was opening the 21-day practice windows for four players at once. His words: “Definitely opening the window for both Pittman and Wilson. Plus Dunker, and Max Hurleman. We’ll have four men coming off the rehab group to work today.”

That’s receivers Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson, rookie guard Gennings Dunker, and practice squad receiver Max Hurleman. All four have been sidelined for weeks. All four were back at practice on the same day.

McCarthy put it simply, according to WPXI: getting players back from multi-week absences “definitely gives us a boost.” But he also cautioned, according to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, that it’ll “take evaluating them all week” to decide who’s ready for Sunday.

ROMAN WILSON: THE CLOSEST TO RETURNING

Of the four, Wilson looks closest.

According to Yahoo Sports, Wilson was a full participant on Wednesday. That’s the best possible first step. He’s been on injured reserve since October 17 with a finger injury and has missed the last four games. Steeler Nation notes that he kept up his conditioning while he was out, which is why his return is expected to come sooner than Pittman’s.

That matters a lot for this offense. Wilson is the receiver on this roster best built to stretch the field, and he already has a 38-yard touchdown catch against Cincinnati this season. After a game where Pittsburgh couldn’t push the ball downfield at all, getting a vertical threat back could change how defenses line up against Rodgers.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: A LONGER ROAD

Pittman’s situation is more complicated.

According to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, he’s been on injured reserve since October 10 with plantar fasciitis in his left foot. McCarthy said he “aggravated a foot injury,” which made things worse. Steeler Nation adds that the foot has bothered him since before the season, and that he’s played in only three games.

Steeler Nation calls a Week 11 return against the Eagles a long shot, though not impossible. So I’d temper expectations. The window opening is real progress. But plantar fasciitis is the kind of injury where you’d rather wait a week than rush back and lose another month.

MAX HURLEMAN: HIS FIRST REAL CHANCE

Here’s a name I’ve been talking about for a couple of weeks now.

Max Hurleman, the undrafted receiver Rodgers publicly asked for, has been on the practice squad’s injured list since September 19. On Wednesday, he was one of the four players McCarthy named. According to Steeler Nation, he’s an undrafted 2025 free agent who has spent two seasons on the practice squad.

Remember what McCarthy said about him earlier this month, according to Sports Illustrated: “I felt that he truly performed to a level that he deserved a 53-man roster spot.” Now he’s finally healthy enough to show it.

He won’t jump straight into the starting lineup. But with his return-game background from Colgate and Notre Dame, a game-day elevation as a special teamer is a realistic first step.

GENNINGS DUNKER: THE LEFT GUARD QUESTION

The fourth name is the one that matters most for the offensive line.

Rookie Gennings Dunker, a third-round pick out of Iowa, has been recovering from an MCL sprain suffered in late September. On Wednesday, he was back on the practice field. Steeler Nation suggests he may need a couple of weeks to get up to speed, and WPXI says the team hasn’t given a return timeline.

The transcript makes a strong case for Dunker. It calls him a massive, aggressive run blocker, exactly what the line needs without McCormick. It also lists the concerns honestly: he played tackle in college, he’s still learning guard, and his balance and pass protection need work.

But the transcript raises a smart alignment question. Brock Hoffman has played well at right guard, and according to the transcript, Dunker worked mostly on the right side in the preseason, though he’s prepared at left guard too. The transcript’s suggestion is to evaluate Dunker at left guard and keep Hoffman on the right, rather than disrupt a combination that’s working. I agree.

WHY THE TRANSCRIPT ISN’T SOLD ON ANDERSON

Spencer Anderson is the current starter at left guard, and he replaced McCormick in Cincinnati. The transcript thinks he handled it reasonably well. But it makes two good points about why he might not be the long-term answer.

First, Anderson struggled at right guard earlier this season before Hoffman took over, and that switch brought more physicality to the run game.

Second, and this is the one I hadn’t considered, the Steelers use Anderson as an extra blocker in jumbo formations alongside Darnell Washington. That package has been an advantage in short yardage and when protecting a lead. Make Anderson the full-time left guard, and you might lose a formation that’s already working.

That’s a real cost for a team that depends this heavily on the run.

CHEMISTRY ON THE RIGHT SIDE

There’s one more reason the transcript wants to keep Hoffman where he is, and it’s a good one.

Offensive line play is about five people moving as one. The transcript points out that Hoffman has built chemistry next to center Zach Frazier on the right side of the line. Those calls, those combination blocks, those little handoffs on stunts, they take weeks of game reps to get right. You don’t want to break up the part of the line that’s working just to fix the part that’s broken.

The transcript is also honest about the risk with Dunker. He hasn’t played a regular-season NFL snap yet, and learning left guard in live action is very different from practicing it. If he isn’t ready, the transcript says it wouldn’t force the issue just to speed up his development. That’s the right attitude. A rookie lineman who gets thrown in too early can lose confidence fast.

THE OTHER OPTIONS UP FRONT

The transcript also floats two more ideas.

One is moving Troy Fautanu inside to guard and letting Dylan Cook play left tackle. The transcript notes Fautanu was viewed as capable of playing guard coming out of Washington. But it wouldn’t go that route unless everything else fails, pointing to the trouble that came from moving Broderick Jones between positions. I agree completely. Don’t move a young tackle who’s settled.

The other option is free agency. The transcript names James Daniels, Alex Cappa and Will Hernandez as available veterans. It’s most interested in Daniels, who knows Pittsburgh and has played both guard spots. But the transcript also flags his health, citing serious Achilles and pectoral injuries in back-to-back seasons. I couldn’t independently confirm the current status of any of those three players, so treat that as the transcript’s list, not a report of interest from the team.

THE DANIELS IDEA, DONE CAREFULLY

I do like one detail in how the transcript handles the Daniels idea. It doesn’t say sign him and start him. It says that if he passes a physical and looks ready, bring him onto the practice squad first. That way, Pittsburgh can see what he still has without promising him the job.

That’s a sensible, low-risk way to add insurance. If Anderson struggles or Dunker isn’t ready, the Steelers would have another experienced guard one move away. Again, I’m not reporting any contact between the team and Daniels. This is the transcript’s idea, and I think it’s a reasonable one.

THE GOOD NEWS INSIDE THE BAD NEWS: MCCORMICK

Now, the biggest update of the day.

On Wednesday, the Steelers placed Mason McCormick on injured reserve, according to Yahoo Sports. That part was expected. But the diagnosis was better than anyone feared.

According to WPXI, citing NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, McCormick dislocated his ankle without any fractures. The trainers popped it back into place on the field, and he won’t need surgery. Rapoport reported that he could return in the postseason.

Think about that. When you saw the air cast and the cart on Sunday night, you thought season-ending, maybe worse. Instead, there’s a real path back for January, if the Steelers get there.

That changes the left guard conversation. This isn’t about finding McCormick’s permanent replacement. It’s about getting through the next six or seven weeks. That’s another reason I’d lean toward giving Dunker a real look rather than signing a big-name veteran.

And remember, putting McCormick on injured reserve also opens a spot on the 53-man roster. That’s the spot the Steelers can use to activate Wilson, Dunker or someone else when they’re ready.

NEW INJURY: NICK HERBIG

Now for the less welcome part of the wave.

According to Yahoo Sports, outside linebacker Nick Herbig did not practice on Wednesday with a hip and ribs designation. He left the field in pain during the loss to the Bengals.

That’s a significant concern. Herbig has been a key part of a pass rush that, as recently as Week 8, was leading the NFL in sacks. And after a game where Pittsburgh got Joe Burrow down only once, losing one of your best rushers would hurt. I’m not going to speculate on how serious it is. Let’s see Thursday and Friday’s reports.

NEW INJURY: PATRICK QUEEN

The other new name is linebacker Patrick Queen.

According to Yahoo Sports, Queen was limited on Wednesday with a hand injury. Limited is better than out, and linebackers often play through hand issues with a cast or a wrap. But Queen had 14 tackles and the only sack in Cincinnati. He’s one of the leaders of this defense, so his status is worth watching.

ELLIOTT BACK ON THE FIELD

There’s also some progress at safety.

DeShon Elliott, who hurt his knee in pregame warmups in Cincinnati, was listed as limited on Wednesday, according to Yahoo Sports. WPXI notes he worked with the rehab group. Either way, being on the report as limited rather than out is a step in the right direction after a scary pregame scene on Sunday.

THE REST OF THE REPORT

A few more notes from Wednesday.

According to Yahoo Sports, cornerback Jalen Ramsey did not practice, listed as non-injury related, personal. That’s not something to speculate on, and it’s not uncommon midweek.

T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward and Darnell Washington were all held out for rest. That’s become the routine for the veterans on Wednesdays, so no alarm there.

And according to Steelers Depot’s headline, the receivers are healthier, but the team has two new defensive injuries. I couldn’t open that article, but the headline matches everything else in the reports.

WHAT THE INJURY REPORT MEANS FOR THE GAME PLAN

Put it all together and you can see the shape of Sunday. On offense, the Steelers are getting healthier at receiver, which gives Rodgers more options than he had in Cincinnati. Up front, they’re shorter at guard. On defense, the questions are on the edge and at linebacker. Thursday and Friday will tell us a lot more, and Friday’s final report will give us game designations.

THE SECONDARY SEARCH

Now let’s talk about the defensive backs who’ve been coming through the building.

On Tuesday, according to NFL insider Jordan Schultz, as reported by BVM Sports, the Steelers hosted former Chiefs cornerback Nazeeh Johnson for a workout. Johnson is 28 and played college football at Marshall. Kansas City drafted him in the seventh round in 2022. According to A to Z Sports, he’s a two-time Super Bowl champion with 66 tackles and a sack in Kansas City, and he’s been a key special teams contributor.

The transcript is right about his value: he won’t transform the secondary, but reliable depth and special teams matter, especially after Pittsburgh signed AJ Finley to the practice squad on the same day.

The transcript also says the Steelers brought in veteran safety Jordan Whitehead. I couldn’t confirm a workout this week. What I can confirm: according to Pro Football Talk, the Steelers hosted Whitehead for a free agent visit back in late July. He played his college ball at Pitt and has 105 career games with 101 starts, 557 tackles and 11 interceptions. The Steelers went with Rayshawn Jenkins in camp instead. And according to Yardbarker, the Titans worked him out on November 13, and he’s still unsigned.

So Whitehead is a familiar name to this front office. Whether he’s back in the picture now, I’d treat as unconfirmed. He also hasn’t played since 2024, after a serious car accident in January 2025, according to Pro Football Talk. He’s since been medically cleared.

WHAT THE WORKOUTS TELL YOU

The transcript makes an important point here. When a team works out several defensive backs and adds another body to the practice squad, it tells you something about how comfortable the coaches feel with what they have.

After Chase put up 231 yards, nobody should be surprised. The Steelers aren’t panicking, but they’re clearly looking for insurance, especially with Elliott limited and Ramsey out on Wednesday.

THE ASANTE SAMUEL JR. DEBATE

That leads to a debate the transcript raises about Asante Samuel Jr.

The transcript says the separation he allowed against Chase and Tee Higgins was concerning. It also acknowledges that those are elite receivers, and that Samuel has had good stretches this season. Its view is that he hasn’t consistently looked like the player from his Chargers days.

Then it lays out the options. Rookie Daylen Everette, the third-round pick out of Georgia, is more physical, a better tackler and better against the run, with the length to help in man coverage. The transcript compares his body type to Joey Porter Jr. He’s not a finished product, and he’s had rookie mistakes. The other option is Sean Murphy-Bunting, who has starting experience from Tampa Bay.

The transcript leans toward Everette for the developmental upside. I’d be a little more cautious. After a game like Cincinnati, throwing a rookie into a bigger role against a team with DeVonta Smith possibly returning is a risk. My preference would be a rotation: Samuel keeps starting, Everette gets more meaningful snaps, and you let the film decide over the next two weeks.

RODGERS’ NO-HUDDLE HINT

Now, back to the offense, and to the hint the transcript mentions in its opening.

After the Bengals loss, according to Steeler Nation, Rodgers was asked about carrying over offensive success, and he said: “There’s no carryover. It starts over every single week.” He then noted the team had been good in the no-huddle this season.

He didn’t demand anything. But the transcript asks the obvious question: if it works, why not use it more?

The transcript says Pittsburgh has run no-huddle on roughly 7.1 percent of its plays, which it ranks 12th in the league. I couldn’t confirm that exact figure, so take it as the transcript’s number. But the logic holds. And the transcript points out that most of Pittsburgh’s offense in Cincinnati came in the fourth quarter, when Rodgers was getting the ball out faster.

WHY TEMPO COULD HELP RODGERS

Here’s the football logic, and the transcript explains it well.

A faster pace makes it harder for defenses to substitute, disguise coverages or make all the adjustments they want. They can still change things, but for a quarterback who built his career on reading defenses, a clearer picture before the snap helps him identify matchups, change the play and find an answer without holding the ball too long.

The transcript compares it to Peyton Manning in his later years in Denver. Manning wasn’t making plays with his legs. He won by getting his offense into the right play before the snap. That’s not a perfect comparison, but the principle fits a 42-year-old Rodgers.

And there’s data that points the same way. According to Yardbarker, citing analyst Tej Seth, Pittsburgh’s play-action dropbacks ranked fourth in EPA per play, while its non-play-action dropbacks ranked 30th. The common thread is giving Rodgers an advantage before he has to make a tough throw.

THE LIMITS OF TEMPO

But the transcript is honest about the limits too.

No-huddle doesn’t make receivers better against man coverage. If a defense lines up and challenges Pittsburgh’s receivers one-on-one, somebody still has to get open. And the transcript says this group has struggled to separate.

It’s also honest about Rodgers. His ability to diagnose defenses is still valuable, but the transcript says his mobility has declined and his deep accuracy has become a concern. So the plan has to be built around where he’s comfortable, not around asking him to carry a deep passing game.

That puts the decision on McCarthy, who calls the plays, and offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio. Keep the same approach and hope execution improves, or lean into tempo when drives stall. The transcript would experiment with more tempo. So would I.

WHAT AWAITS IN PHILADELPHIA

Sunday’s opponent is the Eagles, at 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS.

Philadelphia is 2-7 and has lost seven straight. Joe Milton is the quarterback with Jalen Hurts out for the season. The Eagles are coming off a bye, and they’ve been busy. They brought back former assistant T.J. Paganetti as a consultant, signed 36-year-old Zach Ertz to the 53-man roster with a franchise receptions record in reach, and added nose tackle Leki Fotu to the practice squad after trading Jordan Davis.

And the history: the Steelers haven’t won a regular-season game in Philadelphia since 1965, according to Steelers.com.

This is a game Pittsburgh should win on paper. But the way the Steelers play this week, healthier at receiver but missing a starting guard and possibly a top pass rusher, will tell us a lot about how they respond to Cincinnati.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Roman Wilson: activate him for Philadelphia if he stays full all week. Give him four to five targets, including at least one deep shot.

Michael Pittman Jr.: no rush. Target the Black Friday game against Denver.

Max Hurleman: if he practices well, elevate him as a special teamer. Let him earn the rest.

Left guard: Anderson starts Sunday. Give Dunker reps with the first team at left guard this week and keep Hoffman at right guard. Don’t move Fautanu.

Secondary: Samuel starts, Everette rotates in more, and if Nazeeh Johnson signs, use him on special teams first.

No-huddle: use it on the opening drive and any time the offense stalls. Let Rodgers control the tempo.

Herbig and Queen: no risks. If Herbig can’t go, give the young edge rushers more snaps and pressure from different spots.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. This week’s wave of updates is mostly positive. Four players back at practice. A McCormick diagnosis that leaves the door open for January. Elliott on the field. And a quarterback hinting at a fix that’s already in the playbook.

But Herbig and Queen are new concerns, and the secondary is clearly a worry for the front office. The Steelers don’t need to be perfect in Philadelphia. They need to be healthier, cleaner and faster. If the tempo goes up and the injury list goes down, this team can get back on track quickly.
