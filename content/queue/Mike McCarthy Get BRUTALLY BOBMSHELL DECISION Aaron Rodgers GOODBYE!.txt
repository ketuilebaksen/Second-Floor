Steelers Nation, before we go anywhere today, let's put that headline on the table and look at it honestly.

Mike McCarthy did not get hit with some brutal decision. Nobody fired him. Nobody stripped him of play-calling. Nobody went over his head. In fact, it went the other way. According to RTE and Yahoo Sports, Art Rooney II said publicly that he has "100% confidence in Mike McCarthy." And according to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, as reported by NFL.com, McCarthy has the full support of the organization.

The brutal decision in this story was one McCarthy made himself. He benched Aaron Rodgers. Then, after Rodgers went on The Pat McAfee Show and unloaded on him, the Steelers released him. And then, on Black Friday, the team went out and beat the 8-2 Denver Broncos 27-5.

So yes, it's goodbye, Aaron Rodgers. That part of the headline is true. But the coach is still standing, and right now he's standing on a win.

Today I'm going to do something a little different. I'm going to open the full case file. What Rodgers alleged. What McCarthy said back. What we can actually confirm, and what we can't. The old Green Bay history that suddenly matters again. The money. Where Rodgers might go. And what all of this means heading into the Houston game.

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's get into it.

THE CASE FILE: HOW WE GOT HERE

Let's start with the setup, because the video I'm working from today does a good job laying it out.

McCarthy and Rodgers spent 13 seasons together in Green Bay, from 2006 to 2018. Super Bowl XLV, in February 2011, came against these same Steelers. So when McCarthy took over in Pittsburgh after Mike Tomlin stepped away, and Rodgers came back for one more year, it looked like a storybook.

And Rodgers himself had a hand in it. According to NFL.com, on McAfee he said: "I did vouch for him, so I am accountable with this." He also said, according to NFL.com: "I'm not a victim here. I am accountable."

According to NFL.com, Rodgers said he signed a one-year deal with $22 million guaranteed this spring, partly because the Steelers hired McCarthy. And back on May 20, he announced this would be his final season.

So this was a partnership both men walked into on purpose. That's what makes the ending so jarring.

EXHIBIT A: WHAT RODGERS ALLEGED

Now, the allegations. I want to be careful here, because these are Rodgers' claims. They are not findings. Nobody outside that building can prove them or disprove them right now.

According to RTE and NFL.com, Rodgers said McCarthy fell asleep and was snoring in a quarterback meeting on November 18, the Wednesday before the Eagles game. He said McCarthy missed meetings and walkthroughs later that week.

According to Yahoo Sports, Rodgers also claimed McCarthy was behind on film study.

And the detail that went viral: according to NFL.com and Yahoo Sports, Rodgers said that at halftime of the loss in Philadelphia, McCarthy broke a huddle of offensive players with "1, 2, 3, Packers."

The video I'm working from also quotes Rodgers saying there were five people in the room who would sign an affidavit backing him up. I'm working from the transcript there, so treat that line as the video's version.

EXHIBIT B: THE ULTIMATUM

Here's the part both sides actually agree on: there was an ultimatum.

According to NFL.com, Rodgers said he told McCarthy: "If you try and bench me or don't change the play-calling, then you should just release me."

According to Yahoo Sports, Rodgers framed his push to take play-calling away from McCarthy this way: "Fine, let somebody else call it, that's what's in the best interest of the team."

And according to Yahoo Sports, McCarthy described it like this: "There was an ultimatum made clearly outside of his job description, and so it was my decision to bench him."

So we have the same conversation, told from two angles. Rodgers says he was trying to save the season. McCarthy says a player tried to dictate a coaching decision. Both of those can be true at the same time, by the way. But in the NFL, only one person gets to make that call, and it isn't the quarterback.

EXHIBIT C: HOW RODGERS FOUND OUT

This is the part of the story that I think bothered a lot of people, on both sides.

According to NFL.com, Rodgers said he learned he'd been benched from tight end Pat Freiermuth, not from McCarthy.

But there's context. According to NFL.com, Rapoport reported that the team's announcement came after "continuous" efforts to reach Rodgers that Tuesday, and after the team was in contact with his agent, Dave Dunn. The Steelers announced at 11 p.m. Eastern Tuesday night that Rodgers would be inactive and that Mason Rudolph would start against Denver.

So the Steelers' side is that they were trying to reach him. Rodgers' side is that the head coach didn't tell him. Again, two versions of the same night.

EXHIBIT D: MCCARTHY'S RESPONSE

McCarthy held a news conference a few hours after the McAfee appearance, and he picked his spots carefully.

According to NFL.com, he declined to go through Rodgers' specific claims one by one, and said his focus was on Denver. On the "Packers" huddle, according to NFL.com, he called the halftime moment emotional and embarrassing. According to Yahoo Sports, he admitted the mistake.

On the broader allegations, the video quotes him calling it "a difference of opinion" from what he'd been told about the comments. NFL.com describes it as McCarthy saying he'd heard different accounts. Same idea.

According to Yahoo Sports, he also denied holding any grudge against Rodgers.

And then the line that's going to follow him for the rest of the season, one way or the other. When he was asked whether he's the right man to lead this team, according to NFL.com, McCarthy said: "I know how to win, and that's the facts."

That's a bold sentence to say in the middle of a two-game losing streak. Two days later, his team scored 27 points and gave up five. So, for one week at least, the facts backed him.

EXHIBIT E: THE OWNER SPEAKS

Now, the voice that really settled this inside the building. Art Rooney II.

According to RTE, Rooney said: "I take responsibility for ensuring the team and entire organisation meets our standards."

He also said, according to RTE: "I am disappointed in where this went with Aaron Rodgers, but I have 100% confidence in Mike McCarthy."

And: "Decisions were made on what we believe is in the best interest of the Pittsburgh Steelers."

And then, according to Yahoo Sports, Rooney criticized Rodgers for going public, saying "you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure."

Steelers Nation, when the Rooney family speaks publicly like that, the debate is over. That's not a man hedging his bets on his head coach.

EXHIBIT F: THE RELEASE

According to NFL.com, Rapoport reported that the Steelers' original plan was to keep Rodgers on the roster after the benching. That plan changed after the McAfee appearance.

The release was made official on Wednesday's league transaction wire, according to NFL.com, and Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette was first with the news.

The video points out that the team's announcement was just a handful of words. No thank-you, no tribute. I'm going by the video on the exact wording. But it's fair to say the Steelers didn't send him off with a highlight package.

THE OLD GREEN BAY FILE

Now here's where this story gets a sense of déjà vu.

According to the video, Rodgers said on McAfee that McCarthy still blames him for being fired in Green Bay in 2018. And McCarthy's answer to that, according to the video, was short: "That's not accurate." Yahoo Sports confirms McCarthy denied having a grudge.

The video also says a former Packers staffer told reporters after the McAfee appearance that he remembered Rodgers making similar complaints back in 2017 and 2018. And it says one source texted ESPN after the interview that "It's all true." I couldn't verify either of those, so please treat them as the video's reporting, not mine.

But there is a real, documented piece of history here. Back in 2019, Bleacher Report's Tyler Dunne published a long story on the breakdown of the McCarthy-Rodgers relationship in Green Bay. According to NFL.com, one of the accusations in that piece was that McCarthy skipped team meetings for massages. McCarthy's response at the time, to NFL Network's Tom Pelissero, according to NFL.com: "to say that I skipped a team meeting for a massage is utterly absurd."

And here's the twist. After that 2019 story came out, Rodgers himself pushed back on it. According to theScore and Boston.com, he called the report "a smear attack" by "irrelevant, bitter players." And according to FOX6, he said: "I love Mike McCarthy."

So in 2019, Rodgers defended McCarthy against claims about missed meetings. In 2026, Rodgers is the one making claims about missed meetings. Make of that what you will. I think it tells you how much this relationship changed in one season.

WHAT I CAN'T CONFIRM, AND WHY I'M NOT GUESSING

I want to be straight with you about where the evidence runs out.

The snoring, the missed walkthroughs, the film study. None of that has been confirmed by anybody on the record, other than Rodgers. McCarthy didn't confirm it. The team didn't confirm it. And the players who've spoken since the release have talked about rallying around McCarthy, not about backing up those specific claims.

The one detail both sides agree on is the "Packers" slip. And honestly, a coach who spent 13 years yelling "Packers" in a huddle saying it by accident in a heated halftime locker room? That's embarrassing, but it's not a scandal.

So I'm not going to tell you who's lying. I don't know. What I can tell you is what the people with the power to decide did, and how the players responded.

THE LOCKER ROOM VERDICT

And the players' response is the most important part of this case file.

According to Yahoo Sports, the team's veteran leadership council met on Wednesday to talk through players' concerns about Rodgers' claims. NFL Network's James Palmer reported that the meeting left McCarthy "humbled" and gave him a chance to explain his decisions.

And this is the detail I found most interesting. According to Yahoo Sports, McCarthy admitted he hadn't spent enough time with the defense, a point Rodgers had raised. So the coach took at least one piece of the criticism and owned it.

Then the players spoke after the win.

Jalen Ramsey, according to Yahoo Sports: "For the past couple days, we as players, we were like, 'Coach, you good.' Let us turn this up, let us get in our zone."

Mason Rudolph, according to Yahoo Sports: "I think the leadership council rallied around coach McCarthy." And: "I think in a weird way, the adversity probably brought us closer together."

T.J. Watt, on his conversations with McCarthy about his role, according to Yahoo Sports: "We've had a lot of really good conversations, especially recently."

That's three of the biggest voices in the locker room, all pointing the same direction. When a quarterback and a head coach go to war, players pick a side. This time, they picked the coach.

THE SCOREBOARD RESPONSE: 27-5

And then they backed it up.

The Steelers beat Denver 27-5 on Black Friday at Acrisure Stadium. According to Yahoo Sports, that ended Denver's six-game winning streak, and the Steelers are now 7-4 and still unbeaten at home.

According to Yahoo Sports, Pittsburgh led 14-0 after the first quarter. That matters, because slow starts had been killing this team. Not this time.

Mason Rudolph went 9 of 15 for 142 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions. According to Yahoo Sports, McCarthy told Rudolph Tuesday night that he'd be starting, and it was his first NFL start in 368 days.

And according to NFL Network's Michael Florio, as reported by Yahoo Sports, Rudolph's EPA per dropback of 0.19 was the second-best mark by a Steelers quarterback this season. Rodgers topped that only once, in Week 3 against Cincinnati.

That's not me saying Rudolph is better than Rodgers. It's one game. But it was a clean, efficient game, and that's all the Steelers needed.

THE RUN GAME, FINALLY

The other thing that changed? The Steelers ran the football like they meant it.

According to Yahoo Sports, the Steelers ran for just 73 yards in the loss to Philadelphia. Against Denver, they ran it 31 times for 117 yards.

And McCarthy had been saying it all week. According to Yahoo Sports, on Tuesday he said: "We need to run the ball better, but we need to run it more."

That's a coach responding to criticism with an adjustment, not just a press release. Whether that came from Rodgers' complaints, from the film, or from both, the result is what matters.

WHAT MCCARTHY SAID AFTERWARD

McCarthy didn't take a victory lap after Friday's win.

According to Yahoo Sports, he said: "I do want to recognize the players and coaches. I mean, this is not easy, especially on the players."

He also said: "We got to keep building off of the things that we've done well."

And from his position, he said, "we still have work to do."

He also gave credit to quarterbacks coach Tom Arth, according to Yahoo Sports, for the work he's done with Rudolph and the younger quarterbacks. That's a coach spreading credit around, which is exactly what a locker room needs to hear after a week like that.

THE RUSSELL WILSON TWIST

Here's a fun footnote. Russell Wilson, who played quarterback in Pittsburgh in 2024, had been critical of the way Rodgers handled this on CBS. After the win, according to Yahoo Sports, he posted on X: "Great response by the Steelers and Mike McCarthy! Huge win versus a really good Broncos team."

So the same ex-Steelers quarterback who questioned how Rodgers went out is now publicly toasting the coach. That tells you where a lot of people around the league landed on this.

FOLLOW THE MONEY

Now, let's talk about something the headlines skipped: the money.

According to RTE, because no team claimed Rodgers off waivers, the Steelers owe the roughly $8.6 million left on his one-year, $22 million deal.

RTE also reports that the Steelers may try to get some of that back through a grievance over Rodgers' public conduct. That hasn't happened yet, as far as I can tell, so I'd file that under "possible," not "coming."

And according to RTE, if another team signs Rodgers, it can do so at the veteran minimum, with the Steelers covering the rest. So any team that wants him can get him cheap. The question is whether anyone wants him at all.

WHERE DOES RODGERS GO? THE HONEST ANSWER

According to Yahoo Sports, ESPN's Adam Schefter texted several general managers and came away doubting Rodgers will play in the NFL again. His words: "Now, could somebody give him a shot? Sure. I'm not expecting that."

And according to Yahoo Sports, Dianna Russini reported that his "options appear limited."

There's been plenty of speculation. NBC Boston and Bleacher Report both floated New England, with Drake Maye dealing with a fractured fibula, according to Bleacher Report. They also floated Baltimore, which would put Rodgers back in the AFC North. Atlanta, Minnesota and the Giants came up too.

But here's the key thing. Both NBC Boston and Bleacher Report say there are no reports of any team actually reaching out. Those are analysts' guesses, not news. And Bleacher Report pointed out the obvious problem: any team that signs him has to deal with the public-relations fallout from what he said about McCarthy.

So my honest answer is that I don't know, and right now, it doesn't look like the league knows either.

THE BALTIMORE WRINKLE

I do want to say one thing about the Baltimore idea, because it's the one that would matter most to us.

According to NBC Boston, the Ravens are 4-7, with Lamar Jackson dealing with his thumb injury and Jacoby Brissett not providing enough. And the Steelers play Baltimore twice in the final four weeks of the season, on December 20 at home and in Week 18 in Baltimore.

Could Rodgers end up facing the Steelers in a Ravens uniform? It's possible, in theory. NBC Boston even noted he could offer intel on division rivals. But the Ravens are deep in "too little, too late" territory, according to NBC Boston, and there's no report of interest. So it's a fun what-if. Nothing more, for now.

SECOND TIME AROUND

One more historical note from the video. This is the second time in two seasons that a team has released Rodgers. The Jets let him go before the 2025 season, and now Pittsburgh has done it mid-season.

That's not how anyone pictured the final chapter of a four-time MVP's career. And whatever you think of how he handled this, I don't think any Steelers fan should forget what he did for this team last season, when he helped get them back to the playoffs.

You can be grateful for that and still believe the team made the right call this week. Those two things aren't in conflict.

THE TRANSCRIPT CHECK

Quick housekeeping on the video I'm working from, because a few things need fixing.

The video says the Steelers lost 19-6 in Philadelphia. It was 19-16. That's a big difference, because the Steelers actually had a chance late in that game.

The video also says the Steelers had lost two in a row with a depleted receiving corps. That was true at the time. But Roman Wilson is back now, and he caught a 37-yard pass against Denver.

And the video was made before the Denver game, so it ends with the release. We now know what came next. The team won, and won big.

WHAT CHANGES WITHOUT RODGERS

So what does this team look like going forward?

First, the quarterback room is thinner. Mason Rudolph is the starter, and behind him, according to everything we've seen this season, are Will Howard and rookie Drew Allar, neither of whom has played a regular-season snap. If Rudolph gets hurt, this gets very complicated, very fast.

Second, the offense probably leans even more on the run game and the defense. That's not a bad thing. It's the formula that won on Friday.

Third, the locker room has one fewer storyline. For a team that's been answering Rodgers questions for two months, that alone might be worth something.

THE HOUSTON TEST

And now comes the real test. The Houston Texans.

According to NFL.com, the Steelers' next games are against Houston and then Jacksonville. Houston comes to Acrisure on Sunday night, December 6.

You know the history. Houston ended last season with a 30-6 playoff win in Pittsburgh. That was Mike Tomlin's last game as head coach. So this one is personal for a lot of players in that locker room.

And with the short week behind them, the Steelers now get extra time to prepare. That's a full week for Rudolph to run the offense with the first team. That's time for Alex Highsmith's ankle to heal. And that's time for McCarthy to build on what worked against Denver.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board this week. Opinion, not a report.

The McCarthy question: settled for now. Ownership backed him, the players backed him, and the team won. He still has to prove it over the last six games, but the job isn't in danger right now.

The Rodgers allegations: unproven, and likely to stay that way. I'm not going to treat them as fact, and I'm not going to dismiss them either. What I'll judge is what happens on the field.

The defense lesson: McCarthy admitting he hadn't spent enough time with the defense is a good sign. Keep that going.

The run game: 31 carries is the number. Keep it there.

Rudolph: he's earned the Houston start without any debate. Then we reassess.

The money: if the Steelers file a grievance, fine, but I'd let it go and focus on football.

Rodgers' next stop: I'd be surprised if he plays again this season. But I've been surprised by Aaron Rodgers before.

FINAL WORD

Here's where I land, Steelers Nation.

The headline wanted you to believe McCarthy was the one getting hit with a brutal decision. The truth is the opposite. McCarthy made the brutal decision. He benched a four-time MVP, the team released him, and the coach's job got more secure, not less.

Was it messy? Absolutely. Was it fair to Rodgers? That depends on whose story you believe. But the people who decide these things, the owner, the front office and the players in that locker room, all lined up behind the coach. And then they went out and backed him up with a 27-5 win over the hottest team in the AFC.

Now they have to do it again against Houston. That's the only verdict that really matters.
