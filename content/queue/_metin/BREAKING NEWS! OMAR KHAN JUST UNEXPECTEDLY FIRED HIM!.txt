Steelers Nation, the Jalen Ramsey trade talk isn’t going away. Since ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler floated the idea of the Detroit Lions calling Pittsburgh, it’s spread to Sports Illustrated, to Lions sites, to every corner of the internet, and some of the videos about it are getting the basic facts badly wrong. So today I’m not going to give you another round of “should they or shouldn’t they” based on vibes. I’m going to give you five numbers. Five numbers that tell you exactly who Jalen Ramsey is in 2026, what he’s worth, what a trade would really cost, and why the Steelers’ week of roster moves might matter more than any rumor. And at the end, I’ll tell you which number I think decides this whole thing. This is James with Second Floor, let’s get into it.

First, quick cleanup, because one version of this story I watched was full of mistakes, and if you saw it, you deserve the real facts. Ramsey isn’t a “promising young player.” He’s 31 and turns 32 at the end of this month. The Lions aren’t an AFC contender, they’re in the NFC. Patrick Peterson and Minkah Fitzpatrick aren’t anchoring Pittsburgh’s secondary; Peterson isn’t on the team, and Fitzpatrick went to Miami in the trade that brought Ramsey here in the 2025 offseason. Ramsey didn’t break his wrist “last season.” He broke it in Week 3 this year, against Cincinnati, and played through it in Cleveland four days later. And Fowler never said Kansas City or Detroit would split Ramsey’s salary. The salary-split idea was about Chiefs cornerback Kristian Fulton. Fowler’s actual line about Ramsey was simply: “Is it worth calling Pittsburgh for Jalen Ramsey? Not sure he’s available.” That’s it. That’s the whole rumor. Okay, now let’s do the numbers.

Number one: three.

Three is the number of snaps Jalen Ramsey has played at outside cornerback this season. Three. According to the charting cited this week by Lions-focused coverage, he’s played 104 snaps in the slot, plus time at free safety and down in the box. He hasn’t been a full-time outside corner since 2024, his last year in Miami.

Why does that matter? Because the entire premise of the Lions rumor is that Detroit needs a cornerback. And as one Lions writer put it this week, Ramsey would be more of a slot and safety option for them than a true cornerback fix. That actually lowers the odds of a deal. If Detroit’s biggest need is a boundary corner who can line up across from a team’s number one receiver, Ramsey in 2026 isn’t really that guy anymore.

Now, to be fair to Detroit, its problems aren’t only at corner. The Lions are dead last in the league against the pass, giving up around 304 yards a game. Safety Avonte Maddox needed season-ending foot surgery. Brian Branch is working back from an Achilles injury, and Kerby Joseph has been out too. So a guy who can play nickel and free safety does have value for them right now. But here’s the catch: if Branch and Joseph get healthy over the next few weeks, Detroit’s safety problem shrinks, and its cornerback problem is still there. That’s why a lot of people around the Lions think they’d rather chase a true outside corner. Ramsey fits a short-term hole, not the long-term one.

And from Pittsburgh’s side, it tells you something too. Patrick Graham isn’t using Ramsey as a traditional corner because he doesn’t need to. He’s using him as the movable piece, the guy who goes wherever the leak is. That’s a valuable role. But it’s a different role than the one Ramsey’s reputation was built on, and that’s why his trade value isn’t what fans think it is.

And Detroit has a cheaper path, too. Kristian Fulton, the Chiefs corner Fowler mentioned, has played in only three games this season, and five million dollars of his 9.75 million dollar base salary is guaranteed. That’s exactly why Fowler floated the idea of the two teams splitting that money. If the Lions can add an actual outside corner that way, for a late pick, they have little reason to pay real value for a 32-year-old slot and safety. Which is one more reason I think this rumor is louder than it is likely.

Number two: thirty-two.

Ramsey turns 32 at the end of October. And when you look at his career, the price tag has dropped with every trade. Jacksonville sent him to the Rams in 2019 for two first-round picks and a fourth. The Rams sent him to Miami in 2023 for a third-round pick and a tight end. Miami moved him to Pittsburgh in 2025 in a deal built around Minkah Fitzpatrick. First-rounders, then a third, then a player swap.

Now, his production last season was still solid. In 2025, his first year in Pittsburgh, he started all 17 games and posted 86 tackles, three sacks, an interception and eight passes defensed, and he was voted to the Pro Bowl Games. That’s a good season by any standard, especially at that age. But this year, through four games, he has 17 tackles, one pass defensed, one forced fumble and no interceptions. Pro Football Focus has him at a 68.6 overall grade, with a 69.6 in coverage. Respectable. Not elite. And he’s doing it while playing with a broken wrist, which makes those numbers more impressive, not less. But the market doesn’t grade on a curve for toughness.

And here’s the part I think gets overlooked. Ramsey’s value to Pittsburgh this season is bigger than his value on the trade market, and the gap between those two numbers is the whole argument. To Detroit, he’s a rental. To a Steelers team with a 42-year-old quarterback on his final ride and T.J. Watt in his prime, he’s one of the few experienced voices in a secondary that’s been flipped upside down in a week. When a player is worth more to you than to anybody else, you don’t sell him. You use him.

And let me give Ramsey his due on the toughness part. In Cleveland, four days after breaking that wrist, he missed only one snap. One. He played in a cast, on a short week, on the road, in a division game. You can debate his grade or his position, but nobody can question what he’s willing to put on the line for this team. That matters in a locker room, especially one that’s been through as much as this one has in the last two weeks.

Number three: nineteen and a half.

That’s roughly how many millions of dollars Ramsey counts against the Steelers’ cap this season, about 19.5 million. He’s under contract through 2028, with void years after that. And here’s where it gets interesting, because the contract makes Ramsey look a lot like a rental for whoever holds him next.

Here’s how it works in plain English. If Pittsburgh traded him, the bonus money the Steelers have already paid him would stay on Pittsburgh’s books as dead money. Detroit, or any other team, would only take on the rest of his 2026 salary. Then next spring, that team could decide whether to keep him at his 2027 salary, which Sports Illustrated put around 19 million dollars, or let him go. Some Lions coverage has even pointed out that Detroit could release him before that 2027 money kicks in without getting stuck with dead cap of its own.

Pittsburgh’s own decision next spring looks like this. Keep him at that 2027 number, restructure him to lower it, or move on. Sports Illustrated’s breakdown says that if the Steelers release him, they’d still carry over 16 million dollars in dead money in 2027. That’s not a cheap exit either. So whichever way you slice it, there is no version of this contract where Pittsburgh walks away for free. The only question is when they choose to absorb the cost.

So think about what that means. For the acquiring team, Ramsey is a low-risk rental for a playoff push. For Pittsburgh, trading him doesn’t erase his contract cleanly. They’d still carry dead money. That’s not a deal-breaker, but it means the cap savings people talk about are smaller than they sound. And if the Steelers were already planning to make a decision on him next offseason, they can make that decision then, on their own timeline, without trading him in October.

Number four: five.

As in a fifth-round pick. That’s what one Lions writer suggested would be a fair price for Ramsey this week. Sports Illustrated’s Noah Strackbein, writing on the Steelers side, landed in a similar place, saying Pittsburgh would “likely get a mid-round pick,” and arguing the trade “makes sense,” with Brandin Echols able to take over in the slot. Earlier in the week, another Steelers site suggested something around a fourth.

And put that next to what Pittsburgh got for Joey Porter Jr. a week ago. Dallas sent a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder for a 26-year-old starting corner in the final year of his rookie deal. Ramsey is six years older, on a bigger contract, and playing a different position. So yes, his return should be lower than Porter’s. But there’s a big gap between “lower than a second-rounder” and “a fifth.” If I’m Omar Khan, my starting point is a third-round pick or a young player who can contribute right now. Anything less, and you’re better off keeping him for the season and making the contract decision yourself in March.

So let me put my opinion right on the table, and this is opinion, not a report. A fifth-round pick for a three-time All-Pro who just made the Pro Bowl and is the most versatile defensive back on your roster is not a trade. It’s a donation. And I don’t buy the “Echols can just slide in” argument either, not this week. Which brings me to the most important number of all.

Number five: two.

Two is the number of cornerbacks the Steelers signed off other teams’ practice squads in the span of about 48 hours this week. On Monday, it was Sean Murphy-Bunting from Tampa Bay. And on Tuesday, a move that flew under the radar for a lot of fans: Pittsburgh signed cornerback Terell Smith off Washington’s practice squad and put him on the 53-man roster.

Let me tell you about Smith, because a lot of people missed this. He’s 27, a fifth-round pick by the Chicago Bears in 2023 out of Minnesota. He’s played 26 regular-season games with six starts. In 2024 with Chicago, he had 19 tackles, four pass deflections and an interception, which came against Jordan Love. Then he tore his patellar tendon in August of 2025 and missed that entire season. The Bears waived him at final cuts this year, Washington put him on its practice squad in September, and now he’s a Steeler. Physically, he’s interesting: six feet, 204 pounds, almost 33-inch arms, and he ran a 4.41 forty coming out. And he’s played both outside spots.

And here’s an important correction, including for things I said earlier this week. Signing Smith used the open spot on Pittsburgh’s 53-man roster. So the “open roster spot waiting for Trevon Diggs” isn’t open anymore, at least not right now. If the Steelers want to add Diggs, they’d have to clear room first. I’ll come back to that in a second.

And about that Echols argument from Sports Illustrated. Brandin Echols is a useful player, and he can play the slot. But remember, he only just came back from the concussion protocol before the Cleveland game. Asking him to take on Ramsey’s full-time role, plus the communication responsibilities that come with being the guy who moves around the formation, is a lot to put on him in the middle of a season. It’s one thing to say a backup can play the slot. It’s another to say he can replace a three-time All-Pro who’s been the defense’s Swiss Army knife.

But the bigger point is this. When a team signs two cornerbacks off practice squads in two days, it’s not getting ready to trade a third defensive back. It’s scrambling to find enough healthy bodies to field a secondary. Joey Porter Jr. is in Dallas. Jamel Dean has missed practice all week with his ankle, and while McCarthy said he’s feeling better than expected, he’s still expected to miss Sunday. Ramsey has a broken wrist. Murphy-Bunting missed all of last season. Smith missed all of last season too. Daylen Everette is a rookie. That’s the room. You don’t take another piece out of that room for a fifth-round pick.

So which number decides it? For me, it’s the last one, the two. Two corners off practice squads in 48 hours. That number tells you the front office knows exactly how thin this secondary is. A team in that situation doesn’t sell Ramsey in October. Maybe it revisits it in the spring, when Dean is healthy, when the rookies have a season under their belts, when the 2027 salary decision is on the table. But not now.

Now let’s talk about the rest of the trade picture, because with Smith on the roster and a key receiver going down, things just shifted.

On Thursday, Mike McCarthy said Michael Pittman Jr. will miss “multiple weeks” after re-injuring the foot that cost him Week 2. Pittman was limited Wednesday and didn’t practice Thursday, and the team is still evaluating whether to place him on injured reserve. That’s a real blow on offense. And it’s a cruel twist, because Sunday was supposed to be his first game against the Colts, the team he left this offseason.

But here’s the roster angle. If Pittman goes to injured reserve, that opens a spot on the 53. And that’s where the Diggs conversation can come back to life. Trevon Diggs is still on Seattle’s practice squad, he played five years under McCarthy in Dallas, and signing him would cost nothing but a roster spot. Opinion, not a report: if Pittman lands on IR, I’d use that spot on Diggs before the Tampa game, not on another body at receiver. The defense is where this team is bleeding.

There’s also some good news in this week’s injury report, and you deserve to hear it. Jalen Ramsey was back on the practice field in a limited role Thursday, and he’s expected to play against Indianapolis. Rico Dowdle was limited for a second straight day and was seen working in drills, which means he has a real chance to make his return from that toe injury, although the team could hold him one more week. Cameron Heyward took a rest day Wednesday and practiced fully Thursday. And DeShon Elliott’s 21-day practice window officially opened Thursday. He’s still on injured reserve, but The Athletic’s Mike DeFabo reported that the team is targeting a Week 7 return. So Elliott likely misses the Colts and the Bucs, but he could be back for the Saints game in Paris.

So let’s put it all together for Sunday. Against the Colts, the Steelers’ secondary will probably look like this: Asante Samuel Jr. on one side, with Everette and Murphy-Bunting competing for the other outside spot, Terell Smith as a new depth piece, and Ramsey doing what he’s done all season, moving between the slot and safety with a cast on his wrist. Up front, Derrick Harmon is on injured reserve, so Keeanu Benton and Yahya Black have bigger roles. On offense, no Pittman, maybe Dowdle back, and Jaylen Warren hopefully getting a lot more than the 20 touches he got in Cleveland.

And I’d watch the new guys closely. The Colts will almost certainly test whichever corner lines up opposite Samuel. If it’s Murphy-Bunting, it’ll be his first regular-season action in more than a year. If it’s Everette, it’s another big step for a rookie. And if Terell Smith gets snaps, it’ll be his first meaningful game since that patellar tendon injury. The best thing Ramsey can do on Sunday isn’t to make a highlight play. It’s to make sure the young and new guys around him are lined up right on every snap. That’s the job that doesn’t show up on a stat sheet, and it’s exactly why you don’t trade him.

That’s not the roster of a team that should be selling. That’s the roster of a team that needs everybody it has and probably one more. And it’s T.J. Watt’s birthday on Sunday, so I expect that building to be loud.

Now, to be fair, what would change my mind? Three things. One, if the Steelers lose to the Colts and the Bucs and fall to 2-4 heading toward the deadline. Two, if Dean and Elliott both come back healthy, and the young corners start holding up, so the room isn’t so desperate anymore. And three, if a team offers real value, a Day 2 pick or a young starter, not a fifth-rounder. If all three of those happen by early November, then yes, I’d listen. Until then, I’m not even picking up the phone.

Here’s my final word on Ramsey. Fowler asked a fair question from Detroit’s side. Lions fans are right to dream about it. Steelers writers are right to at least do the math. But when you actually look at the numbers, three outside snaps, 32 years old, 19.5 million against the cap, a fifth-round price tag, and two emergency corners signed in two days, the answer for Pittsburgh in October is no. Keep the veteran. Heal the room. And if anybody’s changing teams before November 10th, make it a cornerback coming to Pittsburgh, not one leaving.
