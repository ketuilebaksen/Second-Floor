Steelers Nation, I've been doing this channel for a long time, and I'm not sure I've ever had to open a video like this.

The headline says Aaron Rodgers was benched. That's true. But it's not the whole story anymore, because it went much further than a benching.

Here's what happened, in the order it happened. On Monday, Mike McCarthy said publicly: "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback." On Tuesday night, the Steelers announced that Mason Rudolph would start Friday against the Denver Broncos, and that Rodgers would be inactive. On Wednesday, Rodgers went on The Pat McAfee Show and openly criticized McCarthy. And on Wednesday afternoon, the Steelers released him.

The four-time MVP, the man McCarthy won a Super Bowl with in Green Bay, the quarterback who said this would be his final season, is no longer a Pittsburgh Steeler. And the team has to play an 8-2 Broncos team on Friday.

So today, we're going to walk through exactly what happened, what both sides said, what it means for Rudolph and this offense, and how the Steelers move forward with a week like this behind them. And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's go.

A QUICK CORRECTION FIRST

The video I'm working from today was recorded after the benching but before the release, so it treats this as a one-game decision. That part has been overtaken by events.

It also says the Steelers lost 19-6 in Philadelphia. The final was 19-16, according to the Associated Press. With everything else going on, I want to keep the basic facts straight.

TUESDAY NIGHT: THE BENCHING

Let's start with the announcement.

According to NFL.com, late Tuesday evening, the Steelers issued a statement through longtime director of communications Burt Lauten. It said simply: "Aaron Rodgers will be inactive for the game on Friday." Mason Rudolph would start.

No reason was given in the statement. And Rodgers wasn't on Tuesday's injury report, according to NFL.com, so this wasn't a health decision.

ESPN's Brooke Pryor reported that it was a coaches' decision, made over the course of the day. According to NFL.com, Pryor reported it was partly to spark the offense and partly because of Rodgers' recent struggles. And the Post-Gazette's Gerry Dulac reported that the situation had been "brewing for a while."

Yahoo Sports noted just how surprising the timing was. A day earlier, McCarthy had given Rodgers what looked like a clear vote of confidence.

WEDNESDAY: THE MCAFEE INTERVIEW

Then Wednesday happened.

According to the AP, Rodgers took an excused absence from practice, and Rudolph took snaps with the starters. Rodgers then appeared on ESPN's Pat McAfee Show and criticized McCarthy at length.

According to the AP, Rodgers said he learned about the benching while on the phone with teammates, including tight end Pat Freiermuth, rather than from McCarthy himself.

And he confirmed the core of what had been reported about Tuesday's meeting. According to the AP, Rodgers said he told McCarthy: "If you try and bench me or don't change the play calling then you should just release me."

He also said: "He responded with, I'm going to do what's right by the organization, which in his mind was getting rid of me." And: "Now obviously he got the owner and the GM on his side."

According to the Ravens' own website, which recapped the day's AFC North news, Rodgers also told McAfee he wouldn't return to the team. His words: "That ship has sailed."

THE ALLEGATIONS

Rodgers went further than play-calling. And I want to handle this part carefully, because these are allegations made by one side of a very public breakup.

According to the AP, Rodgers said his complaints to McCarthy included the coach falling asleep in quarterback meetings and missing other team events in the week before the Eagles game. He also said that at halftime in Philadelphia, McCarthy broke up the locker room meeting by saying "1-2-3 Packers."

Those are Rodgers' claims. McCarthy has not confirmed them. And according to the AP, McCarthy declined to discuss personal conversations when he spoke to reporters. I'm not going to go beyond what's been reported, and I'm not going to speculate about anyone's personal situation. But you need to know what was said, because it's a big part of why this ended the way it did.

WEDNESDAY AFTERNOON: THE RELEASE

According to Steelers.com, the team announced it had released Rodgers on Wednesday afternoon. The team's announcement didn't include a statement from anyone. Just the move, and his numbers.

According to Heavy, citing Dulac, Rodgers gave McCarthy an ultimatum in a Tuesday evening meeting: he wanted to call all the plays, "or just release me." Dulac reported the team called his bluff.

There's an important detail here from NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, as cited by Heavy. The original plan was for Rodgers to stay with the team after the benching. That plan changed after his television appearance on Wednesday.

And NFL Network's Mike Garafolo reported, according to Heavy, that the Steelers had planned to have quarterbacks coach Tom Arth relay plays to Rodgers. That would have changed the communication, while still letting McCarthy call the plays. In other words, there was a compromise on the table, at least on the play-calling side.

WHAT MCCARTHY SAID

McCarthy met with reporters on Wednesday afternoon.

According to Heavy, he said: "There was an ultimatum made that was clearly out of his job description." And: "It was my decision to bench him."

According to the AP, McCarthy said there had been "continuous efforts" to reach Rodgers, whom he's known for two decades. He said he had a "very direct, very honest" meeting with the team's players' council. And he called the situation "a growth opportunity."

He also said he couldn't pinpoint the exact timeline of the release, according to the AP.

HOW THE LOCKER ROOM REACTED

This is the part that matters most for Friday.

According to the AP, when Freiermuth was asked whether McCarthy can lead the team, he said only: "I'm confident."

Receiver Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who played with Rodgers in Green Bay, acknowledged frustration with how the team has been playing. But he said, according to the AP: "I didn't think it was this."

And longtime beat reporter Jim Wexell described a chaotic scene. His words, according to the AP: "This locker room is going to really need somebody to stand up."

That's the challenge now. Not just the X's and O's. The leadership.

WHY THE STEELERS MADE THE MOVE: THE NUMBERS

Let's step back from the drama and look at the football, because the benching didn't come out of nowhere.

According to NFL.com, Rodgers this season threw for 2,241 yards, 16 touchdowns and six interceptions, with an 86.6 passer rating and just 6.2 yards per attempt. He was sacked 31 times.

According to PFN, those 31 sacks cost the Steelers 257 yards, the most in the NFL. And according to the Ravens' recap, citing Next Gen Stats, his 3.7 air yards per completion were the lowest among quarterbacks with at least 300 dropbacks.

In Philadelphia, according to the AP, he averaged half a yard of air distance per attempt in the first half. That was the lowest since ESPN began tracking the stat in 2006.

And according to NFL.com, the Steelers had been held under 200 passing yards in four straight games. They rank 23rd in total offense, according to Yahoo Sports.

Those numbers tell you why McCarthy felt he needed a change. Whether he handled it the right way is a separate question. But the football case for trying something different was real.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE NUMBERS

To be fair to Rodgers, the season wasn't all bad.

According to the AP, he threw three touchdown passes in three straight weeks earlier this season, including a win over Joe Burrow and Cincinnati in September. He led the team to a 6-2 start and first place in the AFC North going into the bye. And his receiver room was decimated by injuries for much of the year.

So this isn't a story of a quarterback who was terrible all season. It's a story of a quarterback, a coach and an offense that stopped working together in November.

THE END OF A GREAT CAREER, MAYBE

Let's take a moment to recognize what we're talking about here.

According to Steelers.com, over 22 seasons, Rodgers started 267 of 274 games, completed 9,104 passes for 68,515 yards, and threw 543 touchdowns against 129 interceptions. He won four MVPs and a Super Bowl.

According to Yahoo Sports, he spent 13 seasons with McCarthy, including that Super Bowl win in Green Bay, and won two of his four MVPs under him. That's what makes this ending so hard to watch. These two did something historic together.

Rodgers has said this would be his final season. NFL.com noted that Sunday's loss in Philadelphia may have been his last NFL game. Whether another team picks him up for the rest of the season, I don't know, and I'm not going to guess.

RODGERS IN PITTSBURGH: THE FULL CHAPTER

It's worth remembering how this chapter started.

According to Steelers.com, Rodgers signed a one-year deal with the Steelers in 2025 and started 16 games, completing 327 of 498 passes for 3,322 yards and 24 touchdowns. The team made the playoffs, but according to the AP, that season ended with a blowout home playoff loss to Houston.

Then, in the offseason, the Steelers hired McCarthy to replace Mike Tomlin, and Rodgers re-signed for one more year, saying this would be his last. On paper, it was a perfect reunion. The coach and quarterback who won a Super Bowl together, back together for one final run.

According to Steelers.com, this season he started 10 games, completing 220 of 361 passes. The team went 6-4 in those starts. And now, about a year and a half after arriving, it's over, at least in Pittsburgh.

WHAT HAPPENS TO RODGERS NOW?

This is the question everyone's asking, and the honest answer is that nobody knows yet.

Rodgers is free to sign with another team, depending on how the release process plays out. He's said this is his final season. He's 42, and he turns 43 next week, according to the AP. He could look for a contender that needs a veteran quarterback for a playoff push. Or he could decide this is the end.

Pat McAfee, who knows Rodgers as well as anyone in media, posted a short message on X after the benching, according to PFN: "This feels large." That about sums it up.

I'm not going to predict where Rodgers lands. But I'll say this: whatever happens next, the way his time in Pittsburgh ended will be part of his story, and that's a shame for a player of his stature.

THE HISTORICAL COMPARISONS

The AP put this in historical context, and it's sobering.

Johnny Unitas was benched by the Chargers for Dan Fouts in 1973. Joe Namath never played again after 1977. And Peyton Manning was replaced by Brock Osweiler in Denver in 2015, before coming back to win a Super Bowl.

Legends don't always get the endings they deserve. This might be one of those cases.

NOW: MASON RUDOLPH

So let's talk about the man who takes over.

According to Yahoo Sports, Rudolph is a seven-year veteran who was drafted by Pittsburgh in the third round in 2018. He has 38 career appearances and 19 starts, with a 64.2 percent completion rate, 30 touchdowns and 22 interceptions.

The video I'm working from says his record as a starter is 9-9-1. I couldn't confirm that exact record, so treat it as the video's figure.

What everyone remembers is the end of 2023. According to the AP, Rudolph came off the bench in December and led the Steelers to a playoff berth. PFN says he went 3-0 as a starter in that stretch.

This season, according to NFL.com, he's appeared in four games and gone 1-of-4 for two yards in mop-up duty. So he's been mostly a spectator for months.

THE 2023 RUN, REMEMBERED

For newer fans, here's what that late-2023 stretch looked like. Rudolph took over in December with the season slipping away. As I recall, he beat Cincinnati, won in Seattle and beat Baltimore to get the Steelers into the playoffs, before the season ended with a Wild Card loss in Buffalo.

That run is a big reason Steelers fans have always had a soft spot for him. He came in with nothing to lose and played loose, confident football. That's exactly the mindset he needs on Friday.

The difference this time is the opponent. Those were three games against teams that, at the time, weren't playing at the level of an 8-2 Denver team. This is a much bigger test.

WHAT RUDOLPH NEEDS TO DO ON FRIDAY

The video asks the right questions. Can Rudolph bring energy and confidence? Can he protect the football? Can he connect with the receivers and make the important throws?

My view is that the first goal is simple: get the ball out and protect it. Denver leads the NFL with 17 fourth-quarter sacks, according to FOX Sports. Rudolph doesn't need to be a hero. He needs to keep the offense on schedule, let Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle carry the load, and take the deep shots when they're there.

And in my opinion, there's one thing a younger quarterback might bring: a little more willingness to push the ball down the field. Rodgers' air yards this season were historically low. If Rudolph can create even a few more downfield chances, and the receivers are healthy, that could open things up.

THE BACKUPS BEHIND HIM

Here's a detail that matters a lot if anything goes wrong.

According to NFL.com, the quarterbacks behind Rudolph are second-year Will Howard and rookie Drew Allar. Neither has played an NFL down. And at least one of them will be active on Friday.

Remember back in August, when the Steelers kept four quarterbacks on the 53-man roster and a lot of people questioned it? That decision looks very different now.

WHAT MCCARTHY HAS TO DO THIS WEEK

This week isn't just about game-planning for Rudolph. It's about leadership.

McCarthy said he had a "very direct, very honest" meeting with the players' council, according to the AP. That's a good first step. But Rodgers' public comments put McCarthy's credibility on the line in front of his own players. And Freiermuth's two-word answer, "I'm confident," tells you players are being careful with what they say.

So McCarthy needs to show his team that he's prepared, organized and in control. Every meeting, every practice, every walkthrough this week is going to be watched more closely than usual. The best way to answer Rodgers' criticism isn't with words. It's with a well-prepared team on Friday.

THE PLAY-CALLING QUESTION GOES AWAY, AND COMES BACK

The play-calling dispute was at the center of this.

Rodgers wanted more control. McCarthy, according to Garafolo's reporting cited by Heavy, was willing to change how plays were relayed through Tom Arth, but still wanted to call them himself.

With Rodgers gone, McCarthy keeps the play sheet. That puts even more on him. If the offense works on Friday, it'll look like he was right. If it doesn't, the criticism is going to come straight at the head coach.

THE OTHER STORYLINES THIS WEEK

With all of this going on, it's easy to forget there's an actual football game on Friday. So let's run through the other updates.

Alex Highsmith: McCarthy said it's going to be "tough for him to make it this week," according to Steelers.com. He didn't practice Tuesday with the ankle injury he suffered in Philadelphia.

Nick Herbig: limited on Tuesday with his hip and rib injuries, according to Steelers.com. Still uncertain for Friday.

That means Jack Sawyer and KJ Henry could carry a much bigger role opposite T.J. Watt.

Michael Pittman Jr.: estimated as a full participant on Tuesday, according to Steelers.com. He's still on injured reserve, but McCarthy said he's "very confident" Pittman should play.

Roman Wilson: back from injured reserve, with three catches for 43 yards in Philadelphia, according to Bleacher Report.

Max Hurleman: six catches for 61 yards and the touchdown in his debut, according to Bleacher Report. The video says he was named the Steelers' player of the week, which I couldn't confirm. He reverted to the practice squad after the game, so he needs another roster move to play.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE RECEIVERS

Here's an interesting angle. The video points out that Pittman's experience and physical style could give Rudolph a valuable target.

That's true. And it's also worth noting that Rudolph has history in this building. He's taken practice reps with this receiver room for two seasons, and Hurleman has been on the practice squad for most of that time. There may be more familiarity there than people expect.

THE UNIFORMS AND THE ATMOSPHERE

One lighter note from the video. The Steelers are scheduled to wear their Color Rush uniforms for the Black Friday game. As the video says, nobody in Pittsburgh will care about the uniforms if the team doesn't get the job done.

But I'll say this. The atmosphere at Acrisure Stadium on Friday is going to be unlike anything we've seen in a long time. A new starting quarterback. A coach under a microscope. And a fan base that just watched a legend leave in the most dramatic way possible.

WHERE THE STEELERS STAND

According to Yahoo Sports, the Steelers are 6-4 and currently the AFC's seventh seed, the final wild-card spot. According to the Ravens' recap, Cincinnati leads the AFC North at 7-3 after losing to Washington, and Pittsburgh and Cleveland are both 6-4.

And the schedule is brutal. According to the AP, the next three games are Denver, Houston and Jacksonville. The Steelers are also 2-4 on the road.

So there's no time to sit and process this. The season is still very much alive. But the margin for error just disappeared.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for this week. Opinion, not a report.

The decision to bench: defensible on the numbers. The offense needed something different, and four straight games under 200 passing yards is hard to ignore.

The way it unfolded: messy on both sides. A Monday vote of confidence followed by a Tuesday benching was always going to look bad. And taking the dispute to national television made a release almost inevitable.

Rudolph: start him and give him a real runway, not just one game. Short passes early. Lean on the run.

The backups: Will Howard active as the No. 2 on Friday. Let Allar keep developing.

Leadership: Cam Heyward and T.J. Watt need to be front and center this week. The locker room needs veteran voices.

Pittman: activate him. Rudolph needs every reliable target he can get.

Hurleman: elevate him again. He's earned it.

Defense: if Highsmith and Herbig are both out, Patrick Graham has to scheme pressure. Don't wait for one-on-one wins.

McCarthy: he chose this path. Now he owns it. The next three games will define his first season.

FINAL WORD

Here's my bottom line. This is one of the most dramatic weeks in recent Steelers history.

A four-time MVP was benched, went on national television, and was released within about 24 hours. The coach who won a Super Bowl with him made the call. And now a backup quarterback who hasn't started a game this season has to face one of the best teams in the AFC on four days of preparation.

There's no sugarcoating it. This is a test of the entire organization. The coaching staff, the leadership in the locker room, and the players who are left.

But here's what I keep coming back to. This team is still 6-4. It's still in playoff position. And it has a chance on Friday, in front of the home crowd, to show that it's bigger than any one player.

The Steelers have been through quarterback turmoil before, and they've come out the other side. Rudolph himself is proof of that from 2023. Nobody expected that run either.

Let's see what they've got.
