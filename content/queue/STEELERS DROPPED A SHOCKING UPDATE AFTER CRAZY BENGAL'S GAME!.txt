Steelers Nation, I’m not going to sugarcoat this one. Sunday night in Cincinnati was ugly. The Bengals beat the Steelers 38 to 18, took sole possession of first place in the AFC North, and did it in front of a national audience.

And the bad news didn’t stop at the final score. Before the game even started, the Steelers lost a starting safety in warmups. Before halftime, they lost a starting guard to a cart. And after the game, the head coach and the quarterback both delivered messages that told you exactly how the locker room is taking it.

So today, we’re doing the full breakdown. The two injury updates that matter most going forward. How the game got away so fast. What Joe Burrow and Ja’Marr Chase did to this defense. What Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers said afterward, including the exchange that’s already making headlines. Where the Steelers stand now. And my board for the Philadelphia trip, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

THE FINAL: BENGALS 38, STEELERS 18

Here are the basics, according to Steelers.com and the Associated Press.

Cincinnati won 38-18 at Paycor Stadium. The Bengals improved to 7-2 and moved into sole possession of first place in the AFC North. The Steelers fell to 6-3, one game back. Pittsburgh’s four-game winning streak is over. Cincinnati has now won five straight.

And it wasn’t close. The Bengals led 24-3 at halftime and 31-3 going into the fourth quarter. Pittsburgh trailed the entire game.

The season series is now split, one game apiece. So the September win in Pittsburgh still matters, but it no longer gives the Steelers the head-to-head edge it once did.

SHOCKING UPDATE NUMBER ONE: DESHON ELLIOTT HURT IN WARMUPS

Let’s start with the news that broke before kickoff, because it shaped everything that followed.

Safety DeShon Elliott, who had practiced in full on Thursday and Friday and had no injury designation, hurt his knee during pregame warmups. The team announced he was “ruled out for tonight’s game vs. Cincinnati,” according to ClutchPoints.

After the game, McCarthy didn’t say much. “During his warm-up, he had something that showed up,” he said, according to ClutchPoints. That was it. No details, no timeline.

That’s a brutal break. Elliott had been healthy for only two games this season, and he was supposed to be back after the bye. Instead, rookie seventh-round pick Robert Spears-Jennings had to step in with almost no warning, against the best passing attack in the league.

And you saw how that went. Without Elliott, the back end struggled to contain Burrow and Chase. The team will wait for more clarity on the knee before the Eagles game.

SHOCKING UPDATE NUMBER TWO: MASON MCCORMICK CARTED OFF

The second injury may be even more significant long-term.

Late in the first half, Rodgers was sacked on third down. On that play, starting left guard Mason McCormick went down with a right lower-leg injury. According to Steeler Nation and Steelers Now, the medical staff put his leg in an air cast, and he was carted off the field. The team announced it as an ankle injury and ruled him out.

What happened next tells you a lot about this locker room. According to Steelers Now, teammates gathered around McCormick, and every Pittsburgh player came over to send him off.

McCarthy spoke about it after the game. “Just the love for him is very evident and it continues in the training room right now,” he said. “We will speak on that when the time is right.”

That’s not the language of a minor injury. I’m not going to guess at a diagnosis, but you should prepare for the possibility that McCormick misses time.

WHAT MCCORMICK’S INJURY MEANS FOR THE LINE

Here’s why this one hurts so much.

According to Steeler Nation, McCormick had played every offensive snap this season. He, center Zach Frazier and tackle Troy Fautanu had built real chemistry on the left side over several months.

His likely replacement is Spencer Anderson, who started the season as the right guard before Brock Hoffman took that job. Anderson knows the system. But continuity on the offensive line is hard to replace, especially in the middle of a season.

And remember the conversation from last week. Post-Gazette reporter Christopher Carter said the Steelers’ line was “middle of the road” in pass protection, and that protecting a 42-year-old quarterback was the real problem with this offense. Losing a starting guard doesn’t make that problem any easier.

HOW THE GAME GOT AWAY

Now let’s walk through how this game unraveled, because it happened fast.

According to Steelers.com, Cincinnati’s first quarter was lopsided, and the Bengals jumped out to a 17-0 lead before Pittsburgh had a real scoring chance. The Steelers didn’t pick up their first first down until well into the second quarter.

And the few chances the offense created kept getting erased. A Jaylen Warren catch-and-run was wiped out by a penalty. Rookie Germie Bernard made a 23-yard catch that was overturned when the Bengals challenged, because he didn’t get his feet down inbounds.

Late in the second quarter, Pittsburgh finally got on the board. Rodgers was sacked on 3rd-and-8 from the Cincinnati 18, the play where McCormick got hurt, and Chris Boswell kicked a 45-yard field goal with 1:25 left. That made it 17-3.

Then Burrow answered with a 57-yard touchdown drive in just 1:16. Halftime: 24-3. That sequence summed up the night. Every time Pittsburgh did something, Cincinnati did more, faster.

THE FOURTH QUARTER

The Bengals pushed the lead to 31-3 heading into the fourth.

Pittsburgh’s offense finally woke up late. According to Steelers.com, Rodgers hit DK Metcalf for a touchdown on 4th-and-4 from the Cincinnati 22, then found Metcalf again for the two-point conversion. That made it 31-11 with 12:16 left.

Cincinnati answered with a 1-yard touchdown run by Samaje Perine to make it 38-11.

Then Rodgers found Pat Freiermuth for a 27-yard touchdown with 7:19 to play. Final: 38-18.

By then, Burrow had already been pulled for Joe Flacco, according to the AP. That tells you how comfortable Cincinnati felt.

WHAT BURROW AND CHASE DID

Let’s give the Bengals their due, because this was a performance.

According to the AP, Burrow completed 35 of 44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns, with a 135.7 passer rating. In the first half alone, he was 26 of 34 for 302 yards and three touchdowns. According to the Bengals, those first-half completion and yardage totals were the second-most in a first half in franchise history.

Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards. According to the Bengals, that’s the most receiving yards by any player in the league this season. Running back Chase Brown caught two touchdown passes. Drew Sample and Dohnte Meyers caught the other two, according to Steelers.com.

As a team, Cincinnati put up 484 yards, according to Heavy, and held the ball for 35 minutes and 9 seconds.

Here’s the stat that worries me most. Neither team turned the ball over. That means Pittsburgh’s defense didn’t lose this game because of a short field or a fluke. It lost because Cincinnati moved the ball at will.

THE PRE-GAME NUMBERS VS. THE RESULT

The transcript for today’s video was recorded before kickoff, and it laid out what Pittsburgh was facing. It cited Burrow with 2,386 passing yards and 23 touchdowns, Chase with 53 catches and seven touchdowns, and Tee Higgins with 758 receiving yards. Those Burrow numbers line up with Sports Illustrated’s pregame figure of 298.3 yards per game.

The transcript’s key questions were: could Pittsburgh pressure Burrow, and could it limit explosive plays from Chase and Higgins?

The answers were no and no. Burrow was sacked only once, by Patrick Queen, according to Steelers.com. And Chase had 146 receiving yards in the first half alone, according to the Bengals.

Higgins, for what it’s worth, was mostly quiet, with five catches for 66 yards. But with Chase doing that much damage, it didn’t matter.

RODGERS’ NIGHT

Now the offense.

Rodgers finished 15 of 29 for two touchdowns and no interceptions. Steelers.com and Heavy list him at 143 passing yards, while the AP listed 129, likely because of how sack yardage is counted. Either way, Heavy noted it was his lowest yardage total of the season. He was sacked three times, according to the AP.

The start was the problem. Sources differ slightly on the exact count, but Rodgers opened the game with six or seven straight incompletions. By the time he connected, the Bengals were already in control.

The transcript had noted that Rodgers entered the game with 1,871 passing yards and 13 touchdowns. His two late touchdowns bring him to 15, by my count. But numbers padded in garbage time don’t change the story of this game.

THE BENGALS’ PLAN WORKED

Remember last week, when we broke down a Bengals fan panel whose key to the game was simple: stop Jaylen Warren, and the game is over?

I disagreed with them. They were right.

According to the AP, Warren had just 41 yards from scrimmage on seven carries. That’s a dramatic drop from the 176 he piled up in the September win. And because Pittsburgh fell behind so quickly, the Steelers couldn’t stay patient with the run game.

Rodgers acknowledged it after the game. According to Steeler Nation, he credited Cincinnati’s run-defense plan and admitted the offense didn’t take advantage of what the Bengals gave them.

That’s the lesson. When Pittsburgh is on schedule, Warren is a weapon. When it falls behind 17-0, he barely touches the ball.

THE FREIERMUTH STORY

One more note from the transcript.

Before the game, Pat Freiermuth was added to the injury report on Saturday with an illness and listed as questionable. The transcript worried about losing him, citing his 25 catches for 235 yards and four touchdowns through eight games.

Freiermuth played. And he scored that 27-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter. If the transcript’s pre-game numbers are right, that’s his fifth touchdown of the season.

One quick correction to the transcript: it describes Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter as quarterbacks. They’re defensive backs. The transcript also mentions a tight end named Robert Tonyan as a possible replacement for Freiermuth. I couldn’t confirm Tonyan’s current status with the team, so I’ve left that out of the analysis.

THE BRIGHT SPOTS ON DEFENSE

There weren’t many, but let’s be fair to the guys who played hard.

According to Steelers.com, linebacker Payton Wilson had 17 tackles, and Patrick Queen added 14 plus the only sack of the night. Jalen Ramsey forced a fumble, and T.J. Watt tipped a pass at the line.

Derrick Harmon, activated from injured reserve on Saturday, was eligible to play. I couldn’t find his individual stat line in the recaps I read, so I won’t guess at his impact. But it’s clear the pass rush, as a whole, couldn’t get home against a quarterback who got the ball out quickly and accurately.

WHAT MCCARTHY SAID

Now to the press conference.

McCarthy didn’t make excuses. “Disappointed in the performance, clearly,” he said, according to the Tribune-Review’s Chris Adamski. “We were beaten decisively.”

According to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, he also said: “That’s clearly not to the standard of the Pittsburgh Steelers.” And: “It’s not to any of our expectations.”

He admitted he had worried about this exact scenario. “You come off a bye week, you worry about a letdown,” McCarthy said. “I’ve seen it happen before. Been part of it.” And he added a line that applies to every good team in this league: “Handling success is the biggest challenge in this league.”

But he also made it clear he won’t let one loss turn into a crisis. “We’re not going to drive down the drama road of those types of things,” he said.

THE LETDOWN McCARTHY WAS AFRAID OF

That bye-week comment deserves a closer look, because it’s a pattern coaches talk about all the time.

Pittsburgh went into the bye on a four-game winning streak, with a dominant defense and a league-leading sack total. The Steelers had a full week off, a full week of practice and almost every key player back healthy. On paper, that’s the ideal setup.

Instead, the offense came out flat. Six or seven straight incompletions. No first down until the second quarter. A 17-0 hole before anything went right.

McCarthy said he’d seen it happen before and had been part of it. That’s an honest admission from a coach with a lot of NFL experience. Long layoffs can disrupt rhythm, especially for an offense that relies on timing. And when your opponent comes out sharp, you don’t get time to shake off the rust.

The good news is that this is fixable. The bad news is that Philadelphia won’t wait for Pittsburgh to find its rhythm either.

RODGERS AND THE “DRAMATIC” EXCHANGE

And then came the moment that’s already making the rounds.

After the game, according to Steeler Nation, ESPN’s Brooke Pryor asked Rodgers how the team could keep this loss from changing its season. Rodgers’ response: “Oh, that’s very dramatic there, Brooke.”

He followed with his reasoning. He’s seen enough “big wins and tough losses,” he said, “so we will deal with this appropriately.”

But he didn’t dodge the result. “We played a division rival on the road and got beaten up pretty good,” Rodgers said. And according to Sportsnet Pittsburgh: “We kind of shot ourselves in the foot with a couple plays and couldn’t really get going and didn’t hit any passes.”

DID RODGERS TAKE A SHOT AT MCCARTHY?

You may also see headlines claiming Rodgers took a direct shot at McCarthy’s play-calling. Let’s look at what he actually said.

Asked about carrying over offensive success, Rodgers said: “There’s no carryover. It starts over every single week.” He added that the team had been good in the no-huddle this season.

That’s it. He didn’t name McCarthy. He didn’t criticize a specific call. Even Steeler Nation, which ran one of those headlines, acknowledged that reading it as a shot at McCarthy is speculative.

So I’d file this under “overblown.” Rodgers and McCarthy said essentially the same thing: they got beat, they own it, and they’re not going to let the drama take over.

WHAT THE SECONDARY HAS TO FIX

Let’s talk about the biggest problem on the field, because it’s not going away on its own.

Chase’s 231 yards weren’t the result of one or two blown coverages. He did damage all night, against multiple defenders, in multiple ways. And Burrow completed nearly 80 percent of his passes. That means Cincinnati found answers to everything Pittsburgh showed.

Part of that is Elliott’s absence. A veteran safety who organizes the coverage and communicates checks is exactly what you need against a quarterback who reads the field that quickly. Losing him in warmups forced Patrick Graham to rework his plans on the fly.

But part of it is depth. Remember, Pittsburgh waived cornerback Terell Smith on Saturday to make room for Harmon. That was the right move at the time, but it left the secondary one body thinner, and then Elliott went down.

Going forward, the Steelers need more help over the top for their young defensive backs, more disguise in their coverages, and a pass rush that gets home faster. Burrow is the best quarterback they’ll face this season. But Philadelphia, Houston and Jacksonville all have weapons too.

THIS ISN’T 2024, AT LEAST NOT YET

Some fans are already bringing up 2024, and I understand why.

That year, Omar Khan bought at the trade deadline, adding Mike Williams and Preston Smith. Then the Steelers lost five straight to end the season. Khan said afterward: “the result was we lost five straight games and that’s not good enough.”

But this is one loss. One. To a division rival on the road, playing as well as any team in football right now. The Steelers are still 6-3, still in the thick of the playoff race and still getting players back from injury.

What would make it look like 2024 is if this loss becomes a streak. That’s why the Eagles game matters so much. A win in Philadelphia, and Sunday night becomes a bad night. A loss, and the questions get much louder.

WHERE THE STEELERS STAND NOW

So let’s zoom out.

At 6-3, the Steelers are one game behind Cincinnati in the AFC North with eight games to play. That’s not a crisis. But it’s a real gap, and the Bengals look like a team that isn’t going to give much back. According to the Bengals, they’ve scored 30 or more points in five straight games, the longest streak in franchise history.

The schedule doesn’t get easier. Next Sunday, November 22, Pittsburgh visits Philadelphia at 4:25 p.m. Then it’s a short week before the Black Friday home game against Denver.

And there’s a personal milestone hanging out there, too. Before Sunday, Rodgers was one win away from tying Drew Brees for the fourth-most wins by a starting quarterback in NFL history, including the postseason, according to Yahoo Sports. That chase continues in Philadelphia.

THE DIVISION MATH

Here’s a quick look at the division picture.

With the season series split, the head-to-head tiebreaker between Pittsburgh and Cincinnati is a wash for now. As the tiebreakers are generally understood, the next step would be division record, then records against common opponents, and so on down the list.

What that means in practice is simple: the Steelers need to win games and hope the Bengals drop one or two along the way. A one-game deficit with eight games left is very much recoverable. But it requires Pittsburgh to play much better than it did Sunday night.

THE INJURY PICTURE GOING FORWARD

Here’s where the roster stands heading into the Eagles game.

Elliott is uncertain with the knee. McCormick is uncertain with the ankle, and the language from McCarthy doesn’t sound encouraging.

On the positive side, Roman Wilson is now eligible to come off injured reserve, since the Bengals game is behind us. Sebastian Joseph-Day is eligible to return for the Denver game, according to Steelers Now. And Michael Pittman Jr. has been running on a treadmill and outside in cleats. He told Steelers Now he hoped to return after the bye, but added: “can’t go out there if I’m not effective.”

So the Steelers will be juggling a lot of moving parts this week. Every activation will need a roster spot, and every loss on the line puts more pressure on the backups.

WHAT AWAITS IN PHILADELPHIA

The Eagles will be a different kind of test.

Philadelphia made one of the biggest moves of the trade deadline, sending defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-rounder, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer. That changes the look of the Eagles’ interior, and it might give Pittsburgh’s run game an opening.

If the Steelers can get Warren going early in Philadelphia, they can play the kind of game they wanted to play in Cincinnati. Control the clock, keep the opposing offense on the sideline, and stay out of obvious passing downs. That’s the formula. Sunday night, they never got the chance to use it.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for Philadelphia. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Left guard: start Spencer Anderson and give him as much help as possible. Chip with tight ends and backs. Keep Rodgers’ drop-backs short early.

Roman Wilson: designate him to return this week. The offense needs another receiver who can get open quickly, and he’s caught a long touchdown this season.

Safety: if Elliott can’t go, give Spears-Jennings a simplified plan and more help over the top. Don’t leave a rookie alone in coverage against a top receiver.

Run game: commit to Warren from the first drive. Even if the first few carries don’t work, stay with it. Falling behind early is what took the run game away on Sunday.

Pass rush: more creative pressure. If the four-man rush isn’t getting there, bring a fifth rusher and make the quarterback decide faster.

Mindset: McCarthy’s right. No drama. One bad loss in November doesn’t define a season, but how the team responds in Philadelphia might.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers didn’t just lose Sunday night. They lost a starting safety before kickoff, a starting guard before halftime, and first place in the division by the end of the night.

But this is also the moment the McCarthy era gets tested for real. The first eight games showed this team could win. Now we find out how it handles getting knocked down. McCarthy said no drama. Rodgers said they’ll deal with it appropriately. Philadelphia is where they have to prove it.
