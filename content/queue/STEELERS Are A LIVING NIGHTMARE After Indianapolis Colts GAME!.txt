Steelers Nation, that is how you answer a bad week. Pittsburgh 34, Indianapolis 22. After the Thursday night loss in Cleveland, after a week where everybody was talking about the sideline, the chemistry, and a coach who said his house wasn’t clean, the Steelers came home and played their most complete game of the season. Jaylen Warren did something nobody in the NFL has done in 40 years. Aaron Rodgers passed two all-time legends in the same afternoon. A rookie receiver made his first career start and played like a veteran. And a defense that got called out all week held one of the best running backs in football to 45 yards. The Steelers are 3-2, and today we’re going through ten things I loved about this win, then we’ll talk about the one problem it created, and what Omar Khan should do about it. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s celebrate this one.

Number ten: the response to adversity, right from the opening drive.

I want to start with something most people will forget by Wednesday. Pittsburgh’s first drive was a disaster. Thanks partly to a holding call, it went backward, minus 15 yards. Warren’s first touch went nowhere, and his second lost five yards. The Colts won the toss, deferred, and kicked a 58-yard field goal to take the early lead. A week ago, that start might have snowballed. Not this time. On the very next possession, the Steelers went 80 yards on one play. That tells you everything about the mood in that building. Nobody panicked. Nobody pointed fingers. They just went back out and fixed it.

Number nine: Mike McCarthy’s week of preparation worked.

Remember what McCarthy said after the Cleveland loss? The team needed to “clean our own house.” Well, after this game, he had the perfect line: “As we say in Pittsburgh, we redd it up, we redded it up.” If you’re not from western Pennsylvania, “redd up” means clean up, and that’s exactly what they did.

And here’s the part I really respect. McCarthy explained that the team spent Tuesday focusing on itself, reviewing its own mistakes before turning to the Colts. He said, “I think taking Tuesday and focusing on ourselves was the right call.” He also said, “The newness of how you do things in year one, this was clearly our best week.” A first-year head coach admitting that the week before wasn’t good enough, changing the routine, and then getting the result? That’s leadership. People questioned him all week. He answered on the scoreboard.

Number eight: Germie Bernard is ready.

With Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve, rookie Germie Bernard made the first start of his NFL career. And he didn’t just survive. He produced. Six catches, 80 yards, both career highs, including grabs of 21 and 25 yards that moved the chains in big spots.

Rodgers loved it. He said, “But I was really proud of Germ the way he stepped in.” And then he added, “He’s a smart guy and knows his stuff, and he’ll be ready to take on a bigger role this week, I’m sure.” When a 42-year-old future Hall of Famer talks about a rookie like that, pay attention. Bernard has earned Rodgers’ trust, and in this offense, trust is everything.

Number seven: Pat Freiermuth was the red-zone weapon.

Two touchdown catches for Freiermuth. The first one was a 1-yard score in the first quarter that put Pittsburgh up 14-6. The second one might have been the biggest play of the game. Early in the fourth quarter, the Colts had cut the lead to 24-22 with a field goal. The building got nervous. Then, on third-and-6, Rodgers found Freiermuth for a 17-yard touchdown to make it 31-22 with 6:35 left. That’s a dagger. Freiermuth finished with five catches for 44 yards and two scores. When the receiver room is banged up, a tight end who can win in the red zone becomes priceless, and Freiermuth delivered exactly when it mattered.

Number six: Chris Boswell closed the door.

Boswell was perfect. A 48-yard field goal right before halftime to make it 17-13. Four extra points. And then the one that ended it: a 54-yard field goal with 3:43 left to make it 34-22, a two-score game. In a game where the Colts’ kicker, Spencer Shrader, was busy all day with five field goals, Boswell matched him with the bigger kicks at the bigger moments. And I’ll come back to special teams in a minute, because there’s a punter video going around this week that we need to talk about.

Number five: the red-zone defense won this game.

Here’s the stat I want every Steelers fan to remember. The Colts kicked five field goals. Five. Every one of those was a drive where Indianapolis moved the ball and then got stopped. The Colts went 1-for-3 in the red zone. And on third down, the Steelers stopped them on 11 of 15 attempts.

Think about how this game could have gone. Daniel Jones threw for 267 yards. The Colts had chances. If even two of those field goals become touchdowns, we’re talking about a completely different ending. Instead, the defense bent and didn’t break, again and again. Cam Heyward said it best: “I think that’s the deciding thing in the game.” That’s old-school Steelers football: make them earn every inch, and when they get close, slam the door.

And the Colts knew they couldn’t play it safe against this defense. They went for it on fourth down five times, and they needed every one of those gambles just to stay in the game. When an opponent has to keep rolling the dice like that, it means your defense is winning the normal downs. That’s a group that played with an edge from the first snap to the last.

Number four: Jonathan Taylor was shut down.

Coming in, the scariest player on that Colts roster was Jonathan Taylor. The week before, he had run for 95 yards and two touchdowns against Washington. On Sunday? Fifteen carries for 45 yards. That’s three yards a carry for one of the best backs in the league.

Alex Highsmith explained it simply. He said the Steelers “stopped him” up front by getting “off blocks.” That’s the formula. When your defensive front is beating blocks, there’s nowhere for a running back to go. And the stars of that effort deserve some love. Payton Wilson had a team-high 10 tackles, two of them for a loss, and he broke up a pass on fourth-and-17. Jalen Ramsey, playing through his wrist injury, made nine tackles. Asante Samuel Jr. broke up four passes. And Heyward got the only sack of the game. Remember all the talk about this defense being in trouble? It made the Colts one-dimensional, and that was the plan from the start.

Number three: Aaron Rodgers, history maker.

Let me just read you what Rodgers did on Sunday, because it’s ridiculous. He completed his first 11 passes. He finished 22 of 31 for 235 yards and three touchdowns. His first incompletion didn’t come until 6:14 left in the second quarter.

And then the history. With this win, Rodgers earned his 166th regular-season win as a starter, which moves him into sole possession of fifth place all-time, ahead of Ben Roethlisberger. He passed Peyton Manning for third place in NFL history in touchdown passes, postseason included, with 580. Only Drew Brees and Tom Brady are ahead of him. He tied Roethlisberger for the most touchdown passes of 80 yards or more in NFL history, with nine. And this was his third straight game with three touchdown passes. In franchise history, only Terry Bradshaw and Roethlisberger had done that, and Rodgers is the first to do it in three straight weeks in a single season.

Forty-two years old. Steelers Nation, we are watching something special, and we need to enjoy it while it lasts.

And remember the noise from the week before? The Thursday night broadcast in Cleveland caught Rodgers and McCarthy swearing at each other on the sideline, and for days that’s all anyone wanted to talk about. Here’s what McCarthy said after this game: “I thought Aaron did a great job of staying on time.” And he added, “I thought his pocket presence was excellent, his ability to move with his feet and make plays out of the pocket.” That’s not a coach and a quarterback who are fighting. That’s a coach and a quarterback who are on the same page. The so-called drama lasted one week. The win is what people will remember.

Number two: Rodgers and the offensive line.

This one doesn’t show up on the highlight reels, but it’s the reason everything else worked. After the game, Rodgers said, “I thought the line blocked really well.” He took only one sack. The running lanes were there all afternoon. And remember, Indianapolis has a strong interior with DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart. The Steelers’ line won that battle.

Not long ago, the line was a question mark after the changes on the right side. On Sunday, it looked like a unit that’s starting to come together. If that continues, this offense has a much higher ceiling than people think.

Number one: Jaylen Warren made history.

And here he is. The star of the show. Jaylen Warren finished with 188 yards from scrimmage, a new career high. He ran for 101 yards and a touchdown on 15 carries, and he caught six passes for 87 yards and another touchdown.

But it’s how he did it. In the first quarter, Rodgers dumped the ball off to him in the flat, and Warren took it 80 yards for a touchdown. Then in the third quarter, with the Steelers up 17-16, he broke a 50-yard touchdown run to make it 24-16. According to the team, he became the first NFL player since Herschel Walker in 1986 to have a touchdown catch of 80-plus yards and a touchdown run of 50-plus yards in the same game. That’s 40 years.

And let me give some credit to a guy who didn’t catch a touchdown on that 80-yard play: DK Metcalf. When Warren turned up the sideline, Metcalf was out in front of him, blocking downfield. That’s a star receiver doing the dirty work for a teammate. Metcalf is getting double-teamed and shadowed every week, and on a day when the ball went elsewhere, he still found a way to impact the scoreboard. That’s what winning teams look like.

And it’s not a one-week thing. That’s his third straight game with at least 125 yards from scrimmage, and his second 100-yard rushing game of the season, which already matches his total from all of last year. He also picked up a huge fourth-and-2 with his legs.

Want some perspective on how good he’s been? Coming into this game, Warren was averaging 5.6 yards per carry. Among NFL running backs, only Kenneth Walker III, James Cook, and Bijan Robinson were higher. That’s elite company. And with Rico Dowdle out since dislocating his toe in Week 2, Warren has carried the load, and McCarthy admitted the workload has been “heavy.” He said, “There’s a number you like to continue to give him,” and that Warren “is crushing it right now.” Asked whether he’s ready to be the bell cow, Warren’s answer was perfect: “Whenever my name’s called.”

Cam Heyward summed up his running style in four words: “He finishes every play.”

The praise after the game was huge. McCarthy said, “He’s the man,” and “He’s hauling it right now for us.” He also called him “a three-down player” who “can beat you inside zone, outside zone.” Rodgers said, “He’s just such a slippery player. First guy rarely brings him down.” And then this: “He’s one of the most liked players on the team, so guys want to block for him.” When a reporter suggested Warren doesn’t get enough national attention, Rodgers said simply that he should.

And Warren? Humble as always. “We won. That’s all that matters to me.” And, “It’s an honor. Like I said, it’s a credit to my teammates for achieving stats like that.” That’s the kind of player you want in your locker room.

And here’s the good news on top of the good news: McCarthy said, “I look for Rico to practice this week and hopefully be up for the game. He was close.” Rico Dowdle has missed three straight games with his toe injury. If he’s back for Tampa Bay, the Steelers have a one-two punch that can wear defenses down.

Now, the problem this win created.

Here’s the bad news, and I won’t hide it. The receiver room got hit hard. Ben Skowronek hurt his calf while covering a punt and didn’t return. Roman Wilson got hurt late in the first half on a target where the ball came loose, and he didn’t return either. CBS reported a dislocated finger, but the severity of both injuries is still unknown. By the end of the game, Pittsburgh had four healthy receivers: DK Metcalf, Bernard, Brandon Smith, and Kaden Wetjen.

T.J. Watt also took a poke to the eye before halftime, but he came back out after the break wearing a visor, so that one looks okay for now. And we’ll get the first official injury report on Wednesday.

Think about what that means. Pittman is on IR. Wilson and Skowronek left this game. And the Steelers still put up 34 points. That’s a credit to the coaches and to Rodgers. But you can’t win every week with two healthy receivers catching passes. And that brings us to the trade market.

Let’s clear up the punter video first.

There’s a video going around this week saying the Steelers could trade for Raiders punter AJ Cole. Let me be clear: there is no report connecting AJ Cole to Pittsburgh. None. Cole is a very good punter. He’s been a first-team All-Pro twice and went to three Pro Bowls. In May of 2025, the Raiders gave him a four-year, 15.8 million dollar extension that made him the highest-paid punter in the NFL. He’s the longest-tenured special teamer the Raiders have, and there’s no sign they want to move him.

And Pittsburgh already has a punter. The Steelers brought back Cameron Johnston for 2026. So while a punter conversation makes for a fun video, it’s not the priority. If Khan is going to spend a draft pick or cap space right now, it has to go to the spot where the Steelers are running out of bodies: wide receiver.

The trade block: who could be coming.

And this is where the Harrison saga finally ended. Marvin Harrison Jr. is officially a Houston Texan. According to Pro Football Talk, Arizona gets a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick, while Houston gets Harrison and a 2027 seventh. So the Texans gave in and used the 2027 second-rounder the Cardinals wanted. And PFT pointed out that Houston may have moved fast in case injuries elsewhere on Sunday created more competition. Funny, isn’t it? Pittsburgh is exactly the kind of team that suffered those injuries.

So who could be next? Here’s my opinion, not a report. With Wilson and Skowronek now hurt, the question isn’t whether Khan should add a receiver. It’s how soon. Before Sunday, I said a cheap veteran like Calvin Ridley of the Titans made the most sense. After Sunday, I feel even more strongly that Pittsburgh needs one more experienced receiver who can play right away. Ridley has barely been used in Tennessee, and the Titans have a young room, so the price should be reasonable.

And the bigger swing is still out there. Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, a 24-year-old who had 1,282 yards as a rookie, has been the Jaguars’ third receiver this year. Jacksonville has said no to big offers before, and its GM called the rumors “fraudulent.” But with Harrison gone to Houston, Thomas is now the biggest young receiver name left in this conversation. And remember, the Steelers still have the 2028 second-round pick they got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. If Khan is going to use it, Thomas is the kind of player I’d use it on, not a short-term fix.

Whatever Khan decides, I don’t expect him to wait until November 10th if the injury news on Wednesday is bad. One more week like this one at receiver, and he has to make a call.

What’s next: Tampa Bay.

The Steelers go on the road to face the Buccaneers next Sunday. There’s a nice storyline here too: Jamel Dean, who missed the Colts game with his ankle injury, has been targeting the Tampa game for his return, and that’s the team where he spent the first part of his career. If Dean is back in the secondary and Dowdle is back in the backfield, this team gets healthier in two key spots right away.

And there’s another familiar face waiting in Tampa: running back Kenneth Gainwell, who signed with the Buccaneers this offseason after his time in Pittsburgh. So there will be some reunions on that field. But this Steelers team can’t afford to treat it like a feel-good trip. A win in Tampa would make it two in a row heading toward the bye, and it would turn one good Sunday into a real trend.

And let’s keep this in perspective. A week ago, this team was 2-2, coming off a loss, with the whole football world asking if the McCarthy era was already in trouble. Now the Steelers are 3-2, with a quarterback putting up historic numbers, a running back playing at an elite level, and a defense that just proved it can still dominate a good offense. Five games into a new coaching staff’s first season, that’s exactly the kind of growth you want to see.

And the momentum is real. Three-touchdown Rodgers, a 188-yard Warren, a rookie receiver who just found his footing, and a defense that just shut down Jonathan Taylor. This team went from “what’s wrong in Pittsburgh?” to “the Steelers are back” in seven days.
