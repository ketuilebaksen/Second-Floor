THE STATEMENT, THEN THE QUESTION

Steelers Nation, what a Sunday. Steelers 27, Browns 6. Eight sacks. Two hundred rushing yards. A 4-0 run since the loss in Cleveland. And Pittsburgh is now sitting alone at the top of the AFC North.

Now everybody’s asking the same thing: is the dream receiver on the way?

I’m going to answer that honestly, because some headlines this morning are saying the Steelers already acquired him. As of right now, they haven’t. No trade has been announced. Josh Downs is still a Colt. Brian Thomas Jr. is still a Jaguar. Nobody new is in the building.

But here’s what did change overnight, and it’s big. The win changed the math, and the reporting changed the market. And one new detail from Mike Florio might have just cleared the path for Pittsburgh to land the guy it actually wants.

I’m James, this is Second Floor. We’ll break down the win first, because it tells you exactly what this team needs. Then the receiver chase. Let’s go.

HOW THE BROWNS GOT BODY-SLAMMED

Let’s start with the game, because it was a beatdown from the first snap.

The defense held Cleveland to minus-1 yard of offense in the first quarter. Minus one. Rico Dowdle punched in a 9-yard touchdown run, and the Steelers led 7-0. Then came a Chris Boswell field goal from 20 to make it 10-0.

Cleveland got on the board with two Andre Szmyt field goals in the second quarter, and Boswell answered with one from 37. Then the moment of the half. Pittsburgh had the ball at the goal line with seconds left and no timeouts. Mike McCarthy passed on the short field goal. And Pat Freiermuth pushed it in on a 1-yard tush push as time was expiring. Troy Fautanu summed up that call perfectly: “Everyone knows what’s going on. See if you can stop it.” Steelers 20, Browns 6 at the half.

The dagger came early in the third quarter. Aaron Rodgers hit Darnell Washington for a 58-yard catch-and-run to the goal line, and Jaylen Warren finished the drive from a yard out. 27-6. Cleveland never scored again.

The numbers tell the story. Warren ran for 143 yards on 17 carries, tying his career high, on his 28th birthday. Dowdle added 56 yards and the opening touchdown. The Steelers piled up 200 rushing yards, a season high, and the Browns managed just 34. Washington finished with three catches for a career-high 94 yards.

EIGHT SACKS AND A RECORD FOR WATT

Then there’s the defense. Eight sacks, spread across seven players, according to the team’s recap. T.J. Watt had two. Patrick Queen and Cam Heyward had a sack and a half each. Nick Herbig had one. Kevin Jobity Jr. got the first sack of his career. Keeanu Benton and Alex Highsmith split one.

Watt also set a remarkable mark. He now has the most career sacks of any player against the Cleveland Browns in NFL history. The team’s own write-ups list slightly different totals, so I’ll skip the exact number, but the record is his. His reaction was perfectly T.J.: “Means I’ve been playing a long time.”

Queen, the guy whose name was on a trade list all week, led the team with 12 tackles, a sack and a half, and a forced fumble. His postgame line: “We just played ball.”

Heyward had six tackles, two for loss, a sack and a half, and a batted pass. And Cleveland went 0-for-3 in the red zone.

One more stat that tells you about this defense: during the four-game winning streak, Pittsburgh has allowed just 14.3 points per game, according to the Tribune-Review.

THE VERSE STORYLINE

You knew this was coming. Jared Verse talked all week. He’d said the Browns were “about to make Pittsburgh ours,” per the Tribune-Review. And early in the game, after the first touchdown, guard Mason McCormick lifted Verse up and slammed him during the extra point. McCormick got flagged 15 yards for unnecessary roughness and said afterward he didn’t know it was Verse.

Fautanu had a sly comment about the approach his line took: “We started not talking with their players, just to tick them off.” And Heyward, asked about the trash talk, simply said, “Bigger fish to fry.”

Rodgers loved it: “A lot of attitude. A lot of trash talking. Good trash talking. Love being out there with those guys.”

WHAT THEY SAID AFTERWARD

The postgame press room was full of good quotes, and they tell you a lot about where this team’s head is.

McCarthy sounded like a coach who knows his team has found something. He said the team is “trending,” and added, “I think we’re exactly what our record says we are.” On the run game: “We wanted to run the ball. It was a focal point today.” On the defense: “Our run defense has really set the bar really high these last two weeks.” And on his backfield: “We’ve got two dynamic backs. Jaylen is on fire. You can’t get him the ball enough.” He even noted players were still tired from the trip to France, which makes the performance even more impressive.

Rodgers was all about the guys up front. “I thought we controlled the line of scrimmage,” he said. “I feel like from the first go we had a good mindset, we protected well, we opened up some holes.” And this one made me laugh: “To be able to call a trap on third and 20, get a first down, is pretty wild.” On Warren, Rodgers went big: “He’s put himself in all the conversations.”

And Warren himself, on his birthday, was as humble as ever. On his long run: “When I got in open space, I like to pick the guy closest to me, I like to hit the sideline.” On how he felt by the end of it: “I was running out of gas.” And on his family watching: “I know my parents and my grandparents didn’t have as much of a heart attack as they told me.” Then the line that sums him up: “I wouldn’t want to be part of any other team.”

Heyward kept it simple: “6-2, taking care of the run,” and, “I thought we controlled both lines of scrimmage.” And Jaquan Brisker gave the whole thing a three-word title: “It’s Pittsburgh football.”

HISTORY AT HOME

One more piece of history from Sunday. According to ESPN, Pittsburgh has now won 23 straight regular-season home games against Cleveland, tying the NFL record for consecutive home wins over a single opponent. Think about that. The Browns came in talking about making Pittsburgh theirs, and they left extending one of the most lopsided home streaks in league history.

There were a few chaotic moments for Cleveland, too. Deshaun Watson left briefly in the first quarter after a hit from Brisker. Shedeur Sanders came in for a handful of plays, and that stint ended with a fumble by tight end Harold Fannin Jr. Cleveland fumbled again on its next possession. Watson finished with 324 passing yards, according to ESPN, but much of that came after the game was out of reach. On the injury front for the Browns, defensive tackle Maliek Collins hurt his elbow in the first half, and corner Tyson Campbell was evaluated for a possible concussion.

On Pittsburgh’s side, the one blemish in the kicking game was a missed 57-yard try by Boswell, though he made his other two field goals and all his extra points. And Mason Rudolph finished the game at quarterback once it was decided.

WHAT THE WIN ALSO REVEALED

Now here’s the part people might miss in the celebration. The win was dominant. But the passing game was not.

Rodgers finished 13 of 25 for 172 yards, according to the team. He threw an interception that came off a DK Metcalf drop, per the Tribune-Review. And outside of Washington’s big catch and a 15-yard grab by Germie Bernard that set up the goal-line touchdown before halftime, the receivers weren’t a big part of the story. Rodgers’ praise for them was about blocking: “the receivers blocked well on the edge.”

McCarthy himself said the offense should have scored more. “We still have things to work on,” he said.

And that’s the key point. Pittsburgh beat Cleveland by 21 points without needing much from the receivers. That’s great. But you can’t count on 200 rushing yards every week, especially against playoff defenses in January. At some point, Rodgers has to win with his arm on third-and-seven. And that’s why this trade conversation isn’t going away.

WHAT THE SPLIT MEANS IN THE STANDINGS

Let’s be precise about where things stand after Sunday. Pittsburgh is 6-2. Cleveland is 5-3. The season series is split, one game each. And inside the division, both teams are now 2-1, after Cleveland came in 2-0 and Pittsburgh came in 1-1.

That’s a huge swing. Before kickoff, a loss would have handed Cleveland a sweep and the head-to-head tiebreaker for good. Instead, the head-to-head is neutralized, the division records are level, and Pittsburgh has the better overall record heading into the bye. There’s a long way to go, with two games against Baltimore and a trip to Cincinnati still ahead, but the Steelers are now the team everybody else in the AFC North is chasing.

THE DARNELL WASHINGTON FACTOR

One more thing about the passing game before we get to the trade market. Darnell Washington’s day matters more than it looks.

Washington has always been viewed as a blocker first, a sixth offensive lineman with soft hands. But three catches for 94 yards, including that 58-yard catch-and-run, show he can be a real weapon in the play-action game. When defenses load up to stop Warren and Dowdle, Washington leaking out behind them is exactly the kind of explosive play Kurt Warner said this offense lacked.

Add a quick slot receiver to that mix, and suddenly defenses have to account for Metcalf outside, Bernard, Washington and Freiermuth up the seam, and a separator underneath. That’s when this offense goes from tough to scary.

THE MARKET JUST SHIFTED

So let’s get to the receiver chase. Here’s what’s new since Sunday.

On Monday morning, Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk weighed in, as relayed by Gridiron Heroics. He said the Chiefs, Steelers, and Commanders have interest in Brian Thomas Jr. But he added a crucial detail: the Steelers have greater interest in Josh Downs. And on Kansas City, he said, “Downs, however, is Kansas City’s Plan B, if they can’t get Thomas away from the Jaguars.”

Read that carefully. If Florio is right, the Chiefs’ first choice is Thomas, and Pittsburgh’s first choice is Downs. Those are two different players. That means the two teams might not be in a direct bidding war after all. If Kansas City goes all in on Thomas, the path to Downs could open up for Pittsburgh.

Meanwhile, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that league executives believe Thomas’s time in Jacksonville is nearing its end, per Pro Football Rumors, and that the Chiefs have “multiple lines in the water” at receiver. But according to Fowler and Tom Pelissero, any Jaguars move would likely come closer to the November 10 deadline. Jacksonville plays Baltimore this coming week and reportedly wants to keep Thomas at least through that game.

So the timelines are different too. Downs could move this week, after Sidery reported a deal was expected “after this weekend.” Thomas might not move until right before the deadline.

HOW DOWNS LOOKED ON SUNDAY

Downs did play Sunday, despite his trade request. The Colts got crushed in Jacksonville, 42-7. Downs caught four of his five targets for 23 yards and a touchdown, a 6-yard score from Daniel Jones on third-and-goal, according to Athlon. He played 46 of the Colts’ 61 offensive snaps, mostly from the slot.

That’s not a huge stat line. But here’s what matters: he played, he produced, he didn’t get hurt, and he showed up as a professional, which is exactly what Colts head coach Shane Steichen said he’d do. His season is now 30 catches for 330 yards and one touchdown in eight games.

And remember what ESPN’s Fowler said on The Rich Eisen Show: Downs “sees the writing on the wall, wants to go play for a Patrick Mahomes in Kansas City maybe, or an Aaron Rodgers in Pittsburgh.”

THE RAMS ENTER THE CONVERSATION

There’s one new wrinkle on Downs. ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported the Rams will at least explore a receiver trade, with Davante Adams out. CBS Sports’ Jordan Dajani listed the Rams as a possible Downs landing spot.

But the Rams have a limitation. According to Athlon, they don’t have their 2027 first- or third-round picks. Cleveland owns their 2027 first. So their trade ammunition is limited. Athlon still lists Kansas City and New England as the likeliest Downs destinations, and says the Rams would have to outbid them.

So the field for Downs might include the Steelers, the Chiefs as a backup plan, the Patriots, and possibly the Rams. Pittsburgh is in the mix, with an offer already reportedly on the table from last week.

WHY THE WIN CHANGES THE MATH FOR KHAN

Here’s why Sunday matters for Omar Khan’s decision.

Before the game, I said a win would make a modest trade look like the final piece for a division leader. Well, now the Steelers are a division leader. They’re 6-2, alone in first, with the season series against Cleveland split at one game apiece.

That changes the calculus. When you’re chasing, a trade is a gamble. When you’re leading, a trade is an investment in protecting what you’ve built. And Pittsburgh’s schedule after the bye is no joke. According to the team, it’s nine games, five on the road, against five 2025 playoff teams. That starts with Sunday night in Cincinnati on November 15.

The defense is clearly good enough. The run game is clearly good enough. The question is whether the passing game is good enough to win a playoff game when the run game gets stopped. That’s what a receiver addition solves.

THE PITTMAN FACTOR

There’s another reason the urgency hasn’t dropped. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with plantar fasciitis, according to Yahoo. Steelers insider Mark Kaboly said the team is “not ruling out, I guess, surgery at this point,” and that “if he needs surgery he could be out for the year.”

Pittman is eligible to return for the Cincinnati game. But eligible doesn’t mean ready. If he can’t go, the Steelers are relying on Metcalf, who had a drop that turned into an interception on Sunday, rookie Bernard, Brandon Smith, Kaden Wetjen, and Ben Skowronek, who missed his third straight game. Roman Wilson can’t come back from injured reserve until the Philadelphia game on November 22 at the earliest.

RODGERS’ MILESTONE WATCH

One fun note before the trade block. Rodgers didn’t throw a touchdown pass on Sunday, but he’s still climbing the all-time list. He passed Peyton Manning for sole possession of third place in career regular-season touchdown passes in Paris. According to pregame numbers cited in the transcript I worked from, he’s now about ten touchdowns away from 550. With nine games left, that’s a real target, and a new receiver could help him get there.

WHAT THE BYE WEEK SHOULD LOOK LIKE

The players are already talking about using the bye the right way. Watt said, “We just need to continue to grow and build off this performance,” and admitted, “Our identity is still being formed as the season goes on. A lot of football left.” Keeanu Benton added a warning against getting comfortable: “No matter how many games we win, we’ve got to stay level-headed.” And Heyward: “We’ve got to continue to keep doing it from here on out.”

For the front office, the bye is a different kind of work week. It’s the best window to bring in a new receiver and get him up to speed. For the coaches, it’s a chance to self-scout the passing game, especially the red zone and third downs. And for the players, it’s rest. Remember, this team went to Paris and back and then played a division war seven days later. They earned a breather.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Go get Downs this week. Florio says Pittsburgh prefers him. Kansas City reportedly prefers Thomas. If the Chiefs chase Thomas and wait for Jacksonville, Khan has a window to land his guy without a bidding war. Make it at a fourth-round pick, maybe a fourth plus a late-round sweetener. Don’t touch the 2028 second-rounder from the Joey Porter Jr. trade.

Don’t chase Thomas at a Day 2 price. He’s talented, with a 1,282-yard rookie season, but this year he has just 10 catches for 117 yards and two touchdowns in seven games, per Gridiron Heroics. And if Jacksonville waits until the deadline, he’d have less time to learn the offense. Downs is the better fit for timing and price.

And keep developing what you have. Brandon Smith earned his roster spot. Bernard is growing every week. Washington just had a career day. Even if Downs comes, this offense isn’t going to become pass-first. It just needs one more reliable option for the moments when the run game stalls.

WHAT’S NEXT

Here’s the calendar. The bye week is now. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern. Kaboly said “early to middle of next week will be the sweet spot,” which means right now. And the next game is Sunday night, November 15, in Cincinnati.

As Watt put it: “Get our bodies back from bye week, come back blazing.”
