One is the Jalen Ramsey trade talk that just won’t die, the idea that the Detroit Lions might call about him. The other one is the storm I think Omar Khan should be creating himself: a big, bold move for a young playmaker at wide receiver. His name is Brian Thomas Jr. And with Michael Pittman Jr. now out for multiple weeks, I think the case for Khan going after him has never been stronger. So today, we’re covering both. What’s really going on with Ramsey, including a side of this story about his loyalty to Joey Porter Jr. that nobody is explaining properly. And then, why Brian Thomas Jr. could be the move that makes the rest of the league take notice. This is James with Second Floor, let’s get into it.

First, let me fix a couple of things from a video that’s been going around, because it’s making Steelers fans look bad. It called Jalen Ramsey the Steelers’ quarterback. Twice. Ramsey is a defensive back. Aaron Rodgers is the quarterback. It also called Joey Porter Jr. a young quarterback. He’s a cornerback, and he’s in Dallas now. And it suggested Ramsey publicly blasted the way Pittsburgh handled Porter’s situation. That’s not what the record shows, and I’ll show you exactly what he did say in a minute. And let me be just as clear about the Brian Thomas Jr. part of this video: there is no report that the Steelers are trading for him. What you’ll hear from me is opinion, not a report. Okay. Let’s go.

Part one: Ramsey, Porter, and a loyalty story that’s been twisted.

To understand why Ramsey’s name keeps coming up, you have to understand his relationship with Joey Porter Jr. And this part of the story gets misread constantly.

Before the trade, while Porter’s contract standoff was dragging on, Ramsey was clear about where he stood. He said, “They got to pay Joey Porter,” and that Porter “needs to get compensated” for what he means to the secondary. That’s a veteran teammate backing a younger one. It’s not a shot at the front office. It’s a guy who sees what a young corner brings and wants him rewarded.

Then on October 4th, when Porter made his Dallas debut against Houston, Ramsey posted two words: “Tapped in brother.” He was watching. He was supporting. And honestly, that’s what you want from a veteran leader. Porter played 24 snaps in that game and didn’t allow a catch on the one target that came his way.

And then this week, on Thursday, Ramsey went on social media to shut down a wild rumor about his role in Porter’s situation, a rumor that claimed he had influenced Porter’s decisions during the standoff. His response was blunt: “the Joey narrative has to stop!” He called the idea “just flat out dumb,” and he described Porter as a guy he thinks is a top five corner. He was not going to let anyone use his friendship with Porter against either of them.

And Ramsey wasn’t alone in how the locker room felt. Cam Heyward talked about the trade on his podcast, and his words tell you a lot. “It was a situation that all the players just wanted to be done with,” he said. “We all wanted this to work out in Pittsburgh. But we don’t make those decisions.” He added, “I honestly thought it would get done, I didn’t ever think it was going to lead to a trade.” And finally: “All I can say is I’m still gonna be a fan of Joey wherever he goes. I have a lot of respect for the Porters.”

So here’s the real picture. The players loved Porter. They wanted him paid. They wanted him to stay. The front office made a different call based on its contract philosophy. And now Ramsey is the one with his name in trade rumors. You can see why some fans connect the dots, but I don’t think there’s any evidence that Ramsey wants out or that Pittsburgh wants him out. What there is, is a veteran who’s loyal to his teammates, past and present.

Part two: why trading Ramsey right now would be a mistake.

We’ve covered the Ramsey rumor in detail on this channel, so I’ll keep this tight. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler asked whether Detroit should call Pittsburgh about Ramsey, and in the same breath said he’s “not sure he’s available.” That’s the whole rumor.

And look at the reason Detroit would even want him. The Lions have one of the worst pass defenses in the league. But Pittsburgh’s pass defense has its own problems. Joe Burrow threw for 282 yards against the Steelers in Week 3, and Deshaun Watson threw for 268 in Week 4. Back-to-back weeks of opposing quarterbacks moving the ball through the air. Jamel Dean is out this Sunday with his ankle. DeShon Elliott is still on injured reserve. Khan just signed two corners off practice squads. You don’t fix a leaking secondary by removing the one veteran who can play the slot, the box and free safety on any given down.

Ramsey is questionable for Sunday with the broken wrist, but he practiced on a limited basis Thursday and Friday and is expected to play. He’ll be right back in the middle of everything. And as long as this team is fighting for the division, that’s exactly where he should stay.

Part three: the storm Khan should create. Brian Thomas Jr.

Now let’s talk about the move I actually want Khan to make. And remember, this is my opinion, not a report.

Let’s start with who Brian Thomas Jr. is, because some people have forgotten how good he was. Jacksonville took him with the 23rd pick in the first round of the 2024 draft out of LSU. As a rookie, he caught 87 passes for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns and made the Pro Bowl. That’s one of the best rookie receiver seasons in recent memory. He’s big, he’s fast, and he can win on his own outside without everything being perfect around him.

Then things went sideways. In 2025, his production dropped to 48 catches for 707 yards and two touchdowns. And this season, through four games, he has just nine catches. He’s now the third receiver in Jacksonville, behind Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers. That’s a former Pro Bowler being used as a WR3.

So what happened? Part of it is circumstance. Jacksonville changed head coaches and offensive systems after his rookie year, with Liam Coen taking over. The Jaguars also drafted Travis Hunter with the second overall pick in 2025, and while Hunter is playing cornerback almost full time this year, the receiver room has been reshuffled around everybody. Players with Thomas’s talent don’t usually forget how to play. More often, they end up in a situation that doesn’t use them the right way. That’s not a guarantee he bounces back. But it’s a reason to believe a change of scenery could unlock the player we saw in 2024.

And here’s why that matters for Pittsburgh. A player who’s been pushed down the depth chart is a player who could be available. Bleacher Report’s Alex Kay named the Steelers as one of the possible landing spots for Thomas, writing that Rodgers needs more help and that Pittsburgh’s receivers outside of Roman Wilson have fallen short. And Kay had a line I love: “Thomas may not be succeeding with the Jacksonville Jaguars, but his best football should still be ahead of him.”

Now, to be fair, the Jaguars have pushed back on this before. Earlier this year, Jacksonville general manager James Gladstone called the Brian Thomas trade rumors “fraudulent.” And according to The Athletic’s Jeff Howe, the Jaguars turned down multiple offers for him, including two before last year’s trade deadline that were described as “significant.” So Jacksonville hasn’t been in a hurry to sell. But that was before he became their third receiver. Things change fast in this league, especially as the deadline gets closer.

Here’s why I think Pittsburgh should go for it. Four reasons.

Reason number one: the need. Pittman is out for multiple weeks after re-injuring his foot. The receiver room right now is DK Metcalf, Roman Wilson, rookie Germie Bernard, Ben Skowronek and depth. Metcalf is a true number one. Wilson is earning a bigger role. But there’s no second big-bodied outside threat to pair with Metcalf. Thomas is exactly that.

And think about what it does for Metcalf. Coming into the Cleveland game, Metcalf was running a route on every single pass play and drawing a 22 percent target share. Defenses know where the ball is going. They roll coverage his way, they bracket him, they make Rodgers look elsewhere. Put another legitimate outside threat on the other side, and suddenly those double teams get a lot harder to justify. One great receiver makes a defense adjust. Two of them make a defense pick its poison.

Reason number two: the contract. Thomas is still on his rookie deal, and his cap hit this season is under four million dollars. That’s an incredibly cheap number for a player with his ceiling. And because he was a first-round pick, he’s under team control beyond this season too. Khan wouldn’t be renting a receiver for ten weeks. He’d be adding a young weapon who could be part of whatever comes after Rodgers.

Reason number three: the fit with Rodgers’ final run. This is Rodgers’ last season. The window is now. And Rodgers has always been at his best with big receivers who can win outside and make contested catches. Thomas and Metcalf on the outside, Wilson working the middle, Freiermuth and Darnell Washington at tight end, Jaylen Warren and hopefully Rico Dowdle in the backfield. That’s a lineup that forces defenses to pick their poison.

Reason number four: Khan has the ammunition. When Pittsburgh traded Porter to Dallas, it got a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder back. That’s real draft capital, and it’s exactly the kind of currency that gets a deal like this done. There’s a nice symmetry to it: the pick you got for losing a young starter on defense turns into a young starter on offense.

And I’d set some limits, too. If Jacksonville asks for young core pieces, players like Daylen Everette, Robert Spears-Jennings or Roman Wilson, I’d walk away. Pittsburgh’s secondary is already thin, and Wilson is the one young receiver who’s actually producing. The deal has to be built on picks, not on the young players who are helping you right now. If the Jaguars want a little more than the Dallas second-rounder, adding a mid-round pick is fine. Gutting the roster to do it is not.

And if Jacksonville simply won’t move him, there are other paths. Tyreek Hill is a free agent coming off a serious knee injury, and his agent said earlier this week he expected Hill to sign somewhere soon, with Kansas City in contact. That’s a very different kind of bet, an older star on a short deal versus a young player with years of control. My preference is Thomas, because he fits both the Rodgers window and whatever comes after it. But the point is the same: with Pittman out, Pittsburgh needs another real outside receiver, and Khan has options to explore.

Now, the honest risks, because you always get both from me. Thomas’s last season and a half have been disappointing, and nobody fully knows whether that’s about him or about Jacksonville’s situation. The Jaguars might still not sell, or they might ask for a price that’s too high; not long ago, the asking price was talked about in first-round terms. And here’s the big one for Pittsburgh specifically: if Thomas plays well, he’ll be heading toward a big second contract down the line. That’s the exact type of extension conversation that ended with Porter in Dallas. Khan would have to be ready to pay a young star this time, not just acquire one.

And the calendar matters here. The deadline is November 10th, which lands right after Pittsburgh’s bye week. By then, the Steelers will have played Indianapolis, Tampa Bay, New Orleans in Paris and Cleveland at home. If they’re 5-3 or 4-4, they’re in the hunt and they should be buyers. And by then, Jacksonville will know a lot more about its own season too. If the Jaguars are fading, a receiver who’s sitting third on their depth chart becomes a lot easier to move. Timing could work in Khan’s favor.

So where do I land? If the price is in the Day 2 range, something built around that 2028 second-rounder from Dallas, Khan should pull the trigger. A former Pro Bowl receiver on a rookie deal, at just 24 years old, available because he’s been buried on a depth chart, is the kind of opportunity that doesn’t come around often. That’s the storm. That’s the move that would make people around the league say, “Pittsburgh’s not done.” And in a season where the Steelers have mostly been reacting, it would finally be Pittsburgh making the other teams react.

And there’s a message in a move like this, too. Remember what Heyward said about the Porter trade: “We all wanted this to work out in Pittsburgh. But we don’t make those decisions.” The players watched the front office move a young starter they loved. A deal for Brian Thomas Jr. would send the opposite message. It would tell that locker room, and Rodgers in his final season, that the front office is going for it, not just managing the cap. Sometimes a trade is about the player. Sometimes it’s about what the player tells everybody else in the building.

And here’s a fun little bonus: the Steelers play at Jacksonville on Monday Night Football on December 14th. Imagine Brian Thomas Jr. going back to Jacksonville in a Steelers uniform, in prime time, with the playoffs on the line. You can’t script it better.

And look at the division while you think about it. Cleveland and Baltimore are both 3-1. Pittsburgh and Cincinnati are 2-2, and the Steelers already own the head-to-head win over the Bengals. Baltimore is dealing with an ankle injury to Lamar Jackson, and Cincinnati just lost to Jacksonville despite Joe Burrow throwing for 428 yards. Nobody in this division is running away with anything. This division is wide open in October. The team that adds the right piece before November 10th could be the team that wins it. That’s why I’d rather see Pittsburgh swing for a young playmaker than sit still, and why I’d never sell a veteran like Ramsey in the middle of a race this tight.

And make no mistake, Rodgers would notice. A 42-year-old quarterback in his final season doesn’t have time to wait for a rookie to develop over two years. He needs targets who can win right now. Thomas has already proven he can do that at this level. Give Rodgers that kind of weapon, and you’re giving him a real shot at one more deep run.

Part four: what it all means for Sunday.

Of course, none of this happens before tomorrow’s game. The Colts come to Acrisure Stadium on Sunday, and Pittsburgh has to win with the roster it has. Without Pittman, the passing game runs through Metcalf and Wilson, and I want to see Bernard get real, designed targets. Rodgers has said young receivers usually take a year or two to develop, and he’s right. But with Pittman out, Bernard’s development can’t wait. Give him routes he can win, and keep throwing to him even if he makes a mistake.

On defense, Ramsey’s role will be bigger than ever with Dean out and Elliott on injured reserve. Watch where he lines up. If you see him moving from the slot to deep safety to the box, that’s Patrick Graham using him exactly the way a team that’s keeping him would use him. And if he makes a big play on Sunday, I promise you, the Detroit rumor gets a lot quieter.

And the matchups will test him right away. The Colts have Keenan Allen listed as questionable, and if he plays, he’s the kind of veteran slot receiver who can pick apart a defense that isn’t communicating. That’s Ramsey’s world. A healthy Ramsey matched up in the slot, with Asante Samuel Jr. and Daylen Everette outside and Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith ready behind them, gives Graham a fighting chance against a Colts passing game that’s been inconsistent all season. Daniel Jones has seven turnovers in four games. If Ramsey reads him right once or twice, that could be the difference.

Offensively, the formula is the same one we’ve been talking about all week. Lean on Warren, run toward the edges against DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart instead of straight into them, get the ball out quickly, and use play action. The Colts will be without defensive end Arden Key. If Pittsburgh’s line holds up, Rodgers should have time, and that’s when Metcalf and Wilson can take their shots.

The Steelers are 2-2. The Colts are 2-2 and haven’t won in Pittsburgh since 2008. It’s T.J. Watt’s birthday. Win tomorrow, and the deadline becomes a chance to add, not subtract. And if Omar Khan is in an adding mood, I know which name I’d put at the top of the list.
