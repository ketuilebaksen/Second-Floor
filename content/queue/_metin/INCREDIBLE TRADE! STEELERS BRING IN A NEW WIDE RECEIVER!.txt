Steelers Nation, I have to start by being straight with you about the headline, because it's not accurate.

There was no trade. The Steelers did not trade for a wide receiver this week. And they couldn't have, because the NFL trade deadline passed on November 10. As it's generally understood, once the deadline passes, teams can't trade players again until the new league year in March.

What the Steelers did do is bring back a familiar face. On Tuesday, according to Steelers.com, they re-signed receiver Brandon Smith to the practice squad, five days after releasing him from the 53-man roster. That's the "new receiver." It's a depth move, not a blockbuster.

But I get why this week has people's heads spinning. Because the receiver news is happening alongside the biggest quarterback story in Pittsburgh in years. Mason Rudolph is starting Friday against Denver, and Aaron Rodgers is no longer on the team.

So today, let's put the receiver room under the microscope: who's back, who's available, how Brandon Smith fits, and how this group has to adjust to a new quarterback in four days. Plus the latest injury report, including a defensive picture that's getting more complicated.

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's go.

AN UPDATE THE VIDEO DOESN'T HAVE

First, a quick note about the video I'm working from today. It was recorded after the Steelers announced Rodgers would be inactive, and it mentions the NFL Network report that he'd been benched.

Since then, things moved much further. On Wednesday afternoon, according to Steelers.com, the Steelers released Rodgers. That came after he criticized Mike McCarthy on The Pat McAfee Show, according to the AP.

I covered that in detail in my last video, so I won't repeat all of it here. But it matters for today's topic, because every receiver on this roster now has to build chemistry with Rudolph, fast.

THE "FAMILIAR FACE": BRANDON SMITH

So let's start with the move itself.

According to Steelers.com, the team released Smith from the 53-man roster on November 19, and then signed him back to the practice squad on Tuesday, November 24. According to Heavy, he's back to give the team a fourth receiver option on the practice squad.

Here's his story this season. According to Steelers.com, Smith was elevated from the practice squad for Weeks 5, 6 and 7. He was promoted to the 53-man roster on October 31, before the Week 8 game against Cleveland, and he also played in Week 10 against Cincinnati.

In five games, he has four catches for 39 yards on five targets, according to Heavy. And Heavy points out something interesting: he played 154 offensive snaps in those games, which it says is close to half the team's offensive snaps. Plus 37 snaps on special teams.

So when the receiver room was decimated by injuries earlier this season, Smith was one of the guys holding it together.

HIS ROAD TO PITTSBURGH

Smith's path to the NFL hasn't been easy.

According to Heavy, he's 27 and in his sixth NFL season. He started as an undrafted free agent with the Dallas Cowboys. He's been with the Arizona Cardinals, played in both the XFL and the UFL, and made his NFL debut with the New York Jets in 2024. Steelers.com adds that he's spent time with the Patriots too.

He signed a reserve/future contract with Pittsburgh after the 2025 season, according to Steelers.com. And now he's back for a second stint this year.

That's the kind of player who doesn't get headlines. But teams need guys like this in November and December, when injuries pile up.

WHY THEY RELEASED HIM, AND WHY THEY BROUGHT HIM BACK

The Steelers didn't publicly explain the move, so I'll be careful here. But the timing tells you a lot.

Smith was released on November 19. That was the week Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. had their practice windows open, and the Steelers needed roster spots to bring players back from injured reserve. Wilson was activated and played in Philadelphia.

Now, with Pittman expected back and the receiver room getting healthier, there wasn't room for Smith on the 53. But there was room on the practice squad. That way, if another receiver goes down, the Steelers have an experienced backup who already knows the offense.

That's smart roster management, in my opinion. It's not exciting, but it's smart.

THE BIG RECEIVER NEWS: PITTMAN

Now, the receiver who could actually change Friday's game.

According to Steelers.com, Michael Pittman Jr. was a full participant on Wednesday, after being estimated as full on Tuesday. He's still on injured reserve, designated for return, so the Steelers have to activate him before Friday.

And Pittman sounds ready. According to Steelers.com, he said: "I feel like I'm ready to go this week, so hopefully I get the nod, but it's out of my control for sure."

McCarthy said earlier this week that he's "very confident" Pittman should play, according to Steelers.com. So all signs point in one direction.

WHY PITTMAN MATTERS EVEN MORE WITH RUDOLPH

The video I'm working from makes a great point about what Pittman brings.

It says he offers size, physicality and dependable hands, the kind of receiver who can make tough catches over the middle and give his quarterback a reliable target when the pressure builds. And then it says something I really liked: not every important play needs to end with a 40-yard touchdown. Sometimes it's an eight-yard catch on third down that changes the direction of a game.

That's exactly right. And it's even more important with a new quarterback. Rudolph hasn't started a game this season. He needs easy completions early to build confidence. A big, reliable target who can win over the middle is the perfect security blanket.

The video also points out that Pittman's physical presence could be especially valuable when Rudolph has to make a quick decision under pressure. Against a Denver defense that leads the NFL with 17 fourth-quarter sacks, according to FOX Sports, quick decisions are going to be a must.

THE ROSTER MATH JUST GOT EASIER

Here's a detail that's easy to miss in all the chaos.

To activate Pittman from injured reserve, the Steelers need an open spot on the 53-man roster. Earlier this season, that was a real problem. They had to waive cornerback Terell Smith to make room for Derrick Harmon. And Brandon Smith's release last week likely helped make room for Roman Wilson.

But with Rodgers released on Wednesday, a roster spot just opened up. As I understand it, that's one less move the Steelers have to make to bring Pittman back. It's a strange silver lining, but it's a real one.

THE FULL RECEIVER ROOM

So let's lay out what the Steelers could have on Friday.

DK Metcalf, who avoided a suspension for his ejection in Philadelphia, according to NFL Network and ESPN.

Michael Pittman Jr., if activated.

Roman Wilson, back from injured reserve, who had three catches for 43 yards in Philadelphia, according to Bleacher Report.

Germie Bernard, the rookie, who had six catches for 40 yards on 10 targets in Philadelphia.

And Max Hurleman, who had six catches for 61 yards and the team's only touchdown in his debut, according to Bleacher Report. He's back on the practice squad, so he'd need another elevation.

Plus Brandon Smith on the practice squad as insurance.

The video I'm working from says it well: having Wilson, Pittman, Metcalf and Bernard together gives the coaching staff more flexibility in designing the game plan. That's the most receiver depth this team has had in a long time.

WHY THERE WAS NO TRADE

Let's go back to the headline for a second, because there's a bigger story behind why the Steelers are relying on internal options.

At the trade deadline on November 10, the Steelers didn't make a single move. Josh Downs, the Colts receiver they were linked to, went to Kansas City. A lot of fans were frustrated, and some outlets gave the Steelers' deadline a poor grade.

So now, with the deadline behind them, the only ways to add a receiver are free agency, the practice squad, or players returning from injury. That's exactly what's happening. Pittman and Wilson coming back from injured reserve are, in a sense, the "acquisitions" this team never made at the deadline.

WHAT ABOUT FREE AGENTS?

There are still a few veteran receivers out there.

Bleacher Report's Moe Moton listed Darius Slayton as a possible fit for the Steelers earlier this month, calling him a big-play receiver who averaged 15 yards per catch in seven seasons with the Giants. But that was an opinion piece, not a report of interest. And with Pittman returning, the need is smaller than it was.

Brandon Aiyuk's situation in San Francisco was coming to a head around November 23. I haven't seen a confirmed update on where he's landed, so I'm not going to guess. And I haven't seen any report connecting the Steelers to him.

My view: the Steelers made their choice. They're going with what they have, plus Brandon Smith as insurance. That's probably the right call this late in the season.

THE ONE RECEIVER WHO'S HURTING: SKOWRONEK

There's one receiver who isn't trending the right way.

Ben Skowronek missed practice again on Wednesday with his calf injury, according to Steelers.com. That's two straight days.

The video makes a point I agree with. Skowronek isn't one of the primary targets, but his special teams value shouldn't be overlooked. Field position wins close games. A well-covered punt or a key special teams tackle can change a possession. If he can't go, the Steelers need someone else to fill that role, and that might be another reason Brandon Smith's return matters.

THE RUDOLPH FACTOR FOR THE RECEIVERS

Now, let's think about how this receiver room fits with Rudolph specifically.

The video I'm working from says Rudolph's goal shouldn't be spectacular numbers. What this team needs is efficient quarterback play, sustained drives and an offense that capitalizes on scoring chances. I agree completely.

According to Yahoo Sports, Rudolph has 19 career starts with a 64.2 percent completion rate, 30 touchdowns and 22 interceptions. He's never been a quarterback who carries a team with his arm. He's been at his best when he's protected, playing on schedule and letting his playmakers make plays.

That's why the receivers matter so much this week. Rudolph needs his targets to win quickly, catch the ball and pick up yards after the catch.

WHY THE TIMING IS SO INTERESTING

The video I'm working from hints at something I want to make clear.

Brandon Smith, Max Hurleman and the other depth receivers have spent most of this season working with the backup quarterbacks in practice. That includes Rudolph. When the starters run the main offense, the backups and the practice squad often work together on the scout team or in separate periods.

So in a strange way, some of these receivers may have more practice reps with Rudolph than with Rodgers. I can't tell you exactly how many, and I won't pretend to know the details of their practice schedule. But it's a reasonable assumption that Rudolph isn't walking into a room full of strangers.

That could help on a short week, when there isn't time to build new chemistry from scratch.

WHICH RECEIVERS FIT RUDOLPH BEST?

Here's my opinion on how the room should shake out with Rudolph.

Pittman should be the go-to on third down and in the middle of the field. He's the most reliable hands on the roster, and Rudolph needs that.

Wilson should get the designed deep shots. If Rudolph has a little more willingness to push the ball down the field than we saw this season, Wilson is the guy who benefits.

Bernard and Hurleman should handle quick-game and slot routes. Easy throws that keep the offense moving.

And Metcalf? He's the wild card. If he plays with discipline and wins on the outside, he gives Rudolph a big target on contested throws. If not, the Steelers have more options now than they did last week.

THE RECEIVERS AS BLOCKERS

There's one more way this receiver room matters that doesn't show up in the catch totals: blocking.

In Philadelphia, the Steelers ran for just 73 yards on 20 carries, according to the video I was working from earlier this week, and Rodgers led the team in rushing. With Rudolph starting, the run game becomes even more important. And receivers like Pittman and Metcalf are big, physical players who can seal the edge on outside runs.

That's part of why I'd keep Metcalf and Pittman on the field on early downs. Not just for their catches, but for what they do for Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle. A healthier receiver room can help the run game as much as the passing game.

THE OFFENSIVE LINE NOTE

The video also brings up rookie guard Gennings Dunker.

According to Steelers.com, Dunker was a full participant on both Tuesday and Wednesday. He's still on injured reserve, designated for return. McCarthy has said he still needs work, particularly in pass protection.

So I don't expect Dunker to be activated for Friday. But the video is right that getting healthier up front would help. For Rudolph especially, every bit of protection counts.

THE DEFENSE: HIGHSMITH STILL OUT OF PRACTICE

Now, the less encouraging side of the injury report.

Alex Highsmith missed his second straight day of practice on Wednesday with his ankle injury, according to Steelers.com. McCarthy said earlier this week it would be "tough" for him to make it this week.

The video makes a great point about why that matters. When Highsmith lines up opposite T.J. Watt, offenses have hard decisions to make. Where do they send extra protection? Which rusher gets the extra attention? Can their tackles win one-on-one? Without Highsmith, those decisions get easier for the opponent.

HERBIG, ELLIOTT AND A NEW NAME: HOLCOMB

Nick Herbig was limited again on Wednesday with his hip and rib injuries, according to Steelers.com. That's two straight limited days. Progress, but as the video says, limited doesn't necessarily mean ready.

DeShon Elliott was also limited on Wednesday with a knee injury, according to Steelers.com. And there's a new name: linebacker Cole Holcomb was limited with a heel injury.

Cam Heyward didn't practice Wednesday, but that was listed as rest, not injury.

So the Steelers are managing a lot of bodies on defense heading into a short-week game.

THE PASS RUSH PLAN

According to Steelers.com, defensive coordinator Patrick Graham said about the linebacker situation: "We'll see who's available and then from there we'll work the plan."

The video points to Jack Sawyer and KJ Henry as the players who could see bigger roles. And it raises the key question for Friday: can the Steelers create consistent pressure without relying too much on the blitz? Because every time you send an extra rusher, you open up something else on the field.

That's the balance Graham has to find. And against an 8-2 Denver team that the video says has won six straight, it won't be easy.

THE BRONCOS' INJURY REPORT

Let's look across the field.

According to Steelers.com, Denver has already ruled out two players: safety Taylor Rapp with a quad injury and defensive end Sai'vion Jones with a hamstring injury.

Running back J.K. Dobbins missed Wednesday's practice for a non-injury, personal reason. Most of the rest of Denver's report was full participants, including receiver Pat Bryant, safety Brandon Jones, running back Tyler Badie, safety JL Skinner and tight end Caleb Lohner.

So Denver is coming in mostly healthy. Losing Rapp in the secondary is something the Steelers' receivers could try to take advantage of.

THE SPECIAL TEAMS ANGLE

The receiver room also affects special teams, and that's worth a quick look.

In Philadelphia, McCarthy said Lew Nichols and Max Hurleman handled the kick returns, and handled the tricky bouncing kicks well. But according to the Eagles' live blog, Hurleman also muffed one kickoff that Philadelphia recovered.

With Skowronek possibly out and the receiver room shifting, the return roles could change again on Friday. Brandon Smith's experience on special teams, 37 snaps this season according to Heavy, is part of why he's valuable as insurance.

On a short week, against a good team, special teams mistakes can decide a game. The Steelers need clean returns and solid coverage.

WHAT THIS ALL MEANS FOR FRIDAY

Let's put the whole picture together.

On offense, the Steelers are getting healthier at receiver. Pittman is likely back. Wilson is back. Metcalf is available. Hurleman is an option. And Brandon Smith is back on the practice squad for depth. That's good news, especially for a new starting quarterback.

On defense, it's the opposite. Highsmith is unlikely to play. Herbig, Elliott and Holcomb are all limited. And the pass rush depends on younger players stepping up.

The video sums it up nicely: there's reason for optimism on offense, but considerable uncertainty on defense.

THE DENVER CHALLENGE

Let's not underestimate the opponent.

According to FOX Sports, Denver is 8-2 and leads the AFC West. The video I'm working from says the Broncos have won six straight. And the Steelers are 2.5-point underdogs at home, according to FOX Sports.

Denver's defense is the biggest test. Seventeen fourth-quarter sacks, the most in the NFL, according to FOX Sports. That tells you they close games. For a new quarterback with a reshuffled receiver room, the fourth quarter is where things could get dangerous.

That's why the first three quarters matter so much. If the Steelers can build a lead with the run game and efficient passing, they can avoid having to throw into Denver's pass rush when it's at its most dangerous.

THE BIGGER CONTEXT

And don't forget what's at stake.

According to Yahoo Sports, the Steelers are 6-4 and currently the AFC's seventh seed, the final wild-card spot. They've lost two straight. And according to the AP, the next three games are against Denver, Houston and Jacksonville.

A win on Friday, with a new quarterback, after the week this team just had, would be one of the most important wins of the season. A loss would put them at 6-5, with two more tough games coming.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for the week. Opinion, not a report.

Brandon Smith: good depth move. Keep him ready on the practice squad. If Skowronek can't go, he's a possible special teams elevation.

Pittman: activate him. Make him Rudolph's go-to target on third down.

Wilson: two or three designed downfield shots.

Hurleman: elevate him again. Quick-game and slot.

Bernard: rotate with Hurleman. Screens and quick throws against the Denver pass rush.

Metcalf: plays, but in a defined role. Keep it clean.

Dunker: stay on injured reserve one more week.

Pass rush: Sawyer and KJ Henry rotate opposite Watt. Herbig on a snap count if active. Use stunts more than blitzes.

Secondary: if Elliott is limited, Spears-Jennings gets more snaps.

Rudolph: short, quick and on time. Let the receivers do the work.

FINAL WORD

Here's my bottom line. There was no trade. There was no blockbuster. But there's still real receiver news this week.

Brandon Smith is back as insurance. Pittman is ready to return. Wilson, Bernard and Hurleman are all playing well. And for a new starting quarterback, that's exactly what you want: options.

The question for Friday isn't whether the Steelers have enough receivers. It's whether they can protect Rudolph long enough to use them, and whether the defense can hold up without Highsmith.

Get both of those right, and the Steelers can beat Denver.
