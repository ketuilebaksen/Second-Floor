Steelers Nation, the most dangerous backfield in Pittsburgh in years is about to be complete, and the missing piece is coming back right on time. Rico Dowdle, the running back Omar Khan landed in free agency after a 1,000-yard year in Carolina, a back with two straight 1,000-yard seasons, practiced on Wednesday, and Mike McCarthy says there’s “a good chance” he plays against Tampa Bay. Add him to a Jaylen Warren who just put up 188 yards against the Colts, and this offense is about to get a serious upgrade, without trading a single draft pick. So today, we’re breaking down the Dowdle return, how McCarthy should split the work, what this means for the receiver problem, and the bigger question about Aaron Rodgers and the future. And I’ll set the record straight on a few names floating around online. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

First, let’s be clear about what’s actually happening.

Some videos this week are making it sound like the Steelers just went out and stole a star running back in a trade. That’s not what happened. No new running back has joined the team. The “steal” happened back in March, and it’s finally about to pay off.

On March 13th, the Steelers signed Rico Dowdle to a two-year, 12.25 million dollar contract. At the time, a lot of people looked at that as a quiet depth move. But look at the resume Khan picked up for that price.

Dowdle went undrafted in 2020 and spent his first five seasons in Dallas. In 2024, he became the lead back and ran for 1,079 yards, the first undrafted player in Cowboys history to reach 1,000 rushing yards in a season. He also caught 39 passes and scored five total touchdowns. And who was his head coach in Dallas that year? Mike McCarthy.

Then in 2025, he went to Carolina on a one-year deal. He started as the backup to Chuba Hubbard, took over in Week 5, and exploded. He ran for a career-high 206 yards against Miami, followed that with 186 yards against his old team in Dallas, and finished the season as the Panthers’ leading rusher, with 1,076 yards and six touchdowns.

Two straight 1,000-yard seasons. Two different teams. And NFL.com’s Gregg Rosenthal had him ranked as the fourth-best running back in the 2026 free agent class. Pittsburgh got him for about 6 million dollars a year. That’s the steal.

And don’t overlook what he does as a receiver. In both of those 1,000-yard seasons, Dowdle caught 39 passes. In Carolina, that was good for 297 receiving yards. In Dallas, three of his five touchdowns in 2024 came through the air. With Pittsburgh this thin at wide receiver, a running back who can catch the ball out of the backfield isn’t a luxury. It’s a necessity. Rodgers loves throwing to backs who can turn a five-yard checkdown into a 15-yard gain, and now he’ll have two of them.

And think about the money for a second. The Steelers are paying Dowdle about 6.1 million dollars a year on average. For a back who just produced back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons, that’s a bargain in today’s NFL. Khan didn’t need a trade, a bidding war, or a draft pick. He just needed to see value before everybody else did, and he locked it up in March.

So why haven’t we seen him?

Here’s the frustrating part. Dowdle dislocated his toe in Week 2 against New England. Reports say it was dislocated twice. He’s missed the last three games, and his season numbers are tiny: 15 carries for 37 yards and four catches for 28 yards. So Steelers fans haven’t seen anything close to the real Rico Dowdle yet.

But the news this week is good. McCarthy said last week that Dowdle was “close” to playing against the Colts, and after the game, he said, “I look for Rico to practice this week and hopefully be up for the game.” On Wednesday, Dowdle was a limited participant in practice. And McCarthy said there’s “a good chance” he returns on Sunday in Tampa.

The perfect partner for a red-hot Warren.

And the timing could not be better, because Jaylen Warren is playing the best football of his career.

Against the Colts, Warren had 188 yards from scrimmage, a career high: 101 rushing yards on 15 carries and 87 receiving yards on six catches. He scored on an 80-yard catch-and-run and a 50-yard touchdown run, and he became the first NFL player since Herschel Walker in 1986 to have both an 80-plus-yard touchdown catch and a 50-plus-yard touchdown run in the same game. It was his third straight game with at least 125 scrimmage yards. Coming into that game, his 5.6 yards per carry ranked behind only Kenneth Walker III, James Cook, and Bijan Robinson among NFL running backs.

So is Warren playing like a top-five running back right now? Over the last three weeks, I’d say yes. And McCarthy has been crystal clear about who’s in charge of this backfield. On Monday, he said, “Jaylen is our featured player right now.” He also said, “There’s a number you’d like to continue to give him,” even after Dowdle comes back. And remember what he said after the Colts game: “He’s the man,” and, “He’s a three-down player.”

So let’s put the worries to rest. Dowdle coming back does not mean Warren loses his job. It means Warren gets help.

How I’d split the work.

This is where it gets fun, because the Steelers now have two running backs who both know how to carry a load. Here’s my opinion on how McCarthy should use them.

Warren stays the lead back. I’d want him somewhere around 18 to 22 touches a game: his carries, plus a real role in the passing game, because that’s where he’s been most dangerous. Screens, swing passes, checkdowns in the flat that turn into 80-yard touchdowns. You don’t take the ball out of the hands of the hottest player in your offense.

Dowdle becomes the hammer. Early downs between the tackles, short yardage, and the four-minute offense when the Steelers are protecting a lead late in games. He’s a physical, one-cut runner, and last season in Carolina he carried the ball a career-high 236 times. He knows how to handle a heavy load when it’s asked of him. That makes him the perfect closer.

And here’s why that matters. McCarthy admitted Warren’s workload has been “heavy.” Warren has been doing it all since Week 2. A healthy Dowdle keeps Warren fresh for December and January, when the Steelers will need him most. It also gives defenses two very different looks: Warren’s elusiveness and burst, and Dowdle’s downhill power.

And I’ll add one more thing. This is exactly the kind of backfield McCarthy loves. In Dallas, he had a one-two punch with Tony Pollard and Ezekiel Elliott, and later Dowdle as the lead back. McCarthy has said a one-two backfield was always the vision for this offense. Now, five games into the season, he finally gets to see that vision on the field.

And here’s the McCarthy connection that makes this even better. McCarthy was Dowdle’s head coach in 2024 when he broke out in Dallas. He knows exactly what Dowdle does well and exactly how to use him. There’s no learning curve, no guessing. When a coach and a player have that kind of history, the player usually ends up in the right spots. Don’t be surprised if McCarthy has a few plays drawn up for Dowdle that look a lot like the ones that worked in Dallas.

Why the running game matters even more right now.

Here’s the bigger picture. The Steelers’ receiver room is down to almost nothing. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot. Roman Wilson, with a dislocated finger, and Ben Skowronek, with a calf injury, both did not practice on Wednesday and have already been ruled out for Tampa. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen as the healthy receivers on the 53, with Brandon Smith, Isaiah Hodgins, and Brandon Johnson on the practice squad.

When you’re that thin at receiver, you run the ball. You use two tight ends with Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington. You use play action to give Rodgers easy throws. And you lean on your backs. That’s why Dowdle’s return is so important. It’s not just another body. It’s the piece that lets the Steelers play the kind of football that wins on the road while the receivers heal.

The receiver question: names you’re hearing, and the truth about them.

Now, let’s go through the receivers people are throwing around online, because some of these names don’t hold up.

Jauan Jennings. Some videos are pitching him as a cheap trade option. But Jennings signed with the Minnesota Vikings this offseason, so he’s not a 49ers trade chip anymore. There’s no report connecting him to Pittsburgh.

Scotty Miller. He was a Steeler in 2024 and 2025, and he knows Rodgers’ timing. But as of October 6th, he’s back on the Chicago Bears’ practice squad. Pittsburgh could sign him to its 53-man roster if it wanted to, but there’s no report saying that’s coming, and in his 2025 stint in Pittsburgh he caught nine passes for 62 yards in 13 games. That’s not a game-changer.

Davante Adams. I get the dream. Adams and Rodgers were one of the great connections of the last decade. But Adams is with the Rams, and the Rams are trying to win now. No report connects him to Pittsburgh, and I wouldn’t build any plan around it.

And Tyreek Hill? Still a free agent, with a targeted return timetable of November, and the Chiefs are the clear favorite in the prediction markets. No report links him to the Steelers either.

So what do I think Khan should do? Here’s my trade block opinion, not a report. The best realistic move is still a veteran like Calvin Ridley in Tennessee, a player who’s barely been used, should come cheap, and could step in right away. If Khan wants to swing bigger, he has the 2028 second-round pick from Dallas from the Joey Porter Jr. trade, and Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville is the dream target. But with the deadline on November 10th, there’s no need to panic before Sunday. McCarthy has backed Brandon Smith, saying he’s “definitely earned this opportunity,” and he called Bernard someone for whom “football comes easy.” With Warren and Dowdle in the backfield, this offense can survive a couple of weeks.

The injury report: good news and one new name.

Let’s run through Wednesday’s report, because there’s a lot to like. Dowdle was limited. Jamel Dean was limited with his ankle, which is encouraging after he missed the Colts game, although his status for Sunday isn’t settled yet. DeShon Elliott was limited, practicing in the second week of his 21-day window off injured reserve. McCarthy said, “He’s definitely someone we would like to get out there sooner rather than later.” T.J. Watt was limited for rest. Cam Heyward didn’t practice, but that was a scheduled veteran rest day. And Jalen Ramsey, playing through his broken wrist, didn’t practice, the same as last Wednesday before he played and made nine tackles against the Colts.

The one new concern is defensive tackle Keeanu Benton, who didn’t practice with an ankle injury. With Derrick Harmon already on injured reserve, the defensive front is getting thin. That’s another reason Khan brought back veteran linebacker Elandon Roberts to the practice squad on Monday. He can be elevated on game day if the Steelers need another physical body against the run.

And there’s a decision coming on Elliott. Because he’s on injured reserve with the designation to return, the Steelers have to officially activate him to the 53-man roster before he can play. That could happen as early as Saturday, or the team could give him another week of practice before making the move. If Dean and Elliott both come back, Pittsburgh’s secondary suddenly looks like a real strength again, with Asante Samuel Jr., rookie Daylen Everette, Ramsey, and the new additions all adding depth. And remember, this secondary didn’t allow a single passing touchdown against the Colts, even without Dean and Elliott. Getting healthier from there is a scary thought for opposing offenses.

Now, the Aaron Rodgers question.

The other big debate this week is about Rodgers and the future. He’s 42, and he’s playing at a level nobody expected. Three straight games with three touchdown passes. He passed Ben Roethlisberger for fifth on the all-time wins list and Peyton Manning for third in career touchdown passes, postseason included. So the obvious question is: would the Steelers want him back in 2027?

Here’s what we know. Rodgers has made it pretty clear that this is his final season. Before the game in Cleveland, Al Michaels asked him where he’d be a year from now, and Rodgers said, “I’ll either be tending to my pregnant wife or I’ll be taking care of my newborn.” That’s not a man planning another training camp. And honestly, that makes every Sunday this season feel a little bigger. We’re watching the last chapter of one of the greatest careers in NFL history, and it’s being written in black and gold. Against the Colts, he completed his first 11 passes. That’s not a quarterback hanging on. That’s a quarterback going out on his own terms.

So I wouldn’t count on another year. But that doesn’t change how special this season is. If Rodgers keeps playing like this, he’s giving Pittsburgh a real chance to make noise in January, and he’s giving the young quarterbacks in the building a front-row seat to how a future Hall of Famer prepares every week. That’s valuable even if the farewell comes in February.

And as for the long-term answer at quarterback, that’s a conversation for the offseason. Some videos have been listing big-name starters as possible targets, but none of those players are on the market, and there’s no report connecting any of them to Pittsburgh. Right now, the only quarterback conversation that matters is how far Rodgers can take this team in his final season.

What it means for Tampa Bay.

The Steelers travel to Tampa Bay on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern. And there’s a fun little storyline in the backfield. Kenneth Gainwell, who left Pittsburgh for the Buccaneers this offseason, is the player Dowdle was signed to replace. Now Dowdle could make his return in the same game, on the same field, as the man whose role he took over.

There’s also a big challenge. The Steelers have lost five straight on the road, including that 27-24 loss in Cleveland. Breaking that streak is the next step for this team. And the best way to win on the road with a thin receiver room is to run the ball, control the clock, and keep the defense fresh. That’s exactly what a Warren and Dowdle combination is built to do.

Picture it: Warren getting the ball in space early, Dowdle pounding away in the second half, Freiermuth working the middle, Metcalf drawing the coverage, and Bernard catching whatever the defense gives him. That’s a game plan built for a tough road win.

Three things I’ll be watching from the backfield on Sunday.

Number one: the snap split. If Dowdle plays, I want to see how McCarthy eases him in. My guess is something like a 65-35 split in Warren’s favor in his first game back. If Dowdle looks sharp early, that number could grow as the weeks go on. If the toe is still bothering him, we’ll see it quickly in how often he’s on the field.

Number two: short-yardage and goal-line work. This is where Dowdle can make an instant impact. Third-and-1, fourth-and-1, first-and-goal from the 2. If McCarthy hands Dowdle the ball in those spots, it tells you exactly how he sees the roles: Warren the explosive playmaker, Dowdle the finisher.

Number three: the passing game out of the backfield. With only three healthy receivers on the active roster, I expect the backs to be a bigger part of the passing game than usual. Watch for both of them on the field at the same time, one in the backfield and one split out wide. That’s a look that can create mismatches against linebackers, and it’s a creative way to make up for the missing receivers.

If those three things go right, the Steelers can win in Tampa even without their receiver depth. And if they win, the conversation about this team changes again. A road win, a 4-2 record, and a backfield that’s finally whole heading toward the bye. That’s how a season turns.

The bottom line.

So no, the Steelers didn’t steal a running back in a trade this week. But they are about to get a 1,000-yard back back into the lineup, a player McCarthy knows better than anyone, at the exact moment the offense needs him most. Warren stays the featured star. Dowdle becomes the hammer. And Pittsburgh’s backfield might be the best one-two punch in the AFC by the end of the month.

Add in a historic season from Rodgers, a defense that just held Jonathan Taylor to 45 yards, and a general manager who keeps finding the right pieces, and this team is heading in the right direction.
