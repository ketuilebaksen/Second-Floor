Steelers Nation, the Josh Downs story has a clock on it now, and that clock just got a lot more specific. We’re not talking about vague “deadline is coming” chatter anymore. We know roughly when Indianapolis plans to make its decision, we know where the asking price has moved, and we know Pittsburgh’s name has been attached to this from the very first week.

Let me be upfront about one thing before we dig in. As I’m recording this, Downs is still a member of the Indianapolis Colts. There’s no completed trade. The update is about timing and price, not a done deal. And honestly, the timing piece might be the most useful information Steelers fans have gotten all week, because it tells you exactly when to start paying attention and when to stop refreshing your phone.

So today: the timeline, the price, the player, how he’d actually fit next to Aaron Rodgers, the draft-pick wrinkle Omar Khan has to solve, and then a little extra on the Browns fallout that’s still rumbling from last week. My board closes it out, opinion only.

THE TIMELINE THAT CHANGES HOW YOU SHOULD WATCH THIS

Here’s the headline. On November 5, NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported this: “The #Colts have received plenty of calls on several players, including Jonathan Taylor, Josh Downs, Grover Stewart and others.” And then the key line: “barring an offer that blows them away, Indianapolis plans to wait until after Sunday’s game vs the Cowboys.”

Translation: the Colts host Dallas today, Sunday, November 8. According to Heavy’s summary of the report, Indianapolis expects to revisit its offers after that game and make moves on Monday or Tuesday, ahead of the deadline at 4 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10.

Why does that matter to you? Because it means silence this weekend is not bad news. If today comes and goes and Downs is still in Indianapolis, that’s the plan working exactly as described. The window that actually matters is Monday morning through Tuesday afternoon.

And there’s a football logic to it. The Colts have a game to play. If they move a receiver on Friday or Saturday, somebody has to absorb those snaps with almost no preparation. A front office that’s already sold off big pieces still has to field a respectable offense against Dallas. Waiting a couple of days costs them nothing and might even help, because bidders get more nervous as the deadline approaches.

There’s also a business angle. After the Cowboys game, the Colts will know more about where their season is headed. Every loss makes selling more obvious. Every buyer also loses a little bit of patience. Both of those things tend to push deals toward the final forty-eight hours.

WHERE PITTSBURGH FITS INTO THIS

Now here’s the part that should get your attention. Back on October 28, Schultz reported that Downs had “received interest from multiple AFC teams, including the #Steelers.”

That’s not a fan site guessing. That’s an insider naming the team. And it lines up with what we’ve heard since: on October 30, reporter Evan Sidery wrote that both the Chiefs and the Steelers had offered Day 3 compensation, and on November 7, Mark Kaboly of The Athletic called Downs a fit that “seems like a perfect fit in terms of availability and price.”

What we don’t know is the exact offer Khan has made, or whether he’s made a formal one at all. Heavy is clear about that too: Pittsburgh’s interest is reported, but no specific offer from the Steelers has been confirmed. So stay grounded. Interest is real. A deal is not.

There’s also a neat connection worth mentioning. Pittsburgh has already done business with Indianapolis this year. Michael Pittman Jr. came over from the Colts. So these two front offices have a recent working relationship. That doesn’t guarantee anything, but it does mean the phone line isn’t cold.

HOW THE PRICE HAS MOVED

Let’s trace the asking price, because this is where the update really matters.

At the end of October, the reported target was a third-round pick. The transcript credits that to Adam Schefter on October 29. I couldn’t locate that specific report from Schefter, so I’ll call it what it is: widely reported as the original ask.

Then on November 5, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport put the Colts’ preference at a third- or a fourth-round pick. That one-round shift is a big deal. It means Indianapolis has gone from “pay us a third” to “a third or a fourth,” and in negotiation language, that usually means the fourth is the real number.

And on November 7, a ClutchPoints summary of reporting from Rapoport and Schefter said the Colts are telling teams they already have a fourth-round offer, while still trying to get a third. The same piece also cautioned that it remains to be seen whether that’s true or just leverage.

Put those three data points together and you get a clear trend. The price has been sliding from a third toward a fourth. That’s exactly the range where a team like Pittsburgh, which wants help without mortgaging the future, can afford to play.

THE PLAYER: WHO JOSH DOWNS ACTUALLY IS

Let’s make sure we’re clear on what Pittsburgh would be getting.

Downs is twenty-five years old. Through eight games this season, he has thirty catches for three hundred thirty yards and a touchdown, according to Heavy. That line includes his most recent game, a 42-7 loss to Jacksonville, where he caught four passes for twenty-three yards and scored the Colts’ only touchdown. Over four seasons, he has two hundred twenty-eight catches for two thousand four hundred seventy yards. His best year was 2024, when he caught seventy-two passes for eight hundred three yards and five touchdowns.

One small fix to the transcript: it says “83 yards” for that 2024 season. It was eight hundred three.

There’s important context behind the current numbers. The transcript notes that Indianapolis lost Alec Pierce, its vertical threat, when he aggravated a surgically repaired heel in Week 2. I couldn’t confirm the injury details myself, so treat that as reported. But the logic is sound. When a defense doesn’t have to respect a deep threat, safeties can creep down, and the underneath receiver has less room to work. Downs’ production this year has come in a tougher environment than his 2024 season did.

And then there’s the trade request. Colts coach Shane Steichen confirmed it, telling reporters he’d told Downs, “Hey, you’re a professional,” and adding, “Obviously, you requested a trade, but you’re on this football team and you got to show up.” Downs showed up. He said, “I’m in the game plan, so I got to be ready to play,” and he played against Jacksonville after the request became public. That matters. A player who keeps competing while his name is on the block tells you something about how he’d handle a new locker room.

What I’d caution against is assuming Downs has Pittsburgh at the top of some wish list. There’s no reporting about where he wants to go. He’s said he’s “open to whatever.” So let’s not invent a storyline that doesn’t exist.

HOW HE’D FIT NEXT TO RODGERS

Here’s where I get excited, and it’s all about situations.

Picture third-and-five in Cincinnati next Sunday. DK Metcalf is split wide, and the corner across from him is playing off, with a safety rolled over the top to take away the deep ball. In that moment, Rodgers needs an answer that can come out quickly. That’s the Downs throw. Slot alignment, a quick break past the linebacker, the ball out on rhythm, first down. It won’t make the highlight package. It will keep the drive alive.

And here’s the thing about Metcalf. His Browns game was rough: one catch for four yards on six targets, according to Steelers.com’s Asked and Answered. Meanwhile, rookie Germie Bernard caught every one of his five targets in that game. The transcript calls him “Jeremy Bernard,” but it’s Germie, the second-round pick out of Alabama. Bernard has been a real bright spot. Adding Downs isn’t about pushing Bernard aside. It’s about giving the offense a second reliable option underneath so Rodgers isn’t forced into low-percentage throws.

Think about the decisions it forces on a defense. If the nickel corner follows Downs inside, does that leave Bernard one-on-one outside? If a linebacker widens to cover the slot, does that open a lane for Jaylen Warren up the middle? Good offenses create those dilemmas. A receiver can help you even on plays where he never touches the ball.

And remember, Roman Wilson is on injured reserve as well. The transcript says it’s a dislocated finger. I couldn’t independently confirm that injury, so take it as reported. Either way, he’s not available right now, and the room is thinner than it should be for a first-place team.

HOW I’D RAMP HIM UP

Let’s say the trade happens Monday or Tuesday. What does Week 10 look like?

Realistically, a limited role. A new receiver arriving midseason has to learn the system, the terminology and, most importantly, what Rodgers expects when the coverage changes after the snap. Rodgers is famously demanding about that. So I’d start Downs with a small package of routes he’s comfortable with. Option routes from the slot. Quick outs. A few third-down concepts. Then you grow the role as the trust builds.

By December, if it clicks, he could be the guy Rodgers looks for on every key third down. That’s when this kind of deal pays off.

THE DRAFT-PICK WRINKLE

Now, here’s the complication that’s specific to Pittsburgh.

If the price really is a fourth-round pick, the Steelers don’t have their own 2027 fourth to offer. It went to Dallas in late August as part of the Broderick Jones trade. In that deal, Pittsburgh sent Jones and its 2027 fourth to the Cowboys and received a third-round pick and a sixth-round pick back. The transcript says both of those Dallas picks are in 2027. I couldn’t confirm the years on those picks, so let me hold off on stating that as fact.

So what are the options?

Option one, a 2028 fourth-round pick. That keeps the 2027 draft intact, which matters if the Steelers want to be aggressive in what’s expected to be a strong quarterback class. The Colts might push back, since a pick two years out is worth less to a rebuilding team than one next spring. But it’s a reasonable place to start.

Option two, a fifth-round pick. That would be a great outcome for Pittsburgh. But with Kansas City reportedly in the mix too, I wouldn’t count on it.

Option three, the one the transcript floats and I find interesting: if Indianapolis insists on a third, ask for something back. A late-round pick coming the other way changes the math. That’s what happened in the Jones deal itself, where Pittsburgh gave up a fourth and got a third and a sixth back. Asking what the Colts would include alongside Downs is a fair negotiating move.

WHERE I DRAW THE LINE, AND WHY

Here’s the core issue. Downs is on an expiring rookie contract. If he arrives, plays well and becomes a key target by December, Pittsburgh would want to extend him. But he would control that decision. He could hit free agency and go wherever he wants.

That’s why the round matters so much. If the Steelers give up a third and Downs walks in March, they’ve spent a premium pick on about ten weeks of football. That’s a steep price. At a fourth, the risk is a lot more comfortable. And if he leaves, there’s at least a chance of a compensatory pick down the line, though that’s never guaranteed.

So for me, a fourth is the ceiling. A third, with nothing coming back, is where I’d walk.

THE BROWNS FALLOUT: JARED VERSE

Now, a quick detour, because the transcript closes with something that’s still being talked about: the Jared Verse saga from the Browns game.

For context, Verse came to Cleveland from the Rams in the trade that sent Myles Garrett to Los Angeles, according to EssentiallySports. And after the Browns won the first meeting with Pittsburgh this season, Verse reportedly accused the Steelers of playing dirty. Before the rematch, he stood by those comments.

Then came the moment that went viral. After Pittsburgh’s first touchdown, Verse tried to block the extra point, and Steelers right guard Mason McCormick caught him in midair and slammed him to the turf. McCormick was flagged for unnecessary roughness. According to the transcript, the penalty was assessed on the kickoff and gave Cleveland good field position, starting its next drive at its own thirty-seven. I couldn’t confirm the exact yard line, but the penalty itself is well documented.

After the game, Verse said McCormick had tried to injure him. I’m not going to judge intent here. What I can tell you is that it was a penalty, and Pittsburgh paid fifteen yards for it. I’d much rather see our line answer trash talk by moving people on the next run play, which, to be fair, it did all afternoon.

Here’s the part the transcript highlighted: what bothered Verse most. According to EssentiallySports, he pointed at his own locker room. “The conversation that we’ve had, we’ve spoken about, I’ve made my concerns known,” he said. He also took some responsibility: “I didn’t do enough on my part, and we all gotta pick it up.” And he said he should have warned his teammates before talking about the Steelers.

Jaquan Brisker had his own take. “He should have kept his mouth shut,” Brisker said. “All that talk, and then to come out with 0 tackles? You didn’t stand on business.” There’s a small discrepancy on the stats. Brisker said zero tackles, while the transcript credits Verse with one assisted tackle and one quarterback hit. Either way, the bigger point stands: eight games into the season, according to the transcript, Verse still didn’t have a sack.

Why does this matter for the Steelers going forward? Because the division is getting nastier, not friendlier. The Browns are frustrated. Pittsburgh has now beaten them 27-6 and taken over first place. And the next AFC North test, against Cincinnati, is just a week away.

WHAT IF KANSAS CITY WINS THE BIDDING?

Let’s be realistic about the downside, because it’s a real possibility.

If the Chiefs outbid Pittsburgh, Downs ends up in an AFC rival’s offense, catching passes in a building the Steelers might have to visit in January. That stings. But it’s not the end of the world, and it shouldn’t push Khan into a panic move.

Here’s why. The Steelers’ offense is built on Jaylen Warren and a run game that ranks among the league’s most efficient. Bernard is developing. Pittman should eventually return from IR. And there are still veteran options on the market at lower prices. The worst thing a contender can do at the deadline is overpay for plan B because plan A slipped away.

So if Downs goes elsewhere, I’d want a calm, cheap fallback, not a desperate reach for the next name on the list. Discipline is what got this team to six and two with its future picks mostly intact.

WHAT TO WATCH IN THE NEXT 48 HOURS

Let’s keep it simple.

First, the Colts-Cowboys game today. If Indianapolis loses badly, expect selling to accelerate. If they win, they might still sell, but the asking prices might firm up a little.

Second, Monday morning. That’s when Schultz’s timeline suggests offers get revisited. If the Steelers are serious, this is when it happens.

Third, Kansas City. If the Chiefs strike first, Pittsburgh will need a backup plan quickly, and names like Calvin Ridley have been mentioned as fallback options for several teams.

And fourth, the final hours on Tuesday. Plenty of deadline deals land in the last few hours before 4 p.m. Eastern. Don’t panic if Monday is quiet.

MY TRADE BLOCK BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board. This is opinion, not a report.

Josh Downs: yes at a 2028 fourth-round pick. Yes at a fifth if somehow it’s available. If Indianapolis demands a third, I only say yes if a pick comes back the other way. A straight third for a player on an expiring deal, I walk away.

Germie Bernard: keep him involved no matter what. Downs should complement the rookie, not replace him.

Mason McCormick: no discipline needed beyond the flag, but I’d like the energy channeled into run blocking, not the penalty column.

The core: untouchable. Highsmith, Watt, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier.
