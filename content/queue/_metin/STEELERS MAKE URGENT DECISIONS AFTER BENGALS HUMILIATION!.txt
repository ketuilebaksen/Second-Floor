Steelers Nation, the film from Cincinnati has been reviewed, the medical reports are coming in, and this is the week the Steelers’ coaching staff and front office have to start making choices.

Let me be clear about the headline. There hasn’t been some dramatic overnight shake-up. Nobody has been fired. The quarterback hasn’t been benched. But there are five decisions on the table that can’t wait very long, and Mike McCarthy addressed several of them in his Monday press conference.

Who plays left guard now that Mason McCormick is hurt. When the two injured receivers come back. Whether to chase the veteran receiver who just hit free agency. How to cover the back end if DeShon Elliott can’t go. And how to fix a passing game that opened Sunday’s game with a long string of incompletions.

So today, we’ll go through each one, using what the coaches have actually said. We’ll also ask the bigger question the transcript raises: was Sunday just a bad night, or a sign of something deeper? And my board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

WHERE THINGS STAND

First, a quick reset.

On November 15, the Bengals beat the Steelers 38-18 on Sunday Night Football. Cincinnati led 24-3 at the half. Joe Burrow completed 35 of 44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns. Ja’Marr Chase caught 11 balls for 231 yards. Aaron Rodgers threw for 143 yards.

The loss snapped Pittsburgh’s four-game winning streak and dropped the Steelers to 6-3, second in the AFC North behind Cincinnati at 7-2.

One small correction to the transcript: near the end, it calls the score 38-8. The final was 38-18. Pittsburgh’s two fourth-quarter touchdowns, to DK Metcalf and Pat Freiermuth, plus a two-point conversion, made up the 18.

URGENT DECISION ONE: LEFT GUARD

Let’s start with the decision that affects every single snap.

Starting left guard Mason McCormick went down late in the first half on a third-down sack. The medical staff put an air cast on his right leg, and he was carted off. On Monday, McCarthy still didn’t have a diagnosis to share.

According to Steelers.com’s Brian Batko, McCarthy said: “I don’t have anything where I can really put a number on it or what the final diagnosis is.”

But he also framed the situation in a way I found interesting. “I don’t look at it as a loss,” McCarthy said. “I look at it more as an adjustment.”

That’s a coach trying to keep the room from panicking. And it tells you the plan: the next man steps up, and the offense adjusts around him.

THE FIRST CHOICE: SPENCER ANDERSON

So who’s the next man?

According to Steelers.com, Spencer Anderson is expected to get the first chance to replace McCormick. He finished Sunday’s game at left guard, and he’s started for this team before.

The transcript also names Ryan McCollum as another depth option, and Pittsburgh Steelers On SI lists Doug Nester and Steven Jones as well.

Then there’s rookie Gennings Dunker, the third-round pick out of Iowa, who’s on injured reserve with a knee injury. McCarthy said on Monday that Dunker’s return is “definitely part of the conversation,” according to SI. Steelers Depot ran a headline saying Dunker is “possibly in the mix,” though I wasn’t able to open that article.

So the decision isn’t just “Anderson or nobody.” It’s whether Anderson holds the job, or whether Dunker gets healthy fast enough to compete for it.

WHY THIS ONE IS SO URGENT

The transcript puts it well: if Pittsburgh wants to protect Rodgers and keep the run game going, the offensive line has to stay dependable.

And McCormick was the definition of dependable. The transcript says he had played 43 consecutive games since entering the league in 2024, and ClutchPoints confirms the 43-game streak. The transcript adds that 40 of those were starts, which I couldn’t independently verify, so take that as the transcript’s number.

Either way, that’s a player the Steelers never had to think about. Now they have to think about his spot every day. And with the Eagles at 1 p.m. on Sunday and a short week before the Black Friday game against Denver, the replacement has very little time to settle in.

URGENT DECISION TWO: THE INJURED RECEIVERS

Now let’s go to the receiver room, because this is where McCarthy sounded most optimistic.

Two receivers are on injured reserve and eligible to have their practice windows opened. According to Steelers.com, Michael Pittman Jr. hasn’t played since Week 4 against Cleveland with a foot injury. Roman Wilson has been out since Week 5 against Indianapolis with a finger injury.

On Monday, McCarthy said he was “hopeful” Wilson could return for Sunday’s game in Philadelphia, according to PennLive’s Nick Farabaugh, as reported by CBS Sports. He also expressed optimism that Pittman could come off injured reserve this week.

And according to Steelers.com, McCarthy said: “Hopefully, we can get those guys out there and get them going.” He added that the team was still working through their return-to-play process, with answers expected as the team prepares for Wednesday’s practice.

So Wednesday is the day to watch. If both practice windows open, the Steelers could have two receivers back for Philadelphia.

HOW THE RECEIVER ROLES COULD SHAKE OUT

If both return, what does the depth chart look like?

According to RotoWire, via CBS Sports, Pittman would be expected to start alongside Metcalf. That would leave Wilson competing with rookie second-round pick Germie Bernard for the third receiver spot.

One correction here. The transcript refers to the rookie as “Jeremy Bernard.” His name is Germie.

The transcript also makes a fair point that the Steelers need more from the whole receiving group, not just Metcalf. Bernard, the tight ends and everyone else have to help create opportunities. Getting Pittman and Wilson back would give Rodgers more options than he’s had in weeks.

WHY ROMAN WILSON COULD MATTER MOST

If I had to pick the more important of the two returns for this offense, I might pick Wilson, even though Pittman is the bigger name.

Here’s why. The biggest weakness in this passing game, according to Athlon, has been the lack of downfield throws. Wilson is the receiver on this roster most built to fix that. In the September win over Cincinnati, he caught a 38-yard touchdown from Rodgers in the first quarter. That’s the kind of play that forces a safety to stay deep, which opens up room underneath for Warren, Freiermuth and Darnell Washington.

Pittman brings something different: size, reliability and the ability to move the chains. Both matter. But when an offense can’t stretch the field, everything gets compressed, and that’s what we saw in Cincinnati.

URGENT DECISION THREE: HOLLYWOOD BROWN

The third decision involves someone who isn’t on the team yet.

The Eagles waived veteran receiver Marquise “Hollywood” Brown last week. On Monday, according to Yahoo Sports, Brown officially cleared waivers and became a free agent. NFL insider Aaron Wilson was among the first to report it.

So any team can now sign him without going through the waiver order. That removes one big obstacle for Pittsburgh.

But the signals don’t point toward Pittsburgh. A HEAVY report from Friday said Steelers interest “might not be very high.” And Steelers Depot ran a headline citing Mark Kaboly that the Steelers have “zero interest” in Brown. I couldn’t open that article, so I’m going by the headline. But if that’s accurate, this decision may already be made.

THE CASE THE STEELERS PROBABLY WEIGHED

It’s not hard to see why the team might pass.

According to HEAVY, Brown suffered a Grade 1 ankle sprain in late September, and he managed just five catches for 63 yards in four games with Philadelphia. He still has speed. HEAVY notes he ran a 4.27 40-yard dash at his pro day, seven years ago. But with Wilson and Pittman both potentially returning this week, adding another receiver would create a crowded room.

If the team believes its two injured receivers are days away, signing Brown doesn’t make much sense. If one of them suffers a setback, the calculation could change quickly. So I’d file this decision under “probably no, but keep the phone nearby.”

URGENT DECISION FOUR: THE BACK END OF THE DEFENSE

Now let’s flip to the defense.

Safety DeShon Elliott suffered a knee injury during pregame warmups in Cincinnati and was ruled out. The transcript notes that Elliott had only recently returned after spending the first six games of the season on injured reserve. That’s a brutal turn of events for a player who was finally healthy.

His replacement, rookie Robert Spears-Jennings, was thrown into a much larger role. The transcript says he finished with six tackles. I couldn’t confirm that number, so take it as the transcript’s figure. But the bigger picture is clear: a rookie seventh-round pick was asked to help defend the best passing attack in the league with no warning, and the results reflected that.

As of what I’ve seen, the team hasn’t given a timeline on Elliott. So the coaches have to plan for both possibilities this week: Elliott back, or Spears-Jennings starting again.

THE INJURY CALENDAR

Let’s map out where the injured players stand, because the next two weeks could look very different from the last one.

Derrick Harmon is already back from injured reserve. Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. are eligible to have their practice windows opened this week. Gennings Dunker is at the end of his original four-to-six-week timeline, according to SI. Sebastian Joseph-Day is eligible to return for the Denver game, according to Steelers Now. And practice squad receiver Max Hurleman, who earned public praise from both Rodgers and McCarthy last week, is on the practice squad injured list.

On the other side of the ledger are the new injuries: McCormick and Elliott, with no timelines.

If most of the returning players come back on schedule, the Steelers could be healthier for Black Friday than they’ve been in a month. That’s the optimistic version. The realistic version is that at least one of those timelines will slip. The front office has to plan for both.

HOW TO PROTECT A YOUNG SAFETY

If Spears-Jennings does start in Philadelphia, there are ways to help him.

The simplest is to play more two-deep coverage, so he isn’t the last line of defense alone. Another is to let veteran cornerback Jalen Ramsey take on more communication responsibility. Ramsey forced a fumble on Sunday and has seen every coverage a quarterback can throw at him.

And the best help of all is a pass rush that gets home. The less time a quarterback has, the less a young safety has to cover. Which brings us to the next decision.

URGENT DECISION FIVE: FIXING THE PASS RUSH

The transcript says Watt, Cam Heyward and the rest of the defense have to find a way to create more pressure. That’s an understatement.

According to Steelers.com, Pittsburgh sacked Burrow only once on Sunday, on a play by Patrick Queen. Burrow completed almost 80 percent of his passes. That’s what happens when an elite quarterback has time.

There’s some good news here. Derrick Harmon returned from injured reserve for the game. One correction to the transcript: it says Harmon missed four games with an ankle injury. It was a foot injury, according to ESPN.

With Harmon back and Sebastian Joseph-Day eligible to return for the Denver game, the interior rotation should get stronger over the next two weeks. Defensive coordinator Patrick Graham will also have to think about moving his edge rushers around, so opposing tackles can’t settle in against one player all game.

THE PASSING GAME: THE BIGGEST QUESTION OF ALL

And that leads to what the transcript calls the most concerning part of Sunday night: the offense.

The transcript says Rodgers opened the game with seven consecutive incompletions. Some reports put the number at six, but either way, that’s not a start any team can survive against a top opponent. Pittsburgh didn’t pick up its first first down until well into the second quarter.

The transcript is fair to Rodgers. He did throw two fourth-quarter touchdowns and no interceptions. But it also points out that the passing game’s problems didn’t start Sunday, and that’s true.

According to Athlon Sports’ midseason review, before the Bengals game, Rodgers ranked 29th in completion percentage and 31st in average depth of target. Athlon also counted 15 dropped passes by Steelers receivers through eight games, already closing in on last season’s total of 22.

So the issues are a mix of everything: protection, drops, missing receivers and a quarterback who can’t extend plays the way he used to. No single change fixes all of it.

WHAT MCCARTHY CAN CHANGE THIS WEEK

So what can the coaching staff actually do before Sunday?

First, script the opening drive for easy completions. Quick throws, screens and play action can get Rodgers into a rhythm before the defense gets comfortable. Opening with a run of incompletions is the worst-case scenario, and the first 15 plays are where coaches have the most control.

Second, protect the new left guard. Chip with tight ends and backs on his side. Use more quick drops. Don’t leave a replacement player one-on-one against the opponent’s best interior rusher.

Third, stay with the run. Jaylen Warren is one of the most productive backs in football this season, but he had just seven carries in Cincinnati because the Steelers fell behind so quickly. Against a team that’s struggling, establishing the run early could set the tone.

And fourth, get the returning receivers involved quickly if they’re active. Even a few targets for Pittman or Wilson would give Rodgers someone new for the defense to account for.

ONE BAD NIGHT, OR A WARNING SIGN?

Now, the transcript’s central question: was Sunday just one terrible performance, or are we seeing problems that could threaten the playoff hopes?

Here’s the case for “one bad night.” The Steelers had won four straight before this game. The defense had been one of the best in the league. They were playing a red-hot division rival on the road in prime time. And according to the Bengals, Cincinnati has now scored 30 or more points in five straight games, the longest streak in franchise history. Good teams lose to teams playing that well.

Here’s the case for “warning sign.” The passing game was already a concern before Sunday. The offensive line just lost its most reliable guard. The secondary was exposed without its veteran safety. And the McCarthy era hasn’t yet proven how it handles adversity.

My honest answer is: it’s too early to know. The Eagles game will tell us a lot. A strong, clean win, and Sunday looks like a one-off. A sloppy loss, and the warning sign gets a lot louder.

WHAT CINCINNATI DID THAT PITTSBURGH CAN’T ALLOW AGAIN

Before we move to Philadelphia, it’s worth naming exactly how the Bengals won, because the Steelers can’t let another team follow the same script.

First, they started fast. According to the AP, Burrow was 26 of 34 for 302 yards and three touchdowns in the first half alone. By halftime, the game was basically over.

Second, they controlled the clock. According to Heavy, Cincinnati held the ball for 35 minutes and 9 seconds and racked up 484 yards. Pittsburgh’s defense spent most of the night on the field.

Third, they didn’t give anything away. Neither team turned the ball over. That meant the Steelers’ defense never got a short field or a momentum-changing stop.

The lesson is simple. Pittsburgh’s formula only works when the offense keeps the game close early and the defense creates mistakes. Neither happened in Cincinnati.

THE DIVISION PICTURE

And it’s worth understanding what’s at stake in the standings.

At 6-3, the Steelers are one game behind Cincinnati with eight games to go. The season series is split one game apiece, so neither team holds a head-to-head edge. As the tiebreakers are generally understood, the next step would be division record.

In plain terms, the Steelers can’t count on a tiebreaker to save them. They need to win games and hope Cincinnati stumbles. A one-game gap is very manageable over eight weeks. A two-game gap would be a much bigger problem. That’s another reason the Philadelphia game isn’t a game Pittsburgh can afford to drop.

PHILADELPHIA: THE OPPORTUNITY

Let’s talk about that game.

The transcript correctly lists kickoff as 1 p.m. Eastern on Sunday, November 22. The game was flexed from its original 4:25 time, according to Steelers Now, and it’s on CBS.

The Eagles are in rough shape. According to Yahoo Sports, Philadelphia has lost seven straight and sits at 2-7, its worst start since 1999. Jalen Hurts is out for the season, and Joe Milton started their last game, a 19-11 loss to the Giants. The Eagles are coming off their bye.

But there’s a reason nobody in Pittsburgh is taking this lightly. According to Steelers.com, the Steelers haven’t won a regular-season game in Philadelphia since 1965. They’re 0-11 there since. That’s not a streak you can ignore.

WHAT THE EAGLES STILL HAVE

Don’t let the record fool you completely.

According to the Steelers’ preseason opponent preview, the Eagles’ defense, under coordinator Vic Fangio, was built around a strong coverage group that included Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean and Riq Woolen. That kind of secondary can make life hard for a passing game that’s already struggling to find open receivers.

Philadelphia also traded defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville at the deadline, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer. That could make the Eagles’ interior easier to run against, which is exactly where the Steelers should want to attack.

So the plan writes itself. Run the ball at the interior. Use the run to set up play action. And avoid forcing throws into a secondary that can make you pay.

WHAT A RESPONSE LOOKS LIKE

The transcript says the Eagles game could tell us a lot about this team’s character, and I agree.

A real response doesn’t just mean a win. It means a fast start on offense. It means the new left guard holding up. It means the pass rush getting home against a quarterback with limited experience. And it means the defense not giving up the big plays that buried them in Cincinnati.

McCarthy said on Sunday night that “handling success is the biggest challenge in this league.” This week, the challenge is the opposite: handling a loss. If the Steelers can do that in Philadelphia, they’ll head into the short Black Friday week feeling a lot better about where they are.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the five decisions. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Left guard: Spencer Anderson starts in Philadelphia. Open Gennings Dunker’s practice window if the medical staff clears him, and let the two compete over the next two weeks.

Receivers: open both windows on Wednesday. Activate Roman Wilson for Philadelphia if he practices fully. Give Michael Pittman Jr. one more week if there’s any doubt, and target the Denver game.

Hollywood Brown: pass for now. If Wilson or Pittman suffers a setback, revisit it.

Safety: if Elliott can’t go, start Spears-Jennings with more two-deep coverage and extra help from Ramsey.

Pass rush: move T.J. Watt around the formation. Let Harmon rotate inside on passing downs. Pressure the young quarterback early.

Offense: script the first drive for quick completions, feed Warren, and chip on the left side to help the new guard.
