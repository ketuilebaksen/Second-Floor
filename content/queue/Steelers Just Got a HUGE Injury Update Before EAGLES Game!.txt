Steelers Nation, rewind to Sunday night for a second.

Mason McCormick is being carted off in an air cast. DeShon Elliott never made it past warmups. Nick Herbig is walking off holding his side. The receiver room is so thin that Germie Bernard, a rookie, is getting targets he isn’t ready for. And the scoreboard in Cincinnati says 38-18.

If you had told me that night where this injury report would be by Friday morning, I wouldn’t have believed you.

That’s what today is about. The injury update you’re hearing about is real, and for the most part, it’s good. But I want to do this a little differently. Instead of just reading you the report, I’m going to go player by player and compare what we feared on Sunday night with what we actually know now. Fear versus reality.

Then we’ll get into a quiet roster move from Wednesday, the outside receivers whose names keep popping up, and what all of it means for Philadelphia.

And yes, the board at the end. Opinion, not a report.

Here we go.

WHY THIS WEEK FELT SO HEAVY

Let’s set the scene, because context matters here.

The Steelers are 6-3. They came into Cincinnati on a four-game winning streak, leading the AFC North. They left a game behind the Bengals, who now hold the season split. And they left with a list of injuries that looked like it could change the shape of the season.

The video I’m working from today puts it well. Watching an offensive lineman leave the field with an air cast, with a playoff run in front of the team, made it feel like we’d seen the last of him this year. That’s exactly how a lot of us felt.

So when a team in that spot gets a run of good medical news, it matters more than usual. It isn’t just about Sunday. It’s about whether this season still has the shape we thought it had in early November.

MASON MCCORMICK: THE FEAR

The fear on Sunday night was simple: season over.

You don’t usually see a guard leave on a cart with an air cast and come back that year. And McCormick isn’t just any guard. He’s been a fixture on the left side, a leader in the room, and one of the reasons this run game took off this season.

MASON MCCORMICK: THE REALITY

The reality is a lot kinder.

According to NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, as reported by WPXI, McCormick dislocated his ankle but didn’t fracture anything. The ankle was put back in place right on the field. He’s not expected to need surgery. And there’s a real chance he returns in the postseason.

He’s on injured reserve now, and the Steelers announced that move on November 18, according to Steelers.com. So he’s not walking through that door in Philadelphia. The video I’m working from is right to be careful here: he’s going to miss significant time, and there’s still uncertainty about his recovery.

But “out for a while, possibly back for the playoffs” is a world away from “done for the year.” If this team keeps winning and gets to January, it could get one of its best linemen back exactly when it needs him most.

THE LEFT GUARD IN THE MEANTIME

So who holds the fort?

The video expects Spencer Anderson to get the first shot at left guard, and that matches what Still Curtain reported this week. The video’s reasoning is simple and fair: Anderson has played both guard spots, he knows the job, and against a team like Philadelphia, experience is worth a lot.

The video also mentions Brock Hoffman as interior depth that gives the Steelers flexibility. I’d frame that a little more carefully. Hoffman is the starting right guard, and he’s played well there. Moving him around just to patch the left side would create a problem on the right. So when I hear “flexibility,” I think of it as an emergency option, not a plan.

GENNINGS DUNKER: THE FEAR

With Dunker, the fear wasn’t really about his health. It was about time.

He went on injured reserve in late September with an MCL sprain suffered in practice. He’s a third-round rookie who’d never played a regular-season snap. And the worry was that by the time he was healthy, the season would have moved past him, and he’d spend his rookie year as a spectator.

GENNINGS DUNKER: THE REALITY

The reality is that he’s back, and he looks ready to practice.

His 21-day window opened on Wednesday. According to the Thursday injury report, as compiled by Still Curtain and PhillyVoice, Dunker was a full participant both Wednesday and Thursday. Two full days in a row for a player coming off a knee sprain is a clean start.

The video I’m working from calls Dunker the most interesting return of the bunch, because he opens a real question about the offensive line. And it makes a point I agree with: he has the physical traits to be an interesting developmental piece, but that doesn’t make him ready to start. What it does is give the coaches another option, so they’re not locked into one arrangement no matter how it performs.

That’s the right way to think about it. Dunker isn’t the answer this week. He’s insurance for the weeks after.

ROMAN WILSON: THE FEAR

Wilson’s injury felt like a slow leak.

He hurt his hand against the Colts on October 11, went on injured reserve and kept missing games. And every week without him, the passing game seemed to lose a little more of its speed. The fear was that he’d come back too late to matter.

ROMAN WILSON: THE REALITY

The reality is that Wilson has been the cleanest return of the week.

He was full on Wednesday and full again on Thursday, according to the injury report. The video gets his numbers right: 14 catches for 188 yards and two touchdowns this season. As the video says, those numbers aren’t huge, but they show he was already contributing.

The video adds that with Aaron Rodgers at quarterback, there’s value in a receiver who already knows the system and has built a connection with him. I’d go a step further. When a 42-year-old quarterback is trying to get the ball out fast, a receiver who’s already earned his trust is worth more than any outside name.

And the video ends that thought the right way: if Wilson gets another chance, he needs a meaningful role, not an afterthought. I couldn’t agree more.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: THE FEAR

Pittman’s fear was the scariest of the receivers, because his injury has a history.

His foot has bothered him since the summer. He’s played in only three games. And plantar fasciitis, as PhillyVoice describes it, isn’t the kind of injury that heals on a calendar. The fear was that he might not be a real factor for the Steelers at all this season.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: THE REALITY

Here’s where the video I’m working from is a step behind, and that’s not its fault.

The video was recorded after Wednesday, when Pittman was limited. Its take was not to get ahead of ourselves, because Pittman wasn’t where Wilson was yet.

But on Thursday, Pittman went full, according to Yahoo Sports and Still Curtain. And PhillyVoice’s Jimmy Kempski wrote that it’s “a good bet he’ll be activated this week and play on Sunday.”

So the reality moved faster than anyone expected. That doesn’t mean Pittman’s foot is fully behind him. It means he’s practicing like a player who’s ready to try. Friday’s practice will tell us more.

DESHON ELLIOTT: THE FEAR

Elliott’s injury might have been the most unsettling, because of how it happened.

He didn’t get hurt on a play. He got hurt in pregame warmups, and he never played a snap in Cincinnati. Then the Bengals’ passing game went off for 409 yards. You couldn’t help wondering how different that night looks with your veteran safety on the field.

DESHON ELLIOTT: THE REALITY

The reality is that Elliott is trending toward playing.

The video saw him limited on Wednesday and said, sensibly, that limited is far better than sitting out entirely, but that the team shouldn’t take unnecessary risks with his knee. Since then, Elliott moved up to full on Thursday, according to the injury report.

That’s a big step for a secondary that needs a communicator back there. Especially with Philadelphia’s top receiver, DeVonta Smith, also trending toward a return, according to PhillyVoice.

NICK HERBIG: THE FEAR

Herbig’s fear was that he took a beating in Cincinnati and might need time.

NICK HERBIG: THE REALITY

And here’s the one case where the reality hasn’t gotten better yet.

Herbig missed practice on Wednesday and again on Thursday with hip and rib injuries, according to the injury report. The video explains why that matters: an edge rusher has to take hits, explode off the line and hold up against blockers. Hip and rib injuries affect all of that.

The video points to Jack Sawyer as another option on the edge. And according to Still Curtain, if Herbig can’t play, Watt, Highsmith and Sawyer would absorb his snaps.

I’m not going to speculate on how serious it is. Two straight missed practices doesn’t automatically mean he’s out. But it means Friday’s practice and the final injury report are the ones to watch.

PATRICK QUEEN: THE FEAR AND THE REALITY

Queen popped up on the report this week with a hand injury, and the video isn’t ready to assume he plays.

That’s fair. But here’s the reality as of Thursday: Queen was limited both days, according to the report. Not out. Limited. Linebackers play through hand injuries all the time, often with a club or a wrap. I’d be surprised if he isn’t out there in Philadelphia, but we’ll get his official status on Friday.

ADDING IT UP

So let’s tally it.

McCormick: feared done for the year, now possibly back for the playoffs.

Dunker: feared lost for the season, now practicing fully.

Wilson: feared late, now practicing fully two days in a row.

Pittman: feared not a real factor this year, now full on Thursday.

Elliott: feared to be lingering, now full.

Queen: limited, but practicing.

Herbig: still the question mark.

That’s six out of seven trending the right way. After the week this team had, I’ll take that every time.

THE QUIET ROSTER MOVE YOU MIGHT HAVE MISSED

Now, the video mentions a smaller move that’s worth explaining.

On November 18, the same day McCormick went on injured reserve, the Steelers made two practice squad moves, according to Steelers.com. Max Hurleman was moved from the practice squad’s injured list back to the active practice squad. And to make room, receiver Isaiah Hodgins was released from the practice squad.

Hodgins is a former Giants receiver who’s been in and out of Pittsburgh’s practice squad. He’s not a household name. But the move tells you something. The Steelers chose Hurleman, a young undrafted player they clearly believe in, over a veteran depth piece. Both Aaron Rodgers and Mike McCarthy have praised Hurleman publicly in recent weeks. This is the front office backing that up.

The video makes a good general point here: watch how a team handles the guys on the edge of the roster this time of year, because those decisions grow in value once injuries force adjustments.

THE ROSTER CRUNCH THAT’S COMING

And there’s a bigger version of that coming this weekend.

Wilson and Pittman are both still on injured reserve. To play Sunday, they have to be activated to the 53-man roster, and as it’s generally understood, that has to happen by 4 p.m. Eastern on Saturday. McCormick’s move to IR opened one spot. If both receivers are activated, another move is likely. I’m not going to guess who.

THE OUTSIDE RECEIVERS: WHY THE NAMES KEEP COMING UP

Now let’s talk about the part of the video that goes beyond the injury report: outside receivers.

The video’s argument is that even with Wilson and Pittman returning, it’s not sure this offense has enough reliable options. That’s why outside names keep coming up. And it names three: Brandon Aiyuk, Tyreek Hill and Darius Slayton.

Let me be very clear before we go through them: there is no report of the Steelers pursuing any of these players. The video says the same thing. This is about who’s out there, not about what Pittsburgh is doing.

BRANDON AIYUK: WHERE THINGS STAND

Aiyuk is the most interesting case right now, because there’s a deadline.

The NFL reinstated him on November 9, according to EssentiallySports via Yardbarker, and that gave the 49ers a two-game roster exemption through November 23. By then, San Francisco has to either add him to the 53-man roster or release him.

And it doesn’t sound like he’s staying. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said on November 18: “We haven’t discussed it simply because the writing has been on the wall for a while now.” Rosenhaus also said Aiyuk is “100% healthy right now, in shape, and working out full force.” And Kyle Shanahan has said the reinstatement “means absolutely nothing to our team.”

Rosenhaus said on November 10 that he expects Aiyuk to play this season, with a team that has a prior connection to him from college or the NFL.

WHY WASHINGTON IS THE NAME TO WATCH

That connection detail is why the video points at Washington, and it’s a fair point.

According to Sports Illustrated, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported on Wednesday that Aiyuk and Jayden Daniels have repaired their relationship. The two were teammates at Arizona State. Things had soured after Aiyuk’s social media comments over the summer, but Fowler reported that Aiyuk reached out about a month ago and the two are “good again.”

The video also notes that Washington general manager Adam Peters used to work in San Francisco’s front office. Put that together with Rosenhaus talking about familiarity, and Washington looks like the most natural landing spot, if Aiyuk gets released.

Pittsburgh doesn’t have that kind of connection. That doesn’t rule anything out, but it does make the Steelers a long shot.

AIYUK’S RISKS

And let’s be honest about the risks with Aiyuk, wherever he goes.

He hasn’t played since October 20, 2024, when he tore his ACL and MCL and damaged his meniscus against the Chiefs, according to EssentiallySports. That’s more than two years without a game. And the AP has reported the league told Aiyuk he owes the 49ers about $13 million in previously paid bonuses. That’s a complicated situation for any team to step into.

The talent is real. In 2023, he had 75 catches for 1,342 yards and was a second-team All-Pro. But in my view, the cost here isn’t money. It’s the time it would take to get him game-ready, and the Steelers don’t have a lot of that.

TYREEK HILL: A DIFFERENT KIND OF QUESTION

The video’s take on Tyreek Hill is that his biggest issue is medical. He’s recovering from a serious knee injury, and the video says he hasn’t been cleared to return. I can’t independently confirm his exact medical status this week, so I’ll treat that as the video’s description.

Bleacher Report’s Moe Moton listed Hill among the top free agents after the trade deadline, with Kansas City and the Los Angeles Rams as the best fits, not Pittsburgh. Hill also has an off-field legal matter that teams are expected to weigh, and that’s all I’ll say about it.

The video makes the right point: for a receiver whose greatest strength is explosive speed, his physical condition matters enormously. Until he’s cleared and signed somewhere, it’s just a name.

DARIUS SLAYTON: THE MOST REALISTIC NAME

Now here’s the one the video actually seems to like, and it’s the most interesting from a Steelers point of view.

Darius Slayton was released by the Giants in September, ending a seven-season run in New York, according to ABC7. He signed with Indianapolis, where a knee issue limited him, and the Colts released him on October 16 without him ever playing a game, according to Colts.com.

And here’s the part that caught my eye. Bleacher Report’s Moe Moton listed Slayton among the top free agents available after the trade deadline, and named the Steelers as one of his two best team fits, along with the Rams. Moton’s description: “Darius Slayton is a big-play receiver who averaged 15 yards per catch in seven seasons with the New York Giants.”

That’s an opinion piece, not a report of interest. But it’s a sign people around the league see a fit.

THE CASE AGAINST SLAYTON

The video’s hesitation is fair, though.

It points out that Slayton has had issues with drops during his career, and that he’s never played with Rodgers. He’d also arrive after a long stretch without game action. And the video’s conclusion is that it would rather give Wilson more chances than rush in someone new.

I land in the same spot, for now. If Wilson and Pittman both get activated, the receiver room goes from desperate to crowded. Adding Slayton this week would be solving a problem that may have just solved itself. If one of them has a setback, or the passing game still struggles after a couple of weeks, Slayton becomes a much more interesting phone call.

THE TAYLOR DECKER WILD CARD

One more name from that same Bleacher Report list, because it ties back to the offensive line.

Moton also linked veteran left tackle Taylor Decker to the Steelers, writing that Decker “may be waiting for the right situation to sign with a contender.” His idea is to sign Decker to play left tackle and move Troy Fautanu back to the right side.

I’ll be honest, I’m not a fan of that one this season. Fautanu has settled in, and the right side with Max Iheanachor has been working. Shuffling two tackle spots to fix a guard problem feels like it creates more questions than it answers. But it shows how much attention this line is getting after McCormick’s injury.

WHAT PHILADELPHIA MEANS IN ALL OF THIS

The video closes with the Eagles, and it’s right that this is no small challenge. The Steelers haven’t won in Philadelphia since 1965, according to Steelers.com. And this is a hard road trip right after the Cincinnati loss.

The video’s ask is simple: a better offensive performance, starting with the run game, and a defense that’s ready no matter who’s cleared. The injuries can’t become an excuse for another letdown.

That’s the right mindset. And here’s why the injury news matters for that game specifically. If Wilson and Pittman are both active, Philadelphia can’t stack the box against Jaylen Warren the way Cincinnati could. If Elliott is back, the secondary has its communicator for DeVonta Smith. And if the pass rush can get after Joe Milton, the Steelers can control this game the way they did against Cleveland.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board after this week’s injury news. Opinion, not a report.

McCormick: no rush at all. Let the ankle heal and aim for January.

Left guard: Anderson starts. Keep Hoffman where he is on the right side.

Dunker: keep practicing. Activate when there’s room and the coaches are comfortable, but no first NFL start in a road game this week.

Wilson: activate and give him a real role, four to six targets.

Pittman: activate if Friday goes clean, with a snap count.

Elliott: play him if he’s full again Friday.

Herbig: no risks. Watt, Highsmith and Sawyer can handle Milton.

Outside receivers: no signing this week. Revisit Slayton only if a setback hits. Aiyuk and Hill: watch, don’t chase.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. On Sunday night, this injury report looked like it could define the second half of the Steelers’ season. By Friday, it looks like a team getting healthier at exactly the right time.

McCormick has a path back. Dunker, Wilson and Pittman are practicing fully. Elliott is trending toward playing. Queen is practicing. The only real worry is Herbig.

The video I’m working from today says it best at the end: what matters most is making smart choices with these returning players. Use Wilson. Prepare Dunker. Don’t rush McCormick. And don’t let the injuries become an excuse.

Do that, and Philadelphia becomes the bounce-back game this team needs.
