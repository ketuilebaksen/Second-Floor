Steelers Nation, let’s get the headline settled in the first thirty seconds, because I know exactly what some of you clicked on. Aaron Rodgers is the starting quarterback on Sunday against the Cleveland Browns. Mason Rudolph is not taking over. Will Howard is not getting a surprise start. Drew Allar is not being thrown into a division war as a rookie. No quarterback change has been announced, and nothing in this week’s injury reports suggests one is coming.

So what is the quarterback decision? It’s bigger than a name on the depth chart. Over the last month, Mike McCarthy has made a choice about what he asks Aaron Rodgers to be. Not the gunslinger who threw 40 passes in Cleveland back in Week 4. Something different. Something that a Hall of Fame quarterback went on national radio this week to say will never win in January.

That’s the fight we’re having today. Kurt Warner versus the Steelers’ new identity. Cam Heyward versus Jared Verse. And Josh Downs, who just asked the Colts to trade him, with Pittsburgh named in the report. Buckle up. I’m James, this is Second Floor, and Browns week just got loud.

THE REAL DECISION: RODGERS AS THE POINT GUARD, NOT THE SCORER

This is the heart of the whole video, so stay with me.

Think back to the first Browns game. October 1, Thursday night, Cleveland. Rodgers dropped back 40 times. He threw for 299 yards and three touchdowns, but he also threw two interceptions, got sacked five times, and got hit ten times. The Browns played man coverage on 73.3 percent of their defensive snaps that night, according to Steeler Nation’s breakdown, and they dared a 42-year-old quarterback to win on his own. Pittsburgh lost 27-24 on a 56-yard field goal with ten seconds left.

Now look at what McCarthy has done since. Against the Colts, Jaylen Warren touched the ball 21 times and piled up 188 scrimmage yards. Against Tampa, the comeback came late, but the formula started shifting. And in Paris against the Saints, it was 30 carries and an ultra-jumbo package with Spencer Anderson reporting as an extra blocker. The final drive was eleven straight runs.

That is a quarterback decision. McCarthy decided that the best version of this offense isn’t Rodgers carrying it. It’s Rodgers steering it. Pick the right run, check out of the bad look, hit the play-action shot when the safety bites, and protect the football. In other words, the quarterback becomes the point guard who runs the offense, and Warren becomes the guy who takes the big shots.

And McCarthy is saying it out loud, just in his own words. On Monday he said, “Jaylen’s hot as a firecracker. Definitely want him to touch the football.” He also dropped a line that sounds like a philosophy statement for the whole building: “You really get what you are tolerant to out there.” Translation: we’re not tolerating a pass-happy, sack-heavy offense anymore.

THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE SHIFT

Here’s why McCarthy made that call. Athlon Sports crunched it this week. From Week 3 through Week 7, Pittsburgh ranks first in the entire NFL in rushing EPA per play. First. On dropbacks, the Steelers rank 18th. Middle of the pack.

When one part of your offense is the best in football and the other is average, you lean on the best one. That’s not conservative. That’s math.

There’s another way to see the cost of the old approach. In Cleveland, with Rodgers throwing 40 times, Warren got just 20 touches despite averaging well over five yards a carry, and the Steelers lost. Since then, the touches have tilted toward the backfield, and the team hasn’t lost since.

And yes, there’s a caution flag. Over the last two games, Athlon noted the offense averaged 17.5 points in regulation. The red zone touchdown rate for the season sits around 47 percent. In Paris it was one touchdown on four trips by McCarthy’s count. That’s where the Warner criticism lands, and it’s fair to hear him out.

WHAT ABOUT THE QUARTERBACK ROOM?

Since the headline is about a quarterback decision, let’s close the door on the depth-chart rumors properly. Pittsburgh has carried four quarterbacks all season, a choice McCarthy made back in late August. Rodgers is the starter. Mason Rudolph, 31 years old with 19 career starts, is the backup. Will Howard is the emergency option. And Drew Allar, the third-round pick out of Penn State, 76th overall, is the developmental rookie who has spent his Sundays inactive.

None of that has changed this week. There’s no injury forcing a switch, no benching, no package for a backup. Rudolph has value as insurance precisely because this team plans to play deep into January, and Allar’s development is a 2027 conversation, not a Browns-week one. Anybody selling you a quarterback switch on Sunday is selling you something that doesn’t exist.

ROUND ONE: KURT WARNER’S CASE

Kurt Warner was on the broadcast for the Saints game, then went on The Rich Eisen Show on Wednesday, and he was blunt. His verdict on a 5-2 team: “I don’t think they’re gonna go very far.”

His argument goes like this. Pittsburgh moved the ball against New Orleans, but scored only 16 points. “How do we generate points?” he asked. “They’re struggling to create explosive plays.” He made the point that Warren can rip off a huge run, but counting on a running back to manufacture explosive plays every single week is a tough way to live. And the transcript version of his comments adds the image that defenses would love playing a team that needs ten or twelve plays to score every touchdown.

Let me be fair to Warner, because he’s not some hot-take merchant. He’s a Hall of Famer who quarterbacked one of the most explosive offenses in league history. When he says long drives are fragile, he’s right about the risk. One penalty, one sack, one dropped pass, and a twelve-play drive becomes a punt. In the playoffs, against Kansas City or Buffalo, you might need to score in three plays to answer a touchdown.

ROUND TWO: WHY I THINK WARNER IS HALF RIGHT

Here’s my pushback. Warner is describing the ceiling. Pittsburgh is trying to win the floor first, and right now the floor is first place in the AFC North.

Long drives aren’t just a style. They’re a weapon against a specific opponent. When you hold the ball for six minutes, the other quarterback is standing on the sideline in a cap. In Paris, the Steelers closed out the game with an 11-play drive that ate 5:52. The Saints never got the ball back with a real chance. That’s not a bug in the system. That’s the system.

And the explosive-play problem has a real-world explanation that Warner didn’t dwell on. Two of the Steelers’ top four receivers are on injured reserve. Michael Pittman Jr. is out with his foot. Roman Wilson is out with his finger. Ben Skowronek has missed the last two games with a calf, though he was limited in practice on Wednesday. You can’t run four-verticals when half your vertical threats are in the trainer’s room.

So my score on Round Two: Warner’s diagnosis is accurate today. His prognosis assumes nothing changes. And with the trade deadline on November 10, things can change.

ROUND THREE: WHAT RODGERS IS SAYING

Rodgers isn’t pretending the passing game is fine. After Paris, he said, “We had a lot of good plays today. We still got to play a little better on offense and help our defense out.”

Then on Wednesday, he went straight at the Cleveland problem. The Browns live in man coverage, so he put it on everybody, himself included: “You got to win your one-on-ones,” and “get open and I got to get them the ball.” Simple. Direct. That’s a veteran telling his receivers that Sunday is a fistfight on the outside.

And he made his pitch for help. Asked about Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who rejoined the practice squad on October 20, Rodgers lit up. “I love Marquez,” he said, calling him “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with,” a “super smart guy” who “knows the game really well,” with “really good hands” and “great instincts and great football intelligence.” Then the line that matters: “It’d be nice to get him up.”

Now, that’s the other QB decision in this story, and it belongs to McCarthy, not Rodgers. Does the head coach give his quarterback the familiar deep threat he’s asking for? MVS has never been elevated this season for Pittsburgh. Brandon Smith has used all three of his elevations and went back to the practice squad Monday. If the Steelers want Smith on the field, they have to sign him to the 53. If they want MVS, they can elevate him. Possibly both. That’s a decision we’ll see on the Saturday transaction wire, and if Rodgers gets his guy, you’ll know whose voice carried the most weight.

One more Rodgers note from the archives. Last Friday McCarthy said that when Rodgers came back this spring, “he still felt some effects from the injury, you know, in New York.” McCarthy didn’t describe any current setback, and Rodgers is not on this week’s injury report. But it’s a reminder of why this staff has been smart about how many hits their quarterback absorbs. Fewer dropbacks means fewer shots on a 42-year-old body. That’s part of the decision too.

ROUND FOUR: THE RUSSELL WILSON CAMEO

Here’s one I didn’t expect. Russell Wilson, a former Steelers quarterback himself, went on CBS’s Set Hut show with Kordell Stewart and Chris Long, and he talked about T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward chasing their first ring. His words: “It’s their time to finally win one, and hopefully Aaron Rodgers can take them all the way.” And then: “If they can win this division, we will see what happens.”

So you’ve got one Hall of Fame quarterback saying this team won’t go far, and another veteran quarterback who played in this building saying the division is the doorway. I know which one I’d rather hang in the locker room.

HEYWARD VERSUS VERSE: WORDS THAT WILL BE ADDRESSED

Now let’s get to the trash talk, because Cam Heyward brought it this week.

Background first. Jared Verse is the former Defensive Rookie of the Year who came to Cleveland from the Rams in June, in the trade that sent Myles Garrett to Los Angeles. After the Browns beat Pittsburgh on October 1, Verse went after the Steelers’ offensive line. According to Heavy, he told reporters he doesn’t have any respect for anyone who plays dirty and does extra stuff after the whistle, and the gist of his complaint was that the Steelers double-teamed him, slid protection to him, and chipped him.

On Tuesday, Rich Eisen, openly stirring the pot, played the Verse clip for Heyward. Cam laughed, then went to work. “Double-teaming, sliding, chipping, are those not allowed in our game?” he asked. “Yeah, I got the bruises to prove it. But we don’t whine about it, baby. It’s part of the game.” And then the line Steelers fans are going to put on a T-shirt: “But those are words that were said and they will be addressed.”

Here’s the part that makes this juicy. Verse has no sacks through seven games, according to Heavy. He was a big name in a big trade, and he hasn’t gotten home yet this season. And now he’s listed as a non-participant on Wednesday’s practice report with a knee. If he plays, Pittsburgh’s line knows exactly who’s coming. If he doesn’t, Cleveland loses a key piece of its edge rush.

Either way, Heyward picked his spot perfectly. He didn’t throw a punch. He just reminded everyone that the Steelers block the way every team blocks a dangerous rusher, and that being double-teamed is a compliment, not a crime.

THE STAKES ONE MORE TIME

Sunday, 1 p.m., Acrisure Stadium, CBS. Both teams are 5-2. The winner sits alone atop the AFC North going into November.

The transcript I worked from made one historical point that a second outlet has also mentioned, so I’ll pass it along with the right caveat: Pittsburgh is trying to avoid being swept by Cleveland for the first time since 1988. I haven’t verified that in the record book myself, so treat it as a reported stat, not gospel. It’s also worth remembering these two teams met in the playoffs after the 2020 season, and Cleveland won that one. That’s the kind of history the Browns will happily bring up all week.

THE BIG TRADE NEWS: JOSH DOWNS WANTS OUT

Now the news that actually broke on Wednesday, and it matters for everything we just talked about.

First, Indianapolis started selling. On Tuesday, October 27, the Colts traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-rounder, according to Heavy. Hours later, they sent DeForest Buckner back to San Francisco, the team that drafted him, for a 2027 fourth-round pick. The Colts are 2-5 and in rebuild mode after firing GM Chris Ballard.

Then on Wednesday, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported, quote, “Colts WR Josh Downs is requesting a trade from Indianapolis.” And Jordan Schultz reported that Downs has received interest from multiple AFC teams, “including the Steelers.” Per Schultz, a deal isn’t considered likely this week, but a trade is expected to happen eventually.

So the Steelers didn’t just get mentioned by a blogger. A national insider put Pittsburgh’s name in the sentence, on the same day the player asked to be moved.

Who is Downs? A 2023 third-round pick out of North Carolina, now in his fourth season. His best year was 2024, when he caught 72 passes for 803 yards and five touchdowns. This year he has 26 catches for 307 yards and no touchdowns through seven games, which still leads the Colts in receiving yards. A lot of that drop-off is the environment. The whole Indianapolis offense has sputtered.

And his contract situation explains the request. He’s in the last year of his rookie deal. Asked about an extension before the season, Downs shrugged it off with, “If it came my way, it came my way, but eh.” That’s a player who knew where this was heading.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Now I’m switching modes. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Should Omar Khan get Josh Downs? Yes, at the right price, and the Colts just showed us the price range. If an eight-time Pro Bowl guard brought back a third and a fifth, and Buckner brought back a fourth, then a 25-year-old slot receiver on an expiring deal who just asked out of town should not cost a second-rounder. My number is a fourth-round pick, maybe a fourth plus a late swap. And I would not touch the 2028 second-round pick Dallas sent Pittsburgh in the Joey Porter Jr. trade.

Why Downs fits the quarterback decision we’ve been talking about: he’s a quick-separation slot receiver. In a run-heavy offense, the passes that matter are the third-and-six throws where the defense knows it’s a pass. Rodgers needs somebody who wins in two seconds against man coverage. Put Metcalf and Germie Bernard outside, Downs in the slot, Freiermuth and Darnell Washington underneath, and suddenly Cleveland can’t play 73 percent man without paying for it.

The risk? He’s a rental unless Pittsburgh extends him, and the transcript cited a Spotrac projection of about $65.2 million over three years. I couldn’t verify that figure myself, so take it as a projection the other video cited, not a fact. If that’s the market, Pittsburgh probably won’t pay it next spring, and you’re renting him for ten games plus the playoffs.

The other names floating around, Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville and Jauan Jennings in Minnesota, are reasonable conversations, but neither has a fresh report tying them to Pittsburgh this week. Downs is the only one with a trade request and a named Steelers link at the same time. That’s why he’s my number one target.

And if the price climbs? Walk away. Get Pittman back after the bye, elevate MVS, and trust the identity.

FIVE THINGS TO WATCH SUNDAY

One. Rodgers’ dropbacks. If he throws 40 times again, Cleveland is winning the game script. If it’s closer to 28 or 30 with 30-plus carries, Pittsburgh is in control.

Two. Third-and-medium. That’s where the passing game earns its money. Watch who Rodgers trusts. Metcalf, Bernard, maybe MVS if he’s up.

Three. Verse versus the right side of the line. If Verse plays, will the Steelers slide to him again? Heyward already told you: they’ll do it, and they won’t apologize.

Four. Red-zone touchdowns. Field goals got it done in Paris. Against Cleveland, every trip inside the 20 that ends with Chris Boswell’s leg instead of a touchdown keeps the Browns in it.

Five. The fourth quarter clock. If Pittsburgh has the lead with eight minutes left, I want Warren and Dowdle on a mission to make sure Deshaun Watson never touches the ball again.

PREDICTION AND YOUR TURN

My call: Steelers 23, Browns 17. Rodgers doesn’t throw 300 yards, and he doesn’t need to. Warren goes over 100 again, the defense gets a takeaway, and Kurt Warner gets reminded that winning ugly in October still counts in January.
