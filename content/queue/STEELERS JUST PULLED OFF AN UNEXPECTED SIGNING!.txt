Steelers Nation, close your eyes for a second and picture it. It’s January. Snow is coming down at Acrisure Stadium. Aaron Rodgers takes the snap in the final season of his legendary career, looks left, and sees DK Metcalf locked in single coverage. He looks right, and there’s Tyreek Hill, already two steps behind the cornerback. The ball comes out, and the whole stadium is on its feet. That’s the dream a lot of Steelers fans are having this week: Tyreek Hill in black and gold, and a real run at a seventh Lombardi Trophy. So today, I’m going to make the full case for why Hill could be the piece that turns this team into a champion. But I’m also going to give you every fact, because there are some big hurdles between the dream and reality. As I record this, Hill has not signed with any team, and there is no report connecting him to Pittsburgh. Everything about Hill in a Steelers uniform is my opinion, not a report. Plus, the final injury report for Tampa is out, and there’s a new name on it you need to know about. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s dream a little, and then let’s get real.

Part one: why the Steelers need a weapon like Hill right now.

Let’s start with the need, because it’s bigger than it was even a week ago. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot. Roman Wilson, with a dislocated finger, and Ben Skowronek, with a calf injury, have been ruled out for Sunday in Tampa. And on Friday, the final injury report added a new worry: DK Metcalf missed practice with an illness and is listed as questionable. Steelers Depot expects him to play, but he’s not feeling 100 percent.

So think about where that leaves Pittsburgh. Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen are the healthy receivers on the 53-man roster, with Brandon Smith likely to be elevated from the practice squad again. That’s thin for a team with playoff hopes and a 42-year-old quarterback playing his final season.

And people around the league are noticing. Rodgers’ old Green Bay teammate James Jones said this week, “I do think that they need to make a move,” and added, “I would love to see a guy like Tyreek Hill.” Steelers insider Mark Kaboly posted that he’s getting “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th trade deadline.

And the competition isn’t standing still. Just last week, the Baltimore Ravens traded for two-time Pro Bowl center Cam Jurgens from Philadelphia, sending the Eagles a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. When a division rival is loading up in the middle of the season, it tells you the AFC North race is going to be a fight to the finish. Pittsburgh can’t afford to just hope its injured receivers heal in time. Every contender in the AFC is looking for that one extra piece, and the Steelers should be too.

Part two: who Tyreek Hill is, at his best.

If you’re a younger fan who’s only seen the last two years, let me remind you what Hill has been in this league. He’s made eight Pro Bowls, every year from 2016 through 2023. He’s been named a first-team All-Pro five times. He won a Super Bowl with Kansas City in Super Bowl 54.

And look at his best years in Miami. In 2022, he caught 119 passes for 1,710 yards. In 2023, he caught 119 passes again, for 1,799 yards and 13 touchdowns, and led the entire NFL in receiving yards. That’s not a good receiver. That’s one of the most dangerous offensive weapons of his generation, a player defenses had to plan their entire week around.

Now imagine that player as the second option. Not the first. The second. Because in Pittsburgh, he’d line up across from Metcalf.

Part three: the championship blueprint, my opinion.

So here’s how I see it, and again, this is my opinion, not a report. If Hill comes to Pittsburgh healthy, this is how he helps the Steelers chase a title.

Number one: he breaks man coverage. After the Cleveland loss, McCarthy pointed to struggles against man coverage. Defenses have been daring Pittsburgh’s receivers to win one-on-one. Put Hill on one side and Metcalf on the other, and you can’t double both. One of them is getting single coverage on every snap, and both of them have spent their careers punishing single coverage.

Number two: he opens the running game. Jaylen Warren is the reigning AFC Offensive Player of the Week after putting up 188 yards from scrimmage against the Colts. Rico Dowdle, a two-time 1,000-yard rusher, practiced fully on Thursday and Friday and is set to return from his toe injury. If safeties have to play deep because of Hill’s speed, there are fewer bodies in the box. That’s more room for Warren and Dowdle to run.

Number three: he fits Rodgers’ final run perfectly. Rodgers has made it clear this is his last season. Before the Cleveland game, he told Al Michaels that a year from now, “I’ll either be tending to my pregnant wife or I’ll be taking care of my newborn.” So the window is now. And Rodgers has been on fire, completing 63 of 105 passes for 826 yards and nine touchdowns over his last three games. Giving a quarterback that hot a weapon like Hill is how you turn a good season into a special one.

Number four: it costs no draft picks. Hill is a free agent. Miami released him in February. There’s no trade, no second-round pick, no young player going out the door. Khan could keep the 2028 second-round pick he got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade for another move.

Number five: the timing lines up. Hill is still rehabbing from the knee injury that ended his 2025 season, and he’s still at least several weeks from returning to the field. Covers, citing The Athletic, reported his targeted return timetable as November. And when is Pittman eligible to come back from injured reserve? Right around the same time, after the Week 9 bye. So picture December: Metcalf, Pittman, Hill, Bernard, Freiermuth, Warren, and Dowdle. That’s an offense that can win in the playoffs, in any weather, against anybody.

And here’s the defensive side of the championship equation. A defense that just held Jonathan Taylor to 45 yards and forced the Colts into five field goals. T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward are healthy. Jalen Ramsey is playing through a broken wrist. Jamel Dean is getting close. DeShon Elliott is working his way back from injured reserve. Add a game-breaking weapon on offense to that defense, and this team would have the pieces to beat anyone in the AFC. That’s the dream.

And here’s the twist that makes this championship story even better. Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers have already won a Super Bowl together. In February 2011, McCarthy’s Packers beat, of all teams, the Pittsburgh Steelers in Super Bowl 45, with Rodgers named the game’s MVP. Fifteen years later, that same coach and that same quarterback are wearing black and gold, trying to win one for the city they beat that night. In Green Bay, McCarthy and Rodgers had dangerous receivers like Greg Jennings, Jordy Nelson, and later Davante Adams. That’s how they won. So when people ask why a receiver like Hill would matter so much, look at their history. This duo wins when it has weapons.

That’s the full picture of the dream. A legendary quarterback in his final season, a coach who’s already won it all with him, a red-hot backfield, a physical defense, and one more game-breaking receiver. It’s the kind of story Hollywood would write. Now let’s see if real life cooperates.

Part four: the reality check, and I owe you the whole truth.

Now let me be the friend who tells you the hard part, because you deserve all the facts.

First, there’s no report linking Hill to Pittsburgh. None. Pro Football Rumors reported this week that the Chiefs are seen as the early frontrunner, possibly his only realistic option this season, even though Kansas City has just over 3 million dollars in cap space. Other teams have shown interest, but no talks have progressed.

And it’s easy to see why Kansas City is the favorite. Hill spent his first six NFL seasons with the Chiefs, from 2016 through 2021, and won his only Super Bowl ring there. Reports earlier this month said Chiefs players were all for bringing him back. And after Kansas City receiver Tyquan Thornton suffered an ankle injury, Hill posted on social media in a way that only fueled the reunion talk. That’s a lot of history for any other team to compete with.

Second, nothing is close. On Thursday, his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said, “Nothing is imminent at this time.” He added that Hill is “continuing his rehab with the goal of being on a team in the immediate future.”

Third, the knee. Hill dislocated his left knee on September 29th of last season and tore multiple ligaments, including his ACL. He had surgery the next day. He’s 32 years old. That’s a serious injury for a player whose whole game is built on speed. We don’t know yet if he’ll be the same player.

Fourth, and this one matters: Hill could face NFL discipline. According to The Athletic, the league could look at discipline under its personal conduct policy after a civil case involving Hill concluded this month. Any team that signs him has to account for the possibility that he might not be available right away even after he’s healthy. That’s a real factor, and a front office as careful as Khan’s will weigh it seriously.

So if you’re asking me whether Hill is coming to Pittsburgh, the honest answer is: there’s no sign of it right now, and the odds are low. If you’re asking whether he could help the Steelers win a title if he were healthy and available, my answer is yes, he absolutely could. Those are two very different questions.

What would it take for Pittsburgh to make it happen?

Let’s play it out anyway, because this is the fun part. If the Chiefs can’t make the money work with just over 3 million dollars in cap space, and Hill decides he wants a different situation, Pittsburgh would be an attractive landing spot. A Hall of Fame quarterback in his final season. A team that’s 3-2 and climbing. A clear path to a starting role while Pittman, Wilson, and Skowronek heal. And a head coach in McCarthy who has built offenses around explosive receivers before.

In my opinion, the smart way to do it would be a one-year deal with a modest base salary and incentives tied to games played and production. That protects the Steelers if the knee isn’t ready or if the league suspends him, and it rewards Hill if he comes back as the player he used to be. Khan has shown all season that he likes deals with protection built in. This would be the same idea, just with a much bigger name.

And here’s the thing about championship teams: they almost always take one big swing at the right time. Last season, Pittsburgh made a midseason trade for safety Kyle Dugger. This year, with Rodgers in his final season and the receiver room this thin, the right time might be now. The question is whether Hill is the right swing, or whether a safer option like Jennings or Jeudy is the smarter one.

Part five: the cornerback video you might have seen.

Now, a quick correction. There’s another video going around this week that spends 15 minutes talking about the Steelers trading for Colts cornerback Charvarius Ward. Even that video calls it “hypothetical” by the end. There is no report connecting Ward to Pittsburgh.

And there are real reasons to be careful. Ward dealt with serious concussion problems in 2025. In November, he told the Colts’ website that during his recovery, “I was kind of doubting if I was gonna play football again because it was like that scary.” He came back, and that’s great to see. But it’s a lot to consider for a midseason trade.

More importantly, Pittsburgh’s cornerback room is getting healthier on its own. Jamel Dean is questionable for Sunday and told reporters he’s “very optimistic.” Asante Samuel Jr. broke up four passes against the Colts. Rookie Daylen Everette is developing. And Khan added Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith off practice squads two weeks ago. The secondary didn’t allow a single passing touchdown against Indianapolis. That’s not a unit in crisis.

Part six: the trade block, if Hill doesn’t happen.

So if the Chiefs land Hill, or if the Steelers decide not to take the risk, what’s Plan B? Here’s my opinion, not a report.

Jauan Jennings of the Vikings is the most realistic target. Pro Football Talk reported this week that he could be traded after Minnesota added Odell Beckham Jr. to its practice squad, and Ian Rapoport said the 49ers “make sense” as a suitor. Jennings caught 77 passes for 975 yards in 2024 and nine touchdowns in 2025, and much of his one-year deal has already been paid through his signing bonus. A late-round pick could get it done.

Jerry Jeudy of the Browns is the more aggressive idea. NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala called him “just an odd man out” in Cleveland. He had 1,229 yards and a Pro Bowl in 2024. It would take an AFC North trade, which is rare, but not impossible.

And Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville remains the dream long-term target, with that Dallas second-round pick as the centerpiece if the Jaguars ever decide to sell.

Any of those players would help. But none of them have the ceiling of a healthy Tyreek Hill. That’s why fans keep coming back to his name.

Part seven: the final injury report for Tampa.

Now let’s talk about Sunday, because the championship dream starts with winning in Tampa.

Here’s the final injury report. Ruled out: Roman Wilson, Ben Skowronek, and DeShon Elliott, who isn’t being activated from injured reserve this week. He needs more full practices and could be ready for Week 7 against the Saints in Paris.

Questionable: DK Metcalf, with an illness, after missing Friday’s practice. Jalen Ramsey, with his wrist. Jamel Dean, with his ankle. Rico Dowdle, with his toe, although he practiced fully on Thursday and Friday. And Keeanu Benton, with his ankle, who was also full on Friday.

T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward have no game status. They’re good to go.

Here’s what I’ll be watching on offense if Metcalf plays at less than full strength. First, how McCarthy uses Dowdle and Warren together. Two backs who can both catch the ball give Rodgers checkdown options when the receivers are covered. Second, how often Pittsburgh uses two tight ends with Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington, especially on early downs. And third, how much Bernard gets targeted. After his six-catch, 80-yard performance in his first start, McCarthy said “football comes easy to him.” Sunday might be the day he becomes a real number two option.

The biggest one to watch is Metcalf. If he’s limited, Bernard, Smith, and the tight ends will have to carry the passing game. That’s when Warren and Dowdle become even more important. And it’s exactly why a weapon like Hill, or Jennings, or Jeudy, would make such a difference over the rest of the season.

On the other sideline, Tampa Bay is 1-4, and Baker Mayfield is likely out with a dislocated thumb. He didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday, and Todd Bowles said he hadn’t resumed throwing. That means undrafted rookie Jalon Daniels will probably start, the same quarterback who led the Bucs to their first win in Dallas last week. The Steelers opened as three-point road favorites. But remember, Pittsburgh has lost five straight games on the road. This is the week to end that.

The bottom line.

So let me bring it all together. Can Tyreek Hill lead the Steelers to a championship? If he’s healthy and available, he has the talent to make this offense one of the most dangerous in the league, and paired with Metcalf, Warren, Dowdle, and Rodgers in his final season, the ceiling would be a Super Bowl. That’s the dream, and I understand why Steelers Nation loves it.

But the reality is that Hill hasn’t signed anywhere, his agent says nothing is imminent, the Chiefs are the favorite, his knee is still recovering, and possible league discipline is hanging over his next contract. So I’d treat this as a long shot for now.

What I do know is this: Omar Khan is reportedly preparing to be active before the deadline, the receiver room needs help, and this team is good enough right now that one more weapon could make it a real contender. Whether that weapon is Hill or someone else, the next three weeks are going to be fascinating. And if the answer does turn out to be Hill, healthy and cleared to play, then the picture I painted at the start of this video, Rodgers throwing to Hill in the January snow, might not be a dream at all.
