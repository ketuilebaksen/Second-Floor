Steelers Nation, the Steelers fly to Paris today, and the news has been coming in from every direction before they even get on the plane. Jalen Ramsey just picked up a league honor, and he also missed practice. DeShon Elliott took a big step toward his return, and Omar Khan cleared a roster spot that points straight at him. A two-time Super Bowl champion receiver is back in the building. There are new names on the injury report you need to know about. And ESPN’s trade insiders just dropped two star safeties into the deadline conversation. So today, we’re going through every update, one by one, and telling you what each one means for Sunday’s game against the Saints and for the rest of this season. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Update one: Jalen Ramsey is the AFC Defensive Player of the Week.

Let’s start with the good news. On Wednesday, the NFL named Jalen Ramsey the AFC Defensive Player of the Week for his performance in the overtime win at Tampa Bay. And when you look at the numbers, it’s easy to see why. Playing with a broken wrist, Ramsey finished with 10 tackles, nine of them solo, four tackles for loss, and two sacks, including the fourth-and-8 sack that ended the game.

According to Steelers Now, he’s the first player since 2023 to put up at least 10 tackles, four tackles for loss, and two sacks in a single game, and the first defensive back to do it since 2000. And here’s the part I love: the only other Steeler to reach those marks since sacks became an official stat in 1982 is T.J. Watt, who did it against Cleveland in Week 1 of 2018. That’s the company Ramsey is keeping.

It’s the third Defensive Player of the Week award of his career. The first came in 2016 with Jacksonville, and the second in 2023 with Miami. And he’s now the third Steeler to win a weekly league honor this season, after Watt in Week 1 and Jaylen Warren on offense in Week 5.

Mike McCarthy summed him up perfectly: “Jalen Ramsey is just a multidimensional player.” On the game-ending sack, McCarthy said it was “exactly how you draw it up,” and he gave credit to Watt and Alex Highsmith for setting the edges so Ramsey could get home.

Why Ramsey’s role is finally clicking.

There’s a bigger story behind the award, too. Before the season, Graham’s plan was to move Ramsey around, using him in the slot, at safety, and as a pass rusher, instead of locking him on one side of the field. Still Curtain’s Allison Koehler wrote this week that the award shows the Steelers have found his best role, because offenses now have to account for him everywhere.

But she also added a fair warning, and I want to share it, because honest analysis matters. She wrote that “one dominant outing against a 1-5 opponent and an undrafted rookie” doesn’t erase Ramsey’s coverage struggles from 2025. That’s true. One game doesn’t define a season. But if Graham keeps using him as a moving chess piece instead of asking him to shadow receivers all day, I think we’ll see more performances like this one.

But here’s the catch.

Ramsey didn’t practice on Wednesday because of that wrist. Now, he’s been playing through it for weeks, and he’s missed practice before and still played on Sunday. So I wouldn’t panic. But with Brandin Echols also out of practice with his hamstring, the cornerback room is thin heading into an overseas trip, and that’s something to keep an eye on when the Thursday report comes out.

And there’s a contract angle that came up this week too. Former Steelers linebacker Arthur Moats floated the idea of an extension, saying it would be “interesting if he can stay at this level.” To be clear, that’s not a report of negotiations. Ramsey is under contract through 2028 after the Steelers reworked his deal following the trade from Miami, so there’s no rush. But when a 31-year-old is playing like this, the conversation is going to come up.

Why the Steelers’ defense keeps winning awards.

Think about this for a second. Three different Steelers have already won weekly league honors this season, and we’re only six games in. Watt won on defense in Week 1. Warren won on offense in Week 5, the week he ran for 101 yards against the Colts. And now Ramsey. That’s a sign of a roster with stars at multiple positions, not a team relying on one player.

And on defense especially, it’s become a group effort. Herbig had two sacks in Tampa. Dean set a franchise record with six passes defended and picked off a pass. Payton Wilson had 11 tackles. When this many players are making plays, offenses can’t focus on stopping one guy. That’s what makes this defense so hard to prepare for.

Update two: DeShon Elliott is knocking on the door.

The second big update is in the secondary. DeShon Elliott was a full participant in practice on Wednesday and is officially designated to return from injured reserve. When McCarthy was asked about him, he kept it simple: “We have a practice plan for him.”

And then Khan made a move that tells you a lot. On Wednesday, the Steelers released veteran safety Rayshawn Jenkins. Jenkins, who’s 32, started in Week 3, but his playing time dropped every week after that, and he’d been limited to special teams. Pro Football Rumors noted that the release could be about opening a roster spot for Elliott to come off injured reserve, with about eight days left in his 21-day window.

Now, he’s not officially activated yet, so we can’t say for sure that he plays in Paris. But the pieces are lining up. And when he does come back, he’ll be joining a safety room where Jaquan Brisker has taken on a bigger role and rookie Robert Spears-Jennings has played well in sub-packages. Patrick Graham has already said there could be competition for snaps. That’s a healthy problem to have, and it’s one most teams would love to deal with in late October.

Update three: a two-time Super Bowl champion is back.

Update number three is on offense. The Steelers brought back wide receiver Marquez Valdes-Scantling on the practice squad after the Chargers released him over the weekend. This is a player with two Super Bowl rings from his time with the Chiefs, and a long history with Aaron Rodgers. They spent four seasons together in Green Bay, where MVS caught 123 passes for more than 2,100 yards and 13 touchdowns.

He was also with Pittsburgh late last season, catching 10 passes for 80 yards and a touchdown in five regular-season games, plus three catches in the playoff game. So he knows the building, the quarterback, and the head coach, who was coaching the Packers when Green Bay drafted him in 2018.

Will he play Sunday? McCarthy’s answer was “We’ll see.” But with Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve and Ben Skowronek still out with his calf, the Steelers need bodies at receiver. A practice squad elevation for Paris would make a lot of sense.

Update four: the injury report you need to know.

Now let’s go through the rest of Wednesday’s injury report, because there are some new names on it.

Alex Highsmith was limited with a groin injury. That’s worth watching, because he’s had groin problems before, including in 2024, when a Week 3 groin injury cost him three games. The hope is that he doesn’t miss any time. If he does, Nick Herbig would take on more snaps opposite Watt, and Jack Sawyer would see a bigger role.

Defensive lineman Sebastian Joseph-Day didn’t practice because of a triceps injury. That matters because the interior is already thin with Derrick Harmon on injured reserve. If Joseph-Day can’t go, more snaps would go to Keeanu Benton, Cam Heyward, and Yahya Black.

Rico Dowdle was limited with a knee issue. That’s one to monitor, because McCarthy said this week that running the ball more is “clearly a focus,” and Dowdle averaged almost six yards a carry in Tampa.

And finally, T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward were given veteran rest days on Wednesday. That’s normal for players their age, especially in a week with long travel. Nothing to worry about there.

Update five: the travel plan and the Saints.

And speaking of travel, here’s an interesting contrast. The Saints are already in France. They held their first practice in Paris on Wednesday, at a stadium in Versailles. The Steelers made a different choice. They stayed home after the Tampa game, practiced in Pittsburgh this week, and they’re flying out after Thursday’s practice. McCarthy explained it this way: “We looked at both options, and we clearly feel that this one, leaving Thursday, is the best one for us.”

There’s a trade-off. The Saints get more time to adjust to the time difference. The Steelers get a more normal week of preparation at their own facility. We’ll find out on Sunday which approach works better. Kickoff is at 9:30 in the morning Pittsburgh time at the Stade de France.

As for the Saints themselves, they’re 2-4, and they have injuries of their own. Running back Alvin Kamara was limited with a back issue, linebacker Kaden Elliss was limited with a calf injury, and right guard Cesar Ruiz didn’t practice with a knee injury. Quarterback Tyler Shough was a full participant despite a left hand injury. Kicker Daniel Carlson stayed in the U.S. for the birth of his fourth child, but the team expects him back before the game. And their first-round receiver, Jordyn Tyson, the eighth overall pick, is still on injured reserve with a hamstring injury and won’t play.

Saints head coach Kellen Moore made it clear how much Elliss matters to his defense, saying, “Kaden is obviously an important part to our defense.” And Saints general manager Mickey Loomis said of Tyson’s injury, “It can happen to any player. It’s unfortunate that it happened at the very beginning of his career.” So New Orleans is not at full strength either, which is something the Steelers have to take advantage of.

Update six: ESPN’s trade intel and two star safeties.

Now, the trade deadline news. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler and Dan Graziano released their latest trade intel this week, and two names jumped out for Steelers fans: Atlanta’s Jessie Bates and the Chargers’ Derwin James.

Let’s start with Bates. He’s 29, one of the best free safeties in football, and he’s set to become a free agent in 2027. The Falcons are 2-4, and ESPN named him as a possible deadline target. Fowler’s view, as I understand it, is that Atlanta doesn’t want to trade him, but his value would be strong if that changes. This season, he has 31 tackles, a forced fumble, and two passes defensed through six games. The Falcons face San Francisco this week, then Tampa Bay and Cincinnati, so if they keep losing, the conversation could change before the deadline.

Then there’s Derwin James. The Chargers are 0-6, and Jason La Canfora expects them to have a “significant sell-off.” James is 30, and he signed a three-year, $75.6 million extension that keeps him under contract through 2029. He’s one of the most versatile defenders in football, a player who can line up at safety, in the box, or in the slot. In three games this season, he has 21 tackles and a pass defensed. He’s also had injuries in his career, including missing all but five games in 2019 and the entire 2020 season.

Trade block: would I make either move?

Let’s get into it, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Picture Derwin James and Jalen Ramsey in the same secondary. Two big, versatile defensive backs who can play anywhere. Patrick Graham would have a field day designing disguises with those two. On paper, it’s a terrifying idea for opposing quarterbacks.

But here’s my problem. This defense is already expensive. Watt is one of the highest-paid defenders in the league. Nick Herbig signed a four-year extension worth up to $100 million in June. Ramsey is under contract through 2028. Adding a 30-year-old safety on a deal running through 2029 would lock in even more money on veteran defenders. And with Elliott coming back, safety might not be the biggest need anymore.

And remember how much the Steelers have already invested to keep this defense together. Herbig’s extension came after a steady rise, from three and a half sacks as a rookie in 2023, to five and a half in 2024, to seven and a half last season. Khan paid him because he’s become a core piece. Every dollar spent on a new veteran safety is a dollar that can’t go toward keeping players like that in Pittsburgh.

Bates makes a little more sense as a short-term move, because he’s in the last year of his deal. If the Falcons fall out of the race and the price is a mid-round pick, that’s a conversation worth having. But I’d only do it if Elliott’s return doesn’t go smoothly.

My priority is still the offense. With Pittman and Wilson out, a receiver who can get open quickly is the biggest need, and as we talked about earlier this week, the Colts’ situation could put Josh Downs on the market after they fired their general manager. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before November 10th. If I’m Khan, I spend on a weapon for Rodgers before I spend on another defensive back.

What all these updates mean for Sunday.

So let’s put this wave of news together and figure out what it means for Paris.

The good news: the defense is playing at a high level, Ramsey is the AFC Defensive Player of the Week, Elliott is close, and MVS gives the offense another option. The concerning news: Ramsey, Echols, Joseph-Day, Highsmith, and Dowdle all showed up on the injury report, and the Steelers are taking a long flight just three days before kickoff.

That’s why depth matters so much this week. Khan has spent the whole month building it. Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith are available at cornerback. Valdes-Scantling is ready at receiver. Jack Sawyer is ready on the edge. If a couple of starters can’t go, the next man up has to be ready, and this roster is built for that.

And the stakes are high. The Steelers are 4-2, tied with Cleveland and Baltimore, and the Browns come to Pittsburgh one week after Paris. A win in France would set up a huge division game with a chance to take control of the AFC North before the bye.

A historic stage.

And let’s not forget how big this weekend is beyond the standings. This is the first regular-season NFL game ever played in Paris, and the Steelers are one of the two teams on that stage. The league said it’s “excited for the first-ever regular season game in Paris,” and despite the protests in France, it has said preparations are continuing as scheduled, with security plans in place. McCarthy has never coached an international game, so this is new territory for him as well.

For a franchise with a fan base as global as Steelers Nation, that matters. There will be Terrible Towels waving in the Stade de France on Sunday. And for a team trying to prove it’s a real contender, there’s no better way to make a statement than winning on a stage the whole world is watching.

What I’m watching on Thursday.

Here’s my checklist for Thursday’s final practice before the flight. First, does Ramsey get on the field in any capacity? Even a limited session would be a great sign. Second, is Elliott activated, or does the team wait until Saturday? Third, does Highsmith upgrade to a full practice? And fourth, does the team make any other roster moves before boarding the plane, maybe to add depth on the defensive line?

And one more thing I’ll be watching: whether the Steelers bring an extra player or two on the trip as insurance. With a long flight and so many players listed on the report, it wouldn’t be surprising to see the team travel with extra depth at cornerback and on the defensive line, just in case someone wakes up sore in France.

Those answers will tell us a lot about what the Steelers’ lineup looks like in Paris. And with the game kicking off on Sunday morning back home, there won’t be much time between the final injury report and kickoff.

The bottom line.

So yes, the Steelers just got a wave of updates before the Saints game. Ramsey is the AFC Defensive Player of the Week. Elliott is close to returning, and Khan cleared space for him. A two-time Super Bowl champion receiver is back. And ESPN just added Jessie Bates and Derwin James to the trade conversation.

Some of it is great news. Some of it, like the injury report, is a reason to be cautious. But the bigger picture is a team that’s playing its best football of the season, with a front office that keeps finding answers before problems become crises. Now it’s time to take that show to Paris, and show the whole world what this team is becoming.
