The Steelers traded Joey Porter Jr. to the Cowboys right before the Browns game. So the question became: what do you do? And honestly, Pittsburgh had three doors in front of it. Let's walk through all three, because the choice they made tells you a lot about how Omar Khan and Mike McCarthy are thinking right now.

Door number one: trust the guys already in the building.

That means Asante Samuel Jr. on one side and rookie Daylen Everette on the other. Samuel is the veteran of that pair. Pittsburgh re-signed him this offseason, and he's been serviceable, not spectacular. He's quick, he's got good instincts, he's got ball skills that run in the family, but he's not the biggest corner in the world, and against the long, physical receivers this team has seen in the first month, there have been moments where he's been out-muscled at the catch point.

Everette is the more interesting story. He was the Steelers' third-round pick out of Georgia this spring, and he's about to get thrown into the deep end. In Cleveland, after Dean went down, Everette got real snaps, and a lot of fans walked away blaming him for the 60-yard catch by Browns receiver Denzel Boston. Here's the thing. Mike McCarthy went out of his way on Monday to defend the kid, and I think it's important you hear it, because it changes how you view that play. Which brings us to door number two, the door Pittsburgh actually opened.

On Monday, the Steelers signed cornerback Sean Murphy-Bunting off the Tampa Bay Buccaneers' practice squad and put him straight onto the 53-man roster. To make room, they placed Harmon on IR. They also waived defensive back Doneiko Slaughter, who had already used all three of his game-day elevations in the first three weeks, and signed linebacker Marte Mapu, formerly of the New England Patriots, to the practice squad.

Now, who is Sean Murphy-Bunting? Let me give you the real resume. He's a seven-year veteran. Tampa Bay drafted him in 2019, and he was a big part of the Buccaneers team that won Super Bowl Fifty-Five. He's played 82 career games with 65 starts, and he's got 11 interceptions, 34 passes defensed, and eight forced fumbles. After Tampa, he went to Tennessee, then to Arizona. And he fits a profile Pittsburgh clearly likes on the outside: he's over six feet tall, a length guy, the same type of body as Porter and Dean.

But you need the full picture, not just the highlight reel, because we don't do hype on this channel. Murphy-Bunting missed the entire 2025 season. Arizona put him on the non-football injury list, and he didn't play a snap. The Cardinals let him go, and he ended up back in Tampa on the practice squad. His last real season was 2024 in Arizona, when he allowed 37 catches on 52 targets for 390 yards and two touchdowns, while picking off three passes. So he's a guy who gives up completions, but he also makes plays on the ball. And he hasn't played a meaningful regular-season snap in well over a year.

So why him? I think the logic is pretty clear. Number one, he's healthy right now and he's been practicing all season. Number two, he's a true outside corner with size, which is exactly the hole Porter and Dean left. Number three, he's not coming in expecting to be the savior. He's coming in to compete, to give Everette a veteran to lean on, and to make sure that if one more corner goes down, the Steelers aren't moving Jalen Ramsey all over the field just to plug holes. And number four, he's cheap and low-risk. If it works, great. If it doesn't, Dean is coming back in a couple of weeks anyway.

And here's a fun little wrinkle. Look at the schedule. After the Colts, the Steelers go to Tampa in Week 6. Murphy-Bunting just came from that building. He knows that offense, he knows those receivers, he practiced against them every day for the last month. And Jamel Dean? He spent his whole career in Tampa before coming to Pittsburgh. Dean and Murphy-Bunting were actually in the same 2019 Buccaneers draft class and won that Super Bowl together. If Dean is back for that game, you could have two former Bucs on the outside in Tampa. After a rough couple of years, including a torn ACL in 2023 and a 2025 season split between Dallas and Green Bay, Diggs signed with the Seattle Seahawks' practice squad in August. He waited. So why did so many Steelers fans want Diggs? Because the connections are almost too perfect. Mike McCarthy coached Diggs in Dallas from 2020 through 2024. And Pittsburgh's secondary coach, Joe Whitt Jr., coached him in Dallas from 2021 through 2023, which includes the 2021 season when Diggs led the entire NFL with 11 interceptions, took two of them back for touchdowns, and made first-team All-Pro. He made back-to-back Pro Bowls in 2021 and 2022. You're talking about a player who knows the terminology, knows the coaches, and knows exactly what they want from him. And now let me clear up the rules, because the transcript floating around got some of this right and some of it wrong. A player on another team's practice squad can be signed by any team, and it costs nothing in a trade. No picks, no players. The catch is that the signing team has to keep him on its 53-man active roster for at least three weeks. The other catch is that the original team can protect a handful of practice squad players each week, and if Seattle protects Diggs, he's off the table for that week. And once Seattle elevated him for Sunday, he reverted back to their practice squad after the game. So technically, the window for Pittsburgh is open again this week, as long as Seattle doesn't protect him or sign him to its active roster first.

Also, one more correction. You'll hear people say Diggs is 29. He's 28. And he's not "two seasons removed" from leading the league in interceptions. That was 2021, five seasons ago. That matters, because the Diggs you're getting in 2026 is not the 2021 Diggs. He's a guy post-ACL, coming off a down 2025, who just got his first snaps of this season.
