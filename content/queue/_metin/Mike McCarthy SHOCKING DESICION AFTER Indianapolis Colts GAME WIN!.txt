Steelers Nation, Mike McCarthy walked into that press room on Sunday with a smile on his face, and he had every reason to wear it. Pittsburgh 34, Indianapolis 22. Three days after a painful 27-24 loss in Cleveland, a fifth straight road loss going back to last season, a week full of headlines about sideline arguments and a coach under pressure, the Steelers delivered the kind of statement win that changes the whole mood of a season. And when McCarthy finally got to the podium, he didn’t gloat. He didn’t fire back at the critics. He talked about preparation, about growth, about his players, and about a running back who just made 40-year history. Today, we’re going through McCarthy’s victory message line by line, and I’ll show you exactly how every word he said showed up on that field. This is James with Second Floor, and this one is fun.

Let’s set the scene first, because you have to remember where this team was a week ago.

The Steelers lost on Thursday night in Cleveland. The broadcast caught Aaron Rodgers and McCarthy cursing at each other on the sideline. The national media jumped all over it. One national writer even suggested the Colts should come to Pittsburgh and put the Steelers “out of their misery.” The record was 2-2, and the questions about McCarthy’s first season were getting louder every day.

And how did McCarthy respond before the game? He didn’t panic. He said, “I’m a huge believer in preparation.” And then he said something that tells you exactly who he is as a coach: “When it doesn’t look right, I prepare more.” He also said, “You are what you put on film and you are what your record says you are.” No excuses. Just work.

And don’t let anybody tell you this was an easy opponent. The Colts came into Pittsburgh playing good football. They had beaten Washington 30-13 in London, they’d come back to beat Houston, and they’d taken Kansas City to overtime on the road before losing. Jonathan Taylor, Daniel Jones, tight end Tyler Warren, a strong defensive interior with DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart. The Steelers were only favored by two and a half points. This was supposed to be a coin flip. It turned into a 12-point win.

Now let’s look at what he said after the game, because every line has a story behind it.

McCarthy’s message, part one: “We redded it up.”

The first thing McCarthy said set the tone. “As we say in Pittsburgh, we redd it up, we redded it up.” That’s real Pittsburgh talk. “Redd up” means tidy up, put things back in order. And it was the perfect answer to his own words from earlier in the week, when he said the team had to “clean our own house.”

Think about how much had to be cleaned up. A road loss. A sideline argument on national TV. An offense that couldn’t finish in Cleveland. And in one week, the Steelers came home and played their best all-around football of the season. McCarthy called it a positive beginning, and that’s the right way to see it. It’s not a finish line. It’s a starting point.

And here’s what I love most about it. A coach who’s been in this league as long as McCarthy doesn’t need to make big speeches. He used one local phrase, and the whole city understood. That’s a coach who’s starting to understand Pittsburgh.

McCarthy’s message, part two: “This was clearly our best week.”

This is the line that tells you the most about how this win happened. McCarthy explained that after the Cleveland loss, he changed the routine. On Tuesday, before turning to the Colts, the team focused completely on itself, studying its own mistakes. He said, “I think taking Tuesday and focusing on ourselves was the right call.” And then: “The newness of how you do things in year one, this was clearly our best week.” He also said, “I think anytime you win a game, it comes off of a healthy week of preparation.”

That’s a coach who’s honest enough to admit the first month wasn’t perfect, and smart enough to change it. And you could see that preparation everywhere on Sunday.

You saw it in Rodgers, who completed his first 11 passes and didn’t throw an incompletion until there was 6:14 left in the second quarter. You saw it in the red zone, where the Colts came away with field goals instead of touchdowns again and again. You saw it on third down, where Indianapolis converted only 4 of 15. That’s not luck. That’s a team that knew exactly what was coming.

And here’s how the game actually unfolded, because the flow of it shows you a team that kept answering every punch.

The Colts struck first with a 58-yard field goal from Spencer Shrader. The Steelers answered with Warren’s 80-yard touchdown. Indianapolis came right back with a long kickoff return and a 56-yard field goal. Pittsburgh answered again, with a long pass interference penalty setting up Rodgers to Freiermuth from a yard out, 14-6. Then the Colts converted on fourth down and Tyler Warren ran it in, 14-13. Before halftime, Boswell made it 17-13.

In the second half, the Colts kicked another field goal, 17-16, and Jaylen Warren answered with the 50-yard touchdown run. Indianapolis kicked twice more, and early in the fourth quarter it was 24-22. And that’s when this team showed its character. Freiermuth’s touchdown, then Boswell’s 54-yarder, and the game was over. Every time the Colts got close, the Steelers had an answer. That’s what confidence looks like.

McCarthy’s message, part three: Jaylen Warren is “the man.”

And now we get to the headliner. The biggest name of this win, without question, was Jaylen Warren. When McCarthy was asked about him, he didn’t hold back: “He’s the man.” And then, “He’s hauling it right now for us.”

Let’s talk about what Warren actually did, because it was one of the best games by a Steelers running back in years. He finished with 188 total yards from scrimmage, a career high. He ran 15 times for 101 yards, and he caught six passes for 87 more. And he scored two touchdowns, both of them highlight-reel plays.

The first one came in the first quarter. The Steelers’ opening drive had gone backward, and the Colts had already kicked a field goal. Rodgers flipped a short pass to Warren in the right flat, and Warren did the rest. He broke away, DK Metcalf threw a block downfield, and 80 yards later, he was in the end zone. One play, and the momentum completely flipped.

The second one came in the third quarter. The Colts had cut the lead to 17-16 with a field goal, and the crowd at Acrisure Stadium was getting tense. Then Warren took a handoff, found a crease, and was gone, 50 yards for a touchdown to make it 24-16.

And here’s why that combination matters. An 80-yard touchdown catch and a 50-yard touchdown run in the same game. According to the Steelers, no NFL player had done that since Herschel Walker in 1986. That’s 40 years. Think about all the great running backs who’ve played since then. Not one of them did what Jaylen Warren did on Sunday.

Want to know how special he’s been all season? Coming into this game, Warren was averaging 5.6 yards per carry, which ranked behind only Kenneth Walker III, James Cook, and Bijan Robinson among NFL running backs. And with this performance, he already has two 100-yard rushing games in 2026, the same number he had in all of 2025. When he was asked if he’s ready to be the bell cow, his answer said it all: “Whenever my name’s called.” And Cam Heyward described him perfectly: “He finishes every play.”

McCarthy also called him “a three-down player,” saying he “can beat you inside zone, outside zone.” And that’s the point. Warren isn’t a change-of-pace back anymore. He’s carrying this offense. With Rico Dowdle out since Week 2, McCarthy admitted the workload has been “heavy,” but Warren keeps answering. This was his third straight game with at least 125 yards from scrimmage.

Rodgers had a great line about him, too: “He’s just such a slippery player. First guy rarely brings him down.” And Rodgers added that Warren is “one of the most liked players on the team, so guys want to block for him.” When the offensive line loves blocking for you and your quarterback is your biggest fan, you know you’ve earned it.

And Warren? He kept it simple: “We won. That’s all that matters to me.”

McCarthy’s message, part four: Rodgers stayed on time.

After a week where everyone wanted to talk about McCarthy and Rodgers yelling at each other, McCarthy used his postgame press conference to praise his quarterback. He said, “I thought Aaron did a great job of staying on time.” And he added, “I thought his pocket presence was excellent, his ability to move with his feet and make plays out of the pocket.”

That’s how you end a story. Not with a long explanation, not with a defensive answer, but with respect.

And Rodgers deserved it. He finished 22 of 31 for 235 yards and three touchdowns. He was 16 of 21 for 166 yards in the first half alone. And he made history, again. This was his 166th regular-season win as a starter, which moves him past Ben Roethlisberger into fifth place all-time. He passed Peyton Manning for third in NFL history in touchdown passes, postseason included, with 580. He tied Roethlisberger for the most touchdown passes of 80-plus yards ever, with nine, thanks to that pass to Warren. And he became the first Steelers quarterback to throw three touchdowns in three straight weeks in the same season.

A 42-year-old quarterback, a first-year head coach, and a supposed feud. And the result was one of the cleanest offensive performances of the year. Sometimes the loudest noise is the least important thing in the room.

McCarthy’s message, part five: “never perfect,” and that’s a good thing.

Here’s the part of McCarthy’s press conference that impressed me the most. Even after a 12-point win, he made it clear the performance was “never perfect.” He pointed to special teams and said, “Special teams, I think we’ve got to continue to work the fundamentals.” He added that it “was really the emphasis all week,” and that it needs to continue.

He’s right. The Colts had a 54-yard kickoff return by Anthony Gould in the first quarter that set up a field goal from the Steelers’ 43. There was an illegal formation penalty on a punt. Those are details that could cost you in a close game.

But that’s exactly why I’m positive about this. A coach who sees the flaws after a win is a coach who won’t let his team get comfortable. That’s the difference between a team that wins one game and a team that wins five in a row. McCarthy even gave credit to the opponent, saying, “I thought their offense did a nice job.” Classy, honest, and focused on what’s next.

Now let’s talk about the heroes McCarthy didn’t have time to mention.

Pat Freiermuth caught two touchdown passes. The first was a 1-yard score in the first quarter. The second was the dagger. After a Colts field goal made it 24-22 early in the fourth, Freiermuth caught a 17-yard touchdown on third-and-6 with 6:35 left. That’s the play that broke Indianapolis.

Germie Bernard, starting for the first time in his NFL career because Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve, caught six passes for 80 yards, both career highs. Rodgers said he was “really proud of Germ the way he stepped in,” and predicted he’ll be ready for a bigger role this week.

Chris Boswell hit a 48-yard field goal before halftime and then put the game away with a 54-yarder with 3:43 left, making it a two-score game.

And the defense, the unit that was supposed to be in trouble, was outstanding. Jonathan Taylor, one of the most dangerous running backs in football, was held to 45 yards on 15 carries. Alex Highsmith said the Steelers “stopped him” by getting “off blocks.” Jalen Ramsey had nine tackles. Asante Samuel Jr. broke up four passes. Cam Heyward got the only sack. And the Colts were held to 1-for-3 in the red zone, forced to kick five field goals.

Heyward said something after the game that captures this team’s attitude perfectly: “We hate losing.” After Cleveland, you could feel that on every snap.

And let me single out Payton Wilson for a second, because his game deserves more attention than it’s going to get. Ten tackles, two for a loss, and a pass breakup on fourth-and-17, one of those plays where the whole stadium exhales at once. Wilson was flying sideline to sideline all afternoon, filling gaps against Taylor, and dropping into coverage against a Colts offense that loves to throw to its tight end. Linebackers who can do both are rare, and Wilson is playing like one of the best young ones in the league right now. When people talk about the future of this defense after the veterans, his name belongs at the top of that list.

And one more piece of history. The Colts still haven’t won in Pittsburgh since November 9th, 2008. Pittsburgh now leads the all-time series 35-11. Some things never change.

So why does this win matter so much for McCarthy?

Because first seasons are hard. Every new coach goes through a moment when people start asking whether the hire was right. For McCarthy, that moment came after Cleveland. And how a coach handles that moment tells you everything. Some coaches change the play-caller. Some coaches start pointing fingers at players. McCarthy did something different. He looked at himself and his process, changed one day of the week, and trusted his players to respond. They responded with their best game of the season.

That builds trust in a locker room. Players notice when a coach takes responsibility instead of blaming them. They notice when a coach defends his quarterback in public after a heated moment. And they notice when the plan works. After this win, the Steelers have something they didn’t have a week ago: proof that this staff can fix things in the middle of a season. You can’t put a price on that.

Now, the one worry, and what Omar Khan can do about it.

I promised this video would be positive, and it is. But I’m always honest with you. The win came at a cost at wide receiver. Ben Skowronek injured his calf covering a punt, and Roman Wilson got hurt late in the first half. Neither one returned. By the end, the Steelers had only four healthy receivers: DK Metcalf, Germie Bernard, Brandon Smith, and Kaden Wetjen. T.J. Watt also took a poke to the eye before halftime, but he came back for the second half wearing a visor. We’ll know more when the first injury report comes out on Wednesday.

So here’s my trade block segment, and it’s opinion, not a report. Nobody has reported that the Steelers are working on a deal. But with Pittman on injured reserve and two more receivers banged up, the need is obvious. The Marvin Harrison Jr. trade is done: Pro Football Talk reported that Houston sent Arizona a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder, getting Harrison and a 2027 seventh back. So he’s off the board.

That leaves two paths for Khan. Path one: a veteran who can play right away, like Calvin Ridley in Tennessee, who’s barely been targeted this season and should come cheap. Path two: the big swing, Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, a 24-year-old with a 1,282-yard rookie season who’s been stuck as the Jaguars’ third receiver. Jacksonville hasn’t wanted to move him, but Pittsburgh still holds the 2028 second-round pick from Dallas from the Joey Porter Jr. deal. If I’m Khan, I’m making the veteran call this week and keeping the Thomas phone line warm until the November 10th deadline.

And the good news on the injury front: McCarthy said, “I look for Rico to practice this week and hopefully be up for the game. He was close.” Getting Dowdle back would give Warren a partner and give this offense another weapon right when it needs one.

What comes next: Tampa Bay.

Next Sunday, October 18th, the Steelers go to Raymond James Stadium to face the Buccaneers. And remember what we said about road games? This team has lost five straight on the road. That’s the next wall to knock down. Win in Tampa, and this team goes into the stretch before the bye at 4-2 with real momentum. And there’s a storyline, too: Jamel Dean, who missed the Colts game with his ankle injury, has been pointing to the Tampa game for his return, and Tampa Bay is where his NFL career began.

McCarthy said it before this game: “You are what you put on film and you are what your record says you are.” Well, on Sunday, the film showed a confident quarterback, a history-making running back, a rookie receiver ready for the moment, and a defense that refused to break. And the record says 3-2.

That’s a team on the rise, Steelers Nation.
