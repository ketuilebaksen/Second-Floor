Steelers Nation, it’s deadline day, and before we go anywhere I need to clear up the headline floating around this morning. As of this recording, the Steelers have not signed two stars. They haven’t signed one. They haven’t traded for anyone either. The deadline closes today, Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern, and right now Pittsburgh’s roster looks exactly the way it did when the team walked off the field after beating Cleveland.

So where does the “two stars” talk come from? It comes from two veteran names that have popped up in the Steelers’ orbit over the last forty-eight hours, both former difference-makers whose careers have taken a turn. One is a receiver whose availability just got a lot more complicated. The other is a safety who used to be one of the best ball hawks in the league and might be available for very little. And sitting on top of all of it is the biggest game of the season so far: Sunday in Cincinnati, a showdown for first place that the guy who coached this team for nearly two decades is now calling from a television studio.

So here’s the plan for today. We’ll go through the receiver situation honestly, including why one name may have just dropped off the board. We’ll look at the safety idea and whether it makes sense. We’ll get into the Bengals, who just won again and are now tied with Pittsburgh. And then I’ll give you my final deadline board, labeled as opinion, not a report.

WHERE THE RECEIVER HUNT STANDS THIS MORNING

Let’s start with the big picture.

The Steelers are six and two. Two of their receivers are on injured reserve, with Michael Pittman Jr. dealing with a foot injury and Roman Wilson also sidelined. That’s left DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard and the tight ends carrying most of the load in the passing game.

The two names connected to Pittsburgh most often have been Josh Downs of the Colts and Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jaguars. Both have their appeal. Both come with a price.

On Downs, the latest reporting from Adam Schefter, as summarized by Heavy, is that the Colts are expected to trade him for a fourth-round pick before the deadline, and that “Teams looking for a receiver are interested.” Pittsburgh has been named among those teams since late October, along with Kansas City and Washington. The complication is that the Steelers’ own 2027 fourth-round pick already belongs to Dallas from the Broderick Jones trade, so matching that price would take a 2028 pick or a creative swap.

On Thomas, the price has been the issue. He’s a more explosive talent, but Jacksonville has given no indication it’s ready to give him away, and Day 2 capital is a lot for a team that will be thinking about its quarterback future next spring.

That’s why cheaper veteran options entered the conversation. And one of them was Calvin Ridley.

THE RIDLEY SITUATION

Here’s what we know, and I’m going to keep this as straightforward as possible.

Ridley, the Titans receiver, was involved in an incident at Nashville International Airport over the weekend, according to multiple reports, including ESPN’s Cameron Wolfe, as cited in the transcript, and Nashville station WKRN. Very few details have been confirmed publicly. On Monday, Titans head coach Robert Saleh called it “a very minor miscommunication,” and then added: “Unfortunately, law enforcement has it, and that’s about as far as I can go with commenting.”

That’s all I’m going to say about the incident itself. I’m not going to speculate on what happened, and you shouldn’t either. The details aren’t established.

What I can talk about is the football side. For a team considering Ridley as a deadline target, an unresolved off-field matter makes a quick trade much harder. Front offices want certainty when they’re adding a player midseason. With the deadline just hours away, there’s simply not enough time to get clarity.

And even before this weekend, there were real football questions. Ridley is in his thirties. His role in Tennessee has shrunk dramatically. He has six catches for ninety-one yards this season. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler described him as “a backup plan for a few teams in the receiver market,” and said “the Titans would need a quality Day 3 pick to part with Ridley.” That’s a meaningful price for a player who hasn’t been productive this year and whose remaining contract money adds another layer of complication.

Now, let’s be fair to his career, because the transcript makes a good point here. Ridley was a first-round pick by Atlanta in 2018 and became one of the league’s better receivers early in his career, including a second-team All-Pro selection in 2020. After missing the 2022 season while suspended for betting on games, he came back with Jacksonville in 2023 and topped a thousand yards with eight touchdowns. One small correction: the transcript says seven touchdowns that season. The number I’ve seen is eight. He then signed a four-year deal with Tennessee.

So the talent has been real. But between the production this season, the price, the contract and now the uncertainty, my take is simple: Pittsburgh should not force this. The deadline isn’t a reason to chase a name.

THE SAFETY IDEA: ANDRE CISCO

Now let’s flip to the other side of the ball, because this one is genuinely interesting.

According to the transcript, Mike DeFabo of The Athletic mentioned New York Jets safety Andre Cisco as a potential low-cost option for the Steelers. I wasn’t able to find DeFabo’s original piece, so I’m passing that along as reported.

Here’s who Cisco is. In his years with the Jacksonville Jaguars, he was a ball-hawking deep safety. The transcript credits him with eight interceptions from 2022 through 2024. ABC7 New York reported earlier this year that, when lined up as a high safety, Cisco is one of only six players in the league with at least eight interceptions and a defensive touchdown over the span they measured. So the playmaking reputation is legitimate.

His time in New York has been rockier. He signed with the Jets for 2025 and was their opening-day starter, but he played only eight games before tearing a pectoral muscle and needing season-ending surgery. He had no interceptions that year. In March, the Jets brought him back on a one-year deal worth two and a half million dollars, with incentives that could push it to five and a quarter million.

This season, according to the transcript, he was benched after Week 3, and he has twenty-three tackles, a sack and a forced fumble. I couldn’t confirm the benching or those exact numbers, so let’s treat them as reported. But if the benching is accurate, that’s exactly why he might be available for almost nothing.

DOES CISCO MAKE SENSE FOR PITTSBURGH?

Let’s think about it honestly.

The case for: he’s cheap, he’s on a one-year deal, and he has a track record of taking the ball away. A team trying to win now could use another veteran body in the secondary for the stretch run, especially one who’s played the deep-safety role at a high level. And there’s no long-term commitment. If it doesn’t work, he walks in March.

The case against: there’s a reason the Jets moved on from him as a starter. A benching after three weeks is a red flag, and Pittsburgh would need to understand exactly what happened. Was it scheme fit? Health? Performance? You’d want answers before you give up even a late-round pick.

And there’s a roster question. The Steelers’ secondary has Jalen Ramsey, Jaquan Brisker and DeShon Elliott, who came off injured reserve in late October. Joe Whitt Jr. is on staff, and he knows defensive backs as well as anyone. Is Cisco a meaningful upgrade, or just depth? For me, it’s depth. But depth matters in November and December, when injuries pile up.

THE BIGGER PICTURE: DEPTH OVER SPLASH

This fits a theme that has come up over and over during this deadline.

Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette has said, according to the transcript, that the Steelers are more interested in adding depth than in overspending for an expensive impact player. He’s also pointed out that Josh Downs, at five-foot-nine and 171 pounds, isn’t the big-bodied receiver McCarthy has historically preferred. “The Steelers coach prefers big receivers who are built to play in the cold-weather climates in the northeast,” Dulac wrote, according to Steeler Nation.

Put those together and you get a picture of a front office that isn’t desperate. This team is six and two. It doesn’t need a rebuild. It needs smart additions that hold up in a playoff push. That might be a receiver. It might be a safety. It might be nothing at all.

THE REAL HEADLINE: SUNDAY NIGHT IN CINCINNATI

Now let’s get to what might be the biggest story of the week, because whatever happens at 4 p.m. today, the game on Sunday is going to define the AFC North race.

The Steelers visit the Bengals on Sunday, November 15. The transcript bills it as Sunday Night Football on NBC, and the listings I’ve seen point to a prime-time kickoff. And here’s the update that changes the stakes: Cincinnati won again this past weekend.

On Sunday, in Madrid, the Bengals beat the Atlanta Falcons 34-17. One correction to the transcript: it says 34-7. The final was 34-17. Joe Burrow was outstanding, completing 24 of 29 passes, which is 82.8 percent, for 271 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions, and he added a one-yard rushing touchdown. Ja’Marr Chase had ten catches for 155 yards and a touchdown. Tee Higgins caught two touchdowns.

That win moved Cincinnati to six and two, with four straight victories. So Sunday’s game is now six and two against six and two, both teams riding four-game winning streaks, with first place in the division on the line.

TOMLIN’S TAKE FROM THE STUDIO

And here’s a storyline nobody could have imagined a year ago. Mike Tomlin, who coached the Steelers for nearly two decades, is now part of NBC’s Football Night in America studio team.

According to the transcript, Tomlin called this game a blockbuster and said, “It’s AFC North football,” adding that he’d love to be “air mailed” right to Cincinnati. I couldn’t locate a transcript of that segment, so treat the quotes as reported. But it would be very on-brand for Tomlin. Nobody understands the intensity of this rivalry better than he does.

The transcript also says Tomlin highlighted the matchup between Joe Burrow and the Steelers’ front, especially T.J. Watt and the four-man pass rush. That’s exactly the right place to start.

WHAT HAPPENED THE FIRST TIME

Before we get to the keys, let’s remember how the first meeting went, because it tells you a lot about this matchup.

Back on September 27, at home, the Steelers beat the Bengals 30-27. It was a shootout. Aaron Rodgers threw for 292 yards and three touchdowns. Burrow made big plays to Chase and Higgins. And the game came down to the final few minutes.

Chris Boswell kicked a 33-yard field goal with 2:18 left to give Pittsburgh the lead. Then Cincinnati drove into Steelers territory in the final minute, and Derrick Harmon sacked Burrow and knocked the ball loose. Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered it. Game over. The key play that set up the winning kick was a 47-yard catch-and-run by tight end Darnell Washington.

After the game, Burrow said: “We’ve got to play more sound football than we did today to beat a team like that.” And Bengals coach Zac Taylor was blunt: “Some games, you just go get your butt kicked and there’s not much to say about it.”

Here’s the part that matters for Sunday. Both Harmon and Joseph-Day, the two players who made the game-sealing play, are now on injured reserve. That’s a reminder of how much this defensive line has been tested since September.

WHY THIS BENGALS OFFENSE IS SO DANGEROUS RIGHT NOW

Let’s take a closer look at what Pittsburgh is walking into, because the Cincinnati offense is playing at a very high level.

A week earlier, on November 1, the Bengals rallied past Tennessee in overtime, and Burrow threw five touchdown passes in that game, according to the Associated Press roundup. He was involved in six touchdowns overall and threw for more than four hundred seventy yards. Then he followed it up with that near-perfect afternoon in Madrid.

That’s two straight weeks of elite quarterback play, and he’s doing it with two receivers who can each take over a game. Chase is the kind of player who can score from anywhere on the field, and his touchdown against Atlanta covered forty-seven yards. Higgins is the red-zone weapon, with two touchdown catches against the Falcons.

And remember what happened in Week 3. That 30-27 loss to Pittsburgh was Cincinnati’s first loss of the season. They haven’t forgotten it. Burrow said afterward that his team had to play more sound football to beat a team like the Steelers. Since then, they’ve cleaned a lot of things up. This is not the same Bengals team the Steelers saw in September.

THE STEELERS DEFENSE HAS CHANGED TOO

But here’s the good news. The Steelers aren’t the same team either.

Since defensive coordinator Patrick Graham moved from the coaches’ booth to the sideline before the Week 5 game against Indianapolis, Pittsburgh hasn’t lost. Four straight wins. The defense held Jonathan Taylor to forty-five yards on fifteen carries in that first game. It held Cleveland to thirty-four rushing yards and piled up eight sacks in the most recent one.

McCarthy explained that move simply: “It needed to be different.” And Graham described the mindset this way: “You have your principles and things of that nature, but you’ve got to be able to be pliable and adjust.”

That ability to adjust in real time is going to matter against Burrow. He’s the kind of quarterback who finds a weakness and attacks it until you fix it. Having the coordinator on the sideline, able to talk face to face with Watt, Queen and Ramsey between series, could be the difference between giving up one long drive and giving up three.

THE INJURY PICTURE HEADING INTO SUNDAY

One more piece of the puzzle: who’s actually available.

On the receiver side, Pittman remains on injured reserve with a foot injury. According to the Steelers’ bye week blog, the bye week doesn’t count toward his required absence, so don’t expect him back for Cincinnati. Ben Skowronek has been practicing and could be in the mix.

On the offensive line, rookie guard Gennings Dunker went on injured reserve on September 26 with an MCL sprain that carried a four-to-six-week timeline. He’s well past that window now, so his return is worth watching.

On the defensive line, Derrick Harmon is on injured reserve, and his return would be a big boost against a Cincinnati offensive line that has to keep Burrow clean. Sebastian Joseph-Day, who went on injured reserve on October 22 with a triceps injury, has an uncertain timeline, and there’s been no official update from the team.

Rookie Gabriel Rubio and undrafted rookie Kevin Jobity Jr. have filled in well. Jobity’s first career sack came against Cleveland. But a healthy Harmon would give this front a lot more punch against a quarterback who can extend plays.

THE KEYS TO SUNDAY NIGHT

So how does Pittsburgh win in Cincinnati? Let me lay it out.

Key one: pressure with four. This is the Tomlin point, and it’s the right one. If the Steelers can get to Burrow with Watt, Alex Highsmith, Nick Herbig and Cam Heyward without sending extra rushers, they can keep seven defenders in coverage against Chase and Higgins. That’s how you slow down an offense this good. The Steelers had eight sacks against Cleveland. They need to bring that same energy.

Key two: don’t give Burrow free plays. No offside penalties. No roughing calls. Against Cleveland, Brisker’s roughing penalty on the opening drive handed the Browns a fresh set of downs. Against Burrow, that kind of mistake turns into seven points.

Key three: run the ball and control the clock. Jaylen Warren is second in the NFL with 998 scrimmage yards. Rico Dowdle has been a strong complement. The best way to slow down Burrow is to keep him on the sideline. Long drives. Short third downs. Win the time-of-possession battle.

Key four: finish drives. Against Cleveland, the Steelers settled for field goals on two red-zone trips in the first half. Against Cincinnati, that’s not going to be enough. Use the tight ends. Hand it off on first-and-goal. Turn those trips into touchdowns.

Key five: protect the football. Rodgers threw an interception against the Browns on a deep ball that went through Metcalf’s hands. In a game between two evenly matched teams, one turnover can decide everything.

WHAT A DEADLINE MOVE WOULD MEAN FOR SUNDAY

Let’s connect the deadline to the game.

If Pittsburgh adds a receiver like Downs today, he’d get a handful of practices before Sunday. Realistically, that means a small package of plays. A few slot routes. A couple of third-down concepts. Not a full role. But even ten or fifteen snaps from a reliable target could matter in a close game.

If Pittsburgh adds a safety like Cisco, the impact would be even smaller in the short term. He’d be a depth piece, a player who’s ready if someone gets hurt.

And if Pittsburgh does nothing, the plan is the one that’s already worked: lean on Warren, trust the defense, and let Rodgers find Bernard, Metcalf and the tight ends.

All three paths are defensible. The worst path would be overpaying for a player who doesn’t fit, just because the clock is running out.

WHAT THIS DEADLINE SAYS ABOUT OMAR KHAN

Before the board, one last thought on the front office.

Whatever Pittsburgh does today, the approach has been consistent. Khan has explored the market, according to Mark Kaboly, who wrote that every receiver option “has been explored by Khan; you can be assured of that.” But the Steelers haven’t panicked. They haven’t chased the most expensive names. They haven’t sold off any of the players who got them to six and two.

That’s the right posture for a first-place team with a quarterback decision coming after the season. Buy if the price is right. Walk away if it isn’t. And never let the clock make the decision for you.

MY FINAL DEADLINE BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my last board before the deadline, and remember: this is opinion, not a report.

Josh Downs: yes for a 2028 fourth-round pick, or a fifth if it’s somehow available. If the Colts demand a third with nothing coming back, I walk.

Brian Thomas Jr.: pass at a Day 2 price.

Calvin Ridley: pass. Between the production, the cost and the uncertainty, it’s not the right move today.

Andre Cisco: yes, but only for a seventh-round pick or a late-round swap, and only after the Steelers understand why he was benched. Low risk, possible depth.

The core: untouchable. Watt, Highsmith, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier. You don’t touch a first-place team’s foundation hours before a first-place showdown.

Whatever happens by 4 p.m., the real test comes Sunday. Six and two against six and two. Four-game winning streaks on both sides. First place in the AFC North on the line. And a former Steelers head coach watching from the NBC studio.
