Steelers Nation, it’s game day. Browns at Acrisure Stadium, 1 p.m. Eastern, first place in the AFC North on the line. But before kickoff, there’s one name hovering over this entire week, and it isn’t on either roster for today’s game.

Josh Downs.

Some of the videos out there are calling this a “bombshell decision.” So let me tell you exactly what the decision was. It wasn’t the Steelers’. It was Downs’. Earlier this week, the Colts receiver asked Indianapolis to trade him. That’s it. That’s the bombshell. No trade has happened, nothing has been agreed, and Downs is expected to suit up for the Colts in Jacksonville today.

But that one decision has turned him into one of the most important names for Pittsburgh’s season, because it opened a door the Steelers had been knocking on all week. And on Saturday, the Steelers made their own receiver decision for today, which tells you a lot about why Downs matters.

I’m James, this is Second Floor, and I’m going to walk you through the Downs story in five questions, plus the roster moves Pittsburgh made on Saturday. Let’s get to it.

QUESTION ONE: WHAT EXACTLY DID DOWNS DO, AND WHAT DID HE SAY?

On Wednesday, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that Downs requested a trade. It came right after the Colts traded away two franchise pillars on Tuesday. Quenton Nelson went to Seattle, and DeForest Buckner went back to San Francisco. Indianapolis is 2-5, and the front office already fired general manager Chris Ballard on October 19.

So Downs looked around and saw a rebuild. And he’s in the last year of his rookie contract. The math wasn’t hard.

What’s interesting is how calm he’s been about it. Here’s what he told reporters: “I’m open to whatever.” And then: “Whatever comes my way, comes my way, and we’ll go from there.”

He didn’t trash the Colts. He didn’t name a dream destination. He actually stressed staying professional: “My feet are here right now, so I’ve got to be right here.” And: “I’m in the game plan, so I’ve got to be ready to go.”

Colts head coach Shane Steichen backed that up on Friday. “He’s a pro. He practiced this week. He’s ready to go. We’re expecting for him to be his best on Sunday.”

That’s how you request a trade the right way. No drama. No holdout. Just honesty about where things are heading.

QUESTION TWO: WHO IS JOSH DOWNS, REALLY?

For a lot of Steelers fans, Downs is a name they’ve only heard this week. So here’s the scouting report.

He’s 25 years old, a 2023 third-round pick out of North Carolina. His best season came in 2024, when he caught 72 passes for 803 yards and five touchdowns. Across his four seasons in Indianapolis, ESPN’s Matt Bowen counted 224 receptions and 11 touchdowns.

This year, the numbers are 26 catches for 307 yards in seven games, about 11.8 yards per catch, with no touchdowns. That’s modest. But context matters. The Colts’ offense has struggled, and Alec Pierce has been sidelined for an extended stretch, which let defenses focus more attention on Downs. And even then, he leads the team in receiving yards.

Bowen summed up his game nicely: “Downs has the sudden footwork and coverage recognition to uncover between the numbers.” Translation: he gets open fast in the middle of the field. That’s a slot receiver’s best skill, and it’s the exact skill Pittsburgh doesn’t have enough of right now.

QUESTION THREE: WHO’S IN THE RACE?

This is where it gets competitive.

Pittsburgh and Kansas City are the two teams with reported offers. Evan Sidery wrote on Thursday that the Chiefs and Steelers “have both offered Day 3 draft compensation to the Colts for Josh Downs.” ESPN’s trade candidate list named those same two teams as Downs’ best fits. Fowler even added that Downs “would probably welcome the chance to play with someone like Patrick Mahomes.” That’s a line Steelers fans won’t love, but it’s honest.

Other teams have been floated as possible landing spots, though without reported offers. Athlon pointed out that New England makes sense because Downs played with Drake Maye at North Carolina in 2022. And some outlets have brought up the Rams, with Davante Adams expected to miss six to ten weeks with a hamstring injury, though one write-up noted that Sean McVay initially indicated the Rams weren’t exploring outside receiver additions. Detroit’s name has come up in speculation too, but I haven’t seen any reporting tying the Lions to an actual offer.

So the real race, based on what’s been reported, is Pittsburgh versus Kansas City. Everything else is speculation.

QUESTION FOUR: WHAT WILL IT COST?

Here’s the negotiation in three steps.

Step one: the asking price. Adam Schefter said on his podcast that the Colts “have told teams they’re looking for third-round value.” And then, “Nobody’s willing to do that right now.”

Step two: the offers. Sidery reported both the Steelers and Chiefs offered Day 3 picks, meaning rounds four through seven.

Step three: where it’s headed. Sidery added that “a Downs deal is expected to go down after this weekend” and that Indianapolis “will likely fetch a 4th or 5th-round pick.” Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox projected a 2027 fifth-rounder. SI’s Jacob Punturi wrote that a fourth could be acceptable for Pittsburgh but that Omar Khan reportedly won’t give up a third.

And Steelers insider Mark Kaboly, without naming a player, said “early to middle of next week will be the sweet spot for a move to get done,” and told fans to “keep your alerts on early next week.”

Put it all together and you get a pretty clear window. After today’s games, early next week, at a fourth or fifth-round price. Nothing is guaranteed. Kansas City could jump the bid. The Colts could hold out. But the pieces are lining up.

QUESTION FIVE: HOW WOULD HE FIT IN PITTSBURGH?

This is the part I’m most excited about.

The Steelers’ offense has found its identity with the run game. Jaylen Warren is, in Mike McCarthy’s words, “hot as a firecracker,” and Rico Dowdle has been a strong second punch. But every run-heavy team eventually faces third-and-six, where the defense knows a pass is coming. That’s where a quick slot receiver earns his money.

Right now, Rodgers’ main targets are DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard on the outside, with Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington at tight end. What’s missing is a true slot guy who wins in two seconds against man coverage. Cleveland played man on 73.3 percent of its snaps in the first meeting, according to Steeler Nation, and plenty of playoff teams will do the same.

There’s also a Pittman connection. Michael Pittman Jr. came to Pittsburgh from Indianapolis in March, and he and Downs were teammates with the Colts. Pittman is on injured reserve with a foot injury and can’t return before November 15, which happens to be the Sunday night game in Cincinnati right after the bye. Picture that receiver room in mid-November: Metcalf, Bernard, Pittman, and Downs. That’s a lot deeper than what Rodgers has had for most of this season.

And the contract makes it simple. Downs is on an expiring deal. He’d be a rental for the rest of 2026. With Rodgers calling this his last season, that’s exactly the kind of move you make.

HOW I’D USE HIM: THREE PLAYS FOR DOWNS IN THIS OFFENSE

Let me get specific about the fit, because “slot receiver” can sound vague. This part is my opinion on how McCarthy could use him.

Play one: third-and-medium against man coverage. Downs lines up inside, runs a quick option route, and breaks away from his defender based on leverage. Rodgers knows where he’ll be before the snap. Ball out in two seconds, first down, drive continues. That’s the play this offense is missing most.

Play two: the play-action crosser. Pittsburgh runs heavy personnel and makes linebackers bite on Warren. A slot receiver who can sprint across the field behind those linebackers is a nightmare. Downs averaged double-digit yards per catch even in a struggling offense. Put him behind a defense that’s terrified of the run, and that number goes up.

Play three: the red zone. The Steelers rank 27th in red-zone offense, per ClutchPoints, and McCarthy admitted, “we could definitely score more touchdowns.” In tight spaces, quickness beats size. A receiver who can win on a slant or a pivot route at the goal line gives Rodgers an answer when the field shrinks.

None of that requires Downs to be a star. It just requires him to do what he already does well.

THE TIMELINE IF A DEAL HAPPENS

Let’s walk through what an actual trade would look like on the calendar, based on the reporting.

Today, Downs plays for the Colts in Jacksonville and the Steelers host Cleveland. Monday through Wednesday is the window Kaboly flagged and the “after this weekend” timing Sidery reported. That’s when a deal would most likely be agreed.

Then come the formalities. A traded player has to pass a physical with his new team. Pittsburgh would also need room on the 53-man roster. The team announced no corresponding move when it signed Brandon Smith on Saturday, which suggests he filled the open spot the Steelers had, so a trade would likely require a roster move of its own. That’s normal, but it’s a detail people forget.

After that, the bye week. Downs would have roughly two weeks of meetings and practices before the next game. The deadline itself is Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern, but if Kaboly’s read is right, Pittsburgh won’t wait that long.

And then the debut: Sunday night, November 15, in Cincinnati. Which, again, is the first day Pittman is eligible to come back from injured reserve. Two former Colts teammates could take the field together in prime time against a division rival. You couldn’t script it better.

WHAT ABOUT THE OTHER NAMES?

Downs isn’t the only receiver on the board, so let’s quickly cover the alternatives.

Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville was ranked second on ESPN’s top 25 trade candidate list, with Pittsburgh named as a fit. He had a Pro Bowl rookie year with 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. But Kaboly said this week the Steelers “were” interested and he hasn’t “heard anything recently about it.” And SI’s Punturi noted Thomas would likely cost a Day 2 pick. Higher upside, higher price, colder trail.

ESPN also named Pittsburgh as a fit for Chargers receiver Quentin Johnston, ranked 20th on its list. And on the defensive side, the same list tied the Steelers to Eagles cornerback Riq Woolen and Titans cornerback Alontae Taylor. But a Steelers Depot headline this week quoted Kaboly saying, “It’s WR or nothing.” I couldn’t open the full piece, so I’m only going on the headline, but that suggests the receiver hunt is the priority.

Of all those names, only Downs has a trade request, a reported Steelers offer, and a price that fits a Day 3 budget. That’s why he’s at the top.

WHAT TO WATCH FROM THE RECEIVERS TODAY

Since this game is the final audition before the window opens, here’s what I’ll be tracking from Pittsburgh’s receivers this afternoon.

Metcalf’s second half. In the first Browns game, he caught five passes for 115 yards, and 93 of those came after halftime. Cleveland will have adjusted. Can he still win late?

Bernard against man coverage. The rookie has earned Rodgers’ trust, including the overtime touchdown in Tampa. But this is a division opponent that loves press-man. A big day from Bernard makes the room look deeper than people think.

Smith’s snaps. He just got a roster spot. If he plays a big role again and catches everything thrown his way, that’s an internal option Khan can lean on.

And Rodgers’ third downs. If he’s constantly searching for an open receiver on third-and-medium, that’s the clearest possible signal that a slot receiver is needed. If those throws are easy, maybe the urgency drops a little.

THE RISKS, IN FAIRNESS

I don’t want to oversell this, so here are the honest risks.

First, Downs is playing today. Any injury in Jacksonville could change or kill a deal.

Second, the Colts might not sell. Stephen Holder reported that it’s unclear whether Indianapolis is willing to trade him at all, even after the request.

Third, Kansas City is a real competitor, with a quarterback Fowler says Downs might welcome playing with.

And fourth, even if he comes, he’s a rental. He might be a great piece for ten games and a playoff run, then leave in March. That’s okay. But it’s worth knowing going in.

SATURDAY’S MOVES: WHAT THE STEELERS DID FOR TODAY

Now, back to today. Pittsburgh made two roster moves on Saturday afternoon.

First, Brandon Smith was signed from the practice squad to the 53-man roster. Smith had already used all three of his elevations, so this was the only way to get him on the field today. He’s earned it. In Paris, he played 29 snaps and caught both of his targets for 24 yards. McCarthy said weeks ago that Smith had “definitely earned this opportunity,” and now he has a real roster spot.

Second, Brandon Johnson was elevated from the practice squad for today’s game. According to Steelers Now, it’s his third and final elevation of the season. He played three offensive snaps in Tampa and eight in Paris without a target.

And here’s the notable omission: Marquez Valdes-Scantling was not elevated. Rodgers had said this week, “It would be nice to get him up,” but the staff went with Johnson instead. Steelers Now also called Johnson’s elevation “a bad sign” for Ben Skowronek, who’s questionable with his calf after three limited practices.

So the receiver group for today looks like Metcalf, Bernard, Smith, Johnson, Kaden Wetjen, and possibly Skowronek. That’s the room. And that’s exactly why Downs keeps coming up.

THE OTHER SIDE OF TODAY: THE SECONDARY

One more thing to watch today. Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both dealing with hamstrings, are out. The starters are healthy: Asante Samuel Jr. and Jamel Dean outside, Jalen Ramsey at nickel after a full practice on Friday, and Jaquan Brisker at safety. But depth behind them is thin, with Sean Murphy-Bunting, Daylen Everette, and Terell Smith next in line.

On Cleveland’s side, offensive tackle Tytus Howard was questionable in the concussion protocol. If he can’t play, Dawand Jones is expected to start at right tackle, likely across from T.J. Watt. Watt’s message on Friday was clear: limit “explosives” and keep stopping the run.

WHY TODAY STILL MATTERS FOR THE DOWNS DECISION

Here’s the connection between today’s game and Downs’ future.

If the Steelers beat Cleveland, they go into the bye at 6-2, alone in first. At that point, a fourth or fifth-round pick for a rental receiver looks like a smart investment in a division title and a playoff run.

If they lose, they’re 5-3, swept by the Browns, and chasing in the division. Khan might still make the move, but the urgency and the logic shift a bit. Either way, this afternoon is the last piece of evidence the front office gets before that early-next-week window opens.

And remember, the Colts play Jacksonville today too. Downs is expected to play his normal role. Any injury to him would change everything, so Steelers fans have a reason to keep an eye on that game as well.

THE COLTS’ SIDE: WHY THEY’LL LIKELY DEAL

Let me give the Colts’ perspective for a second. Why not just keep Downs and try to extend him?

Because the evidence says they’re tearing it down. They fired Ballard. They traded Nelson and Buckner in the same afternoon. Fowler reported they’re open for business, and ESPN put Downs’ trade chances at 45 percent. Downs himself asked out. And he’s on an expiring deal, so if the Colts keep him, they risk losing him for nothing in March.

ESPN’s Stephen Holder did report that while teams have asked about Downs, it’s unclear whether the Colts are willing to trade him. So that’s the one note of caution. But between the rebuild, the request, and the contract, the most likely outcome is a deal.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Do it. If the price lands at a fourth or fifth, Khan should make the call early next week and give Downs the full bye to learn the offense. A quick slot receiver for a Day 3 pick, in Rodgers’ final season, with Pittman coming back after the bye, is a no-brainer.

Don’t overpay. If Kansas City pushes to a third, let them have him. The 2028 second-rounder Pittsburgh got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade stays untouched. And if the Steelers miss on Downs, they still have Pittman coming back, MVS waiting, and Skowronek close.

And one more thing: don’t overthink the long term. Downs can become a free agent in March. If he loves Pittsburgh and fits, great, have that conversation then. If not, you got a playoff-run rental for a late pick. That’s good business.
