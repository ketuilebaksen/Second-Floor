Steelers Nation, the dust has settled on a 27-6 demolition of Cleveland, and the updates have been pouring in ever since. A division race that flipped overnight. A quarterback who overruled a field goal. A receiver under fire. And a trade deadline one week away with two very different targets on the board and two very different opinions about whether Pittsburgh will pull the trigger at all.

Here’s the honest headline before we start. As of Tuesday morning, the Steelers have not made a trade. Josh Downs is still in Indianapolis. Brian Thomas Jr. is still in Jacksonville. Everything you hear about either one is interest, offers, and prices, not a done deal.

But there’s a lot to unpack. So today I’m hitting you with eight updates, in order of how much they matter, with a full Downs versus Thomas breakdown in the middle. I’m James, this is Second Floor. Let’s ride the wave.

UPDATE ONE: PITTSBURGH IS IN THE DRIVER’S SEAT

Start with the standings, because they changed more than the scoreboard did.

Pittsburgh is 6-2, alone in first place in the AFC North, after the win over Cleveland. The Browns dropped to 5-3. Both teams are 2-1 in the division, and the season series is split.

Then the rest of the division got wild. Cincinnati beat Tennessee in overtime to move to 5-2, according to Yahoo Sports, with Joe Burrow piling up 472 yards and six touchdowns from scrimmage but also throwing three interceptions in a game where the Bengals allowed 36 points.

And Baltimore took a gut punch. The Ravens fell to 4-4, and Lamar Jackson injured his throwing hand on the very first offensive snap against Buffalo. According to the report, he’s on injured reserve and having thumb surgery, expected to miss at least four weeks and possibly the rest of the season. Backup Tyler Huntley also hurt his toe.

So here’s the picture. Pittsburgh at 6-2. Cincinnati at 5-2. Cleveland at 5-3. Baltimore at 4-4 and without its MVP quarterback. And the Steelers’ first game after the bye is Sunday night, November 15, in Cincinnati. That’s first place against second place in prime time.

Rodgers summed up the mood: “We’re 6-2 going into the bye, first in the division. We’re right where we want to be.” And he already circled the Bengals game: “Either way, that game in two weeks is going to be really important.”

UPDATE TWO: RODGERS OVERRULED THE FIELD GOAL

The play everyone’s still talking about came right before halftime. Pittsburgh at the goal line, seconds left, no timeouts. A field goal would’ve made it 16-6.

Instead, the Steelers ran their “Nittany” short-yardage play with Pat Freiermuth under center, and he pushed in for the touchdown. And the call came from the quarterback. McCarthy confirmed it: “Frankly, actually Aaron called that play, the Nittany.” Rodgers’ explanation: “I wasn’t coming off the field.”

That’s a big deal for a team that’s struggled to finish drives. And it’s a sign of how much trust has grown between Rodgers and McCarthy since their heated sideline exchange in Cleveland back in Week 4.

UPDATE THREE: THE METCALF DROP AND THE COACH’S RESPONSE

Now the less fun update. DK Metcalf had one catch for four yards on six targets against Cleveland, and a wide-open deep ball went off his hands and into the arms of Browns cornerback Denzel Ward for an interception, according to Heavy.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio addressed it Monday, per PennLive. He said the interception “is one he’d want back,” but added, “We haven’t lost confidence in DK Metcalf whatsoever.”

The season numbers, though, are tough. Per Heavy, Metcalf has been under 50 yards in five of eight games, is averaging career lows of 11.5 yards per catch and 40.6 yards per game, and has one touchdown, which came back in Week 3. ESPN’s Alex Smith didn’t hold back on Sunday: “DK Metcalf, I’m sorry, he looks like a shell of himself.”

That’s the backdrop for everything that comes next. Pittsburgh’s receiver question isn’t just about injuries anymore. It’s about production.

THE DEFENSE THAT MAKES IT ALL POSSIBLE

Before we get to the trade market, a quick appreciation of the unit that’s carrying this team, because it’s the reason Pittsburgh can afford to be patient and picky.

Eight sacks against Cleveland, spread across seven players, according to Steelers.com: T.J. Watt with two, Patrick Queen and Cam Heyward with a sack and a half each, Nick Herbig with one, Kevin Jobity Jr. with the first of his career, and Keeanu Benton and Alex Highsmith splitting one. The Browns had minus-1 yard of offense in the first quarter and finished with 34 rushing yards.

Queen led the team with 12 tackles and forced a fumble, one week after ESPN listed him as a trade candidate. Watt now owns the record for most career sacks against the Browns. And according to Steelers.com’s Bob Labriola, this defense has allowed just one second-half touchdown all season.

Jon Gruden said it on Wake Up Barstool on Monday: “This defense is way better than advertised.” Heavy notes the Steelers rank sixth in the NFL in fewest yards allowed per game. And over the four-game win streak, they’ve given up just 14.3 points per game, per the Tribune-Review.

When your defense plays like that, you don’t need a blockbuster. You need a piece.

THE WARREN ENGINE

One more piece of context before the trade talk: Jaylen Warren.

He ran for 143 yards on 17 carries against Cleveland, tying his career high on his 28th birthday. Labriola noted Warren now has four 100-yard games since the offensive line changes in Week 3 and 848 yards from scrimmage this season. McCarthy said after the game, “Jaylen is on fire. You can’t get him the ball enough.” Rodgers added, “He’s put himself in all the conversations.”

That’s important for the trade decision. Pittsburgh’s offense runs through Warren and Rico Dowdle. Any receiver who comes in isn’t replacing the identity. He’s adding to it, especially on the passing downs when the run game can’t carry the load.

UPDATE FOUR: THE TWO TARGETS, AND WHAT THE REPORTS SAY

Here’s where the trade talk stands right now.

On Josh Downs: Pro Football Rumors reported Monday that a Colts deal before the deadline appears likely. Insider Jordan Schultz said on Ryan Clark’s The Pivot that Indianapolis wants a third-round pick, but he expects the price to end up at a fourth. Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk reported that the Steelers have greater interest in Downs than in Thomas. And last week, Evan Sidery reported that the Steelers and Chiefs both made Day 3 offers.

On Brian Thomas Jr.: Florio reported that the Chiefs, Steelers, and Commanders all have interest. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler said league executives believe Thomas’s time in Jacksonville is nearing its end. And The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones gave us a price: “BTJ caught two passes the entire month of October and would likely net Jacksonville a Day 2 pick if the team deals him.” A rival executive confirmed it, telling Jones, “Yes they would.”

So that’s the framework. Downs is a likely fourth-rounder. Thomas is a likely second or third. And the Steelers reportedly prefer Downs.

UPDATE FIVE: DOWNS VERSUS THOMAS, HEAD TO HEAD

Let’s put them side by side, because they’re very different players, and the right answer depends on what Pittsburgh actually needs.

Round one: the style. Downs is a separator. He wins quickly, mostly from the slot, and gets open between the numbers. One write-up cited Fantasy Points Data ranking him third in the NFL in average separation score. Thomas is a vertical, field-stretching outside receiver with the size and speed to take the top off a defense. Edge depends on need, and right now Pittsburgh’s need is a reliable target on third-and-medium. Slight edge to Downs.

Round two: the production. Thomas has the higher peak. As a rookie in 2024, he caught 87 passes for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. But his yards fell to 707 in 2025, and this season he has just 10 catches for 117 yards and two touchdowns in seven games, per Gridiron Heroics, with only two catches in all of October, according to Jones. Downs has been steadier: 72 catches for 803 yards in 2024, and this year 30 catches for 330 yards and a touchdown in eight games on a struggling Colts offense, per Gridiron Heroics. Ceiling goes to Thomas. Floor goes to Downs.

Round three: the price. Downs: likely a fourth. Thomas: likely a Day 2 pick, meaning a second or third. Clear edge to Downs.

Round four: the contract. Downs is in the final year of his rookie deal, a pure rental who can be a free agent in March. Thomas is under contract through 2027 and becomes a free agent in 2028, according to Heavy. That means Thomas gives you more than this season, but also more financial commitment and more to plan around. Edge depends on your timeline. With Rodgers retiring after this season, I lean Downs.

Round five: the timing. Downs could move this week. Sidery said a deal was expected “after this weekend,” and Mark Kaboly said “early to middle of next week will be the sweet spot.” Thomas, per Fowler and Tom Pelissero, is more likely to move closer to the November 10 deadline, since Jacksonville reportedly wants to keep him at least through its Week 9 game against Baltimore. Every day matters for a player learning a new offense during a bye. Clear edge to Downs.

Final score: Downs wins on fit, price, and timing. Thomas wins on upside. And for a 6-2 team with a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season, fit, price, and timing matter more.

THE SELLERS’ SIDE: WHY JACKSONVILLE IS A PUZZLE

Here’s something I find fascinating. The two teams Pittsburgh is shopping with are in totally different places.

The Colts are clearly sellers. They fired general manager Chris Ballard, traded Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner, and then lost 42-7 in Jacksonville on Sunday. They’re in a full reset, and Downs asked out. That’s a seller’s situation, which is why the price keeps sliding.

The Jaguars are the opposite. That 42-7 win over the Colts improved Jacksonville to 5-2, and the Jaguars face Baltimore on Thursday. They’re contending. So why would they trade a former Pro Bowl receiver? Because, as Fowler put it, there’s a feeling around the league that Thomas’s time there is ending, and his role has shrunk dramatically. But a contender isn’t going to give him away. That’s why the price is Day 2, and why the timing is later. Jacksonville wants to see if it can still use him first.

So if you’re Pittsburgh, you’re dealing with a motivated seller on Downs and a reluctant seller on Thomas. That alone tells you which deal is more likely to happen.

UPDATE SIX: DULAC SAYS DON’T HOLD YOUR BREATH

Now the counterweight. On Sunday morning, before the game, the Post-Gazette’s Gerry Dulac went on the 102.5 DVE pregame show and poured cold water on the trade talk.

“They’re not looking to make a move, and they’re certainly not looking to get rid of one of their players,” Dulac said. And he added, “If they go 6-2, they certainly won’t trade one of their players because they figure they’re a contender.”

Well, they went 6-2.

Here’s how I read that. Dulac’s strongest point is that Pittsburgh won’t sell anybody. That’s not surprising for a first-place team. On buying, he said they’re not actively looking, but he also acknowledged he can’t predict what happens if another team calls with an offer. So it’s not a hard “no.” It’s a “don’t expect a splash.”

UPDATE SEVEN: THE INSIDER TUG-OF-WAR

And that’s the strange part of this week. The insiders are pulling in completely different directions.

On one side, there’s Dulac saying the Steelers aren’t actively looking. On the other, there’s Jason La Canfora, who said in a video for Casino Guru News, “I’d keep an eye on the Pittsburgh Steelers, who are being pretty ambitious,” adding that they “would love to add another playmaker.” He even floated Jets receiver Garrett Wilson.

But ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported a source said there’s “no way” the Jets trade Wilson before the deadline, and La Canfora fired back on X calling out what he described as “corrupt access merchants.” Wilson also carries a four-year, $130 million contract, and ESPN’s Rich Cimini once reported his relationship with Rodgers was icy after Rodgers left New York. So the Wilson idea feels like the longest shot on the board.

Then there’s Kaboly, who before the game told fans to “keep your alerts on early next week.” And Florio, who says Pittsburgh prefers Downs.

My read: the Steelers don’t need to make a splash, and Dulac is probably right that they won’t. But a modest, Day 3 move for a rental receiver isn’t a splash. It’s a patch. And the reporting on Downs suggests that kind of patch is very much in play.

UPDATE EIGHT: THE BYE WEEK HEALTH AND HOMEWORK

Finally, the bye. McCarthy’s goal is clear: “I look to come out of this bye really healthy.”

On the injury front, Roman Wilson was in the rehab group at practice for the first time last week, according to Ray Fittipaldo, and could be eligible to return soon after the bye. Michael Pittman Jr.’s outlook is murkier. He’s on injured reserve with plantar fasciitis, and Kaboly said the team is “not ruling out, I guess, surgery at this point.” Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both out against Cleveland with hamstrings, get two weeks to heal, and Ben Skowronek gets more time for his calf.

And the homework? The red zone. Steeler Nation reported the coaching staff is putting a big emphasis on it during the bye. Angelichio put it simply: “We’ve got to see if we have the right plays down there.” According to that report, the Steelers have scored touchdowns on only about half of their red zone trips, and in the Browns game they reached the 2-yard line, threw twice, and settled for a field goal.

WHY THE CALENDAR MATTERS SO MUCH

Let’s talk about what a new receiver would be walking into after the bye, because it explains why getting someone in the building this week is so valuable.

According to Labriola, after the bye the Steelers play nine games, five on the road, against five teams that made the playoffs in 2025. It starts Sunday night in Cincinnati on November 15. Then it’s a trip to Philadelphia on November 22, Denver at home on Black Friday, Houston at home on Sunday night in Week 13, Monday night in Jacksonville in Week 14, and Baltimore at home in Week 15.

That’s a gauntlet. A receiver acquired on deadline day, Tuesday, November 10, would have four days of practice before a prime-time division game. A receiver acquired this week would have closer to two weeks. That’s why the bye is the window, and why Downs’ earlier timeline matters more than Thomas’ higher ceiling.

THE INTERNAL OPTION NOBODY SHOULD FORGET

If Khan does nothing, there’s still an internal card to play: Marquez Valdes-Scantling.

He’s been on the practice squad since October 20, and he hasn’t been elevated for a game yet. But Rodgers has made his feelings clear: “I love Marquez,” calling him “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with.” MVS is a vertical threat, a two-time Super Bowl champion, and he spent four seasons with Rodgers in Green Bay.

The bye gives him two weeks of practice to earn a spot. And Brandon Smith, signed to the active roster before the Browns game, has earned trust as well. Neither is a game-changer on his own. But combined with a healthy Skowronek and a returning Roman Wilson, the room could look a lot better by Thanksgiving even without a trade.

WHAT THE WAVE ADDS UP TO

Put all eight updates together, and here’s the story.

Pittsburgh is in first place with a dominant run game and a defense that just sacked Deshaun Watson eight times and held Cleveland to 34 rushing yards. Jaylen Warren is rolling after 143 yards on his birthday. Rodgers is making the right calls. The division got easier with Lamar Jackson’s injury, but harder with Cincinnati at 5-2.

The one weak spot is the passing game, specifically the receivers. Metcalf is struggling. Pittman’s future is uncertain. And the trade market offers two paths: a cheap, quick-separating rental in Downs, or a pricier, higher-ceiling deep threat in Thomas.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Make the Downs move this week. A fourth-round pick is fair, maybe with a late-round sweetener. He fits the offense, the timeline, and the budget. And getting him in the building during the bye gives him two weeks before Cincinnati.

Pass on Thomas at a Day 2 price. I like his talent. But two catches in October, a higher price, a contract that runs past this season, and a later timeline make him the wrong fit for this moment.

Pass on Garrett Wilson entirely. The contract, the cost, the Rodgers history, and Schefter’s “no way” make it unrealistic.

And whatever happens, don’t trade anybody away. On that, Dulac and I agree completely. You don’t sell when you’re 6-2 and in first place.
