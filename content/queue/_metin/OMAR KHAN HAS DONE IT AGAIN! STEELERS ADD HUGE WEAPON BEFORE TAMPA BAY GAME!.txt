Steelers Nation, the weapon is back, and Omar Khan isn’t done. On Thursday, Rico Dowdle, the two-time 1,000-yard running back Khan signed in March, was a full participant in practice for the first time since he dislocated his toe in Week 2. That means Pittsburgh is heading to Tampa Bay with the backfield it was built around the reigning AFC Offensive Player of the Week, Jaylen Warren, and a hammer who’s been waiting a month to swing. And while that was happening on the practice field, the front office was busy. Mark Kaboly reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the trade deadline. A familiar receiver’s name popped up in reports about a visit. And an NFL insider floated a rare trade inside the AFC North. So today, we’re going inside Khan’s week the weapon you’ll see on Sunday, the weapons he’s hunting, which rumors hold up and which don’t, and what all of it means for the trip to Tampa. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Part one the weapon you’ll see on Sunday.

Let’s start with the good news, because there’s a lot of it. On Wednesday, Dowdle was limited. On Thursday, he was full. Steelers Depot put it simply “Odds are looking strong he’ll play.”

And if you’ve forgotten what Dowdle brings, let me remind you. In 2024, under Mike McCarthy in Dallas, he ran for 1,079 yards, the first undrafted player in Cowboys history to hit 1,000 in a season. In 2025, he went to Carolina, took over as the starter in Week 5, ran for a career-high 206 yards against Miami, and finished with 1,076 yards and six touchdowns. Two teams, two seasons, two 1,000-yard years. Khan signed him on March 13th for two years and 12.25 million dollars, about six million a year.

That’s why I call this the weapon. Khan didn’t need a trade this week to upgrade the offense. He already had the upgrade on the roster. He just needed the toe to heal.

And the timing couldn’t be better, because the man Dowdle is joining is on fire. Jaylen Warren was named AFC Offensive Player of the Week after the Colts game, and he earned every bit of it 188 yards from scrimmage, an 80-yard touchdown catch, a 50-yard touchdown run, and the first player since Herschel Walker in 1986 to do both in the same game. McCarthy has called him “our featured player right now,” and he made it clear that won’t change just because Dowdle is back. But now Warren has someone to share the load. Warren gives you speed and elusiveness. Dowdle gives you power between the tackles. Put them together and you have a backfield that can beat you two different ways, and a 42-year-old quarterback who doesn’t have to throw 40 times to win.

And here’s a part of Dowdle’s game that matters even more this week. In both of his 1,000-yard seasons, he caught 39 passes. With Pittsburgh down to three healthy receivers on the active roster, a running back who can catch the ball out of the backfield is basically an extra receiver. Rodgers can check it down to Dowdle on third-and-short, throw a screen to Warren on the next series, and keep the chains moving without forcing the ball into double coverage. That’s how you survive a receiver shortage you turn your backs into weapons in the passing game too.

Speaking of that quarterback, look at Aaron Rodgers over the last three games 63 of 105 for 826 yards and nine touchdowns. Three touchdowns in each game. Now hand him a two-back running game. That’s what Khan built this offense to look like.

Part two the rest of the injury report is getting healthier too.

Thursday’s report wasn’t just about Dowdle. T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward were both full after veteran rest days on Wednesday. Jamel Dean was limited again with his ankle, which keeps the door open for him to play against the team where his career began. Jalen Ramsey was limited with his wrist, after not practicing on Wednesday, and remember, he played through that same injury against the Colts and made nine tackles. Defensive tackle Keeanu Benton, who missed Wednesday with an ankle injury, was back on the field as a limited participant.

DeShon Elliott was limited too. He’s practicing on his 21-day window off injured reserve, and he could be activated this weekend, but Steelers Depot thinks a return in Week 7 is more likely. Either way, help is coming in the secondary.

The only two players who didn’t practice were the receivers we already knew were out Roman Wilson with his finger and Ben Skowronek with his calf. And that brings us to the real hunt.

Part three the weapons Khan is hunting.

Here’s where Khan’s “done it again” reputation comes in. Over the last few weeks, he’s added two corners off practice squads, Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith, brought back pass rusher KJ Henry, added linebacker Marte Mapu to the practice squad, brought back linebacker Elandon Roberts, and earlier in the year he built the right side of the offensive line with a cheap free agent, Brock Hoffman, and a first-round rookie, Max Iheanachor. Quiet moves, fast moves, practical moves.

But this week, the signals got louder. On October 15th, longtime Steelers insider Mark Kaboly posted “Getting strong indications that the Steelers are going to be active heading into the NFL trade deadline set for Nov. 10.” That’s not a fan guessing. That’s a beat writer with sources inside the building.

He didn’t name a position. But it’s not hard to guess. With Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve and Wilson and Skowronek out for Tampa, Pittsburgh has just three healthy receivers on the 53-man roster DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen. Brandon Smith, Isaiah Hodgins, and Brandon Johnson are on the practice squad.

Even Rodgers’ old friend is saying it. James Jones, who played with Rodgers in Green Bay, said on his podcast, “I do think that they need to make a move.” He added, “I would love to see a guy like Tyreek Hill,” and, “I would love to see a trade for a wide receiver.”

So let’s go through the names on Khan’s board, one by one, and I’ll tell you what’s real.

Target one JuJu Smith-Schuster, the homecoming that may or may not be happening.

This one got Steelers fans excited on Thursday night. Noah Strackbein of Sports Illustrated reported, citing a team source, that the Steelers were set to host JuJu Smith-Schuster for a visit on Friday. That’s the same JuJu who was Pittsburgh’s second-round pick in 2017, who made his only Pro Bowl here, and who piled up 3,855 receiving yards and 26 touchdowns in five seasons in black and gold.

But here’s the honest part. Not long after that report spread, Behind the Steel Curtain posted an update saying the Steelers are not hosting Smith-Schuster for a visit. So as I record this, the reports conflict, and nothing has been confirmed by the team. I’m not going to tell you he’s coming, because nobody can say that right now.

What we do know is this. Smith-Schuster is a free agent. The Giants released him before the season, and he’s worked out for the Vikings and Chargers this week. He’s 29, he’ll turn 30 later this year, and last season with the Chiefs, he played all 17 games and caught 33 passes for 345 yards and a touchdown. He’s not the explosive player he was at 21. But he’s a tough, physical slot receiver who knows how to block, knows how to play in cold weather, and knows this city. If the Steelers do sign him, it’s a depth move with a lot of heart, not a game-changer. And even the first report said he’s unlikely to be ready for Tampa.

And let’s remember what JuJu meant to this franchise. He came into the league at 20 years old, and in 2018, his second season, he made the Pro Bowl. He later won a Super Bowl ring with Kansas City, Super Bowl 58. He’s played for the Steelers, Chiefs, Patriots, and Giants, and he has 5,624 career receiving yards and 33 touchdowns over nine seasons. He’s also a locker room personality, someone young receivers like Bernard could learn from. And Rodgers would get a receiver who knows how to find soft spots in zone coverage and isn’t afraid to go over the middle. That’s useful when your offense is built on quick, rhythm throws.

Target two Jerry Jeudy, the rare AFC North trade.

This one is the most interesting idea of the week. NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala talked about the possibility of the Steelers trading for Browns receiver Jerry Jeudy. Her words “He’s just an odd man out” in Cleveland. And she pointed out that Browns GM Andrew Berry “has obviously made trades within the division when he feels strongly about somebody.”

Here’s why that’s fascinating. Jeudy was a Pro Bowler in 2024, with 90 catches for 1,229 yards. In 2025, he had 50 catches for 602 yards. And this year, through five games, he has just eight catches for 81 yards. He’s under contract beyond 2026 after signing an extension with Cleveland.

Let me be clear this is an insider discussing an idea, not a report that talks are happening. Division trades are rare, and Cleveland just beat Pittsburgh on Thursday night two weeks ago. But if Jeudy really is the odd man out, and the Browns would rather get something than keep a receiver who isn’t in their plans, he’s a former 1,200-yard receiver who would instantly be the second-best wideout on this roster. That’s a weapon.

And think about what Jeudy would mean for Rodgers. Rodgers loves receivers who are precise, who sell routes, and who separate quickly at the top of the stem. That was always Jeudy’s calling card coming out of Alabama, and it’s what made his 2024 season so good. Pair him with Metcalf’s size and speed on the other side, and suddenly defenses can’t sit in man coverage and dare Pittsburgh’s receivers to win. That’s the exact problem McCarthy talked about after the Cleveland loss. Jeudy could be part of the answer, if the Browns are willing to pick up the phone.

Target three the names from the Kaboly conversation.

Steelers Depot also listed some logical receiver trade targets around Kaboly’s report Quentin Johnston of the Chargers, Jauan Jennings, now with the Vikings, Jeudy, and Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jaguars. None of these are reports of talks. They’re names that fit what Pittsburgh needs.

Thomas is still the dream. A 24-year-old who had 1,282 receiving yards as a rookie, stuck as Jacksonville’s third option. Johnston is a former first-round pick whose contract runs through 2027, though his drops this year are a real concern. And Jennings, as we said earlier this week, signed with Minnesota, so any deal would be with the Vikings.

And here’s where Pittsburgh has an advantage. Khan is holding the 2028 second-round pick he got from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. That’s real currency, and it isn’t even one of Pittsburgh’s own picks.

Target four Tyreek Hill, still out there.

Hill is still a free agent, still rehabbing from the major knee surgery he had on September 30th of last year. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said “nothing is imminent at this time.” The Chiefs remain the favorite in the betting markets. And there’s still no report connecting him to the Steelers. James Jones would love it. A lot of fans would love it. But right now, it’s a wish, not a plan.

And one name I need to update you on Calvin Ridley.

I’ve talked about Calvin Ridley a few times as a cheap veteran option, and I owe you an honest update. This week, Kinkhabwala warned against that kind of move. About Ridley, she said, “He’s had plenty and plenty and plenty and plenty of opportunity to contribute and be helpful to the cause.” And she said a receiver who is “not likely to be a game changer for you” is one you skip.

The numbers back her up. Ridley had 17 catches for 303 yards and no touchdowns in seven games last season, and just five catches for 81 yards this year. Over his last 12 regular-season games, he has zero touchdowns. So I’m moving him down my list. If Pittsburgh is going to spend anything, even a late-round pick, it should be on a receiver who can actually move the needle.

My trade block opinion.

So here’s where I land, and this is my opinion, not a report. If I’m Khan, the realistic weapon worth chasing before November 10th is Jerry Jeudy, if Cleveland is truly willing to move him in the division. He’s proven, he’s still in his prime years, and he would cost far less than Brian Thomas Jr. If the Browns won’t deal with a rival, then Thomas is the swing worth taking, with that Dallas second-round pick as the centerpiece. And if a veteran like Smith-Schuster ends up signing as depth, that’s fine too. It just shouldn’t be the only move.

Why patience might be Khan’s best weapon.

Here’s one more thing to think about. Khan doesn’t have to make a move this week. The deadline is November 10th, which is the Tuesday right after Pittsburgh’s Week 9 bye. That gives him three more weeks to watch the market, let prices drop, and see how Bernard and the practice squad receivers hold up. Teams that are losing in late October start selling. Players who were “untouchable” in September suddenly become available. If Khan waits and stays disciplined, he could land a better receiver for a cheaper price. And with Dowdle back, the offense has enough firepower to survive the wait.

That’s the balance. Be ready to strike, but don’t panic. And from everything we’ve seen this season, that’s exactly how Khan operates.

Part four why “done it again” fits.

Let’s step back for a second. Why do I keep saying Khan has done it again

Because look at what’s happened since the Thursday loss in Cleveland. A defense that got exposed now has more bodies in the secondary, a veteran linebacker who knows Patrick Graham’s system, and safety help on the way. The offense that couldn’t finish in Cleveland just put up 34 points on Indianapolis. And the running back Khan signed in the spring, when most people barely noticed, is about to come back and give this team its best backfield in years.

And the results have followed. Against the Colts, Jonathan Taylor ran for 45 yards on 15 carries. The Colts kicked five field goals. Daniel Jones didn’t throw a touchdown pass. Rodgers completed his first 11 passes. That’s not luck. That’s a roster built with a plan.

Now, Kaboly is telling us the next phase is coming. Khan isn’t satisfied with 3-2. He wants to add before the deadline. That’s the kind of general manager you want when your quarterback is 42 years old and playing his final season.

Part five what this means for Tampa Bay.

The Steelers visit Raymond James Stadium on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern. And there’s pressure. Pittsburgh has lost five straight road games, including that 27-24 loss in Cleveland. This is the game to end that streak.

Here’s what to watch. First, the backfield. With Dowdle back, I want to see McCarthy use both backs from the first drive. Warren in space, Dowdle between the tackles, and maybe even both of them on the field together to create mismatches. Second, the receivers. Metcalf and Bernard will carry the load, with Smith likely elevated again. Bernard just had six catches for 80 yards in his first start, and McCarthy says “football comes easy to him.” Third, the tight ends. Pat Freiermuth caught two touchdowns against the Colts, and with Darnell Washington, Pittsburgh can lean on two-tight-end sets all day.

On defense, keep an eye on Sean Murphy-Bunting. He spent the first month of the season on Tampa’s practice squad, practicing against that offense every day. And on the other sideline, Kenneth Gainwell, who left Pittsburgh for the Buccaneers this offseason. There’s a reunion feel to this whole game.

And after Tampa, the Steelers head to Paris to face the New Orleans Saints in Week 7. A road win on Sunday would send this team overseas at 4-2, with Dowdle back, Elliott possibly returning, and the trade deadline approaching. That’s a very different picture from the team people were writing off two weeks ago. Two weeks ago, the questions were about sideline arguments and whether the McCarthy era was in trouble. Now the questions are about which weapon gets added next. That’s real progress, Steelers Nation, and it all started with a front office that refused to sit still.

The bottom line.

So let me be completely clear about the headline. The Steelers haven’t traded for a new receiver yet, and the JuJu Smith-Schuster visit has conflicting reports. But Pittsburgh did get a huge weapon back before Tampa Rico Dowdle, the two-time 1,000-yard back who just practiced fully. And the front office is sending strong signals that more is coming before November 10th.

That’s Omar Khan. Quiet when he needs to be, aggressive when the moment calls for it. And right now, the moment is calling.
