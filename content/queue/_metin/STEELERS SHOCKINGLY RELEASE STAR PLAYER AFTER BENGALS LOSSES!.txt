Steelers Nation, if you clicked because you heard the Steelers cut a star player after the Bengals loss, here’s the truth up front: they didn’t. I checked the league’s transaction wire. The only Steelers player released this month was cornerback Terell Smith, and that happened on Saturday, the day before the game, to make room for Derrick Harmon.

So no star was released. But the biggest roster story of the week is real, and it’s serious. Starting left guard Mason McCormick was carted off Sunday night with a right ankle injury, and on Monday, Mike McCarthy still couldn’t say how long he’ll be out.

That injury could reshape the offensive line for the rest of the season. It might bring a forgotten rookie back into the picture. And it sets up a big week of decisions before Sunday’s trip to Philadelphia.

So today: what we know about McCormick, who replaces him, why the line matters so much in front of a 42-year-old quarterback, the T.J. Watt question from Sunday night, and what has to change against the Eagles. My board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

NO STAR RELEASE: HERE’S WHAT THE WIRE SAYS

Let’s settle the headline first.

According to NFL.com’s November waiver list, the only Pittsburgh entry this month is Terell Smith, waived on November 14 with no recall. That was the corresponding move when Harmon came off injured reserve before the game.

Smith had appeared in one game for the Steelers since signing on October 6. He’s a depth cornerback, not a star.

As of Tuesday morning, I haven’t found any other release, trade or major signing since the loss. If something changes this week, I’ll cover it. But right now, the real story isn’t who left. It’s who got hurt.

WHAT HAPPENED TO MASON MCCORMICK

Here’s the timeline.

Late in the second quarter Sunday night, Aaron Rodgers was sacked on a third-and-8. On that play, McCormick’s right ankle was badly twisted. According to ClutchPoints, players waved for help, the medical staff put an air cast on his leg, and he was carted off the field. The team ruled him out with an ankle injury.

The transcript describes the situation as looking serious immediately, and I’d agree. Every report I’ve read uses words like “brutal” and “significant.”

Spencer Anderson replaced him and played 40 snaps at left guard, according to Pittsburgh Steelers On SI.

MCCARTHY’S MONDAY UPDATE

On Monday, McCarthy held his weekly press conference, and the update was still vague.

According to SI, he said the team is “just working through the information gathering.” And on the timeline, he said: “I don’t have anything where I can really put a number on it or what the final diagnosis is.” ClutchPoints, citing ESPN’s Brooke Pryor, reported that McCarthy said he’s hoping for “some good news.”

That’s consistent with what he said Sunday night, when he told reporters the team would “speak on that when the time is right.”

So the honest answer is that nobody outside the building knows how long McCormick will be out. The transcript is right to warn fans not to assume he’s done for the season. ClutchPoints expects the Steelers to be without him for multiple weeks, but even that is an estimate, not a diagnosis.

WHY HIS TEAMMATES’ REACTION SAYS SO MUCH

You can learn a lot about a player from how his teammates react when he goes down.

According to Steelers Now, published on Yahoo Sports, Rodgers said: “Yeah, it’s hard. I just saw him in the training room, so. It’s a tough injury.” Tackle Troy Fautanu said it “made a lot of us sick to our stomachs.”

And Pat Freiermuth called McCormick “a really big loss,” describing him as the heart and soul of the offensive line. The transcript highlighted that same quote, and it tells you how the locker room sees him.

Local broadcaster Bob Pompeani posted that McCormick “has been so good and reliable.” That word, reliable, keeps coming up.

ONE CORRECTION TO THE TRANSCRIPT

Quick fix before we go further. In its opening, the transcript describes McCormick as one of the most important players on Pittsburgh’s defensive line. He’s on the offensive line. He’s the starting left guard. The rest of the transcript gets that right, so I think it was just a slip.

The transcript also spells his name a few different ways. It’s Mason McCormick.

WHO IS MASON MCCORMICK?

If you don’t know his background, here’s why the Steelers value him so much.

According to ClutchPoints, McCormick is 26 years old and a fourth-round pick from the 2024 draft. He had appeared in 43 straight games before Sunday, which means this will be the first time in his career he misses a game.

And according to Steeler Nation, he had played every offensive snap this season. He, center Zach Frazier and Fautanu had built real chemistry on the left side of the line.

Yahoo Sports described McCormick and Frazier as the two best linemen on the team. That’s quite a jump for a fourth-round pick in his third season. He’s become exactly the kind of homegrown starter this franchise loves to develop.

WHAT 43 STRAIGHT GAMES REALLY MEANS

That streak deserves a moment, because it explains why this injury hurts more than a typical depth-chart change.

Offensive line play is about trust. Each lineman has to know exactly how the man next to him will react to a stunt, a blitz or a late shift by the defense. That knowledge comes from snaps together, in practice and in games.

McCormick had been the constant. Whatever happened elsewhere on the line, whoever was dealing with an injury, the left guard spot was settled. Frazier knew who was beside him on every play. So did Fautanu.

Now that changes. Even if the replacement is a solid player, the communication has to be rebuilt in the middle of a season, with a short week coming up after the Eagles game. That’s why coaches often say losing a starting lineman costs more than the individual drop-off in talent.

THE DEXTER LAWRENCE DEBATE

There’s one more McCormick storyline that’s been floating around this week, and I want to handle it carefully.

According to Steeler Nation, in the Week 3 win over Cincinnati, McCormick grabbed Dexter Lawrence’s leg and lifted it while Frazier engaged him on a double-team. Former players debated whether that was legal.

On his “Check The Mic” podcast, analyst Sam Monson speculated: “I would imagine Cincinnati made a point of emphasizing that to the officials.”

But Steeler Nation is clear that there’s no public confirmation the Bengals contacted the officials about it. It’s speculation, and I’ll treat it that way. And by the time McCormick left Sunday night, Pittsburgh’s offense was already struggling badly. One block didn’t decide that game.

WHO REPLACES HIM? OPTION ONE: SPENCER ANDERSON

Now, the practical question. Who plays left guard in Philadelphia?

The most likely answer is Spencer Anderson. According to SI, he’s the probable starter. ClutchPoints notes he’s currently the only backup guard on the active roster.

Anderson has starting experience. He opened this season as the right guard before Brock Hoffman took over that job. But Yahoo Sports was blunt in its assessment: Anderson struggled when he got his early shot at starting, and the author sees him as a fine depth player rather than a long-term starter.

Still, 40 snaps against a strong Cincinnati front, with no warning and a hostile crowd, is real experience. A full week of practice with the starters should help him settle in.

That’s not a knock on Anderson’s effort. It’s just the reality that replacing a player who hadn’t missed a snap all season is hard.

OPTION TWO: THE FORGOTTEN ROOKIE, GENNINGS DUNKER

Here’s the name a lot of fans have forgotten.

Gennings Dunker was a third-round pick in this year’s draft out of Iowa, where he played tackle. The Steelers moved him to guard. According to Yahoo Sports, he suffered a suspected MCL sprain in practice and went on injured reserve, with an initial timeline of four to six weeks. SI notes that he’s now at the end of that window.

And McCarthy didn’t rule him out. According to SI, he said Dunker’s return is “definitely part of the conversation.”

Steeler Nation went even further, running a headline saying McCormick’s injury creates a “definitive path” for the rookie to play. I’d be a bit more careful. Dunker would need to practice first, and Yahoo Sports notes that Hoffman originally won the right guard job partly because of Dunker’s early struggles. But the opportunity is there.

A WORD ON BROCK HOFFMAN

While we’re talking about the guards, it’s worth mentioning the man on the other side.

Brock Hoffman took over at right guard early this season, and according to Yahoo Sports, he’s done well enough to keep the job. After the October win over Cleveland, it was Hoffman who credited the coaches for “showing belief” in the players, according to the AP.

That matters now, because the Steelers can’t afford to shuffle both guard spots. Keeping Hoffman settled on the right gives whoever plays left guard one less thing to worry about. The coaches will want as much stability as possible around the new piece.

OPTION THREE: DEPTH AND OUTSIDE HELP

Beyond Anderson and Dunker, SI lists Ryan McCollum, Doug Nester and Steven Jones as depth options.

ClutchPoints also raised the idea of adding a lineman from free agency or another team’s practice squad. Nothing like that has been reported, so I’m not going to speculate on names. But if McCormick is out for an extended period, I’d expect the Steelers to at least explore it.

And here’s a roster note. As the rules are generally understood, if McCormick goes on injured reserve, his spot on the 53-man roster opens up. That gives the front office room to activate Dunker, sign a veteran, or bring back a receiver like Roman Wilson without cutting anyone else.

THE ROSTER DOMINO EFFECT

McCormick’s injury doesn’t happen in a vacuum. It lands in the middle of a week where the Steelers already had several roster decisions to make.

Roman Wilson is now eligible to come off injured reserve after missing time with a finger injury. Sebastian Joseph-Day can return for the Denver game, according to Steelers Now. Michael Pittman Jr. is still working his way back from a foot injury. And Max Hurleman, the practice squad receiver Rodgers and McCarthy praised last week, is on the practice squad injured list.

Then there’s Hollywood Brown, whom the Eagles waived on Friday. As of my check of NFL.com’s transaction list, there’s no record of the Steelers claiming him, but the full outcome of his waiver period wasn’t clear in what I could access.

Every one of those moves requires a roster spot. If McCormick does go on injured reserve, that’s one more opening. If he doesn’t, the front office will have to cut someone to make room for any of these additions. That’s the real “release” question this week, and it hasn’t been answered yet.

WHY THE LINE MATTERS SO MUCH RIGHT NOW

The transcript makes the key point here: this isn’t just one position.

McCormick’s absence affects protection schemes, communication along the line and the run game. And it comes at a time when the quarterback is 42 years old and needs a clean pocket more than ever.

Remember what Post-Gazette reporter Christopher Carter said before the Bengals game: Pittsburgh’s linemen were “still kind of middle of the road pass protectors,” even though they’d been strong run blockers. Losing your most reliable guard doesn’t help either part of that equation.

And the run game was already in trouble Sunday night. According to Steeler Nation, Jaylen Warren ran for just 15 yards on seven carries, compared with 127 yards on 17 carries in the September win. Some of that was game script. Pittsburgh fell behind fast. But some of it was the Bengals winning up front.

THE GAME, IN BRIEF

Since the transcript recaps the game, here’s a quick version for anyone who missed it.

Cincinnati won 38-18. The Bengals led 24-3 at halftime. Joe Burrow went 35 of 44 for 409 yards and four touchdowns, without an interception. Ja’Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards.

Pittsburgh didn’t pick up a first down until well into the second quarter. Rodgers finished 15 of 29 for 143 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions, with both scores coming in the fourth quarter: one to DK Metcalf and one to Pat Freiermuth.

One correction: the transcript says Rodgers completed 15 of 29 in the first half. That was his line for the entire game.

The result moved Cincinnati to 7-2 and alone in first place in the AFC North. Pittsburgh is 6-3, one game back. The four-game winning streak is over.

HOW THE LINE HELD UP OVERALL

It wasn’t just the run game.

According to the AP, Rodgers was sacked three times on Sunday. And the play that injured McCormick was itself a sack on third down. Meanwhile, on the other side, Burrow was sacked only once.

That’s the gap in a nutshell. Cincinnati’s line gave its quarterback time. Pittsburgh’s line didn’t. And now Pittsburgh’s line has to take on the next opponent without its most reliable guard.

THE T.J. WATT QUESTION

The transcript raises another concern on the defensive side: T.J. Watt’s quiet night.

Watt entered the game tied for the league lead with seven and a half sacks, according to the AP. The transcript also credits him with nine and a half tackles for loss, which I couldn’t independently confirm.

On Sunday, the transcript says, Watt finished with one tackle and one pass deflection. And it cites Next Gen Stats reporting that Bengals right tackle Amarius Mims allowed zero pressures on 23 pass-blocking snaps against Watt. Across both meetings this season, the transcript says, Mims has kept Watt without a pressure on 45 pass-blocking snaps.

I couldn’t find that Next Gen Stats report myself, so take those numbers as the transcript’s. But they match what we saw: Burrow had time, and Pittsburgh got him down only once, on a Patrick Queen sack.

The transcript is fair about this. Nobody is questioning Watt’s ability. But against a quarterback like Burrow, the pass rush has to get home, and on Sunday it didn’t.

WHAT PATRICK GRAHAM CAN DO

So how do you fix a pass rush that got neutralized?

If one tackle is winning his matchup, you move your best rusher. Line Watt up on the other side. Put him inside on obvious passing downs. Use stunts and twists to make the tackle pass him off to a guard.

You also get creative with who rushes. Alex Highsmith, Nick Herbig, Queen and the young interior linemen can all create pressure if the scheme frees them up. And with Harmon back from injured reserve, Graham has another interior rusher to work with.

None of this is a criticism of Watt. It’s how good defenses adjust when an opponent takes away their top player.

THE SECONDARY WITHOUT ELLIOTT

The other defensive problem was in the back end.

Safety DeShon Elliott suffered a knee injury in pregame warmups and was ruled out, which forced rookie Robert Spears-Jennings into a much bigger role. The transcript notes the cruel timing: Pittsburgh got Harmon back, then lost Elliott before kickoff.

As of the reports I’ve seen, there’s no timeline on Elliott either. That’s two key starters, one on each side of the ball, with uncertain status heading into Philadelphia.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT: PHILADELPHIA

The transcript closes with the next test: Sunday, November 22, in Philadelphia.

One important update on that game. According to Steelers Now, it was flexed from 4:25 p.m. to 1 p.m. Eastern, still on CBS. So it’s an early kickoff.

The Eagles are 2-7 and have lost seven straight, according to Yahoo Sports. Jalen Hurts is out for the season, and Joe Milton started their last game, a 19-11 loss to the Giants. Philadelphia is coming off its bye.

But Pittsburgh hasn’t won a regular-season game in Philadelphia since 1965, going 0-11 there since, according to Steelers.com. So no matter how bad the Eagles’ record looks, this is not a guaranteed win.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS FOR PHILLY

The transcript lists four questions for this game, and they’re the right ones.

First, can the offensive line protect Rodgers without McCormick? That depends on Anderson, or possibly Dunker, and on how much help the coaches give them with tight ends and backs.

Second, can Pittsburgh establish the run? Warren is too good to have just seven carries again. Even if the first few runs don’t work, the Steelers need to stay with it.

Third, can the defense generate pressure? Graham needs to move his rushers around and make the opposing quarterback uncomfortable early.

Fourth, and most important, can McCarthy get this team to respond? As the transcript puts it, one loss doesn’t destroy a season. But how a team responds to adversity tells you a lot about what it’s capable of.

WHAT MCCARTHY’S TONE TELLS US

One last thing worth noticing: how McCarthy has handled the week so far.

On Sunday night, he called the loss “clearly not to the standard of the Pittsburgh Steelers” and admitted the team was “beaten decisively.” He also said he’d worried about a letdown after the bye, and added that “handling success is the biggest challenge in this league.”

On Monday, he stayed measured on McCormick. No predictions, no promises, just a commitment to wait for the full information.

And former coach Mike Tomlin, now on NBC, was blunter. According to Steeler Nation, he said the Steelers “ran into a buzzsaw” and had “breakdowns in so many areas.”

So the message from both coaches, past and present, is the same. Own the loss, learn from it, and don’t let it become two. That’s exactly the test in Philadelphia.

WHAT A LONG ABSENCE WOULD MEAN FOR THE SCHEDULE

If McCormick does miss multiple weeks, look at the games he could miss.

Philadelphia on November 22. Denver at home on Black Friday, November 27, on a short week. Houston on Sunday Night Football on December 6. A Monday night trip to Jacksonville on December 14.

That’s a tough stretch for any offensive line, and it includes two prime-time games and a short week. Whoever plays left guard will be tested immediately. If the Steelers can find a reliable answer quickly, they can survive this. If they’re still searching in December, the offense could have a hard time finding consistency.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Mason McCormick: if the diagnosis means a long absence, place him on injured reserve and use the roster spot. If there’s a chance he returns in a few weeks, give the medical staff time.

Left guard: start Spencer Anderson in Philadelphia. Open Gennings Dunker’s practice window this week, and if he looks ready, make it a real competition.

Depth: sign a veteran guard off the street or from another practice squad. One more injury, and the line has no margin.

Pass rush: move Watt around. Make the tackles guess where he’s coming from.

Run game: at least 20 carries for Warren and Rico Dowdle combined, no matter the score in the first half.

Elliott: no rush. If the knee isn’t right, Spears-Jennings starts with extra help over the top.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers didn’t release a star after the Bengals loss. But they may have lost one, at least for a while. Mason McCormick hadn’t missed a game in his career, and he was one of the two best linemen on this team.

Now the front office has to decide how to replace him, the coaches have to fix a pass rush that got shut down and an offense that couldn’t get started, and the whole team has to prove Sunday night was a bad night, not the start of a slide.

The answers start in Philadelphia, at 1 p.m. on Sunday.
