Steelers Nation, the Steelers made a roster move on Saturday, less than two days before kickoff in Cincinnati, and the internet turned it into something it isn’t. So let’s start with the honest version.

Pittsburgh did release a player before the Bengals game. But it wasn’t a star, and it wasn’t a quarterback, whatever you may have heard. It was cornerback Terell Smith, a depth player who had appeared in one game for the Steelers. And the reason was simple: the team needed a roster spot to activate Derrick Harmon from injured reserve.

In other words, the headline move was really about who’s coming back, not who’s leaving.

So today, we’ll go through exactly what happened Saturday and who Terell Smith is. We’ll look at what Harmon’s activation means for tonight. Then we’ll get into a sharp take from a Post-Gazette reporter who says the real problem with this offense isn’t the receivers at all. And we’ll finish with what Jaylen Warren said about the difference between Mike Tomlin and Mike McCarthy. My board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

THE MOVE: HARMON IN, SMITH OUT

Here’s what happened, according to ESPN.

On Saturday, November 14, the Steelers activated defensive tackle Derrick Harmon from injured reserve. To make room on the 53-man roster, they waived cornerback Terell Smith. ESPN’s Adam Schefter put it in one sentence: “Steelers activated DT Derrick Harmon from injured reserve and waived CB Terell Smith.”

That’s the whole move. One player in, one player out.

Now, a quick correction to the transcript, which at one point calls Smith a quarterback. He’s a cornerback. And his first name is spelled with one R, T-E-R-E-L-L, although even some outlets have spelled it both ways.

WHO IS TERELL SMITH?

Let’s give Smith his due, because his story is a tough one.

According to Heavy, Smith played college football at Minnesota and was a fifth-round pick of the Chicago Bears in 2023. His rookie season was promising. In 12 games, including four starts, he recorded 49 tackles, forced a fumble and recovered another.

In 2024, he played 14 games with two starts, finishing with 19 tackles, four passes defensed and an interception.

Then injuries took over. Smith tore his patellar tendon in the preseason and spent the year on season-ending injured reserve, missing all of 2025, according to Heavy and the transcript. After that, he was let go by Chicago and landed on the Washington Commanders’ practice squad.

Pittsburgh signed him to its active roster on October 6. He appeared in one game for the Steelers before Saturday’s move. For his career, according to Heavy, he has 27 games, 69 tackles, an interception, a forced fumble and a fumble recovery.

That’s a player who has fought back from a serious injury. It’s not a star.

WHY THIS ISN’T A STATEMENT ABOUT SMITH

The transcript makes the right point here: this move isn’t really a verdict on Smith’s ability. It’s roster math.

Pittsburgh had no open spot on the 53. Harmon needed one. And Smith, a recent addition at a position where the team just got healthier, was the logical choice.

Remember, Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter both practiced in full all week after their hamstring injuries. With those two back, the Steelers had cornerback depth to spare for the first time in weeks. That made Smith the odd man out.

And this may not be the end of his time in Pittsburgh. As the process is generally understood, a waived player goes through waivers, where any team can claim him. If nobody does, the team that released him can try to bring him back on the practice squad. Heavy even ran a piece suggesting the Bears could reunite with him. I’d keep an eye on where he lands this week.

DERRICK HARMON IS OFFICIALLY BACK

Now, the main event.

According to ESPN, Harmon was carted off with a foot injury on October 1 against the Cleveland Browns. He missed the minimum four games on injured reserve. And a source told NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport that the team never considered the injury long-term.

This week, he was limited in the first two practices and then a full participant in Friday’s walk-through. He’s still listed as questionable for tonight, but activating him the day before the game tells you the team expects him to be available.

Harmon kept his comments simple during the week. He said his main goal was: “Just make sure my foot feels good.” And he added: “I can still strike blocks, and I got the playbook down, of course, but still just fine-tuning that part of things.”

That last line is honest. He knows his assignments. What he needs is game rhythm.

HOW THE TIMELINE WORKED OUT

It’s worth appreciating how well the calendar broke for Pittsburgh here.

Harmon got hurt on October 1. Injured reserve requires a player to miss at least four games. The Steelers then had their bye. So by the time Pittsburgh got back to work this week, Harmon had served his minimum, rested through the off week and had a full practice week to ramp up.

That’s about as clean a return as you can draw up. The team didn’t have to rush him, and it didn’t have to keep him out longer than necessary. He comes back for the exact opponent he beat in September, in the biggest game of the season so far.

It also shows the value of the 21-day practice window. The Steelers could have waited and brought him back for Philadelphia next week. Instead, three practices were enough for the medical staff and the coaches to feel comfortable. That’s a strong signal about how he looked.

WHAT HARMON BRINGS TONIGHT

You know the play by now. In the Week 3 win over Cincinnati, Harmon strip-sacked Joe Burrow in the final minute, and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered to seal the 30-27 victory.

But Harmon is more than one play. As a rookie in 2025, according to ESPN, he recorded 27 tackles and three sacks. He’s a second-year first-round pick with the size to collapse the pocket from the inside, which is exactly what you need against a quarterback like Burrow.

And Burrow is playing at an elite level. According to Sports Illustrated, he leads all quarterbacks in passing yards per game at 298.3, in touchdowns with 23, and in completion percentage at 74 percent. If you give him a clean pocket, he will pick you apart. Harmon is one of the guys who can make that pocket uncomfortable.

The transcript also notes that McCarthy has said he loves divisional football. Tonight, he gets the biggest divisional game of the season, with his top young interior lineman back.

THE ODDS: PITTSBURGH IS THE UNDERDOG

The transcript mentions that Pittsburgh is heading into Cincinnati as an underdog, and the betting numbers back that up.

According to Sports Illustrated’s odds piece from November 11, the Bengals opened as 3.5-point favorites, with a moneyline of minus 190 for Cincinnati and plus 160 for Pittsburgh. The total was set at 49.5. Those numbers can move, so check the latest line before kickoff.

But here’s a fun trend from the same piece: the Steelers are 7-3 against the spread in the last 10 meetings with the Bengals, and 6-2 against the spread this season. Pittsburgh has made a habit of beating expectations in this rivalry.

WHAT CINCINNATI’S DEFENSE BRINGS

Before we get to the offense debate, let’s look at what Rodgers and his line are up against tonight.

Cincinnati will be without safety Kyle Dugger, the former Steeler, who was ruled out with a quad injury. Cornerback Jalen Davis, who was hurt in the September game, is doubtful after returning to practice from injured reserve. Defensive tackles Jonathan Allen and B.J. Hill were on the report for rest only. And Dexter Lawrence appeared on the report without a game status.

After the first meeting, Allen admitted the Bengals’ defense “left too much out there.” That’s a group that will come into tonight motivated. And with a home crowd behind them in prime time, Cincinnati’s front will want to make this a long night for a 42-year-old quarterback.

That’s why the pass protection question isn’t theoretical. It’s the matchup that could decide the game.

THE REPORTER’S TAKE: IT’S NOT THE RECEIVERS

Now let’s get to the second story, and this is the one I think deserves the most attention.

The trade deadline came and went without a receiver. The transcript lists Josh Downs, Calvin Ridley and Brian Thomas Jr. among the names linked to Pittsburgh in the weeks before. Some of those were real reported talks, like the Ridley conversation ESPN’s Dan Graziano described. Others were mostly speculation.

But Christopher Carter of the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, speaking on The Fan Morning Show, offered a different perspective. According to Steeler Nation, he said: “I don’t think the problem is as much receiver right now as it is just the fact that the quarterback’s old.”

That’s a blunt way to put it. And he didn’t stop there.

On the offensive line, Carter said Pittsburgh’s linemen are “still kind of middle of the road pass protectors,” even though they’ve run-blocked well this year. And on the deadline, he said: “I just don’t know if there was a huge difference maker and what receiver they could have gotten.”

In other words, Carter’s view is that a new receiver wouldn’t have fixed the core issues: a 42-year-old quarterback and average pass protection.

DO THE NUMBERS SUPPORT CARTER?

Let’s test that take, because there’s data on both sides.

Here’s what supports it. According to an Athlon Sports midseason review, published on Yahoo Sports on November 12, Rodgers ranked 29th in completion percentage at 60.8 percent through eight games. His average depth of target was 7.4 yards, 31st. His dropback EPA ranked 26th, and his dropback success rate was 27th. Athlon also projected him on pace for 48 sacks, up from 33 last season.

Rodgers turns 43 on December 2. Athlon described him as “playing like a 42-year-old signal-caller.” That’s not a knock on his intelligence or his arm. It’s a recognition that he can’t escape pressure the way he used to.

And the transcript nails why that matters: if the pocket collapses before Rodgers finishes his reads, even a great receiver might never get the chance to make a play.

BUT THERE’S ANOTHER SIDE

Now here’s the counterpoint, and it comes from the same Athlon review.

In the games where Pittsburgh had all its weapons available, the Steelers went 2-1, and Rodgers threw seven touchdowns, averaged about 270 passing yards and had an average depth of target well over 10 yards. That suggests the problem isn’t only age. It’s also who’s on the field with him.

And receivers have dropped 15 passes already this season, according to Athlon, compared with 22 all of last year. That’s a pace that hurts any quarterback’s numbers.

The Browns game is the perfect example. According to Athlon, Rodgers went 13 of 25 for 172 yards with no touchdowns and an interception. But that interception came when DK Metcalf bobbled what Athlon called a “wide-open throw,” and Denzel Ward caught the deflection. The writer argued the turnover wasn’t Rodgers’ fault.

So I’d say Carter has a point, but it’s not the whole story. Age is real. Pass protection is real. But so are the drops and the injuries in the receiver room.

THE DEADLINE QUESTION, REVISITED

There’s one part of Carter’s take I want to push back on a little.

He said he doubts any available receiver would have been worth a top-two-round pick. That’s probably true for the big names. But not every deal at this deadline was expensive.

Josh Downs went to Kansas City for 2027 fourth- and fifth-round picks, according to NFL.com. And Keenan Allen went to San Francisco for a 2027 sixth-rounder, according to the San Francisco Standard. Allen had 40 catches for 351 yards in eight games with Indianapolis before the trade.

So there were mid-level options that didn’t cost a premium pick. Whether either player would have changed Pittsburgh’s offense is a fair debate. Carter’s point is that the bigger limits are age and protection, and a depth receiver doesn’t fix those. But it’s not quite accurate to say the only options were blockbuster deals.

Omar Khan weighed the price, the draft capital and whether any of those players would make the Steelers significantly better. He decided the answer was no. Tonight is the first chance to see whether that call holds up.

WHY THE OFFENSIVE LINE IS THE KEY TONIGHT

Here’s where this all connects to tonight’s game.

Carter’s argument and the transcript’s argument land on the same conclusion: protecting Rodgers has to be the priority. And the offensive line has already shown what it can do in the run game. Center Zach Frazier and the guards have been moving people all season, and Warren has been the beneficiary.

The challenge tonight is that Cincinnati will try to make Pittsburgh one-dimensional, as the transcript puts it. If the Bengals stop the run on early downs, Rodgers ends up in obvious passing situations, and that’s when an average pass-protecting line gets exposed.

So the formula is simple, even if it’s not easy. Run the ball well on first and second down. Keep Rodgers in manageable third downs. Use play action to buy him time. And get the ball out quickly when the pocket starts to close.

METCALF’S ROLE IN ALL OF THIS

The transcript also makes a fair point about Metcalf. Whatever his struggles, he’s still one of the biggest weapons in this offense. He can stretch the field, and he forces defenses to account for him on every snap.

The question, as the transcript puts it, is whether the line can give Rodgers enough time to get him the ball. Deep routes take time to develop. If the protection holds for an extra half-second, Metcalf’s size becomes a real problem for Cincinnati’s secondary, especially with former Steeler Kyle Dugger ruled out at safety.

That’s a matchup to watch. If Rodgers gets time, Metcalf has a chance for the breakout game fans have been waiting for.

WHAT HAPPENED IN THE FIRST MEETING

It’s also worth remembering that Pittsburgh’s passing game did work against Cincinnati in September.

According to the Steelers’ recap of the 30-27 win, Rodgers completed 19 of 34 passes for 292 yards, three touchdowns and an interception. The Steelers outgained the Bengals 411 to 352 and converted 7 of 12 third downs. Roman Wilson caught a 38-yard touchdown, Ben Skowronek caught one from 23 yards, and Metcalf scored on a 6-yarder in the third quarter.

That’s a quarterback who looked anything but old. The difference that day was balance. Warren had 176 yards from scrimmage, and the threat of the run kept Cincinnati’s pass rush honest.

If Pittsburgh can recreate that balance tonight, Carter’s concerns become much easier to manage.

JAYLEN WARREN ON TOMLIN VS. MCCARTHY

Now let’s go to the locker room, because Jaylen Warren gave us a rare look inside it this week.

Warren appeared on The Pat McAfee Show on Friday, according to Steeler Nation, and talked about the difference between his old coach and his new one.

On Tomlin, he was full of respect. “I was used to my first four years was Tomlin, one of the best coaches I’ve ever played under,” he said.

On McCarthy, he admitted he didn’t know what to expect. “Mike McCarthy, I didn’t have too many thoughts about him,” Warren said.

But then he described what surprised him. “What he did surprise me with, sometimes in meetings, he will be talking and he will just get fired up randomly,” Warren said. And he added: “He’s serious, and he’s willing to do whatever it takes to win.”

The transcript also quotes Warren as saying: “You couldn’t ask for more from a guy.” I couldn’t find that exact line in the coverage I saw, so I’d treat it as the transcript’s paraphrase. But it fits everything else he said.

WHY WARREN’S WORDS MATTER

Here’s why I think this is more than a feel-good quote.

Replacing a coach who spent 19 years in one building is one of the hardest jobs in sports. Tomlin built the culture, the expectations and the identity of this franchise. McCarthy didn’t try to copy that. He brought his own personality, and the players are noticing.

When your best offensive player goes on a national show and says the new coach is “willing to do whatever it takes to win,” that’s a sign the transition is working. It’s also a sign the players are buying in, which might be the most important thing for a first-year staff in a big November game.

WARREN’S SEASON BY THE NUMBERS

And Warren has every reason to like the new system, because it’s built around him.

According to Steelers.com, heading into the bye, Warren had 704 rushing yards on 119 carries, among the league leaders, and 998 yards from scrimmage, second in the NFL. In the last game before the bye, he ran for 143 yards against Cleveland.

McCarthy couldn’t stop praising him. “He’s hot as a firecracker,” McCarthy said. And: “Jaylen is on fire. You can’t get him the ball enough.”

Warren, meanwhile, kept it humble: “I don’t know. I’m just playing ball.”

The transcript adds two milestones to watch. It says Warren is chasing a third straight 100-yard rushing game, and that he’s 254 yards away from his career-best season rushing total of 958. I couldn’t independently confirm those two numbers, so take them as the transcript’s figures. But the math lines up: 704 plus 254 is 958. With nine games left, a new career high looks very likely.

HOW IT ALL COMES TOGETHER TONIGHT

So let’s put the three stories together.

The roster move tells you Pittsburgh’s priority is the defensive front. Harmon is back to help pressure Burrow, and the Steelers were willing to give up cornerback depth to do it.

The Carter take tells you where the offense’s real pressure point is: the pass protection in front of a 42-year-old quarterback.

And the Warren interview tells you the answer to that pressure point. Run the ball, keep Rodgers out of obvious passing downs, and let one of the most productive backs in football carry this team.

If all three work, Pittsburgh has a real chance to win as an underdog tonight. If the line can’t protect Rodgers and Burrow gets comfortable, it’ll be a long night.

THREE THINGS TO WATCH IN THE FIRST QUARTER

If you want an early read on how this game is going to go, here’s what I’ll be watching in the opening 15 minutes.

First, Warren’s first three carries. If he’s getting four yards or more on early downs, the offense is on schedule, and McCarthy can open up the playbook.

Second, Rodgers’ time in the pocket on the first third down. If he has a clean pocket and gets the ball out on rhythm, the pass protection concerns can wait. If he’s under immediate pressure, expect more quick throws and screens the rest of the night.

Third, Harmon’s first snaps. Watch whether he’s in the rotation on Cincinnati’s first passing down. If the coaches trust him early, it means he’s ready for a real role.

Those three signals will tell you a lot about whether Pittsburgh can win as an underdog.

THE ROSTER MOVES STILL AHEAD

One last thing on the roster, because Saturday’s move is only the beginning.

Roman Wilson becomes eligible to come off injured reserve after tonight. Michael Pittman Jr. is still working toward having his practice window opened. Sebastian Joseph-Day can return after the Philadelphia game. Each of those activations will need a corresponding move, just like Harmon’s did.

So expect more decisions like the Terell Smith one over the next few weeks. Some will be just as quiet. Some might be harder. But every one of them is a sign that this roster is getting healthier, which is exactly what you want heading into December.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for tonight. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Terell Smith: if he clears waivers, bring him back on the practice squad. He’s a useful depth body who knows the system now.

Derrick Harmon: play him 20 to 25 snaps, mostly on passing downs. Pair him with Heyward inside and let Watt and Herbig work the edges.

Offensive line: chips and help on the edges early. Make Cincinnati’s rushers work through extra bodies before you trust the tackles one-on-one.

Jaylen Warren: 22 to 25 touches, including screens. Let him chase that career high.

Rodgers: quick throws early, play action after the run game is established, and one deep shot to Metcalf per half when the protection holds.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers didn’t release a star before the Bengals game. They waived a depth cornerback with one game in Pittsburgh so they could get Derrick Harmon back on the field. That’s not a shock. It’s a smart, routine move, and it tells you the team is going all-in on stopping Burrow.

The bigger questions are on offense. Can the line protect Rodgers? Can Warren keep running wild? And can the Steelers win as an underdog in prime time?
