Steelers Nation, the morning after a loss like this, you want answers. And Mike McCarthy actually gave us more of them than you might think.

Most of the attention after Sunday's 19-16 loss in Philadelphia has gone to DK Metcalf's ejection. And we'll get to that. But if you listen to the full postgame press conference, McCarthy said a lot of things that tell you how this team sees what went wrong, what changed during the game, and what he plans to do on a short week before Denver comes to town on Friday.

So today, I'm going through the wave of updates coming out of that press conference and the box score. Ten of them. Some are good news, some are bad, and a couple could shape the rest of the season.

Before we start, one quick correction. The video I'm working from today says the final was 19-6. It was 19-16, according to the Associated Press. Max Hurleman's touchdown with 1:21 left made it a three-point game. That matters, because a 19-6 loss and a 19-16 loss tell two different stories.

And as always, the board at the end. Opinion, not a report.

Let's get into it.

UPDATE ONE: MCCARTHY PUT IT ON HIMSELF

Let's start with the tone, because it tells you a lot.

McCarthy was asked how he gets this team to bounce back from its first back-to-back losses of his tenure here. His answer, from the press conference: a clear vision on the plan for the week, and being accountable, "starting with me."

Then he said this: "This leaves a bad taste. It's one game, though. We're six and four."

And later, when he was asked about the systems he says are in place to handle adversity, he said: "Great confidence. I think history will speak to that."

So you get both sides of McCarthy in two minutes. He owns the loss. And he refuses to panic. Whether you love that or hate it probably depends on how frustrated you are this morning. But it's consistent with how he's talked all season.

UPDATE TWO: HE ADMITTED THEY EXPECTED TO WIN

There was one more line that I think stung a little.

McCarthy said the team expected to win this game, especially given the challenges and changes the Eagles were going through. And he said: "I didn't get it done today."

That's honest. The Eagles came in having lost seven straight, with Joe Milton at quarterback, Jalen Hurts out for the year and Tanner McKee benched, according to the AP. Pittsburgh was the favorite. And McCarthy didn't pretend otherwise. He knew this was a game the Steelers should have won.

UPDATE THREE: THE RUN GAME DIAGNOSIS

Here's where the press conference got really interesting from a football standpoint.

McCarthy was asked about the offense, and he went straight to the run game. According to the AP, the Steelers had just 13 rushing yards in the first half. And McCarthy explained why, at least in part. He said a couple of double-team blocks got split, and the Eagles played their linebackers behind those double teams in a way the Steelers didn't react to quickly enough.

In plain English: Philadelphia had a plan for Pittsburgh's run game, and the Steelers didn't adjust fast enough.

Then he said something that every Steelers fan wanted to hear. He said the team has to get the ball to its backs, and: "I'll take responsibility for that."

Jaylen Warren finished with just nine carries for 28 yards, according to CBS Sports. For a player who's been one of the most productive backs in football this season, nine carries is not enough, no matter what the defense is doing. McCarthy knows it.

UPDATE FOUR: THE NO-HUDDLE CHANGED THE GAME

This one is the most important football detail from the entire press conference, and I haven't seen many people talk about it.

McCarthy said that once the Steelers went to the no-huddle in the second quarter, it "standardized the calls a little more." What he meant is that the Eagles had been throwing different fronts at them, different numbers of linemen and overhang players, and when Pittsburgh sped up, Philadelphia couldn't disguise as much. McCarthy said Aaron Rodgers managed it well. And he said the Steelers stayed with that approach for the whole second half.

Now, think back to what we talked about on this channel last week. Rodgers had hinted that the offense had been good in the no-huddle, and the question was why they didn't use it more. On Sunday, they did, and the head coach is now saying it helped.

That's a real takeaway. Going into Denver, I'd expect to see more tempo from the very first drive. Not just when they're behind.

UPDATE FIVE: THE FIVE SACKS

Now the protection.

During the press conference, McCarthy was asked about Rodgers taking five sacks. I could only confirm three of them in the play-by-play I was able to see on CBS Sports: one by Byron Young in the third quarter, and two in the fourth by Nolan Smith and Gabe Hall. So treat five as the number reporters used in the room.

When he was asked how the Eagles got so much pressure, McCarthy's answer was short. He said it came down to the matchups up front, and that it'll be part of the team's process going forward.

Translation: he's not going to call out any specific lineman publicly. But with Spencer Anderson making his first start at left guard in place of Mason McCormick, the line was always going to be under a microscope. And it'll be under a bigger one on Friday.

UPDATE SIX: HURLEMAN EARNED IT

Now, a positive update. Maybe the most positive one.

Max Hurleman was elevated from the practice squad and ended up being one of the stories of the game. According to Bleacher Report's box score, he had six catches for 61 yards and the touchdown on seven targets.

When McCarthy was asked about the decision to play him, he said: "Max earned it." Then he gave some background I hadn't heard before. He said an injury in the preseason game against Buffalo was what kept Hurleman off the 53-man roster in the first place. McCarthy said that with a healthy training camp, Hurleman would have been in a different position, and the team was fortunate to get him on the practice squad, get him healthy, and get him ready.

And then: "Here his opportunity was today, and I thought he did a really good job."

McCarthy also praised the trust Rodgers showed in him and his route running. That's a big statement about an undrafted player in his first real NFL action.

WHO MAX HURLEMAN IS

If you're new to the Hurleman story, here's why it's such a good one.

He's from Wyomissing, Pennsylvania. He played at Colgate as a running back and returner before transferring to Notre Dame as a walk-on, where he played cornerback and returned punts. He signed with the Steelers after an undrafted tryout in 2025, scored a touchdown in a preseason game, and then spent two seasons bouncing between the practice squad and the practice squad's injured list without playing a regular-season snap.

Rodgers publicly asked for him to get opportunities. McCarthy said earlier this month that he'd deserved a 53-man spot. And on Sunday, in his first real NFL action, he caught a touchdown pass from a four-time MVP in a one-score game. Whatever else happened in Philadelphia, that's a moment Hurleman will never forget.

UPDATE SEVEN: THE KICK RETURN CHANGE

There's a smaller update connected to Hurleman that's worth explaining.

McCarthy was asked about a decision involving Kaden Wetjen, the rookie returner. His answer was that it's about all 68 players in the building, that Wetjen has been given a lot of opportunities, and that this was a battle back in training camp. He said Lew Nichols has come on strong, and when Hurleman was given his chance, McCarthy felt it was his time.

According to the CBS Sports box score, Nichols had three kickoff returns and Hurleman had two. McCarthy said the two of them handled the tricky, bouncing kicks well. That's a big compliment, because those kicks are a nightmare under the new kickoff rules.

To be fair, it wasn't spotless. According to the Eagles' live blog, Hurleman misjudged one kickoff and muffed another. But the overall message from the coach is clear: the return job is open, and Wetjen has competition.

UPDATE EIGHT: METCALF, IN MCCARTHY'S WORDS

Now, Metcalf.

The first question of the press conference was about what happened. McCarthy's answer: the referee told him Metcalf poked the defender in the eye.

Then he was asked about his confidence in Metcalf. McCarthy said: "We need him. We need him in those games, and that's not acceptable to get thrown out of a game." He also pointed out the team had only five receivers active, which made the ejection even more costly.

And when he was asked whether there'd be internal punishment, McCarthy said he wanted all the information first, because emotions were high on several fronts. But he said everything would be addressed. And: "We can't have players getting ejected from games."

He also mentioned that only 48 players dress on game day. You can hear the point he's making: when one of those 48 is gone, everyone else has to cover for him.

UPDATE NINE: WHERE THINGS STAND WITH METCALF THIS WEEK

Here's where we are on Monday morning.

According to Yahoo Sports, the NFL reviews every ejection for possible further discipline. That means a fine is very likely, and a suspension can't be ruled out. Nothing has been announced. And according to Pro Football Talk, McCarthy hasn't ruled out discipline from the team either.

The Steelers play Friday. So whatever is coming, from the league or from the team, has to happen fast. I'd expect clarity by midweek.

UPDATE TEN: HIGHSMITH'S ANKLE

And the last update might be the most worrying one for the defense.

Alex Highsmith left the game with an ankle injury and was ruled out in the second half, according to the AP. And it came on a day when he was playing really well. According to the Eagles' live blog, he had two sacks of Milton, including a strip-sack.

McCarthy was asked about him at the end of the press conference. He said he "can't say enough about him," and praised his energy and professionalism. But then: "I don't really have a diagnosis for you on the ankle."

So no timeline yet. With Nick Herbig already dealing with hip and rib injuries, and a Friday game coming, this is the one to watch most closely this week.

WHAT MCCARTHY DIDN'T SAY

Sometimes what a coach leaves out is just as telling as what he says.

McCarthy didn't blame the officials. He didn't single out a lineman for the sacks. He didn't criticize Rodgers. And even with Metcalf, he kept it to "we need him" and "that's not acceptable," without piling on.

That's a deliberate approach. He's protecting the locker room in public while promising that everything will be addressed in private. Some people, like former Steeler Bryant McFadden on CBS, think he should have been tougher. But on a five-day turnaround, with a team that needs to stay together, I understand why McCarthy chose this path.

The real test isn't what he says at the podium. It's what the Steelers look like on Friday. If the plan he described shows up on the field, with tempo early, more runs and better protection, then the message got through.

WHAT ABOUT PITTMAN?

One more note that isn't in the press conference but matters a lot.

Michael Pittman Jr. doesn't appear among the Steelers' pass catchers in the CBS Sports play-by-play. Roman Wilson, Hurleman, Germie Bernard, Pat Freiermuth, Darnell Washington, Rico Dowdle, Metcalf and Jaylen Warren all do. And McCarthy mentioned five receivers being active.

So it looks like Pittman didn't play, but I couldn't find an official confirmation of whether he was activated. If he wasn't, the obvious question is whether he's ready for Denver, and what that does to the receiver room if Metcalf is suspended. That's something we'll be watching on the first injury report of the week.

THE WILSON RETURN WAS QUIETLY GOOD

While we're on the receivers, let's give Roman Wilson his due.

In his first game back from injured reserve, he had three catches for 43 yards on six targets, according to Bleacher Report. That's not a huge stat line. But it's a receiver who hadn't played since October 11 coming in and immediately making plays. And on a short week, that kind of return matters.

If Metcalf misses time, Wilson becomes the most experienced healthy receiver Rodgers has. That's a big jump in responsibility for a third-year player. But based on Sunday, he looks ready for it.

WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY ABOUT THE GAME

Let's look at the team stats for a second, because they tell an interesting story.

According to CBS Sports, the Steelers actually out-gained the Eagles, 233 yards to 206. They had more first downs, 15 to 10. They held the ball for almost 35 minutes. And they didn't turn it over once, while Philadelphia lost a fumble.

So how do you lose that game? You lose it in the first quarter, when the Eagles scored 13 points. You lose it on third down, where the Steelers went just 4-of-16, per CBS Sports. You lose it with 13 rushing yards in the first half. And you lose it when your top receiver gets ejected with the game still in reach.

That's not a team that got dominated. That's a team that beat itself in a handful of key moments. Which is both encouraging and frustrating at the same time.

RODGERS, ONE WEEK BEFORE HIS 43RD BIRTHDAY

One more thing about Rodgers. According to the AP, he turns 43 on December 2. And Sunday's game was a perfect snapshot of what a 42-year-old quarterback can and can't do.

In the first half, he went 17-of-20 for 95 yards and averaged half a yard of air distance per attempt, according to the AP. That's a quarterback being asked to get the ball out immediately because the protection wasn't holding.

But once the Steelers went no-huddle, he was able to manage the game at the line of scrimmage, which is exactly what McCarthy praised. Rodgers finished 30-of-44 for 227 yards and a touchdown, according to Bleacher Report, with no interceptions. And he ran five times for 29 yards.

That's the formula for this offense. Rodgers' brain at the line, quick decisions and tempo. When the Steelers lean into that, they move the ball. When they don't, they stall.

THIRD DOWN IS THE REAL PROBLEM

That 4-of-16 number on third down deserves its own spotlight.

When you're converting only one in four third downs, you're giving the ball back over and over. And in a game where the Eagles' offense struggled, every extra possession you give them is another chance for Jake Elliott to kick a long field goal. He made four of them, including kicks from 53 and 55 yards, according to the AP.

If the Steelers convert even two or three more third downs, this is probably a win. That's how thin the margin was.

THE DEFENSE DESERVES CREDIT

And let's not forget the other side of the ball.

The defense gave up a 38-yard touchdown run to Saquon Barkley in the first quarter and then didn't allow another touchdown the rest of the game. Milton finished 11-of-21 for 141 yards, according to the AP. The Eagles had only 206 total yards.

The problem is that the offense didn't give the defense any help early, and the Eagles got just enough field position to keep kicking field goals. But this defense played well enough to win. After what happened in Cincinnati the week before, that's an important sign.

THE STANDINGS REALITY

Let's be honest about where this leaves the Steelers in the big picture.

According to FOX Sports, the Steelers are now 6-4 and third in the AFC North. Two weeks ago, they were alone in first place. And according to Yahoo Sports' summary of the postgame coverage, a win in Philadelphia could have moved them ahead of Cleveland.

That's how fast things change in this division. Two losses, and you go from leading the pack to looking up at two teams. The good news is that there are still seven games left. The bad news is that the next one is against one of the best teams in the AFC.

THE DENVER CHALLENGE

And that's not an exaggeration.

According to FOX Sports, Denver is 8-2 and leading the AFC West. The Broncos beat the Raiders 38-24 on Sunday. And Denver is favored by 2.5 points in Pittsburgh, which tells you how the market sees this Steelers team right now.

There are two numbers from that FOX Sports preview that jump off the page. Denver has the most fourth-quarter sacks in the NFL this season, with 17. And Pittsburgh has the fewest yards in goal-to-go situations and the lowest yards per play in the red zone.

Put those together. The Steelers just gave up five sacks, according to reporters at the press conference. Their offense struggles to finish drives. And now they face a defense that gets after the quarterback when games are on the line. That's a tough matchup on a short week.

FOX Sports also lists five Steelers as questionable heading into the week: Gennings Dunker, DeShon Elliott, Nick Herbig, Michael Pittman Jr. and Patrick Queen. Highsmith's ankle and Metcalf's status will be the two biggest additions to watch.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DENVER

So let's bring all ten updates together.

The Steelers host Denver on Friday, five days after this loss. Here's what I think the wave of updates tells us about that game.

The offense is likely to lean into tempo earlier, after McCarthy said the no-huddle standardized the Eagles' looks.

The run game needs a fix up front, especially the double teams McCarthy talked about, and Warren needs more than nine carries.

The protection needs to hold up better, with Anderson getting his second start at left guard.

The receiver room could look very different, depending on Metcalf's status and whether Pittman is ready.

Hurleman has earned a bigger role, and the return job is now a real competition.

And the defense might be without Highsmith and Herbig on the edge, which would put a lot on T.J. Watt and Jack Sawyer.

That's a lot of moving parts for a five-day turnaround.

And one more thing about the timing. The Steelers will be playing their third game in 12 days, and their second in six. That's why every practice rep this week has to count, and why the coaches may simplify the game plan rather than add to it. A clean, simple plan executed well is better than a clever one executed badly.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for the week. Opinion, not a report.

Tempo: start in the no-huddle on the first drive against Denver. McCarthy himself said it helped. Don't wait until you're behind.

Run game: 20 carries for Jaylen Warren, minimum. Fix the double teams and stay with it, even if the first few runs don't work.

Protection: give Rodgers more help with chips and quick throws. Five sacks can't happen again.

Metcalf: if the league doesn't suspend him, play him, but with a reduced role, and make him earn the snaps back.

Hurleman: keep him active. He's earned a real role in the offense, not just special teams.

Pittman: activate only if he's 100 percent. Don't rush a foot injury into a short week.

Return game: keep the competition open between Nichols, Hurleman and Wetjen.

Edge rush: if Highsmith and Herbig are both out, scheme pressure with blitzes and stunts, and lean on Watt and Sawyer.

FINAL WORD

Here's my bottom line. This loss was frustrating. But when you listen to the whole press conference, you don't hear a coach who's lost. You hear a coach who knows exactly what went wrong.

The run game got out-schemed early. The no-huddle fixed some of it. The protection broke down. A star receiver lost his cool. And an undrafted second-year receiver stepped up and almost saved the day.

McCarthy says it's one game. He says the Steelers are 6-4 and everything is still in front of them. He's right about that. But he also knows that saying it isn't enough. On Friday, against Denver, they have to show it.
