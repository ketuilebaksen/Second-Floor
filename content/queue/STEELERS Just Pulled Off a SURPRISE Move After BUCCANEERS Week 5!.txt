Steelers Nation, everybody is talking about the overtime comeback in Tampa, but almost nobody is talking about the surprise decision Pittsburgh made before kickoff, the one that ended up shaping the whole game. When the inactive list came out on Sunday, the two cornerbacks Omar Khan had claimed off other teams’ practice squads just two weeks ago, Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith, were both in street clothes. Instead, the Steelers rolled with Jamel Dean in his first game back, rookie Daylen Everette as “part of the rotation,” and young Doneiko Slaughter as the backup slot corner. Then the gamble got tested in real time, because veteran Brandin Echols went down with a hamstring injury in the first half. And on offense, Aaron Rodgers delivered a message about DK Metcalf that every Steelers fan needs to hear. So today we’re breaking down the surprise move, how it paid off, the injury that could force a new decision this week, and what Rodgers wants fixed before Paris. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The surprise move: trusting the young corners.

Let’s start with the decision itself. Two weeks ago, after the loss in Cleveland, Khan scrambled to rebuild the depth of his secondary. He claimed Sean Murphy-Bunting off Tampa Bay’s practice squad, a Super Bowl champion with the 2020 Buccaneers and a veteran with 11 career interceptions. The next day, he claimed Terell Smith off Washington’s practice squad. Both went straight to the 53-man roster.

So you’d think that heading into Tampa, with Murphy-Bunting’s inside knowledge of the Bucs’ offense, he’d be suiting up. Instead, according to Steelers Now, both Murphy-Bunting and Smith were made inactive. The coaching staff chose to trust the corners already in the system: Dean, who was questionable with an ankle injury after missing the Colts game, Asante Samuel Jr., rookie Daylen Everette, and Jalen Ramsey in the slot, with Echols and Doneiko Slaughter behind him.

When McCarthy was asked afterward about Everette’s role, his answer was simple: “That was part of the rotation.” No drama, no explanation. Just a coaching staff showing confidence in a rookie.

Why keep them inactive? Here’s my read, and it’s my opinion. Both players are still getting comfortable in Graham’s system after arriving in early October. Murphy-Bunting’s last full season was 2024 in Arizona, when he allowed 37 catches on 52 targets for 390 yards and two touchdowns, but also picked off three passes. Smith, a 2023 fifth-round pick of the Bears out of Minnesota, tore his patellar tendon in August of 2025 and missed that entire season. So both are smart depth additions, but neither had a long track record in this defense. The coaches clearly felt more comfortable with players who’d been running the system all year.

And remember the rules here. Because both players came off other teams’ practice squads, the Steelers have to keep them on the active roster for at least three weeks. So making them inactive doesn’t mean they’re being cut. It means they’re being kept ready. That’s the definition of insurance.

And then there’s Doneiko Slaughter. When Pittman went on injured reserve before the Colts game, the open roster spot went to Slaughter, who was signed from the practice squad. At the time, it looked like a minor move. On Sunday, he was the backup to Ramsey in the slot. That’s another example of how quietly the front office has been building layers of depth in this secondary.

How the surprise move paid off.

And did it pay off? Look at the numbers. Dean, in his first game back, had six passes defended and the biggest defensive play of the day, a 40-yard interception return to the Tampa 3-yard line late in the fourth quarter, which set up Chris Boswell’s go-ahead field goal. Nick Herbig said it was “like a storybook ending for that to happen in your own place that drafted you.”

Everette came up with the first interception of his NFL career, picking off Jalon Daniels after Cam Heyward pressured him. That turnover helped spark the fourth-quarter comeback from 16-3 down.

And the secondary as a whole held Tampa to 164 passing yards, with Daniels completing just 22 of 43 passes. The Bucs finished with 224 total yards and went 6-for-19 on third down. Then Ramsey, the veteran playing through a broken wrist, ended the game with a blitz sack on fourth-and-8 in overtime.

So the young corners and the healthy veterans delivered. And the corners Khan claimed stayed ready as insurance. That’s exactly how depth is supposed to work. Not every move a general manager makes has to show up on Sunday. Sometimes the best moves are the ones that let your coaches sleep at night, knowing that if something goes wrong, there’s a capable veteran waiting. And with a hamstring injury hitting the secondary in the very same game, that insurance suddenly looks a lot more valuable.

The other pregame moves.

And the cornerback decision wasn’t the only move the Steelers made before kickoff. On Saturday, Roman Wilson went on injured reserve with his finger injury, which means his earliest possible return is Week 11 against Philadelphia. Running back Lew Nichols was signed to the 53-man roster because he’d used up all three of his practice squad elevations. And receivers Brandon Smith and Brandon Johnson were elevated for the game. Ben Skowronek was inactive with his calf injury. So the Steelers went into Tampa with a receiver room built around Metcalf, Bernard, and two practice squad call-ups.

Put all of that together, and you see a coaching staff making very specific bets. Trust the young cornerbacks. Trust the rookie receiver. Lean on the veterans who know the system. Keep the newcomers ready as insurance. For three quarters, those bets looked shaky. By overtime, every one of them had paid off.

The injury that complicates everything.

But here’s the concern. During Tampa’s final punt of the first half, Brandin Echols, who was working as a jammer on the punt return unit, got hurt on the play. Steelers Depot reported it as a hamstring injury, and he was ruled out for the rest of the game.

Now, Echols isn’t a household name, but he matters to this roster. According to Steelers Now, he’s the team’s backup slot cornerback, plays mostly on special teams and as the dime corner, and had appeared in all six games this season with four tackles. He came to Pittsburgh from the New York Jets, who drafted him in the sixth round out of Kentucky in 2021. Last year in New York, he played 16 games with three starts, two interceptions, a forced fumble, a fumble recovery, six passes defended, and 48 tackles. He’s the kind of player who doesn’t make headlines but keeps the special teams and sub-packages running.

There’s no official word yet on how long he’ll be out. Hamstring injuries can range from a week to much longer, so we’ll know more after McCarthy’s Monday update and this week’s practice reports. But if Echols misses time, the decision Pittsburgh made on Sunday could flip. Murphy-Bunting or Smith could move into the active lineup, and Everette and Slaughter could see an even bigger role.

And there were a few other bumps and bruises. Jaquan Brisker was briefly sidelined with a groin injury, but he came back and finished with seven tackles. Pat Freiermuth, who came into the game with a calf issue, limped at one point but waved off the trainers and finished. And McCarthy himself had his foot stepped on before halftime and spent the break in the medical tent, though he said he’d be fine.

Rodgers’ message about Metcalf.

Now let’s talk about the offense, because Rodgers said something after the game that got a lot of attention.

For three quarters, Pittsburgh’s offense was stuck. According to Steelers Nation, the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters. DK Metcalf had only two catches for 11 yards in the first half. The week before, against the Colts, he had just three catches for 12 yards.

Then the fourth quarter came, and Metcalf took over. He finished with 11 catches for 93 yards, his highest catch total of the season. And in overtime, he made one of the biggest plays of the game, catching a pass on third-and-13 that kept the winning drive alive. Without that catch, Germie Bernard never scores the game-winning touchdown.

After the game, Rodgers said: “Once we got DK involved, the offense just shifted. So we can get that going a little earlier next time.”

Let me be clear about what that is and what it isn’t. That’s not Rodgers attacking Metcalf. And it’s not Rodgers publicly blaming McCarthy. It’s a 42-year-old quarterback who’s seen everything telling his coaches and his teammates exactly what he saw: when the offense runs through its best receiver, it works. And waiting until the fourth quarter to figure that out nearly cost the Steelers the game.

Why getting Metcalf going early matters.

Here’s my take. Getting Metcalf involved doesn’t mean forcing him the ball 15 times a game. Defenses will keep rolling coverage toward him, especially with Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve. But it does mean designing ways to get him easy touches early. Quick throws. Crossing routes. Motion to keep him from getting pressed at the line. Plays that let him catch the ball on the move and use his size and speed after the catch.

And when that happens, it opens everything else up. That’s exactly what we saw in the fourth quarter and overtime. Once Metcalf got going, Bernard found space too. The rookie also finished with 11 catches, for 87 yards and the game-winning touchdown. Together, Metcalf and Bernard accounted for 22 of Rodgers’ 33 completions.

Think about what that means. If defenses double Metcalf, Bernard is getting single coverage. If they start paying attention to Bernard, Metcalf gets more room. That’s the kind of two-receiver combination that can carry the passing game until Pittman comes back.

And Rodgers himself deserves credit for how he handled it. He finished 33 of 49 for 253 yards, two touchdowns, and one interception, despite being sacked five times. His overtime touchdown to Bernard was his 539th regular-season touchdown pass, tying Peyton Manning for third in NFL history, and it was his seventh career overtime win. When a quarterback with that resume says the offense needs to get its best receiver going earlier, coaches listen. And honestly, it’s the kind of honest, constructive message you want from a veteran leader after a win, not after a loss.

Bernard’s rise also gives McCarthy more options in designing those early touches. The rookie has now caught 17 passes in his first two career starts, with a touchdown. Rodgers said after the game that Bernard’s “confidence starts to match the potential.” If both receivers are confident and involved from the first drive, the Steelers won’t need a miracle comeback to win.

The lesson from the comeback.

But let’s be honest about the bigger picture too. Pittsburgh didn’t score a touchdown until early in the fourth quarter, when Rodgers hit Darnell Washington from five yards out to make it 16-10. Against a team that finishes drives better than Tampa did, a 16-3 deficit might have been too much to overcome.

The defense deserves enormous credit for keeping the Steelers within reach. Tampa’s only touchdown came on an eight-play, 60-yard drive early in the second quarter, and the rest of its points came on field goals. But a defense can only carry a team for so long. And if the secondary is getting thinner with the Echols injury, it becomes even more important for the offense to start fast and stop putting the defense in tough spots.

That’s the message for McCarthy and his staff heading into Paris: the comeback was great, but the goal is to never need one. Fast starts win road games. Slow starts turn every road game into a coin flip.

The defense is still learning, and that’s good news.

And there’s one more piece of context I want to share. Remember, this is Patrick Graham’s first season running Pittsburgh’s defense. Some concepts look familiar to veterans like Nick Herbig, a 2023 fourth-round pick now in his fourth NFL season, but the details are new, and players and coaches are still adjusting to each other.

That matters because a lot of the mistakes this defense has made this season have been communication issues, not talent issues. Two players see the same formation and expect different responsibilities, and suddenly a receiver is wide open. The encouraging part is that those mistakes are shrinking. Against the Colts, the defense held Indianapolis to 4-of-15 on third down and didn’t allow a passing touchdown. Against Tampa, it held the Bucs to 6-of-19 on third down and forced two turnovers. Herbig himself had two sacks in Tampa. When the communication is clean, this defense is dangerous.

And that’s why the Echols injury and the secondary depth decisions matter so much. Every new face in the lineup is another player who has to be on the same page as everyone else. Keeping the rotation stable is part of what’s helping this defense grow.

Why this matters more in Paris.

There’s another reason the depth question matters this week. International games come with long travel, different routines, and time zone changes. Teams that travel overseas often lean on their depth more than usual, because players can feel the effects of the trip. If Echols can’t go, and if anyone else picks up a minor injury, having Murphy-Bunting and Smith ready to step in could be the difference. Khan built this roster for exactly this kind of situation.

What happens next.

So what should we expect this week? Here’s my opinion, not a report.

If Echols misses time, I think we’ll see Murphy-Bunting or Smith activated for Paris, likely Murphy-Bunting because of his experience. That would be the payoff of Khan’s claims from two weeks ago: depth that was ready when it was needed.

DeShon Elliott is another name to watch. He’s been practicing on his 21-day window off injured reserve, and he wasn’t activated for Tampa. If he’s cleared this week, the secondary gets a major boost in leadership and communication right when it needs it.

And at receiver, the trade deadline question doesn’t go away. Bernard and Metcalf were terrific in Tampa, but with Pittman and Wilson out until after the bye at the earliest, Khan could still look for a bridge veteran before November 10th. Jauan Jennings of the Vikings, who Pro Football Talk reported could be traded after Minnesota added Odell Beckham Jr. to its practice squad, remains the name I like most. There’s no report linking him to Pittsburgh. And Darius Slayton is still available as a free agent after the Colts released him, though his knee is a question.

And if Khan wants a bigger swing, the options are still out there. NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala floated the idea of a rare AFC North trade for Browns receiver Jerry Jeudy, calling him “just an odd man out” in Cleveland. And Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline. None of that is a done deal. But after a win like this, Khan can shop from a position of strength instead of desperation.

Next up: the Saints in Paris.

The Steelers are 4-2, they’ve won two straight, and they’ve finally won on the road. Now they head to Paris to face the New Orleans Saints in Week 7. It’s a big stage, a long trip, and a chance to prove that this team’s momentum is real.

Here’s what I want to see. Get Metcalf involved in the first quarter, not the fourth. Let Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle carry more of the load than the 18 carries they had in Tampa. Keep the defense healthy and communicating. And make the depth decisions in the secondary work, whether that’s Echols coming back or Murphy-Bunting stepping in.

The bottom line.

The surprise decision before the Tampa game was to trust the corners already in the building and keep Khan’s newest additions as insurance. It worked. Dean and Everette each came up with interceptions, Ramsey ended the game with a sack, and the defense carried the Steelers until Rodgers and Metcalf woke up in the fourth quarter.

Now the challenge is to start faster, protect a secondary that just lost another body, and turn a thrilling comeback into a consistent winning formula. If Rodgers gets his wish and Metcalf is involved early next week, this offense could be a lot harder to stop. And with the depth Khan has built, this team is ready for whatever Paris brings.
