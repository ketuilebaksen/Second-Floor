DeShon Elliott’s practice window is open. Omar Khan added two cornerbacks this week. And there’s one more name I want to talk about today, a name Steelers fans already know very well, a corner who picked off five passes in black and gold just two seasons ago: Donte Jackson. So today, we’re going to do two things. First, I’ll lay out exactly how Pittsburgh beats Indianapolis tomorrow with the roster it has. And second, I’ll make my case for why a Donte Jackson reunion before the November 10th deadline could be the move that turns this secondary from a problem into a strength. This is James with Second Floor, let’s get into it.

As of right now, there is no report that the Steelers have talked to the Chargers about Donte Jackson. He’s under contract in Los Angeles, and he’s actually been ruled out of the Chargers’ game this week with a hamstring injury, after leaving their game against Buffalo in late September with a concussion. So he is not going to be in Pittsburgh for the Colts game. What I’m going to give you is my opinion on why he should be on Khan’s list as the deadline gets closer. Opinion, not a report. Got it? Good.

Part one: the reinforcements, and why this team is getting stronger.

Let’s start with what’s real right now, because the injury report on Friday told us a lot.

Jalen Ramsey is listed as questionable with the broken wrist, but he was limited on Thursday and Friday after sitting out Wednesday, and everything points to him playing. That’s huge. Ramsey has been the movable piece in Patrick Graham’s defense all season, sliding between the slot and safety, and he played through that same injury in Cleveland four days after breaking his wrist against Cincinnati, missing only one snap.

Rico Dowdle is questionable too, after being limited in practice all week. He’s missed the last two games after dislocating his toe against New England. If he goes, Pittsburgh finally has a real one-two punch with Jaylen Warren. If he doesn’t, Warren carries the load again, and honestly, after 17 carries for 93 yards in Cleveland, Warren deserves the ball even more.

Jamel Dean is out for Sunday with his ankle, but here’s the encouraging part: he’s targeting a return for Week 6 in Tampa Bay, against the team he spent his whole career with before coming to Pittsburgh. And DeShon Elliott, who’s been on injured reserve, has had his 21-day practice window opened. He’s out Sunday and the team is giving him extra time, with a reported target of Week 7, the Saints game in Paris. But think about what this secondary looks like in two or three weeks: Dean back outside, Elliott back at safety, Ramsey healthier, and the new guys with a month of practice in the system. That’s a very different group from the one that gave up 21 points in a quarter in Cleveland.

And that’s before we even talk about what Omar Khan did this week. On Monday, he signed Sean Murphy-Bunting off Tampa Bay’s practice squad. On Tuesday, he signed Terell Smith off Washington’s practice squad. Both are outside corners with size. Murphy-Bunting has 65 career starts and a Super Bowl ring. Smith had an interception against Jordan Love in 2024 before a patellar tendon injury wiped out his 2025. They’re depth today. They could be real contributors by the end of the month.

Up front, there’s good news too. Cameron Heyward took a rest day on Wednesday, then practiced fully Thursday and Friday, and he carries no injury designation. After the night he had in Cleveland, seven tackles and the highest Pro Football Focus grade on the defense, having him fresh against Jonathan Taylor is a big deal. With Harmon on injured reserve, Heyward is the anchor that holds that interior together.

The one big loss is Michael Pittman Jr. He’s out with a re-aggravated foot injury, and McCarthy said he’ll miss “multiple weeks.” That means Roman Wilson, rookie Germie Bernard and Ben Skowronek all get more work. Wilson, by the way, was Pro Football Focus’s top-graded player on the offense in Cleveland, with three catches for 74 yards and a touchdown. He’s ready for a bigger role.

Part two: why Donte Jackson makes sense.

Now let’s talk about the name in the title, and let me explain why I think a reunion makes so much sense, as an opinion.

Start with the history. Pittsburgh traded Diontae Johnson to Carolina for Jackson back in March of 2024. And Jackson delivered. In 15 games, all starts, he picked off five passes and broke up eight more. He played outside corner, he made plays on the ball, and he fit right in. Then he hit free agency and signed a two-year, 13 million dollar deal with the Chargers. In 2025, he kept producing: 17 games, 15 starts, four interceptions and 12 passes defensed.

So here’s the first reason he fits. He’s a true outside corner, and that’s exactly what Pittsburgh is missing. Remember, Jalen Ramsey has played only three snaps at outside corner this entire season, according to charting cited this week. He’s a slot and safety piece now. With Joey Porter Jr. in Dallas and Dean banged up, the Steelers need someone who can line up on the boundary, snap after snap, against a team’s best outside receiver. That’s Donte Jackson’s whole career.

And think about what that 2024 secondary looked like. Jackson on one side, Joey Porter Jr. on the other. That was the outside pairing. Porter is gone now, and the Steelers haven’t found a replacement who can handle that boundary job every snap. Bringing Jackson back would literally put one of those two original pieces back in place.

Second reason: the contract is small and short. This is the final year of his deal with the Chargers. His base salary this season is about six million dollars, and his cap hit is around 8.5 million. By the trade deadline, a big chunk of that salary would already be paid, so whoever takes him on is only responsible for the rest of the year. For a team trying to win now without wrecking next year’s cap, that’s ideal. No long-term commitment. No massive guarantee. Just a veteran corner for a stretch run.

Third reason: the ball skills. Five interceptions in 2024, four in 2025. That’s nine picks in two seasons. Pittsburgh’s defense lives on takeaways. Coming into the Cleveland game, the Steelers already had six takeaways in three games, and they added two more on Thursday night. A corner who consistently gets his hands on the ball fits this defense’s identity perfectly.

Fourth reason: he knows Pittsburgh. He’s played in that building. He’s played in this division. He’s been in that locker room with Cam Heyward and T.J. Watt. That doesn’t replace knowing Patrick Graham’s system, but it does shorten the adjustment period, which matters a lot when you’re trying to plug a hole in the middle of a season.

And compare him with the other options people keep throwing around. Trevon Diggs is free to sign off Seattle’s practice squad, but he’s post-ACL, coming off a down 2025, and Pittsburgh no longer has an open roster spot after signing Terell Smith, so they’d have to clear room. Houston’s Kamari Lassiter is younger and better long term, but analysts have him valued around a first-round pick, and he’ll want a huge extension next year, the same contract fight that sent Porter to Dallas. Jackson sits right in the middle. He’s proven, he’s an outside corner, he’s cheap for the rest of this season, and he walks off the books after it. For a team trying to win with a 42-year-old quarterback on his final ride, that middle lane might be the smartest lane.

And the roster math can work too. If Pittman goes on injured reserve, which the team is still evaluating, that opens a spot on the 53. And once Dean and Elliott return, Pittsburgh can shuffle the bottom of the roster. There’s a path if Khan wants it.

Now, the honest risks, because you always get both sides from me. Jackson turns 31 next month. He’s dealing with a hamstring injury right now, and he had a concussion in late September. The Chargers would have to be willing to move him, and nobody has reported that they are. And he’s never played in Graham’s defense, since Graham and Mike McCarthy both arrived after Jackson left. So this isn’t a guaranteed home run. But if the Chargers are open to it, and the price is a late-round pick, I’d make that call before November 10th. Of all the outside corners Pittsburgh could realistically get, Jackson might be the one with the lowest risk and the most familiarity.

So what would it cost? Here’s my number, and this is purely opinion. A late-round pick, a sixth or seventh, and maybe a swap of late picks to balance out the remaining salary. The Chargers wouldn’t be giving away a starter for nothing, but a 31-year-old corner in the final year of his deal, coming off a hamstring injury, isn’t going to command a premium either. That’s a price Khan can pay without blinking, and it’s exactly the kind of practical deal he’s made all week.

And there’s a nice storyline waiting for Dean next week. If he does make it back in Tampa, he’ll be lining up against the Buccaneers, the team that drafted him in 2019 and where he spent his whole career before signing with Pittsburgh. Sean Murphy-Bunting was in that same Bucs draft class, and the two of them won a Super Bowl together there. Two former Bucs on the outside in Tampa would be a fun subplot, and a sign that this secondary is finally getting healthy.

Part three: how Pittsburgh beats the Colts tomorrow.

Okay. Let’s get to Sunday, because no trade matters if the Steelers don’t take care of business at Acrisure Stadium. Both teams come in at 2-2, and Indianapolis has won two straight, including a 30-13 win over Washington last week. But the Colts have something hanging over them in this building: they haven’t won in Pittsburgh since November 9th, 2008. And on T.J. Watt’s birthday, with this crowd, I don’t want that streak ending tomorrow.

On offense, the blueprint is simple, and we’ve already seen it work. Go back to the Cincinnati game. Pittsburgh won 30-27 by playing physical, balanced football. They ran the ball, they used short passes, they mixed in screens, and they didn’t make every possession depend on a difficult throw. Then in Cleveland, they got away from it. Rodgers threw 40 times, took five sacks, and the offense managed only 101 yards over six possessions after the opening drive. That can’t happen again.

So here’s what I want to see. Warren early and often. And specifically, I’d like to see more of Warren running toward the edges instead of constantly pushing into a crowded middle. The Colts have real size inside with DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart. Running straight into those two is how you end up in second-and-nine. Getting Warren outside, forcing linebackers to run sideways, testing their tackling in space: that’s where he’s dangerous. And if Dowdle can go, he’s the one who can grind out the tough inside yards.

The offensive line has to make it work. Zach Frazier at center, Mason McCormick, Troy Fautanu, and the revamped right side with Brock Hoffman at guard and rookie Max Iheanachor at tackle. Add Darnell Washington as a sixth blocker on early downs, and you’ve got a front that can move people. When that group is rolling, Rodgers gets play action, and play action is where he’s most dangerous.

And when the Colts start crowding the line to stop Warren, that’s when you hit them with screens and quick throws. Rodgers doesn’t have to hold the ball. Get it out fast to Wilson, to Freiermuth, to Bernard on a designed route, and let them create after the catch. That also takes care of the pass rush. Indianapolis will be without defensive end Arden Key, who’s out with a hamstring injury, and Buckner sat out practice for rest. If the Colts can’t generate pressure with four, Pittsburgh should be able to stay on schedule all afternoon.

The Colts’ secondary has a question mark of its own, too. Cornerback Charvarius Ward Sr. is questionable with a groin issue. If he’s limited or out, that’s an opportunity for DK Metcalf, who had five catches for 115 yards in Cleveland. Pittsburgh should test the outside early, with Metcalf on the boundary and Wilson working underneath, and find out quickly whether Indianapolis is healthy back there.

Part four: the defense, and the turnover opportunity.

On defense, this game is about two names: Jonathan Taylor and Daniel Jones.

Taylor is the engine. Against Washington last week, he ran 20 times for 95 yards and two touchdowns. With Derrick Harmon on injured reserve, Keeanu Benton and Yahya Black have bigger jobs in the middle, and the linebackers, Patrick Queen and Payton Wilson, have to tackle better than they did in Cleveland. The Steelers missed 14 tackles against the Browns. Do that against Taylor, and he’ll run all over you and keep the Steelers’ offense on the sideline.

But here’s the opportunity. Daniel Jones has been careless with the football. According to Colts coverage, he has seven turnovers through four games, including fumbles that were described as avoidable ball-security lapses. And against Washington, he threw for just 143 yards on 19-of-34 passing with an interception. Pittsburgh’s defense is built to take the ball away. If the Steelers can stop Taylor on early downs and force Jones into third-and-long, the turnovers will come.

The key is how they rush. Watt is going to get his, but the rest of the front has to help. Nick Herbig, Alex Highsmith and Benton need to collapse the pocket together, not just pin their ears back and leave escape lanes. Jones can hurt you with his legs if you lose contain. Keep him in a shrinking pocket, make him hold the ball, and let the mistakes come to you.

In the red zone, watch for Jones on quarterback sneaks and for tight end Tyler Warren. Pittsburgh can’t assume every goal-line snap is a Taylor run. And on the outside, the Colts’ receiving group is banged up too: Keenan Allen is questionable, Darius Slayton is questionable with a knee, and Ashton Dulin is out. That’s good news for a Steelers secondary that’ll be starting Asante Samuel Jr. and rookie Daylen Everette outside, with Murphy-Bunting and Smith ready behind them, and Robert Spears-Jennings likely getting more snaps at safety.

Here are my five keys for Sunday. One: Warren gets at least 22 touches, with plenty of runs outside the tackles. Two: Rodgers gets the ball out quickly, and the offense lives in manageable second and third downs. Three: tackle Jonathan Taylor. No more 14 missed tackles. Four: win the turnover battle, because Jones has given the ball away seven times in four games and this defense knows how to take it. And five: protect the young and new corners with pressure, so Everette, Murphy-Bunting and Smith never have to cover for too long. Hit four of those five, and I think Pittsburgh wins comfortably.

And look at what a win sets up. At 3-2, the Steelers head to Tampa with Dean likely back, then to Paris with Elliott likely back, then host the Browns before the bye. That’s a stretch where a healthier, deeper team can stack wins and turn the trade deadline into a buying opportunity instead of a panic. A loss, and every one of those reinforcements arrives to a 2-3 team facing a lot more pressure. Sunday sets the tone for the whole month.

So let’s put it together. The Steelers are getting stronger in waves. This week: Ramsey through the wrist, maybe Dowdle, and two new corners in the building. Next week: hopefully Dean back in Tampa. The week after: Elliott, in Paris. And if Khan wants one more piece before the November 10th deadline, a familiar face like Donte Jackson would give this secondary the true outside corner it’s been missing since Porter left.

But none of that matters if Pittsburgh doesn’t win tomorrow. Beat the Colts, and the Steelers are 3-2 heading to Tampa with reinforcements coming. Lose, and it’s 2-3 with a lot of tough questions. The plan is clear: run the ball with patience, protect Rodgers with a quick game, stop Taylor, take the ball away from Jones, and give T.J. Watt the birthday present he deserves. Do that, and this team won’t just be getting stronger. It’ll be getting dangerous.
