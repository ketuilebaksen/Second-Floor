Steelers Nation, the biggest game of the season so far is three days away, and everybody wants to know one thing. What weapon did Pittsburgh add for Cleveland?

Let me give you the honest answer first, because that’s how we do it on Second Floor. As of Thursday, October 29, there is no blockbuster trade. There is no star receiver walking through the door at the facility. Nobody has been acquired, nobody has been signed off the street to the active roster this week, and if you saw a headline telling you otherwise, it got ahead of itself.

But here’s the twist. Pittsburgh doesn’t need a new name to walk into Sunday better armed than it was in Paris. Reinforcements are trickling back into practice. A veteran receiver with a long history alongside the quarterback is one call-up away from the field. A roster spot is sitting open. And the most dangerous weapon on this team right now isn’t a receiver at all. It’s a style of football that just mauled two opponents in a row.

I’m James, this is Second Floor, and today we’re running a full weapons check before Browns week. Four real weapons, one transcript we have to clean up, a scouting report on a Cleveland team tied with us at the top, and a trade-block segment where I give you my opinion on who Omar Khan should call before November 10. Let’s go.

THE STAKES: WINNER TAKES THE NORTH

Start with what’s on the line, because it explains everything else. Pittsburgh is 5-2. Cleveland is 5-2. Kickoff is Sunday, November 1, at 1 p.m. at Acrisure Stadium on CBS, and whoever wins walks out of the building alone in first place in the AFC North.

And the Browns have the receipt from the first meeting. Week 4, Thursday night in Cleveland, 27-24, a long field goal in the final seconds, and a Pittsburgh loss that still stings. Mike McCarthy didn’t hide from it on Monday. He said, quote, “We need to beat them. They got us once already, and it’s a rivalry game for us.” He also gave Cleveland its due: “They’re playing good football.”

Here’s why this one carries extra weight. Cleveland is already 2-0 inside the division. Pittsburgh is 1-1. Lose Sunday, and the Browns own the head-to-head sweep plus a division-record edge that could haunt this team in January. Win Sunday, and the series is split, the division records are dead even, and Pittsburgh heads into the bye week as the team everybody else is chasing.

Still Curtain also pointed out a piece of history worth knowing. According to their tracker, a Browns sweep of the Steelers would be Cleveland’s first since 1988. I haven’t independently confirmed that note, so take it as their research, but if it’s right, you understand why this building is going to be loud.

SETTING THE RECORD STRAIGHT ON THE TRANSCRIPT

Before the weapons check, I have to clean something up, because the video that’s been circulating with this exact theme mashes together old news and new news, and some of it is just wrong.

First, the record. One version says Pittsburgh “has scored 52 points” after beating New Orleans. That’s a garbled reading of the record. The Steelers are 5-2. They beat the Saints 16-10 in Paris, at the Stade de France, not against “Paris St. Germain.” That’s a soccer club. Our guys played New Orleans.

Second, and this is the big one. That same transcript resurfaces an old CBS Sports idea about shipping Joey Porter Jr. to Detroit, and it calls Porter a quarterback. Porter is a cornerback. And more importantly, he isn’t even on the team anymore. Pittsburgh traded Porter to the Dallas Cowboys back in early October for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth. The talk about him “missing the first two games” and being “close to returning in Week 3” comes from September. That ship has sailed, docked in Dallas, and unloaded.

Third, the dream package in that old column had Porter plus DK Metcalf going to Detroit for Jameson Williams and a first-round pick. Even setting aside that Porter is gone, Metcalf’s contract was restructured in August, which makes moving him a lot more painful on the cap than people assume. And Metcalf just caught a 51-yard bomb on third-and-14 to set up the only touchdown in Paris. He isn’t going anywhere.

Fourth, a couple of names. It’s Kaden Wetjen, the rookie out of Iowa, and the receiver the transcript calls “Jeremy Bernard” is Germie Bernard, the second-round pick from Alabama. And Brandon Smith isn’t on the active roster right now. More on that in a minute, because that detail is actually one of our four weapons.

Okay. Record straightened. Let’s arm up.

WEAPON NUMBER ONE: BEN SKOWRONEK IS BACK ON THE PRACTICE FIELD

The first real addition this week isn’t a new face. It’s a familiar one returning. Ben Skowronek was listed as a limited participant on Wednesday’s injury report with his calf.

Why does that matter? Because Skowronek has been out since he got hurt covering a punt in the Colts game. He missed Tampa. He missed Paris. And with him out, this offense has been leaning on a skeleton crew at receiver: Metcalf, Bernard, Wetjen, plus practice-squad call-ups.

Now, limited is not full. One limited practice doesn’t put him on the field Sunday, and we’ll know a lot more when the Friday report drops. But here’s what Skowronek brings that the box score never shows. He is a special-teams core player, he is a willing blocker on the perimeter, and in a run-first offense that has been hammering people with heavy personnel, a receiver who will put his face in a linebacker’s chest is worth more than a guy who catches four passes for 40 yards.

If he gets cleared, it’s a quiet upgrade in three phases at once. Coverage units get their leader back. The perimeter blocking on those Jaylen Warren outside runs gets a little nastier. And Rodgers gets one more trusted body in the huddle.

WEAPON NUMBER TWO: MARQUEZ VALDES-SCANTLING, ONE CALL-UP AWAY

The second weapon has been sitting right there on the practice squad since October 20. Marquez Valdes-Scantling.

Quick refresher on who he is. Fifth-round pick of the Packers in 2018, McCarthy’s final year in Green Bay. Four seasons catching passes from Aaron Rodgers. Two Super Bowl rings with Kansas City. Nine NFL seasons, more than 3,700 career receiving yards, and a reputation as a vertical threat who makes safeties respect the deep half of the field. He was with the Chargers and Dallas earlier this year before Pittsburgh brought him back to the practice squad.

He was not elevated for Paris. But Rodgers clearly wants him involved. Steelers Depot’s headline this week quoted the quarterback saying, “It’d be nice to get him up.” That’s a quarterback telling you, politely, that he’d like his old deep threat available for a division game.

Think about what that does schematically. The Steelers just won in Paris with 30 carries and an ultra-jumbo package that put an extra lineman on the field. Defenses are going to start loading the box. And the best punishment for a defense that crowds the line is a receiver who can run past everybody. Even if MVS plays fifteen snaps, the threat of a shot play off play-action changes how Cleveland’s safeties line up. That’s how you make the run game even better without changing a single blocking assignment.

The catch? Getting him up costs either an elevation or a roster spot. Which brings us to weapon number three.

WEAPON NUMBER THREE: THE OPEN ROSTER SPOT AND THE BRANDON SMITH DECISION

This is the one nobody’s talking about, and it might be the most interesting roster decision of the week.

Brandon Smith reverted to the practice squad on Monday, October 26. Against the Saints, he played 29 offensive snaps and caught both of his targets for 24 yards. Not flashy, but clean and reliable. Here’s the problem. Smith has used all three of his practice-squad elevations for the season, in Weeks 5, 6, and 7. Under NFL rules, he can’t be elevated again. If Pittsburgh wants him on the field Sunday, it has to sign him to the 53-man roster.

And after DeShon Elliott was activated off injured reserve for Paris, by my count there’s room to make a move. If the Steelers don’t need that spot for an injury replacement, they could use it to lock Smith in, and then use a fresh elevation on Valdes-Scantling. That’s two receivers added to the game-day group without a single trade, without giving up a single draft pick.

Is that guaranteed to happen? No. That’s my read of the roster math, not a report. But if you see a transaction on Saturday afternoon signing Brandon Smith to the active roster, now you know exactly why. That’s the “weapon” move hiding in plain sight.

WEAPON NUMBER FOUR: THE GROUND GAME ITSELF

And now the real weapon. The one Cleveland actually has to solve.

Over this three-game winning streak, Pittsburgh has outrushed its opponents 364 yards to 186, according to Still Curtain’s tracker. In Paris, it was 30 carries for 148 yards against a Saints defense that managed just 18 rushing yards on 17 attempts of its own. Jaylen Warren went 18 carries for 101 yards at 5.6 a pop. Rico Dowdle added 41 more. And the Steelers closed the game with an 11-play, 5:52 drive where every single snap was a run.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler described what Pittsburgh is doing as “creating an identity with star running back Jaylen Warren.” He also had a criticism, saying the heavy-personnel approach isn’t producing much offensively, and that he sees this as a nine or ten-win playoff team that is “lacking juice beyond that.” Fair enough. The red zone was a problem in Paris. McCarthy himself said one-for-four down there was “not where you want to be clearly.”

But here’s my counterpoint. The Browns didn’t see this offense in Week 4. That night, Pittsburgh threw the ball on a huge share of its snaps, Rodgers took five sacks, and Warren got just 20 touches in a game where he averaged well over five yards a carry. The Steelers that Cleveland game-planned for a month ago don’t exist anymore. Spencer Anderson as an extra blocker, Warren and Dowdle in rotation, Darnell Washington as a sixth lineman with hands, that’s a different animal. Anderson put it perfectly after Paris: “We’re going to run it again, again, and again.”

And watch the Browns’ injury report on the defensive line, because that’s where this weapon gets its edge.

SCOUTING THE BROWNS: HEALTH, HEADLINES, AND A REAL DEBATE

Cleveland’s Wednesday injury report was long. Jared Verse did not practice with a knee. Offensive tackle Tytus Howard did not practice with a concussion. Center Elgton Jenkins did not practice with a knee. And a long list of defenders were limited: safety Grant Delpit with a neck, defensive tackles Mason Graham and Mike Hall Jr. with ankles, linebacker Carson Schwesinger with an ankle, safety Daniel Thomas with an ankle, and corner Myles Harden with a hamstring. Running back Dylan Sampson was limited with a knee too.

Wednesday reports are early, and a lot of limited guys play on Sunday. But if Verse can’t go, and if Graham and Hall are playing banged up, the interior of that Browns front is softer than it was in Week 4. That’s exactly the matchup you want for a team that just decided to become a downhill rushing offense. And if Howard and Jenkins are out on the other side, T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith, and Nick Herbig get a reshuffled offensive line to attack.

Now, how good are the Browns really? This is a genuine debate, and the ESPN crew split right down the middle. Fowler called Cleveland’s start “no fluke,” pointing to young talent, Deshaun Watson’s improved play, and a soft schedule. Dan Graziano went the other way. He noted Watson ranks 27th in QBR, that the Browns have actually allowed 13 more points than they’ve scored, and he called the start “a mirage.” He also didn’t spare Pittsburgh, saying, “I don’t believe the Browns or Steelers are as good as their records indicate.”

I’ll take that as motivation. Records are what they are. Both teams are 5-2. One of them won’t be after Sunday.

One more number for the gamblers and the nerds. Covers reported the Steelers opened as 3.5-point favorites, and the line moved toward Cleveland, with the author noting +2.5 by the time he wrote. He also flagged a trend worth respecting: teams playing the week after a trip to Europe, without a bye in between, were 0-5 against the spread this year. Pittsburgh just flew home from Paris. That’s a real factor, and it’s the reason I care so much about this team’s legs in the fourth quarter.

OUR OWN INJURY REPORT: THE SECONDARY IS THE WORRY

Let’s be honest about our side too. Wednesday’s report had three defensive backs not practicing: Jalen Ramsey with his wrist, Brandin Echols with his hamstring, and Doneiko Slaughter, also with a hamstring. Highsmith was limited with his groin. And Watt, Cam Heyward, and Darnell Washington got veteran rest days, which is normal and not a concern.

Ramsey has been playing through a broken wrist since Week 3, and he has missed Wednesday practices before and played on Sunday, so I’m not panicking there. But Echols has now missed several weeks, and Slaughter popping up is new. When three DBs miss the same practice, depth gets tested fast. That’s where DeShon Elliott’s return matters. McCarthy said about Elliott on Wednesday, “He looks good,” though his workload for Cleveland hasn’t been set.

Now the receivers on injured reserve. The two names to track are Michael Pittman Jr. with his foot and Roman Wilson with his finger. Neither one appeared on Wednesday’s practice report, which tells me their windows to return haven’t been opened yet. Pittman went on IR before the Colts game, so the bye week lines up nicely with the earliest point he could be back. Wilson’s earliest return lands a bit later.

And McCarthy liked the vibe. On Wednesday he said, “I feel like the energy has been very good so far today,” and added, “We’re off to a good start.” Coming off an overnight flight from France, that’s what you want to hear.

WHAT THE INSIDERS ARE SAYING ABOUT A TRADE

Now, the question everybody keeps asking me in the comments. Is Omar Khan about to make a move?

Here’s what the reporting actually says. Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette was asked about it this week, and he was blunt. He said, “They are not adding talent on the outside.” And then: “I don’t expect that. Unless there is a significant injury.” His reasoning: “They are getting their 3 WRs back and the secondary isn’t as bad as people seem to think.”

So the most plugged-in local voice is telling you to pump the brakes. When he says three receivers coming back, that most likely means Skowronek, Pittman, and Wilson, which matches the timelines we just walked through.

On the other side, there’s Josh Downs. Jordan Schultz reported that multiple AFC teams, including Pittsburgh, have interest in the Colts receiver. ESPN’s Fowler said Downs “has emerged as a hot name,” that he’d likely be open to a new team, and Graziano noted Downs is on an expiring contract after not getting an extension this offseason. But Fowler also flagged that Indianapolis is already thin at receiver and that moving Downs “would severely limit the offense,” and he named Kansas City as a natural fit.

And on Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, Mark Kaboly confirmed there was interest at one point. His words: “They were, they were. I really haven’t heard anything recently about it.” That’s not a live trade talk. That’s history.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, this is the part where I take off the reporter hat. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report. Nobody told me any of this is happening.

Target one, Josh Downs. I keep coming back to him because he fits this offense like a glove. He’s 25. He’s a slot receiver who wins quickly, which is exactly the kind of option Rodgers needs on third-and-six when the defense knows a run isn’t coming. He’s on an expiring deal, so he’s a rental unless you extend him. My price? A mid-round pick, something in the Day 3 range, and I would not touch the 2028 second-rounder Dallas sent us for Porter. If the Colts want more than that, walk away. Pittsburgh is winning without him.

Target two, Brian Thomas Jr. The upside is huge. A Pro Bowl rookie year with 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. But Kaboly hasn’t heard anything recently, and Jacksonville has publicly pushed back on trade talk all year. Fowler called him his top pick to be traded, which tells you the market thinks something could shake loose. I’d make one phone call. If the Jaguars ask for a first, hang up politely.

Target three, the name from that old transcript: Jameson Williams. Let me be crystal clear. There is no report linking Pittsburgh to Williams. He signed an extension with Detroit in September of 2025 worth up to $83 million over three years, not the giant number some videos throw around. He’s had back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons. He’s a legit deep threat. But his contract changes the math completely, and the Lions are trying to win now. For me, this is a fantasy-football trade, not a real one.

And honestly, my favorite option is the cheapest one. Sign Brandon Smith to the 53, elevate MVS, wait for Skowronek and Pittman, and keep every draft pick. Dulac’s read and my read match: if this team is healthy, it doesn’t need a rescue.

FOUR KEYS TO BEATING CLEVELAND

Let me give you the game plan in four lines.

One. Run it until they prove they can stop it. The Browns’ interior defensive line is banged up. Warren and Dowdle should get 30-plus carries again, and that controls the clock and keeps Watson on the sideline.

Two. Protect Rodgers on third down. In Week 4 he took five sacks. This time, quicker throws, more chip help from Washington and Freiermuth, and fewer third-and-longs because the run game creates manageable downs.

Three. Turn red-zone trips into touchdowns. Field goals won in Paris. They will not beat a division rival in a game where both offenses are capable of a late drive. Get Darnell Washington the ball in tight. Metcalf himself said Washington “should definitely get an opportunity to catch a touchdown.”

Four. Win the turnover battle. Payton Wilson’s interception in Paris saved at least three points and maybe seven. If Watt, Herbig, or Highsmith can force Watson into one bad decision, this game probably tilts our way.

MY PREDICTION

I’ll put it on the record. Pittsburgh by four. The run game wears Cleveland down in the second half, the defense gets one big takeaway, and the Steelers head into the bye week alone in first place in the AFC North.
