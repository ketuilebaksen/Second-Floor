Steelers Nation, if you’ve seen headlines this week saying Jaylen Warren is devastated, let me give you the real story, because it’s actually more interesting than the clickbait. There’s no report that Warren is upset, and he hasn’t said anything publicly to suggest it. But there is a real debate raging in Pittsburgh right now about how Mike McCarthy used him in Tampa, and some respected voices are calling it a coaching mistake. At the same time, the Steelers just landed in Paris with a brand-new name on the injury report: T.J. Watt. So today, we’re breaking down the Warren usage debate from both sides, what McCarthy has said about his backfield, and every update from Thursday’s final practice before the flight. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The numbers that started the debate.

Let’s start with what actually happened in Tampa. According to Still Curtain’s Andrew Falce, Warren played about 74 percent of the offensive snaps against the Buccaneers, and Rico Dowdle, in his first game back from a toe injury, played about 27 percent. On its own, that sounds like a clear lead back.

But here’s the split that has people fired up. Through the first three quarters, when the Steelers scored just three points, the snaps were much closer: about 55 percent for Warren and 42 percent for Dowdle. Then, in the fourth quarter and overtime, Warren played about 84 percent of the 37 offensive snaps. And that’s when the offense finally came alive.

Falce didn’t hold back. He wrote that McCarthy “is botching his breakout season,” and that “a 55-45 split like what the Steelers were favoring early in the game is not going to work.” He even called the early handling “inexcusable.” That’s strong language, and it tells you how frustrated some people are.

A former Steeler weighs in.

And it’s not just writers. Former Steelers offensive lineman Trai Essex talked about it on his Snap Count podcast, and he was just as direct. He said he was frustrated by “how they consistently moved away from him when it looked like he was getting a hot hand.” His reaction? “It confuses, confounds, flabbergasts.”

Essex also said of Dowdle, “But they were using him too much.” Now, that’s not a knock on Dowdle as a player. It’s about the plan. The argument is simple: when one back is rolling, ride him. Don’t take him off the field to keep a rotation going.

Why Warren had earned the ball.

To understand why people care so much, you have to remember what Warren did the week before. Against the Colts in Week 5, he carried 15 times for 101 yards, caught six passes for 87 yards, and scored two touchdowns. He was named the AFC Offensive Player of the Week for that game, the first time he’s won that award.

And McCarthy himself has praised him over and over. After the Colts game, he called Warren the “featured player right now.” And on Monday after the Tampa win, he said, “I think Jaylen’s one of the hottest players in the National Football League as we stand here.”

So you can see why fans were confused. If your head coach is calling a player one of the hottest in the league, why is he splitting snaps almost evenly for three quarters?

How far Warren has come.

It’s also worth remembering the journey here. Warren came into the league in 2022 as an undrafted free agent out of Oklahoma State. Nobody handed him anything. He spent years as the change-of-pace back, the guy who did the dirty work in pass protection and made the most of limited touches. Now, in 2026, he’s the lead back on a 4-2 team, an AFC Offensive Player of the Week, and the player his head coach calls one of the hottest in the league.

That’s why fans get so protective of him. When a player has worked his way up from nothing, and finally gets his moment, nobody wants to see that moment slowed down by a rotation. I understand that feeling completely. But I also think Warren is the kind of player who doesn’t care about snap percentages as long as the team wins. And on Sunday, the team won.

What the fourth quarter proved.

There’s one more detail in Falce’s breakdown that I think matters. He pointed out that Warren produced more than half of his yards once he got the bulk of the work in the fourth quarter and overtime. That doesn’t prove the early split cost the Steelers the game. But it does show that Warren gets better as the game goes on, when defenses are tired and he gets into a rhythm.

Some backs need a few carries to warm up and feel how the defense is playing. Warren seems to be one of them. If that’s true, the answer isn’t necessarily giving him every snap. It’s giving him enough early carries to find that rhythm sooner, so the offense doesn’t wait until the fourth quarter to wake up.

The other side of the story.

Now, I always want to give you both sides, because the full picture matters. And there’s a strong case that this debate is a little overblown.

First, Dowdle was really good when he got the ball. He carried seven times for 40 yards, which is 5.7 yards per carry. Warren carried 14 times for 50 yards. So on that day, Dowdle was actually the more efficient runner. After the game, Dowdle said he “was running angry,” and it showed.

Second, look at the opponent. According to Steelers Depot, Tampa Bay came into that game with the second-best run defense in the league. And the Steelers trailed by two scores for most of the afternoon, which naturally pushes an offense toward the pass. When you’re down 16-3, you’re not going to run the ball 35 times.

Third, Depot also pointed out that Warren’s overall workload in Tampa was similar to the games Dowdle missed. He played 59 snaps against the Bucs, compared with 52 against the Colts. Overtime also gave him four extra carries. So it’s not like Warren disappeared.

And fourth, McCarthy has been clear about his plan from the start. Before the season, he envisioned a one-two punch, closer to a 50-50 split between the two backs. When Dowdle got hurt, Warren took over. Now that Dowdle is back, McCarthy has said Warren is still the featured player, but it makes sense that he’d want to get Dowdle involved, too.

My take: both sides are right.

So here’s where I land. I think the critics have a point about the early game. When your offense is stuck, you go to your best player, and right now that’s Warren. Fifty-five percent of the snaps in the first three quarters wasn’t enough, especially for a back coming off a 101-yard rushing day and an AFC Offensive Player of the Week award. Even against a tough run defense, you keep feeding the hot hand.

But I also don’t think this is a crisis. Dowdle earned his touches with how he ran, and a healthy two-back rotation is going to be important over a long season. Warren isn’t going to hold up with 90 percent of the snaps every single week. The key is the balance. I’d like to see something closer to 65-35 early in games, with the ability to ride the hot hand when one of them gets rolling.

And remember what McCarthy said on Monday: running the ball more is “clearly a focus.” The Steelers had only 22 rushing attempts in Tampa while Aaron Rodgers threw 49 passes. If the run game gets more volume overall, both backs can get what they need, and nobody has to be devastated about anything.

Dowdle deserves credit too.

And let’s not forget what Dowdle went through to get back on the field. He hurt his toe in Week 2 and missed several games. In his first game back, against one of the best run defenses in the league, he averaged almost six yards a carry. That’s not a player who should be pushed to the side. That’s a player who gives the Steelers a real one-two punch when he’s healthy.

The question is how the knee injury affects him now. If he’s limited on Sunday, McCarthy will have to lean on Warren, and we’ll see what this offense looks like with one back carrying the load from the opening drive.

Why Sunday could look very different.

And here’s the twist. The usage debate might settle itself this week, because Dowdle is now on the injury report with a knee issue. He was limited in practice on both Wednesday and Thursday. If that knee limits him on Sunday in Paris, Warren could be in line for a much bigger workload against the Saints, which is exactly what his supporters have been asking for.

The Saints have injuries of their own on defense, too. Linebacker Kaden Elliss was limited with a calf injury, and defensive end Anfernee Jennings was limited with a knee injury. So there could be room to run if the Steelers commit to it.

Why McCarthy is under the microscope.

This debate is also part of a bigger conversation about McCarthy this week. On Monday, he defended the two pass calls near the goal line at the end of regulation in Tampa, saying, “I get the post evaluation, but those are solid plays.” The Steelers Depot podcast crew also questioned some of his clock management late in that game. And now there’s the running back split.

To be fair, McCarthy has a team that’s 4-2 and has won two straight. Head coaches always get second-guessed, especially when an offense struggles for three quarters. But in a division where the Steelers, Browns, and Ravens are all 4-2, small decisions add up. McCarthy has never coached an international game, and Sunday in Paris is a chance for him to show that he’s learned from Tampa: run the ball early, trust the hot hand, and give the defense a lead to protect.

The big injury news: T.J. Watt.

Now, let’s get to the rest of Thursday’s news, starting with the name that made everyone nervous. T.J. Watt was added to the injury report with a calf issue. On Wednesday, he was listed as a rest day, but on Thursday, he was limited, and the injury report now lists his calf.

How worried should you be? Right now, there’s no game designation for Sunday, and he did practice in a limited capacity. That’s usually a good sign. But calf injuries can be tricky, especially with a long flight involved. The Friday report, which comes out in Paris, will tell us a lot more.

And Alex Highsmith is still limited with his groin injury. If both Watt and Highsmith are limited on Sunday, Nick Herbig and Jack Sawyer could see a lot more snaps. That’s where the depth Omar Khan has built really gets tested.

Jalen Ramsey takes a step forward.

There was good news on Thursday too. Jalen Ramsey, the AFC Defensive Player of the Week, went from not practicing on Wednesday to limited work on Thursday with his wrist. He’s played through this injury before, and the upgrade is encouraging.

But the depth behind him took a hit. Brandin Echols was ruled out for Sunday with his hamstring, along with wide receiver Ben Skowronek, who’s out with his calf. That means if Ramsey’s snaps are limited, the Steelers don’t have an easy replacement at nickel. Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith could be called on. Murphy-Bunting has 11 career interceptions and a Super Bowl ring, so the Steelers wouldn’t be turning to an inexperienced player in that spot. Still, keeping Ramsey healthy for the stretch ahead matters more than any single game.

Joseph-Day goes on injured reserve.

Another roster move came on Thursday. The Steelers placed defensive lineman Sebastian Joseph-Day on injured reserve with a triceps injury. He’d played in all six games as a reserve, with 10 tackles, a tackle for loss, and a fumble recovery. Now he’ll miss at least four games, and he joins Derrick Harmon on injured reserve.

That leaves the interior line thinner, with Cam Heyward, Keeanu Benton, and Yahya Black carrying more of the load. And Heyward sat out practice again on Thursday with a rest designation, which is normal for a veteran in a travel week.

The open roster spots and DeShon Elliott.

Here’s where it gets interesting. With Rayshawn Jenkins released on Wednesday and Joseph-Day going on injured reserve on Thursday, Pro Football Talk noted that the Steelers now have two open spots on the 53-man roster.

And one of those spots looks like it’s waiting for DeShon Elliott. He practiced fully again on Thursday, his second straight full session. He still has to be officially activated from injured reserve, and the Steelers haven’t confirmed that yet. But everything is pointing in that direction. The other spot could go to a defensive lineman, a cornerback, or even a receiver like Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who’s on the practice squad.

The trip to Paris.

Now let’s talk about the trip itself. The Steelers flew out on Thursday night on an eight-hour charter and landed in Paris on Friday morning. The time difference is six hours, and it shrinks to five on game day as Europe changes its clocks.

The message from the coaching staff has been clear. Keeanu Benton said McCarthy made sure the players understood that “it’s a business trip.” And Pat Freiermuth was honest about the challenge: “It’s tough, but that’s what we’re here to do.”

McCarthy also took time this week to praise the young players. “I’m proud of our rookie class,” he said, naming right tackle Max Iheanachor, safety Robert Spears-Jennings, and receiver Germie Bernard. And quarterbacks coach Tom Arth described Rodgers’ comeback performance in Tampa as “vintage Aaron Rodgers.”

The young offensive line matters here too. Iheanachor, the first-round rookie, took over at right tackle earlier this season, and the line has had to adjust on the fly. A running game that gets more volume early also helps young linemen settle in, because run blocking lets them fire off the ball instead of sitting back against a pass rush on every snap. More carries for Warren could help the entire offense, not just the stat sheet.

Trade block: does the backfield need help?

Let’s talk about the deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Running back is not where I’d spend. Warren and Dowdle are a strong pair when both are healthy, and the conversation is really about how to divide the work, not whether the talent is there. If Dowdle’s knee turns into something longer, the Steelers might add a veteran back for depth, but that would be a minor move.

The bigger needs are still on the outside and up front. With Pittman, Wilson, and Skowronek all out, a reliable receiver is at the top of my list, and the Colts’ situation after firing their general manager could make Josh Downs available. And with Harmon and Joseph-Day both on injured reserve, a veteran interior defensive lineman makes a lot more sense than it did a week ago. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline. Those two open roster spots give Khan flexibility.

What to watch on Sunday.

So, what am I watching when the Steelers take the field at the Stade de France? First, the running back split. If Dowdle is limited, does Warren get the 70 or 80 percent workload fans have been asking for? Second, the pass rush. Do Watt and Highsmith play their normal snaps, or does Herbig carry the load? Third, the secondary. Is Ramsey close to full speed, and is Elliott back on the field?

And fourth, the start. In Tampa, the Steelers didn’t score a touchdown until the fourth quarter. Against the Saints, I want to see the offense come out and establish the run early, with Warren carrying it, and not wait until the fourth quarter to find its rhythm.

What a win in Paris would mean.

Let’s also zoom out for a second. If the Steelers beat the Saints, they’ll be 5-2 and riding a three-game winning streak into a home game against the Browns, the team that beat them in Cleveland earlier this season. That’s the kind of stretch that can define a season. And a running game that’s firing, with Warren leading the way, would be a huge part of that. The defense has carried this team for two weeks. Now it’s the offense’s turn to help, and it starts with the backfield.

The bottom line.

So, is Jaylen Warren devastated? There’s no report of that, and I wouldn’t believe it until he says it himself. What we do have is a fair debate about how the Steelers used him in Tampa. The critics say McCarthy took the ball away from the hot hand. The other side says Dowdle earned his snaps, the game script pushed the offense toward the pass, and Tampa’s run defense was elite. I think both sides have a point, and the solution is simple: run the ball more, and lean on Warren early.

Now the Steelers are in Paris with a few new injury concerns, including Watt’s calf, but also some good news with Ramsey improving and Elliott close to returning. If they take care of the Saints, they’ll head home 5-2 with a huge division game against Cleveland next.
