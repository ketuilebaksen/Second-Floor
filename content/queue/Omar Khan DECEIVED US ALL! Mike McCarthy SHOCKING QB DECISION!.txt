Steelers Nation, it’s game day in Tampa, and the moves Pittsburgh made on Saturday tell a very different story from the one a lot of fans were sold all week. For days we heard about “strong indications” that the Steelers would be active, a possible JuJu Smith-Schuster homecoming, and trade buzz around every receiver with a pulse. Then Saturday came, and Omar Khan didn’t add a single outside receiver. Instead, Roman Wilson went to injured reserve, and Pittsburgh is heading into Raymond James Stadium with two practice squad receivers elevated and DK Metcalf listed as questionable with an illness. So did Khan fool us And what about the quarterback decision Mike McCarthy made back in August, the one that suddenly looks a lot more important this week Today we’re answering both, breaking down exactly who will be catching passes from Aaron Rodgers this afternoon, and giving you my keys and prediction for the game. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Part one the Saturday moves.

Here’s exactly what Pittsburgh did on Saturday. Roman Wilson was placed on injured reserve with the finger injury he suffered against the Colts. That means he’ll miss at least four games, and his earliest return would be Week 11 against the Eagles. Running back Lew Nichols was signed from the practice squad to the 53-man roster, because the team had already used all three of his allowed elevations. And two receivers were elevated from the practice squad for this game Brandon Smith and Brandon Johnson.

That’s it. No trade. No free agent. No JuJu.

And remember, the JuJu story was messy from the start. Sports Illustrated’s Noah Strackbein reported, citing a team source, that the Steelers would host Smith-Schuster for a visit on Friday. Then Behind the Steel Curtain posted an update saying the Steelers were not hosting him. As of Saturday’s moves, he isn’t on the roster.

And think about what the Wilson move means for the calendar. Pittman went on injured reserve before the Colts game, so he can’t return until after the Week 9 bye at the earliest. Wilson now joins him, with Week 11 against Philadelphia as his earliest possible return. That means Tampa today, the Saints in Paris next week, and one more game before the bye, all without two of the top three receivers this team planned to use. Skowronek’s calf injury will be week to week as well. This isn’t a one-Sunday problem anymore. It’s a three-week problem at minimum.

Part two did Omar Khan deceive us

So let’s answer the headline honestly. Did Khan deceive Steelers fans No. And here’s why.

Khan never promised a receiver this week. The “strong indications” came from Steelers insider Mark Kaboly, and what he actually said was that the Steelers are going to be active “heading into the NFL trade deadline set for Nov. 10.” That deadline is still more than three weeks away. The JuJu report and the trade rumors around names like Jauan Jennings and Jerry Jeudy were exactly that reports and rumors. Fans, and a lot of YouTube thumbnails, turned those into promises.

What Khan actually did is consistent with how he’s run this team all season. He fills holes fast with low-cost moves, and he waits on the bigger ones until the price is right. And honestly, this week he had a reason to wait. Earlier this week, McCarthy made it clear he trusts Brandon Smith. McCarthy said Smith has “definitely earned this opportunity,” and, “We feel very good about Brandon moving forward.” He also said, “Those conversations are always going on,” when asked about outside help. That’s not deception. That’s a front office telling you it isn’t going to panic.

But I’ll also say this, and this is my opinion the patience has a limit. With Pittman and now Wilson both on injured reserve, Skowronek out, and Metcalf sick, Khan can’t wait forever. If the passing game struggles in Tampa, the pressure to make a move before the bye is going to explode.

Part three the QB decision that suddenly matters.

Now, the second part of the headline. McCarthy’s “shocking” quarterback decision. Let me be clear there’s no change at starting quarterback. Aaron Rodgers is starting today. The decision people are talking about is the one McCarthy confirmed back in late August the Steelers decided to carry four quarterbacks on the 53-man roster.

Here’s how that breaks down, according to Sports Illustrated at the time. Rodgers is the starter. Mason Rudolph is the backup. Will Howard is the third quarterback, likely the emergency option on game days. And rookie Drew Allar is the fourth, likely inactive. When a reporter laid out that plan, McCarthy said, “Very accurate evaluation on your part.” The idea was to keep every option possible while the team looks for its quarterback of the future, especially with Rodgers saying this is his final season.

At the time, it was mostly a curiosity. Now, in the middle of a receiver crisis, it’s a real debate. That fourth quarterback spot is a roster spot that could belong to another receiver. It was also part of why the team couldn’t keep linebacker Elandon Roberts on cutdown day, before bringing him back to the practice squad last week.

And the reasons behind it were real. According to that same report, the team wanted “every option they can possibly have” while it searches for a franchise quarterback. Rudolph impressed in the spring and summer, and McCarthy saw him as a reliable backup with some untapped potential. Howard threw three interceptions in the preseason, but the team stayed optimistic about his future. And Allar, the rookie, is still a long-term evaluation. The same report even suggested the Steelers might draft a quarterback next spring anyway. So the four-quarterback room is really an audition for 2027, happening in the middle of 2026.

So here’s my opinion. I understand the long-term logic. Rodgers is 42, he’s leaving after this season, and the Steelers need to find out what they have in Howard and Allar before the 2027 draft. But in a week when you’re elevating two practice squad receivers just to have enough bodies, carrying a quarterback who doesn’t dress on Sundays is a luxury. If the receiver injuries continue, I think McCarthy and Khan will eventually have to choose between that fourth quarterback and a fifth or sixth receiver. That’s the quarterback decision worth watching, not a change at the top.

Part four who’s catching passes today.

So let’s look at the receivers Rodgers will actually have this afternoon.

DK Metcalf is questionable with an illness after missing Friday’s practice. Steelers Depot expected him to play, but he won’t be at 100 percent. Even a limited Metcalf changes how a defense plays, so his status at inactives is the biggest thing to watch before kickoff.

Germie Bernard is the rookie who just caught six passes for 80 yards in his first career start against the Colts. McCarthy says “football comes easy to him.” Today, he could be the number one or number two option.

Brandon Smith went undrafted out of Iowa in 2021. He played three games with the Jets earlier in his career, but he still doesn’t have a regular-season catch. Last week, he played 16 offensive snaps.

Brandon Johnson is the most experienced of the group. He went undrafted out of UCF in 2022, and he has 26 career catches for 335 yards and five touchdowns, most of it with Denver. And here’s a fun twist Johnson spent nearly two months on Tampa Bay’s practice squad before coming back to Pittsburgh, so he knows that building and some of that defense. He’s also the cousin of former Bengals star Chad Johnson.

And Kaden Wetjen is on the active roster as a return specialist who can line up at receiver if needed.

That’s not a scary group on paper. Which is exactly why the plan has to change.

The inactive list to watch.

About 90 minutes before kickoff, the Steelers will announce their inactives, and here’s what I’ll be checking. First, Metcalf. If he’s active, the offense gets its best player back, even at less than full strength. Second, Dean and Ramsey. If both cornerbacks play, Asante Samuel Jr. and rookie Daylen Everette can settle into their usual roles, and Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith become depth. Third, Dowdle. He’s listed as questionable, but after two full practices, I’d be shocked if he doesn’t play. And fourth, the quarterbacks. If the plan from August holds, Allar will be inactive and Howard will be the emergency third quarterback, which tells you nothing has changed at the position.

Part five how McCarthy should attack Tampa without his receivers.

Here’s my game plan, and it’s built around what this roster does best.

First, run the ball, a lot. Jaylen Warren is the AFC Offensive Player of the Week after putting up 188 yards from scrimmage against the Colts. And Rico Dowdle, a two-time 1,000-yard rusher, practiced fully on Thursday and Friday and is set to return from his toe injury. Warren gives you speed. Dowdle gives you power. Rotate them all afternoon and make Tampa tackle for four quarters. And with Lew Nichols now on the 53, there’s a third back available if needed.

Second, use the tight ends. Pat Freiermuth caught two touchdowns against the Colts. Darnell Washington is a massive blocker who can also catch. Two-tight-end sets let the Steelers run the ball and throw play action without asking an inexperienced receiver to beat man coverage on every snap.

Third, get the young receivers open with scheme. Motion, bunch formations, stacked releases. Don’t ask Smith or Johnson to win one-on-one on the outside all day. Create easy throws, let Rodgers get the ball out quickly, and take what the defense gives.

And fourth, let Rodgers be Rodgers. He’s thrown three touchdowns in three straight games, completing 63 of 105 passes for 826 yards and nine touchdowns over that stretch. He knows how to manage a game. He doesn’t need to throw 40 times today. He needs to make good decisions and avoid giving Tampa short fields.

Part six the defense, and the communication question.

On defense, there’s one absence that matters. DeShon Elliott is still out. He’s on injured reserve with his 21-day practice window open, but the team isn’t activating him this week. Elliott is one of the leaders who organizes this secondary, so others have to step up. Jaquan Brisker, the Pittsburgh native and former Penn State safety the Steelers signed in March after four seasons in Chicago, and young safety Robert Spears-Jennings will have to handle more of the communication.

The good news is the rest of the defense is getting healthier. T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward are fine after rest days. Keeanu Benton practiced fully on Friday. Jalen Ramsey and Jamel Dean are both questionable, and Dean told reporters he’s “very optimistic” about playing against the team where his career started.

And the two big keys are tackling and containment. Tampa will likely start undrafted rookie quarterback Jalon Daniels, because Baker Mayfield is still out with a dislocated thumb. Daniels is mobile. In his first start against Green Bay, he ran for 55 yards. Then last week, he completed 76 percent of his passes to beat Dallas as a 9.5-point underdog. And Bucky Irving had a big day running the ball in that game. So the pass rush has to stay disciplined in its lanes, and the defense has to finish tackles. No letting first contact turn into five extra yards.

Remember, this defense just held Jonathan Taylor to 45 yards and didn’t allow a passing touchdown against the Colts. Do that again, and Pittsburgh wins.

And let’s talk about the reunions, because this game is full of them. Sean Murphy-Bunting spent the first month of the season on Tampa’s practice squad before Khan claimed him, so he’s seen the Bucs’ offense every day in practice. Brandon Johnson spent almost two months on Tampa’s practice squad too. Jamel Dean started his career there. And on the other sideline is Kenneth Gainwell, who signed with the Buccaneers this offseason after his time in Pittsburgh. Players never admit it, but games like this always have a little extra edge.

On Tampa’s side, there’s more than just Mayfield to watch. Safety Antoine Winfield Jr. missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday with a rib injury. On the other hand, receiver Chris Godwin and left tackle Tristan Wirfs were full participants on Thursday. So the Bucs will still have weapons, even with a rookie at quarterback.

What a win would mean.

Let’s not overlook the stakes here. A win today makes the Steelers 4-2 and ends that five-game road losing streak, including the loss in Cleveland. It would send them to Paris next week to face the Saints on an international stage with real momentum. And it would prove that this team can win without its receiver depth, by running the ball, playing defense, and leaning on a Hall of Fame quarterback who doesn’t make many mistakes. That’s the formula that wins in November and December, when the weather turns and the games get tighter.

A loss, on the other hand, would put the Steelers at 3-3, with a thin receiver room and a long trip to Europe. That’s when the noise about Khan’s patience and McCarthy’s roster decisions would get very loud. So yes, this is a big one.

Part seven the receiver market, my opinion.

Now, because I know you’ll ask, let’s talk about what comes next at receiver. Here’s my opinion, not a report.

There’s a brand-new name on the market. On Friday, the Colts released Darius Slayton, just 24 days after signing him. He never played a game for Indianapolis after a knee issue. Slayton is 29, and in his career he has 296 catches for 4,435 yards and 22 touchdowns, with a 15-yard average per catch. Last season with the Giants, he had 37 catches for 538 yards. He’s a free agent, so he’d cost nothing but money. But the knee is a question, and he’s not a game-changer at this point.

Jauan Jennings of the Vikings is still the trade target I like most. Pro Football Talk reported he could be traded after Minnesota added Odell Beckham Jr. to its practice squad. He caught nine touchdowns last season with San Francisco, and much of his one-year deal has already been paid through his signing bonus. There’s no report linking him to Pittsburgh, but for a late-round pick, he’d be a smart rental.

And Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland and Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville remain the bigger swings, with that 2028 second-round pick from Dallas as the ammunition.

The point is this Khan has options. He didn’t use them this week. That doesn’t mean he won’t.

And here’s a thought about the bigger picture. The trade deadline falls on November 10th, the Tuesday right after Pittsburgh’s bye. That means Khan can watch the next three games, Tampa, Paris, and the game after that, and then decide. If the young receivers hold up, he can make a smaller move. If they don’t, he can go bigger. Either way, he’ll have three more games of tape before he has to commit a draft pick. That’s why waiting this week wasn’t deception. It was strategy.

Part eight the prediction.

So here’s where I land. The Steelers are 3-2 and opened as three-point road favorites. Tampa is 1-4 with an undrafted rookie at quarterback. Pittsburgh has the better roster, the better quarterback, and a backfield that’s finally whole.

But I won’t ignore the warning signs. Pittsburgh has lost five straight road games. The receiver room is as thin as it’s been in years. And a mobile young quarterback at home can make life uncomfortable if the defense isn’t disciplined.

My prediction Steelers 27, Buccaneers 17. Warren and Dowdle combine for over 150 rushing yards, Freiermuth finds the end zone again, and the defense makes Daniels look like a rookie in the second half. And if everything clicks, it could be even more lopsided than that. But even an ugly, grind-it-out win would be a huge step for a team that hasn’t won on the road in a long time.

The bottom line.

So no, Omar Khan didn’t deceive anyone. He made the moves he believed in Wilson to injured reserve, Nichols to the 53, Smith and Johnson elevated, and no panic trade. And McCarthy’s quarterback decision isn’t a change at starter. It’s the August choice to carry four quarterbacks, which looks more expensive every week the receiver room shrinks.

Today is about answering those questions on the field. If the Steelers win in Tampa with this many receivers out, Khan’s patience looks smart and the road losing streak is over. If they struggle, expect the phones in the front office to start ringing before the bye.
