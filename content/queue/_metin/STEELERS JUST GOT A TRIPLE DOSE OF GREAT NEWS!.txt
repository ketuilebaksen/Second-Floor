Steelers Nation, I want to start today with a confession. Back in January, when the news broke that Pittsburgh had hired Mike McCarthy, a lot of us didn’t know what to feel. This franchise had spent more than half a century doing it one way. Find a young, hungry coach who’d never run his own team. Hand him the keys. Ride with him for a decade or more. And suddenly the Steelers went the other direction, hiring a sixty-something veteran who had already coached two of the biggest brands in the sport.

Fast-forward to November. Six and two. First place in the AFC North. Four straight wins heading out of the bye. And this week, the conversation around this team took a turn that every Steelers fan should enjoy, because three different pieces of good news all landed on the same desk: the head coach’s.

Let me be clear about what this video is and isn’t. There’s no signing here. No trade. If you’re looking for a deadline update, the deadline is still Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern, and I’ll touch on that near the end. Today’s triple dose is about the man running the show. One: a respected former Steeler went on national media and said, flat out, that McCarthy was “the right guy.” Two: the evidence that McCarthy’s willingness to change has actually turned this season around. And three: what all of this means for the only standard that matters in this city, which is January.

Let’s get into it.

GOOD NEWS NUMBER ONE: A FORMER STEELER SAYS “THE RIGHT GUY”

Let’s start with Merril Hoge.

If you’re a longtime fan, you know the name. Hoge played running back for Pittsburgh, then spent years as one of the most recognizable NFL analysts on television. He’s not a guy who hands out praise just to be nice. And on a recent episode of The OG Football Show, which posted his comments on November 5, he made a case for McCarthy that I think is genuinely smart.

He started by acknowledging the skepticism. His words, as printed by Heavy: “Let me tell you one thing. When he was hired, everyone was like, ‘I can’t believe they hired Mike McCarthy.’”

Then he explained why he thought the critics had it backwards. He pointed to the Steelers’ last three coaching hires. Chuck Noll, Bill Cowher and Mike Tomlin were all first-time head coaches when Pittsburgh hired them. And then he laid out what each one inherited. “Well, Chuck Noll handed off to Bill Cowher a playoff team. Bill Cowher handed off to Mike Tomlin a Super Bowl team.”

Think about what Hoge is really saying there. When Cowher arrived, he got a team that knew how to win. When Tomlin arrived, he got a roster that had just won a championship. Both of those first-time coaches stepped into stable, established situations. They had time to learn on the job.

This time was different. The roster McCarthy inherited was full of veterans in win-now mode, starting with a quarterback in his forties. There was no runway for on-the-job training. In Hoge’s view, that’s exactly why Pittsburgh needed someone with stability and experience, someone who’s already been through the pressure, the criticism and the playoff runs. He called McCarthy “the right guy.”

And here’s what I love about that argument. It’s not just “he’s experienced, so he’s better.” It’s “the situation called for a specific kind of coach, and the Steelers found him.” That’s a front office reading its own roster correctly.

The Heavy piece also noted that Mark Schlereth, in the same segment, talked about McCarthy’s experience managing big personalities, including Aaron Rodgers. That matters. A first-time coach walking into a locker room with Rodgers, T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward and Jalen Ramsey would have had to earn authority from scratch. McCarthy walked in with a résumé that included a Super Bowl ring from his Green Bay years and a long history with Rodgers himself.

WHAT ROONEY SAID BACK IN JANUARY

It’s worth going back to what Art Rooney II said when he made the hire, because Hoge’s argument lines up with it.

On January 26, Rooney told Steelers.com, according to Front Office Sports: “We’re really not worried about winning the initial press conference.” And he added: “It’s about picking the coach we believe will help us win games, and social media really doesn’t enter into it.”

At the time, those comments felt like an owner bracing for backlash. Now, at six and two, they look like an owner who knew exactly what he was doing. He didn’t care whether the hire trended well on social media in January. He cared whether it won games in November. So far, it has.

Rooney also addressed the quarterback question that same day: “We don’t know what Aaron’s plans are right now, and that did not weigh heavily in the decision.” That’s an interesting detail. McCarthy wasn’t hired just to recruit Rodgers. He was hired to coach whoever was under center. The Rodgers connection ended up being a bonus.

GOOD NEWS NUMBER TWO: THE COACH WHO ACTUALLY CHANGES THINGS

Now let’s get to the heart of it, because this is where the praise gets specific.

The source material for today’s video includes a fan-show discussion that kept coming back to one idea, and I think it’s the most important thing anyone has said about this team all year. Paraphrasing the panel: the offensive line wasn’t right, so it got changed. The run game wasn’t good enough, so it got fixed. The defense had communication problems, so it got adjusted. One of the voices put it simply: give this man his flowers.

And here’s the thing. That’s not just fan enthusiasm. The facts back it up.

Steelers.com’s Brian Batko opened an October 12 article with this sentence: “Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy is not resistant to change.” Then he laid out exactly what that looked like. Let’s walk through the biggest example.

THE WEEK THAT CHANGED THE SEASON

Go back to Week 4. The Steelers lost a Thursday night game to Cleveland, 27-24. It was ugly in stretches. The Browns scored three straight touchdowns to take a 21-10 halftime lead, and according to EssentiallySports, T.J. Watt grabbed a headset from a coach and spoke directly to defensive coordinator Patrick Graham, who was calling the game from the coaches’ booth. After the game, Watt said it was “extremely frustrating to not be sitting here 3-1. It isn’t acceptable.”

That’s a moment that could have broken a season. A star player frustrated, a defense that looked disconnected, and a new coaching staff facing its first real crisis.

Here’s what McCarthy did. Before the next game, against Indianapolis, Graham moved from the booth down to the sideline. According to EssentiallySports, McCarthy had wanted that since the preseason. And he explained the decision plainly: “It needed to be different.”

He also said something that I think captures his whole philosophy. “You can’t wait until Monday,” McCarthy said. “We can’t have things that we miss that we’re talking about after the fact.”

Read that twice. That’s a head coach saying the old way of fixing problems, waiting for film study after the game, isn’t fast enough. You fix it in real time, face to face, while the game is still being played.

And Graham bought in. “You have your principles and things of that nature, but you’ve got to be able to be pliable and adjust,” he said.

The results? Pittsburgh beat the Colts 34-22 that Sunday. Jonathan Taylor, one of the best running backs in football, managed forty-five yards on fifteen carries, and Indianapolis gained just sixty-six net rushing yards. Patrick Queen called Graham’s sideline presence “great” because of the immediacy it created. “He’s always fired up,” Queen said. Payton Wilson and Jaquan Brisker also credited the on-field communication.

And here’s the kicker. That Colts game was the start of the four-game winning streak. Tampa Bay. New Orleans in Paris. Cleveland at home. Since McCarthy made that change, the Steelers haven’t lost.

Now, I’m not going to pretend one coaching adjustment explains everything. Players won those games. But the willingness to look at a problem and fix it immediately, rather than defending the original plan, is exactly the kind of thing the panel was praising.

THE OFFENSIVE LINE STORY

The second big example is up front.

The transcript points to the offensive line as something that wasn’t working early and got changed. And the numbers since then tell the story. According to Batko’s November 3 piece on Steelers.com, the line had played six games in its current configuration going into the bye. In that time, Pittsburgh climbed to third in the NFL with 4.9 yards per carry and into the top ten in both yards before contact and yards after contact.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio described the group’s chemistry this way: “this group is up there with any of them as far as the connectivity.” And he credited center Zach Frazier in particular: “Fraz does as phenomenal a job as anyone I’ve ever been around.”

That’s the payoff of making a change and then sticking with it. You don’t just shuffle bodies for the sake of it. You find the right combination, and you let it grow.

THE RUN GAME STORY

And the run game has become the engine of the entire offense.

In that same October 12 article, McCarthy said: “Jaylen’s our featured player right now.” He wasn’t kidding. Through eight games, Jaylen Warren has 998 yards from scrimmage, second in the NFL, according to Steelers.com. He ran for 143 yards on 17 carries against Cleveland, tying his career high, and scored a touchdown. Rico Dowdle added 56 yards and a touchdown of his own.

When McCarthy says something is the plan, he follows through. Warren is the featured player, and the offense is built around him. That’s not stubbornness. That’s identity.

THE COMMUNICATION THEME

If there’s one word that keeps showing up in everything about this staff, it’s communication.

McCarthy, in that October 12 article: “Communication is, obviously, very important.” And the panel in the transcript made the same point about Graham, praising him for building relationships with stars like Watt and Ramsey rather than insisting on doing everything one rigid way.

I think that’s the underrated part of this whole turnaround. A veteran defense with Pro Bowlers and All-Pros doesn’t respond to a coach who just tells them what to do. It responds to a coach who listens, adapts and treats them like partners. The Watt headset moment could have been the beginning of a breakdown. Instead, it became the beginning of a fix.

McCARTHY’S YOUNG PLAYERS ARE GROWING TOO

There’s one more angle to the “willing to change” story, and it involves the young players.

McCarthy described rookie receiver Germie Bernard this way: “Football comes easy to him.” And when injuries hit the receiver room, McCarthy didn’t hesitate to give opportunities to players who earned them. About practice-squad receiver Brandon Smith, he said: “He’s earned this opportunity.”

On defense, rookie Gabriel Rubio and undrafted rookie Kevin Jobity Jr. have stepped into a banged-up defensive line. Jobity got his first career sack against Cleveland, part of an eight-sack day for the defense.

A coach who’s flexible with his game plan is also flexible with his depth chart. That creates a culture where young players know that if they perform, they’ll play. That’s a big deal for a roster that will have to replace aging stars over the next few years.

THE STAFF McCARTHY BUILT AROUND HIM

One more piece of the “right guy” argument doesn’t get enough attention: the people McCarthy surrounded himself with.

Patrick Graham, the defensive coordinator, came over from the Raiders. Brian Angelichio runs the offense. James Campen coaches the offensive line, and Angelichio has made a point of saying that “Campy’s got input on stuff he likes” when it comes to building the run game. Joe Whitt Jr., the assistant head coach, ran the defense from the sideline before Graham moved down, and he spent the previous two seasons as Washington’s defensive coordinator. And on the defensive line, Domata Peko has been developing the young players who stepped in when injuries hit. “I feel like we’re on the rise,” Peko said of his group.

Why does that matter? Because a veteran head coach doesn’t need to do everything himself. He needs to hire people he trusts, give them room to coach, and step in when something isn’t working. That’s exactly what happened with Graham. McCarthy didn’t take over the defense. He changed the structure so his coordinator could do the job better.

That’s a very different leadership style from a first-time coach trying to prove himself on every decision. It’s the confidence of someone who has already won, and who knows his job is to put the right people in the right spots.

WHAT THE CRITICS GOT WRONG, AND WHAT THEY MIGHT STILL GET RIGHT

It’s only fair to revisit the criticism from January, because some of it had a point.

The biggest knock on the hire was that McCarthy’s later years in Dallas ended without a deep playoff run, and that his teams sometimes stalled when the games mattered most. That concern hasn’t gone away. Nobody can answer it in November. The only answer comes in January.

But the other big criticism, that a veteran coach would be set in his ways and unwilling to adapt, has been flat-out wrong so far. The Graham move, the offensive line, the featured role for Warren, the quick trust in young players: all of it points to a coach who is adjusting, not repeating.

So here’s the honest scorecard at the halfway point. On adaptability: passed. On managing big personalities: passed. On the playoff question: still to be answered.

GOOD NEWS NUMBER THREE: THE TABLE IS SET FOR JANUARY

Now let’s get to the third piece of good news, and it comes with a challenge attached.

The Steelers are six and two, in sole possession of first place in the AFC North. They’ve won four straight. They’re coming off a bye week with a chance to get healthier. And next up, on November 15, is a road game in Cincinnati against the team chasing them in the division.

That’s a fantastic position. But the panel in the transcript said something that every Steelers fan needs to hear, and I agree with it completely. The standard in Pittsburgh can’t just be winning regular-season games.

Here’s why that hits so hard. The last time the Steelers won a playoff game was the 2016 season, an 18-16 win in Kansas City in the divisional round. Since then, Pittsburgh has made the playoffs several times and come home without a single postseason win. That’s nearly a decade of good regular seasons followed by early exits.

One of the voices in the transcript put it perfectly. Paraphrasing: the goal isn’t to go from winning season to winning season. The goal is to go from winning seasons to winning championships.

That’s the real reason McCarthy was hired. Not to keep Pittsburgh respectable. To push this franchise past the wall it’s been running into every January.

WHY McCARTHY GIVES PITTSBURGH A REAL SHOT AT THAT WALL

So does McCarthy have what it takes to break through? Here’s my case for optimism.

First, he’s done it before. He’s won a Super Bowl as a head coach. He knows what a championship team looks like in December and January, and he knows how different the playoffs feel from the regular season.

Second, his quarterback has done it before. Rodgers and McCarthy won that title together in Green Bay. Whatever happens with Rodgers after this season, right now Pittsburgh has a coach and quarterback who have been to the mountaintop together.

Third, the roster is built for playoff football. A defense that can take over games. A running game that can control the clock. Those are the traits that travel in January, when the weather turns and the margins shrink.

And fourth, the willingness to adapt. Playoff games are won by coaches who adjust at halftime, not on Monday. McCarthy’s whole season has been built on that principle.

THE CAUTIOUS SIDE: WHAT STILL HAS TO IMPROVE

Now, in the interest of honesty, let’s talk about what isn’t fixed yet.

The passing game has been inconsistent. Against Cleveland, Rodgers went 13 of 25 for 172 yards, and his biggest completion was a 58-yard catch-and-run by tight end Darnell Washington. DK Metcalf caught one pass for four yards. Rodgers himself said afterward: “Defense played excellent. Special teams did fine. But offense should have had a lot more points.”

Angelichio has pointed to timing as the issue. His words: “A lot of it is within the time clock of the quarterback’s feet.” That was one of the main things the staff planned to work on during the bye.

And the receiver room is thin. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are both on injured reserve. That’s why the trade deadline matters so much for this team, which brings us to a quick update.

THE DEADLINE CHECK-IN

As I’m recording, no Steelers trade has been announced. The deadline is Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

The name to watch is still Josh Downs of the Colts. According to Yahoo’s tracker, the Steelers were named alongside the Chiefs and Commanders as teams to watch, and Indianapolis reportedly has a fourth-round offer on the table while still trying to get more. Jordan Schultz reported that the Colts planned to revisit offers after their Sunday game against Dallas, which means the next day and a half is when things could move.

Here’s how this connects to today’s theme. A coach who’s willing to change is also a coach who’s willing to add. If McCarthy and Omar Khan believe one more receiver gives this team a better shot in January, I’d expect them to be aggressive within reason. If the price is too high, I’d expect them to trust the system that’s already working.

MY TRADE BLOCK BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Quick board, and remember: opinion, not a report.

Josh Downs: yes for a 2028 fourth-round pick. Pittsburgh’s own 2027 fourth went to Dallas in the Broderick Jones deal, so a future pick is the cleanest way to match the reported price. If the Colts insist on a third, only if something comes back the other way.

Brian Thomas Jr.: pass at a Day 2 price. Too much capital with a quarterback decision coming next spring.

Calvin Ridley: backup plan only, and only cheap.

The core: untouchable. Watt, Highsmith, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier. You don’t break up a first-place team built on exactly the identity McCarthy wanted.

WHAT THE CINCINNATI GAME WILL TELL US

Let’s end with the next test, because it’s a perfect measuring stick for everything we’ve talked about.

The Bengals are the closest team behind Pittsburgh in the division. Pittsburgh won the first meeting back in Week 3. Cincinnati has an explosive offense led by Joe Burrow, who was involved in six touchdowns and threw for more than four hundred seventy yards in an overtime win over Tennessee before the Steelers’ bye.

So what are we watching for?

We’re watching to see whether the defense communicates as cleanly on the road against an elite passer as it did at home against the Browns. That’s the Graham adjustment under pressure.

We’re watching to see whether the run game can control the clock and keep Burrow on the sideline. That’s the offensive line change paying off.

And we’re watching to see whether the passing game shows the timing improvements the staff worked on during the bye. That’s the next fix.

If the Steelers win in Cincinnati, they’ll have at least a one-game cushion over the Bengals in the loss column, plus a season sweep that would give them the head-to-head tiebreaker. That’s the kind of advantage that turns a good regular season into a home playoff game. And a home playoff game is the first step toward that standard we keep talking about.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The triple dose this week is about the man in charge. A former Steeler with decades in the game says McCarthy was the right guy. The evidence shows a coach who fixes problems in real time instead of defending them. And the standings put this team in position to finally chase what this city actually cares about.

Give McCarthy his flowers for the first half. He’s earned them. But the second half is where the real grade gets written. Six and two is a great start. It isn’t the finish line. Not in Pittsburgh. Not with six Lombardi trophies in the building and nearly a decade since the last playoff win.
