Steelers Nation, the trade market just cracked wide open, and it happened in the one place Pittsburgh fans know very well: Indianapolis. On Monday, the Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard after a 2-4 start, and by Wednesday, ESPN was reporting that rival teams expect Indianapolis to start selling before the deadline. That’s a team the Steelers just beat 34-22 two weeks ago, and now some of its best players could be on the market. At the same time, Omar Khan made a roster move of his own on Wednesday, one that tells us exactly where his head is at heading into Paris. So today, we’re going to break down the opportunity, the Colts players who could actually help Pittsburgh, a surprising Aaron Rodgers reunion idea out of Los Angeles, and what Khan has already done this week. And just so we’re clear from the start: Khan hasn’t made a trade, and there’s no report of talks with the Colts. Everything about specific trades is my opinion, not a report. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Why the Colts just became the most important team on the trade market.

Let’s start with what happened. After a loss to a previously winless Titans team in Week 6, the Colts fired Ballard, who had been their general manager since 2017. Owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon said “a change in leadership is necessary” to meet the team’s standard. And things were already tense in that building. After the loss, star defensive tackle DeForest Buckner said he was “sick and tired of the same song and dance every year.”

Officially, the Colts say their goals for this season haven’t changed. But around the league, people see it differently. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler wrote that “Rival teams believe Indianapolis will be open for business ahead of the trade deadline,” and that “it feels as if everything is on the table.” Fowler even compared it to the 2025 Dolphins, who fired their GM before the deadline and then sold.

And there’s one more detail that matters a lot. The Colts don’t have a first-round pick next year. They sent it to the Jets in the Sauce Gardner trade at last year’s deadline. So if they’re rebuilding, they need draft picks, and that gives buyers like Pittsburgh leverage.

According to The Sporting News, any Colts player who isn’t signed through the 2027 season could be available. And ESPN named several players on expiring deals who could draw interest: guard Quenton Nelson, defensive tackle DeForest Buckner, receiver Josh Downs, receiver Keenan Allen, and defensive tackles Grover Stewart and Jerry Tillery.

What Khan already did this week.

Before we get into trade targets, let’s talk about the move Khan actually made. On Wednesday, the Steelers released veteran safety Rayshawn Jenkins. He’s 32, he started in Week 3, but his playing time had dropped every week since, and lately he’d been limited to special teams.

Why does that matter? Because of DeShon Elliott. Elliott practiced fully on Wednesday, and he’s on the official injury report as designated to return from injured reserve. Pro Football Rumors noted that his 21-day window has about eight days left, and that releasing Jenkins could be about opening a roster spot for Elliott to come off injured reserve. When McCarthy was asked about Elliott, he simply said, “We have a practice plan for him.”

That’s classic Khan. He’s not waiting until the last minute. He’s clearing the path for a starter to come back, and he’s doing it before the trade deadline, so he knows exactly what his secondary looks like before he spends any picks.

The injury report that shapes the shopping list.

And that brings us to Wednesday’s injury report, because it tells you where Pittsburgh might need help. Jalen Ramsey didn’t practice because of his wrist, although he’s played through it before. Brandin Echols missed practice with his hamstring. Defensive lineman Sebastian Joseph-Day didn’t practice with a triceps injury, and the interior line is already thin with Derrick Harmon on injured reserve. Alex Highsmith was limited with a groin injury, and Rico Dowdle was limited with a knee issue. Ben Skowronek is still out with his calf. T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward got veteran rest days.

On the receiver side, McCarthy said “We’ll see” when asked whether newly signed Marquez Valdes-Scantling could be available Sunday. So the needs are clear: cornerback depth, interior defensive line depth, and another receiver. And the Colts have players at all three spots.

Target one: Josh Downs, the slot receiver.

The first name I’d look at is wide receiver Josh Downs. He’s 25, and he’s on an expiring deal, which makes him exactly the kind of player a rebuilding team moves. His best season came in 2024, when he went over 800 receiving yards with five touchdowns. This year has been quieter. According to Still Curtain, he has 289 yards and no touchdowns through six games.

Why would he fit? Downs is a route runner who wins in the slot, on short and intermediate throws. That’s exactly what this offense needed in Tampa, when the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters. With Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve, Rodgers needs someone who can get open quickly and keep drives alive. Downs could slide in next to DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard and give Rodgers a reliable third option right away.

The question is role. Wilson has played much of his snaps from the slot, and when he returns, the coaches would need a plan for both. But if Khan sees Downs as more than a rental, Pittsburgh could try to work out a longer deal, which would make the cost easier to justify.

Target two: help on the defensive line.

The second direction is the interior. With Harmon on injured reserve and Joseph-Day banged up, Pittsburgh’s defensive line depth is getting tested. The Colts have several veteran defensive tackles who could be available, including Grover Stewart and Jerry Tillery.

And then there’s the big name: DeForest Buckner. He has four sacks, eight quarterback hits, and four tackles for loss this season, according to Still Curtain, and he’s one of the best interior defenders of his generation. But he’s also in a contract year with a big salary, and Still Curtain noted it could take a “hefty price” to get him. Picture Buckner next to Heyward, though. That would turn an already disruptive front into something special.

Target three: the corner and the safety.

The third option is in the secondary. Charvarius Ward is a strong man-to-man cornerback, and pairing him with Jamel Dean would give Graham two corners who can handle one-on-one coverage. But Ward is 30, he’s dealt with injuries, and Still Curtain suggested the Colts would need to cover most of his salary to make it work. His contract also runs through 2027, which makes him less likely to be moved under that “not signed through 2027” logic.

The more interesting name might be safety Camryn Bynum. He’s 28, and he signed a four-year deal with the Colts in 2025 that runs through 2028. He played well in Brian Flores’ defense in Minnesota, and Patrick Graham has worked with Flores before, including as his defensive coordinator in Miami. The systems aren’t identical, but there’s familiarity. If Elliott’s return doesn’t go smoothly, a reliable safety under contract for multiple years would be worth a phone call.

What about Sauce Gardner? Forget it. He’s young, elite, and on a long-term deal, and Indianapolis gave up two first-round picks to get him. A rebuilding team keeps players like that.

Target four: the offensive line splash.

Now let’s talk about the biggest name on the Colts’ list, at least on the offensive side: Quenton Nelson. He’s been one of the best guards in the NFL since the day he was drafted, with eight Pro Bowls, three first-team All-Pro selections, and three more second-team selections, including last season. He’s in the final year of his contract, and according to ESPN, the Colts didn’t pursue an extension with him before the season.

Why would Pittsburgh care? Look at Tampa. Rodgers was sacked five times, and McCarthy has made it clear he wants to run the ball more. A guard like Nelson would make an immediate difference in both areas. He’d protect a 42-year-old quarterback from interior pressure, and he’d open lanes for Jaylen Warren and Dowdle.

The obstacle is money. Still Curtain noted that fitting his cap hit could be a challenge, and any team trading for him would need to make the numbers work midseason. He’d also cost far more than a mid-round pick. So Nelson is a dream target, not a likely one. But if the Colts truly hold a fire sale and the price drops, he’s the kind of player who could change a season.

The veteran receiver and the running back.

There are two more names worth mentioning briefly. Keenan Allen is a veteran receiver on an expiring deal who’s been one of the best route runners of his era. He wouldn’t cost much, but at this stage of his career, he’d be more of a short-term bridge than a long-term answer.

And The Sporting News even mentioned Jonathan Taylor as a possible trade candidate. I’d file that under “very unlikely.” Pittsburgh already has Warren and Dowdle, and spending big at running back isn’t the priority. But it tells you how wide open the Colts’ situation could become.

The Davante Adams wild card.

Now, here’s the idea that’s going to get a lot of attention. Davante Adams. The Rams lost 18-16 to the Cardinals on Sunday, and Adams ruptured his hamstring on a non-contact play in the fourth quarter. Sean McVay announced Monday that he’ll miss six to 10 weeks. Before the injury, Adams had 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns.

So why would anyone bring this up? Because of Rodgers. Adams and Rodgers have one of the most productive quarterback-receiver partnerships of their generation, from their years together in Green Bay and their reunion with the Jets. They know each other’s timing instinctively.

But let’s be realistic. Adams is 33, he’s in the final year of his contract, and a ruptured hamstring at that age is serious. If he’s out six to 10 weeks, he might not be ready until late December or even January. There’s no report that the Rams are selling, and no report linking Adams to Pittsburgh. It’s a fascinating idea, but it’s a long shot.

Trade block: what I’d actually do.

So let’s put it all together, and remember, this is my opinion, not a report.

If I’m Khan, my first call goes to Indianapolis about Josh Downs. He fills the biggest need, he’s young, and as a player on an expiring deal for a rebuilding team, he shouldn’t cost a premium pick. I’d be comfortable offering a mid-round pick, maybe a fourth, and I’d want permission to talk about an extension.

My second call would be about interior defensive line depth, whether that’s Stewart, Tillery, or someone else on the market. That’s a cheaper move that protects the strength of this team: its defense.

I’d keep the 2028 second-round pick from the Joey Porter Jr. trade in my pocket for a bigger opportunity, like Buckner if the price drops. And I’d pass on Adams unless the price is a seventh-round pick and the medical reports look much better than expected.

And what about selling? Patrick Queen’s name came up in trade speculation earlier this season, but his coverage has looked better this year. Trading him would open a new hole that needs its own fix. I don’t see that happening.

The Khan pattern.

If you’ve been following this channel all season, you know there’s a pattern to how Khan operates. He doesn’t make panic moves, and he doesn’t chase headlines. He identifies a need early, and he fills it before it becomes a crisis. When the secondary got thin, he claimed Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith off practice squads. When the receiver room got hit by injuries, he brought back Valdes-Scantling, a player who already knows Rodgers and McCarthy. And now, with Elliott close to returning, he cleared a roster spot by releasing Jenkins.

None of those moves made national headlines. But each one solved a problem. That’s why I believe that if Khan does trade with the Colts, it’ll be a targeted move for a specific need, not a splashy blockbuster for the sake of it. And it’ll come at a price he’s comfortable with, because he’s shown all year that he values his draft picks.

Why the timing works in Pittsburgh’s favor.

Here’s something else to consider. The trade deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday after the Steelers’ Week 9 bye. That gives Khan two more games to evaluate this roster: the Saints in Paris this Sunday and the Browns at home the following week. By then, he’ll know whether Elliott is back, how Ramsey’s wrist is holding up, and how the receiver room looks with Valdes-Scantling in the mix.

And the Colts’ situation might get even more desperate over the next two weeks. If they keep losing, the pressure to sell will grow, and prices could drop. Patience could save Khan a draft pick or two.

And remember, Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the deadline. Khan has been aggressive all season, from claiming two cornerbacks off practice squads to bringing back MVS. I’d be surprised if he stands still.

Why Pittsburgh knows the Colts so well.

There’s also a little extra spice to this. The Steelers just beat the Colts 34-22 in Week 5. Pittsburgh’s defense held Indianapolis to 4-of-15 on third down and didn’t allow a passing touchdown. Daniel Jones has completed a career-low 59.6 percent of his passes this season, with four touchdowns and six interceptions. So the Steelers’ coaches have very recent film on every Colts player we just talked about. They saw Downs, Buckner, Nelson, and the rest up close. That’s a real advantage when you’re evaluating a trade. Khan and his staff don’t need to guess how these players look against NFL competition. They just watched them do it against their own team.

The Paris test before the shopping trip.

And the Saints have their own injury questions heading into Paris. According to Wednesday’s report, quarterback Tyler Shough was a full participant with a left hand injury, while running back Alvin Kamara was limited with a back issue and linebacker Kaden Elliss was limited with a calf injury. Guard Cesar Ruiz didn’t practice. If the Steelers’ defense plays the way it did against the Colts and the Buccaneers, it has a real chance to take over this game, and that would give Khan even more confidence heading into the deadline.

What a trade would mean for the young players.

There’s one more angle that doesn’t get talked about enough. Every trade affects the young players already on the roster. Rookie Germie Bernard has caught 17 passes in his first two starts, and rookie safety Robert Spears-Jennings has been improving every week. If Khan adds a receiver like Downs, Bernard doesn’t lose his role. He gets a partner who takes some attention away, which could actually help him grow. And if Elliott returns, Spears-Jennings can keep developing without being asked to carry too much in a playoff race. The best deadline moves don’t block young players. They protect them.

The bigger picture: the race in the AFC North.

And don’t forget why this matters. The Steelers are 4-2, tied with Cleveland and Baltimore, with the Browns holding first place on their division record. Every move Khan makes before November 10th could decide who wins the division. A team with this defense, this quarterback, and one more piece on offense could be the favorite in the AFC North.

But first, there’s Paris. The Steelers fly out after Thursday’s practice to face the Saints on Sunday at the Stade de France, with a 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh-time kickoff. Win that game, then beat Cleveland at home, and Khan can go shopping from a position of strength.

The bottom line.

So did Omar Khan just find a huge trade opportunity? The opportunity is real. The Colts fired their general manager, rival teams expect them to sell, and they have players who could fill real needs in Pittsburgh, especially Josh Downs. Khan hasn’t made a trade, and there’s no report of talks. But he’s already clearing roster space for DeShon Elliott, and he’s positioned to strike before November 10th.

If he lands the right piece at the right price, this could be the move that turns a good Steelers team into a real contender. And if the price isn’t right, I trust him to walk away, because that’s exactly what he’s done all season. Patience and aggression at the same time. That’s the Omar Khan way.
