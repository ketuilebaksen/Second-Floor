Steelers Nation, the trade deadline came and went without a single new face walking into the building. But this week, two pieces of genuinely good news arrived anyway, and neither one cost Omar Khan a draft pick.

The first is on defense. Derrick Harmon, the rookie defensive tackle who made the biggest play of the first Bengals game, is back on the practice field. The second is on offense. The two injured receivers this team has been waiting on, Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson, are trending in the right direction.

Now, I want to be precise with you, because the details matter here. Harmon practicing is not the same as Harmon playing. And Pittman and Wilson making progress is not the same as Pittman and Wilson catching passes on Sunday night. In fact, one of them can’t legally play in Cincinnati no matter how he feels.

So today, we’re going to separate what’s real from what’s hopeful. We’ll go through Harmon’s return and the roster puzzle it creates. We’ll walk through the exact receiver timelines. We’ll cover a bonus piece of good news in the secondary that came out Thursday. We’ll talk about the offense, the Brandon Aiyuk chatter, the quarterback future and the brutal stretch of schedule ahead. And as always, my board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

GOOD NEWS NUMBER ONE: DERRICK HARMON IS BACK ON THE FIELD

Let’s start with the big man in the middle.

Derrick Harmon has been on injured reserve with a foot injury. On Wednesday, November 11, the Steelers opened his 21-day practice window, and he went through practice as a limited participant. On Thursday, according to Yahoo Sports, he was limited again for the second straight day.

The transcript describes him working through drills, moving his feet and showing explosiveness, like a player trying to make his case. And according to Steelers Now, Harmon himself said he feels “100% healthy.” The team, though, is taking it one day at a time.

Here’s the part a lot of people are missing. Opening the window doesn’t put Harmon on the active roster. He’s still on injured reserve until the team activates him. And according to Yahoo Sports, Pittsburgh doesn’t currently have an open spot on the 53-man roster. So if the coaching staff wants Harmon on the field Sunday night, the front office has to make a corresponding move first. That might be a release, a move to injured reserve or another transaction.

That’s a real decision, not a formality. The 21-day clock means there’s no rush. The team could activate him this week, next week or the week after. But if he’s activated, someone else’s roster spot goes away.

WHY HARMON MATTERS SO MUCH AGAINST BURROW

You don’t need me to remind you what Harmon did the last time these teams met. But I’m going to anyway, because it’s that good.

Week 3, September 27, at Acrisure Stadium. In the final minute of a three-point game, Harmon got to Joe Burrow, knocked the ball loose, and Sebastian Joseph-Day fell on it. That strip-sack sealed Pittsburgh’s 30-27 win.

The transcript makes a smart point here: Harmon isn’t being asked to rescue a broken unit. The defense has been excellent without him. What he offers is another interior player who can collapse the pocket from the inside. Against Burrow, that’s the most valuable thing you can have. Edge pressure is great, but Burrow is elite at stepping up and climbing the pocket. Push from the middle takes that escape route away.

And Cincinnati can score. The Bengals put 27 on this defense in September. They just put 34 on Atlanta in Madrid, with Burrow going 24 of 29 for 271 yards and three touchdowns and Ja’Marr Chase catching ten balls for 155 yards. You don’t stop that offense with a good game plan alone. You need bodies who can win one-on-one.

THE DEFENSE HE’D BE JOINING

To understand why Harmon’s return is a luxury rather than a lifeline, look at where this defense is.

According to the Associated Press, after the Week 8 win over Cleveland, the Steelers led the NFL with 23 sacks. T.J. Watt had seven and a half, tied for the league lead, and the rest were spread across ten different players. In that 27-6 win over the Browns, Pittsburgh sacked Deshaun Watson eight times.

The run defense has been just as nasty. According to Yardbarker, this defense held Jonathan Taylor to 3.0 yards per carry, Bucky Irving to 2.4 and Quinshon Judkins to 2.3. Through Week 8, it ranked second in the league in rushing success rate.

The transcript also says the defense allowed just three touchdowns over its previous four games. I couldn’t independently confirm that exact count, so take it as the source’s number. But the trend is real. Since the Week 5 game against Indianapolis, when defensive coordinator Patrick Graham moved down from the booth to the sideline, the defense hasn’t lost a game.

And the transcript names the guys driving it. Watt bringing the heat. Cam Heyward doing the dirty work inside. Patrick Queen and Payton Wilson flying around at linebacker. When Watt was asked about this group, he said: “It’s huge to show that we have it in us.” Heyward put it another way: “When multiple guys can add to the party, it leads to ‘this can be sustainable.’”

That’s the defense Harmon would be walking back into. Not a unit that needs him. A unit that could become even harder to beat with him.

BONUS GOOD NEWS: DESHON ELLIOTT PRACTICES IN FULL

Here’s a piece of news the transcript didn’t have yet, because it came out Thursday.

Safety DeShon Elliott, who was limited on Wednesday with an ankle injury, was a full participant on Thursday. That’s according to Yahoo Sports and CBS Sports. For a defense about to face Chase and Tee Higgins, that’s a big deal.

The transcript was worried about Elliott, and for fair reasons. He was limited on Wednesday, and the source suggests Pittsburgh might manage his snaps carefully to keep him fresh for later in the season. That’s still possible. Full practice doesn’t mean a full workload on Sunday. But it does mean the team’s starting safety looks on track to play.

If Elliott had been unable to go, Steelers Now noted that rookie Robert Spears-Jennings would have stepped into the role. Now that looks less likely, at least for this week.

And Elliott wasn’t alone. On Thursday, cornerback Brandin Echols, defensive back Doneiko Slaughter, receiver Ben Skowronek and tight end Darnell Washington were all full participants. Echols and Slaughter are both coming back from hamstring injuries. Watt and Heyward were held out for rest, which is standard for veterans at this point in the season.

So when Steelers Depot headlined its Thursday report by saying the roster “looks very healthy,” I’d say that’s a fair read. I couldn’t open the full article, but the details line up with every other report I found.

GOOD NEWS NUMBER TWO: THE RECEIVERS ARE GETTING CLOSER

Now let’s flip to the offense, and to the question that’s hung over this team for a month: where is the receiving help going to come from?

The trade market didn’t provide it. So the answer, at least for now, is the training room.

Let’s start with Michael Pittman Jr. The Steelers placed him on injured reserve on October 10 with a foot injury. Steeler Nation describes it as plantar fascia trouble that had bothered him since July. Before the injury, he’d played in only three games, with seven catches for 89 yards.

Pittman is technically eligible to return for Sunday’s game in Cincinnati. But as of the Wednesday report, the Steelers hadn’t opened his practice window, and Steeler Nation called a return this week a long shot. On Monday, Mike McCarthy said: “I think Michael is getting close.” He also mentioned workout checkpoints on Tuesday and Wednesday.

The transcript says it wouldn’t be shocked if Pittman’s return stretches into December. That’s the host’s opinion, not a reported timeline, but it’s a reasonable one. Plantar fascia injuries are notoriously tricky, and the team has every reason to be patient.

ROMAN WILSON’S TIMELINE

Then there’s Roman Wilson.

Wilson went on injured reserve on October 17 with a hand injury. The transcript calls it a finger injury, and Steelers Now lists it as his left hand. Either way, the key detail is the calendar.

Injured reserve time is counted in games, not weeks. Because of when Wilson was placed on the list, he isn’t eligible to return until after the Bengals game. So no matter how good he looks, Wilson won’t be on the field Sunday night.

The transcript thinks Wilson is the closer of the two receivers, and it floats the Black Friday game against Denver on November 27 as a possible return. Again, that’s the host’s expectation, not a confirmed date. But it lines up with the eligibility rules. Wilson could be designated to return as early as next week, and the Broncos game would give him a few days of practice to get his timing back.

One quick clarification. Neither receiver has officially returned to team practice yet. When you hear “encouraging signs,” that means rehab and individual work. The practice window is the next real step, and it hasn’t opened for either player.

THE METCALF QUESTION

The reason those two returns matter so much is what’s happened to the passing game without them.

The transcript spends a good chunk of time on DK Metcalf, and I think it’s one of the most balanced takes on him I’ve heard. The issue, according to the source, isn’t one thing. It’s a mix. Some of it is drops. Some of it is separation. Some of it is Aaron Rodgers’ ball placement. And some of it is play design.

The numbers back that up. In the Browns game, according to the AP, Rodgers threw to Metcalf six times, and Metcalf caught one. One of the misses turned into an interception.

The transcript’s argument is simple: Metcalf can still be important without being the answer on every passing play. When a defense knows where the ball is going, you’re doing its job for it. What the source wants is for Rodgers to find the open man, whoever that is, and for the offense to spread the ball around.

And there’s a lot of evidence that works. Darnell Washington has become a real weapon at tight end. And Jaylen Warren has quietly been one of the most productive backs in football. Through Week 8, according to Yardbarker, Warren had 704 rushing yards, sixth in the NFL, plus 294 receiving yards, second among running backs. He also led the league in expected points added and led all running backs in yards after the catch. Watt called him a “bowling ball with teeth,” which might be the best nickname anyone on this team has gotten all year.

When the offense runs through Warren, Washington and the run game, Metcalf gets single coverage and easier throws. When it runs through Metcalf first, everything gets harder. That’s the balance the transcript is asking for, and I agree with it.

THE OFFENSIVE NUMBERS

Here’s the number the transcript uses to explain its frustration. It says Pittsburgh is averaging about 328 offensive yards per game. The audio is garbled at that point, so I’m treating 328 as the most likely figure, and I couldn’t independently verify it.

The host wants to see that number moving toward 350. I like how the transcript frames it: 350 doesn’t magically make you a champion, but the gap shows why this offense needs another level.

There’s a related issue I think deserves attention: the red zone. Against Cleveland, according to the AP, Pittsburgh called five passes in six plays near the goal line across consecutive series, even though the offensive line had been dominating in the run game. Warren and Rico Dowdle combined for nearly 200 rushing yards that day.

McCarthy owned it afterward. He said: “I wish I could have that sequence back.”

That’s a coach being honest about a self-inflicted problem. And it’s encouraging, because it’s fixable. You don’t need a new receiver to hand the ball to Warren at the 2-yard line. You need the play-caller to trust the line that’s been winning all day.

THE DEADLINE STILL LINGERS

None of this good news erases the trade deadline debate, and the transcript doesn’t pretend it does.

The source notes that McCarthy made the division of labor clear: his job is to coach the players in the building, while Omar Khan and the personnel department handle acquisitions. That’s how it’s supposed to work. But the transcript also asks the fair question: if you believe this is a team capable of winning in January, was standing still enough?

I don’t want to rehash the entire deadline here. The short version is that prices were high, the Steelers protected their draft capital, and they’re betting on internal reinforcements. Harmon, Elliott, Pittman and Wilson are that bet. This week was the first sign it might pay off.

THE BRANDON AIYUK CONVERSATION

Now, the transcript brings up Brandon Aiyuk, and I appreciate how carefully it handles him. It separates the facts from the possibilities, so let’s do the same.

The facts: The NFL reinstated Aiyuk on November 9. San Francisco has a roster exemption through November 23 to decide what comes next. He hasn’t been released. And there’s no reported agreement or even reported interest connecting him to Pittsburgh.

There’s also the financial dispute. According to the transcript, Aiyuk owes the 49ers roughly 13 million dollars, which lines up with the reporting I’ve seen. The host views that as separate from whether the Steelers would want him at all, and that’s a fair distinction.

The possibility: the transcript wonders whether Pittsburgh would consider a low-cost practice squad arrangement if he became available. I’d treat that as pure speculation. A player with Aiyuk’s résumé landing on a practice squad would be unusual, and nothing I’ve found suggests it’s on the table. Plus, as the rules are generally understood, a player released after the deadline would go through waivers first.

The host also points out that Aiyuk has been away from game action for a long time, and nobody should expect him to show up ready to contribute. That’s the right attitude. Until there’s an actual decision from San Francisco, Aiyuk is a conversation, not a solution.

THE SECONDARY AND THE DEFENSIVE DEPTH

Even with all this good news, the transcript has one area it’s still worried about, and I think it’s the right one.

The secondary. As we covered, Elliott practiced in full on Thursday, and Echols and Slaughter are healthy again. That’s a lot better than where things stood a week ago. But depth is still thin. One injury late in a game, and Pittsburgh is leaning on young players against an offense built to exploit mismatches.

The transcript says that in its view, the absence of a deadline addition means Pittsburgh is relying on the same secondary depth it had before. The audio at that point actually says “quarterback move,” but given the context, the host clearly means a cornerback move. I’ve corrected that here.

Then there’s the defensive line depth. Sebastian Joseph-Day, the man who recovered Harmon’s forced fumble in Week 3, went on injured reserve on October 22 with a triceps injury. According to Steelers Now, he can’t return until after the Week 11 trip to Philadelphia. The transcript wouldn’t rule out a return later this season, and neither would I. But for now, if Harmon gets activated, he’d be filling a spot in a rotation that’s down one of its veterans.

That’s another reason Harmon’s return matters. Rookie Kevin Jobity recorded the first sack of his career after Harmon went down, which is a good sign for the future. But a healthy Harmon gives this front a level of quality that’s hard to replace.

THE STRETCH THAT DECIDES THE SEASON

The transcript makes a point I want to expand on: this season is bigger than one trade deadline, and the next few weeks will reveal what kind of team this is.

Here’s the stretch, according to CBS Pittsburgh’s schedule:

Sunday, November 15: at Cincinnati, 8:20 p.m. Eastern. Sunday Night Football.

Sunday, November 22: at Philadelphia, 4:25 p.m.

Friday, November 27: home against Denver, 3 p.m. That’s the Black Friday game.

Sunday, December 6: home against Houston, 8:20 p.m.

Monday, December 14: at Jacksonville, 8:15 p.m.

That’s three prime-time games and two road trips to playoff-caliber teams in a five-week window, plus a short week between Philadelphia and Denver. The transcript says a win over Cincinnati would matter for the division, but it wants to see a team that’s getting better, not merely surviving. This is exactly the stretch that will answer that question.

And look at how the injury timelines fit into it. Harmon could be back for Cincinnati or Philadelphia. Wilson could be back for Denver. Joseph-Day could return after Philadelphia. Pittman could be back by December. If those dates hold, the Steelers would be getting healthier exactly as the schedule gets toughest.

THE QUARTERBACK FUTURE

Toward the end, the transcript shifts to a longer-range question: quarterback plans for 2027.

Aaron Rodgers said back in May that this is his final season. So the succession question is real, and the transcript notes that keeping draft picks at the deadline gives Pittsburgh flexibility if it wants to pursue a rookie next spring.

But the Steelers already have two young quarterbacks in the room. Will Howard is a 2025 sixth-round pick out of Ohio State. Drew Allar was a 2026 third-round pick, number 76 overall, out of Penn State. Mason Rudolph is there too as the veteran.

Allar made some noise in the preseason. According to Pro Football Talk, in his debut against Green Bay on August 13, he completed 10 of 13 passes for 153 yards and two touchdowns, leading three straight touchdown drives. Howard went 10 of 11 for 93 yards in the same game. When the Steelers drafted Allar, quarterbacks coach Tom Arth said: “I don’t think he has reached his potential yet as a player.”

The transcript says it can see why people are talking about a future competition. I agree, but I’d add a caution: preseason numbers against backups don’t tell you much about a starting job. The real evaluation happens in practice, every day, where the fans can’t see it.

And as the transcript puts it, Rodgers is still running this offense right now. The more urgent question is how much better it can get before January.

THE POSITIVES ON OFFENSE

The transcript closes its offensive section by acknowledging what’s already working, and I want to give that its due.

Warren has been the most dependable weapon on the team. The offensive line has turned the run game into a real strength. And the source says center Zach Frazier has played well enough to inspire serious All-Pro talk. I didn’t find an official midseason All-Pro list to confirm that, so take it as the host’s view. But anyone who watched Frazier move people in the Browns game would understand where it’s coming from.

That’s exactly why the passing-game inconsistency is so frustrating. The foundation is there. The line is improving. The run game is creating balance. If the receivers get healthy and the play-calling trusts the ground game in the red zone, this offense has a real path to becoming dangerous.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week, and remember: this is opinion, not a report.

Derrick Harmon: if the medical staff clears him, activate him for Sunday night. Make the roster move. Put him in the rotation on obvious passing downs and let him chase Burrow. Don’t give him 50 snaps in his first game back. Twenty to twenty-five is plenty.

DeShon Elliott: play him, but watch his snap count. If the game gets out of hand either way, get him off the field.

Receivers: no reinforcements this week, so Germie Bernard and Skowronek have to step up next to Metcalf. I want Metcalf to get two or three easy completions in the first quarter to settle him in.

Red zone: inside the 5, give it to Warren or Dowdle until Cincinnati proves it can stop it.

Roman Wilson: designate him to return next week, with an eye on Black Friday against Denver.

Michael Pittman Jr.: no rush. Open the window when he’s ready, and target the Houston game in December.

Brandon Aiyuk: monitor only. No moves until San Francisco decides.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The double dose of good news is real, but it comes with an asterisk. Harmon is practicing, but he’s still on injured reserve and needs a roster spot. The receivers are getting closer, but neither can help on Sunday night. And Elliott’s full practice on Thursday might be the most immediately useful news of all.

What I like most about this week is the timing. Pittsburgh is about to enter the hardest five-week stretch of its season, and for the first time in a while, the injury list is getting shorter instead of longer. That doesn’t guarantee anything. But it gives the guys already in this building a real chance to prove the front office right.

It starts Sunday night in Cincinnati, with first place in the AFC North on the line.
