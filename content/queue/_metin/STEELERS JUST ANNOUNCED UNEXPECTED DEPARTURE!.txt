Steelers Nation, if you’ve been scrolling this week, you’ve probably seen the word “departure” attached to the Pittsburgh Steelers, and you’ve probably seen one name over and over again: Mason Rudolph. So let me clear this up right away, because you deserve the truth before the hype. As of today, the Steelers have not announced any departure. Rudolph has not been traded. Nobody has reported talks between Pittsburgh and anybody else. But there is a real story here. A serious health situation in Philadelphia has left the Eagles scrambling at quarterback, and several analysts are now pointing at Pittsburgh’s veteran backup as a possible solution. So today, we’re going to walk through exactly what happened with Jalen Hurts, why Rudolph’s name is suddenly everywhere, the honest case for and against a trade, and the one roster decision the Steelers actually have to make this week. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

What happened to Jalen Hurts.

Let’s start in Philadelphia, because this is a scary situation, and it deserves to be handled with care before we talk about football.

On Monday, the Eagles and Hurts’ agent, Nicole Lynn, released an update on his condition. What was first diagnosed in London as a superficial clot in his left calf turned out to be a deep vein thrombosis, along with blood clots in both of his lungs. The good news is that he’s stable, he’s being treated with blood thinners, and the team said, “Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery.” They added, “We anticipate that he will have no long-term effects.”

But while he’s on that treatment, he can’t take part in contact activities, and that includes football. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport and ESPN’s Adam Schefter both reported that recovery is expected to take around three months, which puts the rest of his season in serious doubt. The Eagles had already placed him on injured reserve last week.

Here’s how the timeline unfolded. Hurts got cleated in the Week 4 loss to the Rams. He reported calf issues a few days later. A small clot was found in London, and doctors determined he could safely play against Jacksonville. Then a follow-up exam showed the clot had moved, he was ruled out for the Carolina game, and he went on injured reserve. When Nick Sirianni was asked about the handling of it, he said, “I’m not a medical expert,” adding, “I have full trust and full faith in them.”

First and foremost, everyone in football wants Hurts healthy. That comes before anything else we talk about today.

The Eagles’ quarterback problem.

Now, the football side. Philadelphia is 2-4. The Eagles started 2-0, and they’ve lost four straight since. Tanner McKee started against Carolina on Sunday and struggled to move the offense in a double-digit loss. Behind him is rookie Cole Payton. And last week, the Eagles signed Joe Milton off the Cowboys’ practice squad. According to CBS Sports, Milton has never started an NFL game and has thrown 53 career passes.

Add it all up, and the Eagles have a quarterback room with very little starting experience. According to Still Curtain, McKee has three career starts, and Payton and Milton have none. The Giants are leading the NFC East at 3-2-1, so the division is still very much within reach for Philadelphia. That’s why people are asking whether the Eagles will look for a veteran before the November 10th trade deadline.

Why Mason Rudolph’s name is everywhere.

And that’s where Pittsburgh comes in. Over the last couple of days, several outlets have floated Rudolph as a trade target for Philadelphia. Matt Reed of The Big Lead said Rudolph’s “biggest draw as a trade prospect” is his experience, and that he’d come cheap. Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox had already listed Rudolph among the top quarterbacks on the trade market, saying he “should be available to teams that need backup help.” And on Tuesday, Still Curtain’s Allison Koehler wrote that the Steelers should consider cashing in if the Eagles come calling, summing up her argument this way: “safety isn’t sacred.”

Now, notice what all of those have in common. They’re opinions. They’re columnists and analysts making suggestions. None of them is a report that the Eagles have called the Steelers, and none of them is a report that Pittsburgh is shopping Rudolph. That distinction matters, and it’s exactly why I’m not going to put “BREAKING” on something that isn’t breaking.

This isn’t the first time, either.

It’s also worth remembering that Rudolph has been in trade conversations before. Back in April, after the Steelers drafted Penn State quarterback Drew Allar in the third round, 76th overall, Pittsburgh’s 93.7 The Fan host Andrew Fillipponi posted a rumor that the Buccaneers were interested in trading for Rudolph. The reported price was a sixth- or seventh-round pick. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport had also reported that Pittsburgh was open to dealing him.

And what happened? Nothing. Khan kept Rudolph, and he’s spent the season as the No. 2 quarterback behind Aaron Rodgers, with Will Howard and Allar developing behind him. That tells you something. When the Steelers had a chance to move him for a late-round pick in the offseason, they decided his value as insurance was higher.

Rudolph’s résumé: what he brings.

So who exactly are we talking about? Rudolph is 31 years old and has 19 regular-season starts, which is far more starting experience than anyone in Philadelphia’s current quarterback room.

His best stretch came at the end of the 2023 season. With the Steelers’ playoff hopes on the line, he started the final three regular-season games and won all three. He completed 74.6 percent of his passes for 716 yards, three touchdowns, and zero interceptions, and Pittsburgh punched its playoff ticket. Steelers fans remember how that offense suddenly came alive with him under center.

Then came a tougher year. In 2024, he signed with Tennessee and played in eight games, starting five. The Titans went 1-4 in those starts, and he finished with 1,530 passing yards, nine touchdowns, and nine interceptions. He came back to Pittsburgh after that, and when Rodgers was hurt in 2025, he filled in, throwing for 310 yards, two touchdowns, and two interceptions.

So he’s not a franchise quarterback. But he’s exactly what a team in a pinch wants: someone who has seen NFL defenses, who knows how to run an offense, and who isn’t going to panic when the lights come on.

Pittsburgh knows this lesson better than anyone.

If you want to understand why backup quarterbacks matter, you don’t have to look any further than the Steelers’ own history. In 2019, Ben Roethlisberger’s season ended in Week 2 with an elbow injury. Pittsburgh had to turn to Rudolph and then undrafted rookie Devlin Hodges, and a team with an elite defense missed the playoffs. Then in 2023, the script flipped. Rudolph came off the bench late in the season, won three straight, and helped push the Steelers into the postseason.

And just last year, when Rodgers got hurt, it was Rudolph who stepped in again. Every one of those seasons tells the same story: when your starter goes down, the quality of your backup decides whether the season survives. The Steelers have lived both sides of that, and they know which one they’d rather repeat.

The case for trading Rudolph.

Let’s be fair and lay out both sides, starting with the argument for making a deal.

First, the Steelers have three quarterbacks behind Rodgers. Rudolph, Howard, and Allar. That’s an unusual amount of depth at one position, and roster spots are valuable. Second, if Philadelphia is desperate, the price could go up. A team that just lost its franchise quarterback for possibly the rest of the season might be willing to pay more than the sixth- or seventh-round pick Tampa Bay reportedly floated in the spring. Third, moving Rudolph would give Khan extra draft capital to use in other deals before the deadline, maybe even for the receiver help this offense still needs.

And there’s a development angle too. If Howard or Allar becomes the No. 2, he gets more practice reps with the starters and a chance to grow. That’s real value for a franchise that has to think about life after Rodgers.

The case against trading Rudolph.

Now, here’s the other side, and honestly, I think it’s stronger.

Aaron Rodgers is 42 years old. He was sacked five times in Tampa alone. The protection has been inconsistent, and the Steelers’ season is built around him staying upright. If Rodgers goes down in November or December, do you want a rookie or a second-year quarterback walking into an AFC North race? Or do you want the guy who went 3-0 the last time Pittsburgh needed him in a playoff push?

As Koehler herself pointed out, trading Rudolph would leave Howard or Allar one injury away from starting. That’s a huge risk for a team that’s 4-2, tied with two other teams atop the division standings, and heading into the toughest stretch of its schedule.

And there’s one more detail that should make Steelers fans pause. The Eagles are on Pittsburgh’s schedule. The Steelers face Philadelphia in Week 11. Trading your experienced backup to a team you’re going to face in a few weeks, and helping it stabilize its season, is not exactly the kind of favor you want to do.

What a fair price would look like.

Let’s say, just hypothetically, that Philadelphia did pick up the phone. What should Khan ask for? Again, this is pure opinion. In the spring, the reported price from Tampa was a sixth- or seventh-round pick, and Pittsburgh said no. Now the situation is different. The Eagles have a real emergency, and the Steelers are a contender with no reason to sell. If I’m Khan, I’m not even listening unless the conversation starts at a fourth-round pick, and I’d want a plan in place to protect Rodgers’ backup spot before I agree to anything.

And it’s worth noting that Philadelphia has other paths. According to CBS Sports, there was speculation the Eagles might bring back veteran Andy Dalton before they chose to sign Milton instead. There are also free agents on the market. So the Eagles don’t have to overpay for Rudolph, and the Steelers don’t have to accept a lowball offer. That’s why I think the most likely outcome is that nothing happens at all.

My verdict.

So here’s where I land, and this is my opinion. Unless the Eagles blow the Steelers away with an offer, Rudolph should stay in Pittsburgh. A late-round pick isn’t worth the risk when your starter is 42 and your division is a three-way tie. Backup quarterbacks are like insurance: you hope you never use it, but when you need it, nothing else matters. And Khan has already shown once this year that he values Rudolph more than a Day 3 pick.

What a move would do to the locker room.

There’s also a human side to this. Rudolph has been with the Steelers for most of his career. He’s been a steady presence in that quarterback room, a guy who’s ready every week even though he rarely plays. Moving him in the middle of a winning streak could send a mixed message to a locker room that’s starting to believe. Mike McCarthy said this week that “the organic development and growth of your locker room” is what the locker room is all about, and part of that growth is trusting the people next to you. Rudolph has earned that trust.

The real roster decision this week.

Now, if you want an actual roster move to watch this week, it’s not Rudolph. It’s DeShon Elliott.

Elliott’s 21-day practice window is about to close, and according to Steeler Nation, he has to be activated before the Saints game, or the Steelers lose him for the season. He missed the first six games after his 2025 season ended with a hyperextended knee and a torn hamstring that required surgery. McCarthy said Monday he hopes Elliott is ready, adding, “I thought he had a great week last week.”

But here’s the twist. Patrick Graham didn’t promise him his old job back. “We’ll see what happens this week,” Graham said, “but I think the group is really coming together.” While Elliott was out, Jaquan Brisker took on a bigger role and has been reliable against the run, and rookie Robert Spears-Jennings has improved every week. So if Elliott is activated, he may have to win his snaps back. That’s a good problem to have, and it’s a sign of how much this secondary has grown.

The other injury to watch is Brandin Echols. He hurt his hamstring in Tampa, there’s still no timeline, and one report suggested he could be out until after the bye. That’s why the depth at cornerback, with Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith both available, matters so much.

Trade block: what Khan should do instead.

Let’s talk about the trade deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

If Khan is going to be a seller of anything, it should be a surplus piece at a position where the Steelers have real depth, and only if the return helps right now. If he’s going to be a buyer, the target is clear: wide receiver. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are on injured reserve, DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard are carrying the passing game, and Rodgers needs a third option he can trust. Jauan Jennings of the Vikings remains the name I’d call about, though there’s no report linking him to Pittsburgh. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before November 10th.

And if I’m Khan, I’d want my backup quarterback secure before I go buying anything. The worst-case scenario is trading Rudolph in October, losing Rodgers in December, and watching a winnable season slip away. That’s not a gamble worth taking.

What this means for the Steelers’ quarterback room.

Let’s also talk about Howard and Allar, because they’re part of this conversation whether they like it or not. Both are young quarterbacks still learning the NFL game. Allar was a third-round pick this spring, and Howard is only in his second NFL season. They have talent, and the coaching staff clearly sees a future for them. But there’s a big difference between developing on the practice field and being asked to save a season in the middle of a playoff race.

That’s why Rudolph’s value to Pittsburgh is bigger than his stat line. He’s a bridge. He protects the present while the young guys get ready for the future. Keep him, and Howard and Allar can develop on their own timeline. Trade him, and you might be forcing one of them onto the field before he’s ready.

The bigger picture: Paris and the Browns.

And the timing of all of this couldn’t be more important. The Steelers fly to Paris after Thursday’s practice to face the Saints on Sunday morning, with a 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh-time kickoff at the Stade de France. Despite the unrest in France, the NFL has said the game is moving forward with security plans in place. Then, seven days later, the Browns come to Pittsburgh.

Think about the trip itself, too. An overseas flight, a time change, an early kickoff in a stadium none of these players has ever played in. That’s exactly the kind of week when strange things happen, when a player tweaks something in practice or doesn’t feel right on game day. In a week like this, having a backup who has started 19 NFL games and has played in big moments is not a luxury. It’s a necessity.

And when you look at the secondary, Elliott’s return could matter just as much. He was an important piece of this defense in 2024 and 2025, a hard hitter and a strong voice in the back end. Getting him back right before a two-game stretch against the Saints and the Browns would be like adding a free-agent signing without giving up anything at all.

The Steelers, Browns, and Ravens are all 4-2, with Cleveland holding first place on its division record. These next two games could decide who sits on top of the AFC North going into the bye. That’s not the moment to weaken your quarterback depth. It’s the moment to protect it.

The bottom line.

So did the Steelers just announce an unexpected departure? No. What we have is a set of analysts suggesting that Mason Rudolph could be a fit for an Eagles team dealing with a frightening situation involving Jalen Hurts. There’s no report of talks, and Pittsburgh already passed on moving Rudolph once this year.

My view is simple. Wish Hurts a full recovery, keep Rudolph in Pittsburgh, make the right call on DeShon Elliott, and focus every ounce of energy on Paris and the Browns. That’s how you win a division, and that’s how you make sure a long road trip doesn’t turn into a long season.
