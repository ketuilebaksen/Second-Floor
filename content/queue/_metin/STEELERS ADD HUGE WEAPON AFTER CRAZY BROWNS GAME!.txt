Steelers Nation, the Browns game is in the books, and the real deadline clock just started. Win or lose, the most important stretch of this season might not happen on a field. It happens on Omar Khan’s phone over the next few days.

Because here’s what was waiting on the other side of Sunday. Steelers insider Mark Kaboly said before the game that “early to middle of next week will be the sweet spot for a move to get done.” He said, “It will not last until the weekend.” And he told fans to “keep your alerts on early next week.”

Well, that week starts now.

Now, I want to be honest from the jump, because that’s how we do it on Second Floor. As I’m making this, no trade has been announced. Nobody new has arrived at the facility. If a headline told you the weapon is already in the building, it’s ahead of the facts. But every piece of reporting this week points to Pittsburgh adding a receiver during the bye, and today I’m going to show you exactly who, why, and how much.

I’m James. Let’s get into it.

WHY THE WEAPON IS NO LONGER OPTIONAL

Let’s start with the news that changes the urgency, because it didn’t get nearly enough attention.

Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with a foot injury, and the outlook isn’t great. According to Yahoo, the issue is plantar fasciitis, and Kaboly said, “They are not ruling out, I guess, surgery at this point.” He added that “if he needs surgery he could be out for the year.” He even admitted he gets “bad vibes” about Pittman returning, though he said Pittman “very well could” come back.

Think about that. The receiver Pittsburgh traded for in March, the guy who was supposed to be the steady number two behind DK Metcalf, might not play again this season. He’s eligible to return for the Cincinnati game on November 15, but eligible and healthy are two different things.

And Roman Wilson, the other injured receiver, went on IR in mid-October with his finger injury. The earliest he could be back is the Philadelphia game on November 22.

So right now the room is Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, Brandon Smith, who was signed to the 53-man roster on Saturday, Kaden Wetjen, and Ben Skowronek once his calf is right, plus practice-squad bodies. That’s not a Super Bowl receiver room. That’s a room surviving.

THIS FRONT OFFICE HAS DONE THIS BEFORE

If you’re wondering whether Khan will actually pull the trigger, look at his history.

At the 2024 deadline, Pittsburgh went shopping and came back with Mike Williams from the Jets and Preston Smith from Green Bay. Last year, the Steelers grabbed Kyle Dugger from New England midseason. And this year, before the season even started, Khan traded for Metcalf, traded for Jalen Ramsey, and brought in Pittman.

Khan buys when the team is in contention. And with this team sitting near the top of the AFC North at the bye, it’s in contention.

WHO’S ON THE BOARD: THE REALISTIC RANKING

Lots of names are floating around. Let me rank them by how realistic they actually are, based on the reporting.

Number one: Josh Downs, Colts. This is the warm trail. Downs requested a trade on Wednesday. Jordan Schultz named Pittsburgh among the interested teams. Evan Sidery reported that the Steelers and Chiefs “have both offered Day 3 draft compensation,” and that “a Downs deal is expected to go down after this weekend,” with Indianapolis likely to fetch “a 4th or 5th-round pick.”

And here’s the line that should make Steelers fans smile. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler went on The Rich Eisen Show and said Downs “sees the writing on the wall, wants to go play for a Patrick Mahomes in Kansas City maybe, or an Aaron Rodgers in Pittsburgh.” So it’s not just Pittsburgh wanting Downs. Downs reportedly sees Pittsburgh as a place he’d want to be.

Number two: Brian Thomas Jr., Jaguars. Higher ceiling, colder trail. ESPN ranked him second on its top 25 trade candidate list and named Pittsburgh as a fit. But Kaboly said the Steelers “were” interested and he hasn’t “heard anything recently about it.” SI’s Jacob Punturi wrote that Thomas would likely cost a Day 2 pick.

Number three: the Chargers duo. ESPN listed Quentin Johnston as a possible Steelers fit. Johnston is a bigger downfield target who’s had real drop issues, but sometimes a change of scenery helps. Then there’s Ladd McConkey, whom Jason La Canfora reportedly linked to Pittsburgh, citing a scout who thought he could help sustain drives.

Number four: DeVonta Smith, Eagles. A big name, but Post-Gazette beat writer Ray Fittipaldo wondered out loud whether Philadelphia even wants to part with him: “Who knows if the Eagles want to part ways with DeVonta Smith.”

THE MCCONKEY REALITY CHECK

Let’s spend a minute on McConkey, because his name got a lot of buzz this week, and Fittipaldo threw some cold water on it.

On 93.7 The Fan, Fittipaldo said, “I heard McConkey’s name yesterday,” but then added, “I think McConkey is a little bit too similar to some of the guys they have right now.”

That’s a fair point. McConkey is a precise route runner who works underneath and in the slot. He’s talented. Steelers fans remember what he did to them in 2025: four catches, 107 yards, and a touchdown. But this season he’s been banged up, with 13 catches for 200 yards and one touchdown in parts of five games.

And there’s the price. McConkey is a 2024 second-round pick still on his rookie deal, which means the Chargers would want real value for him. Downs offers a similar skill set at what looks like a much lower cost. If you’re going to buy a slot receiver, why pay a premium when a cheaper option is actively asking out?

THE BIDDING WAR WARNING

Fittipaldo also issued a warning in his Thursday mailbag that’s worth repeating: “There might be a bidding war for some of these receivers who are on the block.”

And we already know who Pittsburgh’s main competition is for Downs: Kansas City. The Chiefs have been short on healthy receivers, and they’ve got the quarterback Fowler mentioned first. So the question for Khan is simple. How high is he willing to go before he walks away?

The reporting suggests discipline. Schefter said the Colts originally asked for “third-round value,” and “nobody’s willing to do that right now.” Punturi reported Khan won’t give up a third. That’s the line in the sand.

WHY DOWNS FITS THIS OFFENSE

Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox put it well when he paired Downs with Pittsburgh: “Downs’ route-running and change-of-direction skills would fit well with the Pittsburgh Steelers’ precision-passing offense.”

Here’s the football reason. This offense has found an identity running the ball with Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle. That’s great until third-and-six, when everybody in the stadium knows Rodgers has to throw. That’s when you need a receiver who can win quickly against man coverage. Cleveland played man on 73.3 percent of its snaps in the first meeting, according to Steeler Nation. Plenty of playoff defenses will do the same.

Downs doesn’t need to be the star. He can slide into the role Pittman and Wilson were supposed to fill, line up inside or out, and take advantage of defenses that are focused on Metcalf and Bernard.

There’s also a familiarity angle. Downs and Pittman were teammates in Indianapolis. If Pittman does make it back, that’s an instant comfort level. And Downs had a front-row seat in Week 5, when Pittsburgh beat his Colts 34-22, with Rodgers throwing three touchdowns, two of them to Pat Freiermuth, while spreading the ball around. He’s seen what this offense can do when it clicks.

THE CONTRACT MAKES IT EASY

Downs is on the final year of his rookie deal. He can become a free agent in March. So this is a rental, and that’s exactly what you want in Rodgers’ final season.

A rental means low long-term risk. You’re not committing big money while Metcalf is already paid and Pittman’s future is uncertain. If Downs plays well and wants to stay, the Steelers can talk extension in the spring. If not, you got a receiver for the playoff push at a Day 3 price.

The Colts have been selling bigger names at bargain prices. Quenton Nelson went to Seattle for a third and a fifth. DeForest Buckner went to San Francisco for a fourth. With that market, a fourth or fifth for Downs is right in line.

THE BYE WEEK ADVANTAGE

Here’s why timing matters so much. Kaboly put it plainly: “If you acquire somebody during the bye week span, it would be the ideal time to get him acclimated to everything.”

If the Steelers make the deal early this week, the new receiver gets the entire bye to learn the playbook, sit in meetings, and build timing with Rodgers before the next game. And that next game is a big one: Sunday night, November 15, in Cincinnati. Then Philadelphia on November 22, Denver on Black Friday, and Houston on Sunday night in Week 13.

If they wait until deadline day on Tuesday, November 10, the new guy has days, not weeks. That’s the difference between a contributor and a spectator.

WHAT STEPHEN A. WARNED ABOUT BEFORE THE GAME

Before Sunday, Stephen A. Smith went on First Take with a message for the Steelers, and it ties directly into why Rodgers needs help.

He liked Pittsburgh’s home edge: “They’re in Pittsburgh. They’re on their home turf,” and noted, “Pittsburgh’s undefeated at home thus far this season.” But then came the warning about Cleveland’s defense, which he backed with numbers: 46 tackles for loss, 20 sacks, sixth in the NFL, and 11 different players with at least one sack. His verdict: “They are going to come for Aaron Rodgers.”

That’s the point. A 42-year-old quarterback can’t hold the ball forever against a defense like that. Rodgers took five sacks in the first meeting in Cleveland. A receiver who gets open in two seconds isn’t just a luxury. It’s protection.

WHAT HAPPENED SATURDAY: A CLUE

One more clue about how the front office is thinking. On Saturday, the Steelers signed Brandon Smith from the practice squad to the 53-man roster, and elevated Brandon Johnson for his third and final elevation. Marquez Valdes-Scantling, the veteran Rodgers openly wanted, was not elevated.

That tells you two things. First, the staff values Smith, who caught both of his targets for 24 yards in Paris, enough to give him a real roster spot. Second, the receiver room is still being held together with practice-squad tape. And the team announced no corresponding move when it signed Smith, which suggests the open roster spot is now filled. So a trade would likely require another roster move. That’s normal, but it’s a detail to watch.

THE PITTMAN TRADE, IN HINDSIGHT

It’s worth pausing on how the Pittman situation got here, because it explains why Khan can’t afford to wait.

Pittsburgh acquired Pittman from Indianapolis in March. The idea was simple: a big, reliable target to pair with Metcalf, a guy who’d been productive for years with the Colts. But the foot started bothering him early. He missed the Week 2 game in New England, then re-aggravated it, and the Steelers placed him on injured reserve on October 10, right before the Colts rematch he’d circled.

When he did play, the production was modest: seven catches for 89 yards in three games, according to Yahoo. And in the first Cleveland game, he caught two passes for 15 yards on four targets, with Steelers Depot noting he didn’t seem to have much chemistry with Rodgers yet.

None of that is a knock on Pittman as a player. Injuries happen. But it means the plan for the second receiver spot hasn’t worked, and now there’s a real chance it won’t work at all in 2026. That’s why this trade window matters so much.

WHAT ABOUT MVS?

There’s also an internal option that hasn’t gotten a real chance yet: Marquez Valdes-Scantling.

He’s been on the practice squad since October 20. He’s a nine-year veteran with 119 games, more than 3,700 career receiving yards, and two Super Bowl rings with Kansas City. He spent four seasons catching passes from Rodgers in Green Bay. And Rodgers has been his biggest advocate this week: “I love Marquez,” he said, calling him “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with,” a “super smart guy” with “really good hands” and “great instincts and great football intelligence.”

So why wasn’t he elevated for Cleveland? The staff hasn’t explained it publicly. Maybe they wanted Johnson’s special teams value. Maybe MVS needs more time to get his legs under him after joining midseason. But whatever the reason, the bye week is a perfect chance to give MVS a real look in practice alongside whoever Khan brings in. He’s a vertical threat, which is something Downs isn’t. In a perfect world, Pittsburgh adds Downs for the middle of the field and gets MVS ready to stretch it.

HOW A NEW RECEIVER CHANGES THE RED ZONE

There’s one more area where a new receiver could matter immediately: the red zone.

Pittsburgh’s red-zone offense ranked 27th in the league heading into the Browns game, per ClutchPoints. In Paris, the Steelers settled for field goals again and again. And McCarthy admitted it last week: “we could definitely score more touchdowns.”

Inside the 20, the field shrinks, and quickness becomes more valuable than size. A slot receiver who can win on a quick slant or a pivot route gives Rodgers a reliable option when the windows get tight. Pair that with Darnell Washington and Pat Freiermuth at tight end, and suddenly defenses can’t just sit on the big bodies near the goal line.

That’s the kind of upgrade that doesn’t always show up in yardage totals but shows up on the scoreboard. Turning two field goals into two touchdowns is an eight-point swing. In this division, that’s the difference between a win and a loss.

IF THE STEELERS WAIT, HERE’S THE RISK

Let me play devil’s advocate for a second. What if Khan decides to stand pat?

The argument for waiting goes like this: the offense has a clear identity with the run game, Bernard has emerged, Smith has earned a roster spot, Skowronek is close, and Pittman might come back. Why spend draft capital on a rental?

Here’s the problem with that argument. It assumes everything breaks right. It assumes Pittman doesn’t need surgery. It assumes Skowronek’s calf holds up. It assumes Bernard keeps producing like a veteran in his first season. It assumes no other receiver gets hurt down the stretch. That’s a lot of assumptions for a team with a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season.

The cost of a Day 3 pick is small. The cost of being one receiver short in a January playoff game is enormous. That’s why, in my view, standing pat is the riskier choice.

THE DIVISION PICTURE AT THE BYE

Whatever happened Sunday, here’s why the AFC North makes this trade matter even more.

Going into Week 8, Cleveland and Pittsburgh were both 5-2, Cincinnati was 4-2, and Baltimore was 4-3 and 0-2 in the division. As Stephen A. Smith pointed out, the two teams most people picked to run this division, the Ravens and Bengals, were sitting third and fourth heading into this week.

Right after the bye, Pittsburgh heads to Cincinnati on Sunday night, November 15. Later, there are two games against Baltimore, including the regular-season finale on the road. Every one of those games is going to be tight, physical, and likely decided by a handful of plays. Having one more receiver Rodgers trusts could decide more than one of them.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Make the Downs deal this week. A fourth-round pick, maybe a fourth plus a late-round sweetener, is my ceiling. Don’t pay a third. Don’t touch the 2028 second-rounder from the Joey Porter Jr. trade. If Kansas City outbids that, let them.

If Downs goes elsewhere, my backup plan is Quentin Johnston, a bigger target who adds something different from what Pittsburgh already has. I’d pass on McConkey at the price the Chargers would likely want. Fittipaldo is right: he overlaps too much with what’s already in the room, and Downs does a similar job for less.

And whatever happens, the Pittman news means one thing for me. Pittsburgh shouldn’t plan on him. If he comes back healthy, great, that’s a bonus. But the trade should be made as if he’s not walking through that door.

WHAT TO WATCH THE NEXT FEW DAYS

One. The Colts’ next move. Indianapolis played Jacksonville on Sunday. With that game done, Downs talks should pick up.

Two. Kansas City’s aggressiveness. If the Chiefs strike first, Pittsburgh pivots.

Three. Pittman’s medical update. If surgery is confirmed, the urgency jumps.

Four. A corresponding roster move. If you see the Steelers release or place someone on injured reserve early this week, that could be the signal a deal is close.
