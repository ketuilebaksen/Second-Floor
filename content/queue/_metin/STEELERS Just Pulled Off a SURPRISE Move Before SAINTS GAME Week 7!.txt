Steelers Nation, the Steelers are in Paris, and the final picture for Sunday’s game against the Saints is coming into focus. Some of the news is a relief, some of it is a surprise, and one decision still has to be made today. T.J. Watt went from a calf scare to a full participant. The Steelers’ walkthrough at the Stade de France got wiped out by the weather. DeShon Elliott is listed as questionable, but reporters say Pittsburgh plans to have him on the field. And with two defensive linemen on injured reserve, Patrick Graham has a puzzle to solve up front. So today, we’re breaking down the final injury report, the moves that set up Sunday, how the defensive line handles its injuries, and the trade ideas that keep coming up. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The surprise from Paris: weather wins the first round.

Let’s start with something nobody planned for. After landing in Paris on Friday morning, the Steelers were scheduled to hold a walkthrough at the Stade de France. Instead, according to Pro Football Talk, bad weather canceled it. That means Friday’s injury report was an estimate, not a real practice report, and Saturday’s session becomes the final full chance to prepare before kickoff.

It’s not a disaster. Walkthroughs are light work anyway. But it’s a reminder of why McCarthy kept the team in Pittsburgh for most of the week. The Steelers got their real practices done at home, and they knew the trip itself could bring surprises. On Friday, it did.

Why Saturday’s practice matters so much.

With Friday’s walkthrough canceled, Saturday’s session in Paris is now the most important practice of the trip. It’s the last chance for Elliott to show his knee is ready, for Dowdle to test his knee, and for Watt and Highsmith to confirm they can go full speed. It’s also the first time many of these players will get a feel for the conditions in France. Expect the coaching staff to keep it crisp, focus on communication and situational football, and get everyone off their feet quickly. Then the decisions come: who gets activated, who gets elevated, and who sits.

Update one: T.J. Watt is good to go.

Now the best news of the week. On Thursday, Watt showed up on the injury report with a calf issue and was limited in practice, and Steelers fans everywhere held their breath. On Friday, he was listed as a full participant with no game designation. According to Pro Football Talk, McCarthy said he does not expect the injury to affect Watt’s availability. When he was asked whether Watt’s status was in question, his answer was short: “I would say no.”

That’s a big relief. But I still want to be careful. Being cleared to play and being at full strength aren’t always the same thing, especially with a calf, which affects how a pass rusher pushes off and bends around the edge. If Watt plays, I want to see the burst that makes him so dangerous, not just his jersey on the field. And that’s why the rotation behind him matters this week.

Update two: Highsmith, Ramsey, and Heyward are trending up.

Alex Highsmith, who was limited on Wednesday and Thursday with a groin injury, was listed as a full participant on Friday’s estimated report, with no game designation. The same goes for Jalen Ramsey and his wrist, and for Cam Heyward, who had been getting veteran rest days. All three are expected to play.

For Highsmith, that’s encouraging, because groin injuries have been a recurring problem for him. For Ramsey, the AFC Defensive Player of the Week, it means the Steelers will have their most versatile defensive back on the field. And for Heyward, the rest days were about managing a veteran in a travel week, not about an injury.

And remember what Ramsey did last week. Playing with a broken wrist, he put up 10 tackles, four tackles for loss, and two sacks, including the sack that ended the game in overtime. He’s the first defensive back to post those numbers in a single game since 2000. Having him at full participation heading into Sunday is a huge boost, especially with Echols out and the cornerback depth thin.

Update three: Dowdle is questionable.

On offense, Rico Dowdle is listed as questionable with a knee issue. He was limited all three days this week. That’s a new injury, coming right after he returned from the toe injury that cost him three games, and right after he ran for 40 yards on seven carries in Tampa.

If Dowdle is limited or can’t go, Jaylen Warren becomes the clear workhorse. And after this week’s debate about how the backfield was split in Tampa, a lot of fans won’t mind seeing Warren carry the load from the opening drive. Behind him, the depth chart lists Eli Heidenreich, Travis Homer, and Lew Nichols, who was signed to the active roster last weekend.

What a full Warren workload could look like.

If Dowdle can’t go, this game could become a real test of what Warren can do as a true workhorse. Two weeks ago against the Colts, with Dowdle out, Warren carried 15 times for 101 yards, caught six passes for 87 yards, scored twice, and won AFC Offensive Player of the Week. McCarthy has called him the “featured player right now” and “one of the hottest players in the National Football League.” Against a Saints defense that’s had its own injuries this week, that’s an opportunity, not a problem.

The key is how McCarthy uses him early. In Tampa, the offense didn’t find its rhythm until the fourth quarter. If Warren gets 8 or 10 touches in the first half instead of splitting them, it could set the tone for the entire afternoon.

Update four: the DeShon Elliott decision.

And now the biggest decision of the weekend. Elliott is listed as questionable, and he’s still technically on injured reserve. According to Pro Football Talk, the Steelers have to activate him by Saturday afternoon if he’s going to play Sunday. And because the roster has open spots after the Jenkins release and the Joseph-Day injury, they don’t need to cut anyone to make room.

According to Heavy, reporters including Mark Kaboly indicate the Steelers plan to have Elliott on the field Sunday morning. McCarthy said the final call comes after Saturday’s practice. And he made it clear how much Elliott means to this team: “It’s been a long road back, but he’s a big part of what we’re about in Pittsburgh.”

Elliott is 29, and he’s been a productive player for a long time. He was a sixth-round pick of the Ravens in 2018, played a season each with Detroit and Miami, and joined Pittsburgh in 2024, when he set a career high with 108 combined tackles. Last season, he played just five games before the knee and hamstring injuries ended his year. He has 433 career tackles, six forced fumbles, and five fumble recoveries in 77 games.

And remember, his 21-day window is nearly up, so this isn’t a decision the Steelers can push back for long. Here’s my take. If his knee responds well on Saturday, activate him. But don’t throw him into 70 snaps right away. Give him a defined role, keep rookie Robert Spears-Jennings involved, and let Jaquan Brisker keep his spot in the rotation. The Steelers need Elliott for November, December, and January, not just for one game in Paris.

Why Elliott matters against this offense.

There’s a matchup reason to want Elliott back, too. The Saints have playmakers who can hurt you if the back end isn’t communicating, including Chris Olave. When safeties assume someone else has the deep help, that’s when big plays happen. Elliott’s biggest strength has always been his voice, getting everyone lined up and making sure the coverage is on the same page. Even in a limited role, that communication could be worth a lot against an offense looking for one explosive play to change the game.

The defensive line puzzle.

Now let’s talk about the part of this defense that worries me most heading into Sunday: the interior line. Derrick Harmon is on injured reserve with a foot injury. Sebastian Joseph-Day joined him on injured reserve this week with a triceps injury. Those are confirmed absences, not game-time decisions.

That means the snaps inside fall to Heyward, Keeanu Benton, Yahya Black, and Gabriel Rubio. Heyward is still the standard-setter, but I don’t want the plan to be asking a veteran to play every snap. Benton has to anchor the middle, and he’s been earning that role, especially against the run in Tampa. For Black and Rubio, the job is simple but important: hold your ground, understand the call, and let the next defender make the play.

Why early downs matter so much.

And here’s why that matters. If the Saints can run the ball for comfortable gains on first down, the Steelers’ pass rush never gets to take over. Watt, Herbig, and Highsmith are at their best when the quarterback is facing third-and-long. To get there, the interior has to win on first and second down.

That’s exactly what happened in Tampa. Bucky Irving had just 46 yards on 19 carries, the Buccaneers went 6-for-19 on third down, and the pass rush finished with five sacks. The formula is clear. Stop the run, create obvious passing downs, and let the edge rushers go to work.

The Saints will test that. Alvin Kamara was limited in practice all week with a back issue, but he didn’t receive a game designation, so he’s expected to play. Running back C.J. Donaldson was a full participant on Friday. Linebacker Kaden Elliss and edge rusher Anfernee Jennings were also full participants, and quarterback Tyler Shough practiced fully despite a left hand injury. Guard Cesar Ruiz and defensive lineman Nathan Shepherd are questionable.

The edge rotation.

If Watt or Highsmith needs fewer snaps, Nick Herbig is the first man up. And I don’t want to judge Herbig only by sacks. Can he keep the quarterback contained? Can he stay disciplined against the run? A young pass rusher can make an exciting play and still hurt the defense if he abandons his assignment on the next snap. Herbig has shown he can do both. He had two sacks in Tampa, and the Steelers committed to him with a four-year extension in June.

Jack Sawyer could also get more snaps. I’m not asking him to be Watt. That’s an unfair expectation for anyone. I want him to give the starters a breather without leaving a weakness the Saints can attack. Useful snaps, steady effort, smart decisions.

What Graham has to do.

That puts the spotlight on Patrick Graham. With a thinner rotation, he can’t call every pressure as if the front were fully healthy. I’d rather see clear assignments carried out well than an aggressive call that leaves somebody confused. When he sends extra rushers, the coverage behind them has to know exactly where the ball is going.

And the numbers say this defense has earned some trust. According to Heavy, the Steelers are now in the top half of the league in yards allowed per game, at 307.5, and in passing yards allowed, at 204.3. That’s real improvement from where this unit started the season. The goal on Sunday is to keep that going with fewer bodies.

Finishing drives on defense.

One more thing I’ll be watching: getting off the field. Forcing third down only helps if the defense actually makes the stop. A missed tackle short of the sticks or a penalty away from the ball can erase two good plays. For a rotation that’s already missing players, every extra snap adds up. The young players need to know the distance, stay aware of the quarterback, and make New Orleans earn every first down.

How the offense can help the defense.

And the offense has a big role in this too. In Tampa, the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters, which kept the defense on the field. Against the Saints, I want long drives, smart decisions, and points. Rodgers doesn’t need to avoid every risk, but a short field after a turnover is the last thing a thin defensive line needs.

At receiver, the Steelers are short again. Ben Skowronek was ruled out before the trip with his calf. Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. are on injured reserve. That leaves DK Metcalf, Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen on the active roster, with Brandon Smith, Brandon Johnson, and Marquez Valdes-Scantling as practice squad options. McCarthy said MVS “looked good in practice,” and added that he “looked like he stepped right in, like he never left.” Whether he’s elevated for Sunday is another decision that should come on Saturday.

Three keys for Sunday.

Let me boil it all down to three keys for this game.

Key number one: win on early downs. With the interior line thinned out, the Steelers have to keep the Saints from running for easy yards on first and second down. If they force third-and-long, the pass rush can take over, just like it did in Tampa.

Key number two: start fast on offense. No more waiting until the fourth quarter. Feed Warren early, get Metcalf involved in the first quarter like Rodgers asked for, and give the defense a lead to play with. A defense that’s missing bodies plays a lot better when it’s protecting a lead.

Key number three: handle the environment. A foreign stadium, a time change, an early kickoff back home, and no walkthrough on Friday. The team that handles the distractions better usually wins these games. The Steelers’ veterans, from Rodgers to Heyward to Watt, have to set that tone.

Trade block: help on the defensive line?

Let’s talk about the deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

With Harmon and Joseph-Day both on injured reserve, interior defensive line depth has moved up my list of needs. Some names I’d at least think about are Zach Sieler in Miami, Da’Ron Payne in Washington, and Jordan Davis in Philadelphia. To be clear, that’s my list of possibilities, not a claim that Pittsburgh is talking to any of those teams, or that those teams want to trade those players.

Finding a player you like is easy. Figuring out whether he’s available, affordable, and worth the picks is the hard part. What does the other team want? What contract would Pittsburgh take on? How much would the addition still matter once Harmon and Joseph-Day are back? I can support paying for a meaningful upgrade. I can’t support spending heavily just to make one ugly injury report feel better for a few days.

And the Colts’ situation is still worth watching. After firing their general manager, ESPN reported that rival teams expect Indianapolis to sell, and the Colts have several veteran defensive tackles, including Grover Stewart and Jerry Tillery. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline.

What I don’t want to see.

There are also a couple of ideas I’d pass on. I don’t see much reason to trade Mason Rudolph to Philadelphia, even with the Eagles dealing with Jalen Hurts’ health situation. Quarterback depth matters too much behind a 42-year-old starter. And I’m not convinced moving Patrick Queen would help this defense right now either. Every trade has to make the roster better, not just different.

A historic morning in Paris.

And let’s not lose sight of how special Sunday is. This is the first regular-season NFL game ever played in Paris, and the Steelers are part of that history. Art Rooney II flew in on Thursday, and after joining the security conversations with the league this week, he said the NFL will have heightened measures in place, but “they don’t seem to be concerned.” The league has said it’s confident in its plans. For the players, the message from McCarthy has been simple: it’s a business trip. Enjoy the moment, but take care of business.

The bottom line.

So what’s the surprise heading into Sunday? Mother Nature canceled the Steelers’ first look at the Stade de France, but the injury news is mostly good. Watt is good to go after his calf scare. Highsmith, Ramsey, and Heyward are all trending toward playing. Dowdle is questionable, which could put more on Warren’s plate. And the big decision, activating DeShon Elliott, is expected to come today, with reporters saying the Steelers plan to have him on the field.

The challenge is up front. With two defensive linemen on injured reserve, the Steelers need Heyward, Benton, Black, and Rubio to hold the line on early downs so the pass rush can take over. If they do that, this defense has a chance to dominate again.

The standings make this even bigger. The Steelers, Browns, and Ravens are all 4-2, with Cleveland holding first place on its division record. The Browns come to Pittsburgh next week. Win in Paris, and that game becomes a battle for first place in front of the home crowd.

Kickoff is Sunday at 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh time. A win would make it three straight and put this team right where it wants to be heading into the biggest game of the first half.
