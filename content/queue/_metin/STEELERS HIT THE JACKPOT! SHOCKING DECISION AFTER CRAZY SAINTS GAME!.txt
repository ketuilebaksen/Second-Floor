Steelers Nation, the Steelers are leaving Paris with a win, a piece of history, and a brand-new identity on offense. Pittsburgh beat the New Orleans Saints 16-10 at the Stade de France in front of 78,488 fans, in the first regular-season NFL game ever played in France. They’re now 5-2, they’ve won three straight, and next week the Browns come to Acrisure Stadium with first place in the AFC North on the line. But the real story of this game is the decision Mike McCarthy made before kickoff, a decision that would have shocked you if you’d watched this offense for the first six weeks. A team that had been one of the most pass-heavy in the league walked into Paris and ran the Saints into the ground. So today, we’re breaking down how the Steelers hit the jackpot with the run game, the defensive dominance, Aaron Rodgers’ new place in the record books, and what it all means for the Browns showdown. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The shocking decision: own the line of scrimmage.

Let’s start with the decision, because it’s the headline. Coming into this game, according to NFL.com, the Steelers had the second-highest pass rate in the NFL, throwing on about 68 percent of their plays. Just a week earlier in Tampa, they ran the ball only 22 times while Rodgers threw 49 passes.

In Paris, they flipped the script. The Steelers ran 30 times for 148 yards, an average of 4.9 yards per carry. And they finished the game with an 11-play drive that took five minutes and 52 seconds off the clock, and every single play on that drive was a run.

McCarthy explained it simply after the game: “We felt as a football team that we needed to own the line of scrimmage on both sides.” And then: “That was the goal to get the rush attempts up.”

Offensive lineman Spencer Anderson put it even more bluntly: “We’re going to run it again, again, and again.” That’s the kind of mentality Steelers fans grew up with, and it showed up in Paris.

The jackpot: Warren and Dowdle.

And the two backs delivered. Jaylen Warren ran for 101 yards on 18 carries, 5.6 yards per carry, and he added a 26-yard catch out of the backfield. He ripped off a 29-yard run on just the second offensive play of the game, and that set the tone for the afternoon.

Rico Dowdle, who was questionable all week with a knee injury, was active and productive. He carried nine times for 41 yards and was also a regular target for Rodgers as a receiver. Between the two of them, the Steelers had a one-two punch that New Orleans never figured out.

Remember the debate all week about how McCarthy split the backfield in Tampa? This was the answer. Warren got the bigger share, Dowdle got meaningful work, and both made plays. McCarthy summed it up perfectly: “I think anytime we can just line up and give you Jaylen and Rico all day, that’s a good day for us.”

Rodgers said the team knew this was coming: “We knew coming in, we could run against them.” And about Warren’s consistency, he added that the team is “almost expecting Jaylen to have 100 yards.” That’s now two 100-yard games in his last three. Warren himself explained the plan: “We knew what we knew about them. So we wanted to, you know, use our strength against their weaknesses.”

McCarthy’s travel gamble paid off.

There’s another decision that deserves credit. All week, people questioned McCarthy’s choice to keep the team in Pittsburgh until Thursday night, while the Saints flew to France right after their previous game. Even Rodgers reportedly said he would have preferred getting overseas earlier. Then the Steelers’ Friday walkthrough at the Stade de France was canceled because of weather.

None of it mattered. The Steelers came out sharp, controlled the line of scrimmage, and finished stronger than the team that had been in France all week. That doesn’t mean one approach is always right. But McCarthy made a call, stuck with it, and his team delivered. When the head coach’s plan works on both the travel and the game plan in the same week, it builds trust in the locker room.

The defense: 18 rushing yards.

Now let’s talk about the other side of the line of scrimmage, because this might be the most impressive number of the season. The Saints ran the ball 17 times and gained 18 yards. That’s 1.1 yards per carry. Alvin Kamara, who was limited all week with a back issue, and the rest of the New Orleans backfield had nowhere to go.

And remember, the Steelers did that without Derrick Harmon and Sebastian Joseph-Day, who are both on injured reserve. All week, the interior line was the biggest concern heading into this game. Cam Heyward, Keeanu Benton, Yahya Black, and the rest of that rotation answered every question. When you hold a team to 18 rushing yards, you make them one-dimensional, and that’s exactly what happened.

Shough threw it all day, but the defense bent at the right moments.

Because the Saints couldn’t run, Tyler Shough had to throw, and he threw a lot. He completed 30 of 38 passes for 278 yards, with a touchdown and an interception. Chris Olave hurt the Steelers with a 49-yard catch. So this wasn’t a perfect day for the secondary.

But when it mattered most, the defense made the plays. Let’s walk through them.

The key defensive plays.

Early in the second quarter, T.J. Watt sacked Shough and forced the Saints to settle for a field goal. Watt also appeared to force a fumble on the play, but it was overturned on review because Shough was ruled down before the ball came out. So the calf scare from earlier in the week? Watt answered it on the field.

Then came the biggest play of the game. To open the second half, the Saints put together a 13-play drive that reached the Steelers’ 11-yard line. And Payton Wilson stepped in front of a pass intended for Olave and picked it off. Zero points for New Orleans. That interception led directly to the Steelers’ final score.

In the fourth quarter, Saints kicker Daniel Carlson missed a 52-yard field goal. And then, with about six minutes left, Patrick Queen sacked Shough on third down to force a punt. Queen said afterward that he remembered advice from veteran linebacker Elandon Roberts: “Be who you are.” After that punt, the Steelers got the ball back and never gave it up.

How the game unfolded.

Let’s go through the scoring. Chris Boswell opened it with a 28-yard field goal in the first quarter. The Saints answered with a 38-yarder early in the second, after that Watt sack stalled their drive, to tie it at three.

Then DK Metcalf made the play of the day on offense, a 51-yard catch that set up a touchdown. From inside the 5, Rodgers threw a fade to Darnell Washington for a 4-yard touchdown, making it 10-3.

The Saints tied it at 10 with 1:45 left in the half on a 1-yard touchdown pass from Shough to Devaughn Vele. But Boswell drilled a 47-yard field goal as time expired, and the Steelers took a 13-10 lead into halftime.

After Wilson’s interception in the third quarter, Boswell added a 39-yarder to make it 16-10. And from there, the defense and the running game closed it out. Boswell finished perfect on his three field goals, including the 47-yarder at the end of the half, and after last week’s miss in Tampa, that’s exactly the kind of bounce-back you want from a veteran kicker.

Payton Wilson’s big moment.

I want to single out Payton Wilson for a second. A week ago in Tampa, he led the Steelers with 11 tackles. In Paris, he made the play that decided the game, jumping in front of a pass at the Steelers’ 11-yard line on a drive where the Saints had been moving the ball easily. Linebackers who can make plays against the run and in coverage are rare, and Wilson is becoming one of the most important players on this defense. If he keeps this up, he’s going to be a huge part of this team’s identity for years.

A historic day.

And let’s not lose sight of how special this was. The first regular-season NFL game ever played in France, in front of more than 78,000 fans at the Stade de France, with Terrible Towels waving across the stadium. The week leading up to it came with real concerns about the unrest in France, and the league and the Steelers worked through all of it. Art Rooney II flew in to be there in person. And the Steelers walked out of Paris with a win that will be part of franchise history.

Rodgers passes Peyton Manning.

And now the history. That touchdown pass to Washington moved Aaron Rodgers past Peyton Manning into sole possession of third place on the NFL’s all-time regular-season touchdown passes list. A week after tying Manning in Tampa, he passed him in Paris.

And here’s a fun one: according to CBS Sports, Rodgers is now the first quarterback to throw touchdown passes in both Ireland and France. The Steelers played in Dublin last season, and now Rodgers has made history on two international stages.

His line wasn’t flashy: 19 of 30 for 212 yards, one touchdown, and a passer rating of 95.4. But late in the game, the 42-year-old even ran for a first down to help run out the clock. On a day built around the running game, he managed it perfectly. And after the game, he praised Warren in four words: “He’s just so dynamic.”

Why the run game changes everything.

Think about what this means going forward. For six weeks, defenses could sit back and wait for Rodgers to throw. They knew the Steelers wanted to pass, so they could drop extra defenders into coverage and dare the offense to run. That’s part of why the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters in Tampa.

Now opponents have film of the Steelers running 30 times and closing a game with 11 straight runs. Defensive coordinators will have to respect that. They’ll have to bring a safety down closer to the line of scrimmage. And when they do, that opens up one-on-one matchups for Metcalf and play action for Rodgers. That’s exactly what happened on the 51-yard catch that set up the touchdown. The run game doesn’t just pick up yards. It makes the passing game easier.

It also protects the defense. The Steelers were 6-for-9 on third down at halftime and held the ball for more than 16 minutes in the first half alone. When your offense keeps the ball, your defense stays fresh. And a fresh defense is a big reason the Saints were held to 18 rushing yards.

What wasn’t perfect.

Now, I always want to be honest with you, and this game wasn’t perfect. The Steelers went just 1-for-4 in the red zone. They settled for three field goals in a game where touchdowns would have made it much more comfortable. A 20-yard completion from Rodgers to Germie Bernard was wiped out by a penalty on guard Mason McCormick.

And NFL.com pointed out some ongoing concerns: the passing game’s consistency against man coverage, sacks on third down, and finding the right mix of pressure on defense. Shough completing almost 79 percent of his passes is something Patrick Graham will look at closely this week.

There’s also this from NFL.com: the Steelers’ three straight wins have come against the Colts, Buccaneers, and Saints. Their only win this season over a team that currently has a winning record came against the 4-2 Bengals. That’s not a knock on the team. You can only beat who’s on your schedule. But it does mean next week is a real measuring stick.

DeShon Elliott’s return.

And let’s not forget the other storyline from this weekend. DeShon Elliott was activated from injured reserve on Saturday after missing the first six games, and he was back in uniform for the first time since a knee injury ended his 2025 season five games in. For a defense that held a team to 18 rushing yards, having another physical safety in the back end is a big deal, and getting him through his first game back is a win in itself.

Now, the race for the AFC North.

So where does this leave the Steelers? Let’s look at the division, because it got very interesting on Sunday.

The Browns won too, coming back from a 14-0 deficit to beat the Titans 27-24. Cleveland is 5-2 and 2-0 in the division, which keeps the Browns in first place on the tiebreaker. The Bengals stunned the Ravens in Baltimore, coming back from a 13-0 halftime deficit to win 35-13. Cincinnati is 4-2. And the Ravens, after that second-half collapse, are 4-3 and in last place in the division.

That means next Sunday, the Steelers and Browns meet at Acrisure Stadium, both at 5-2, with first place on the line. Kickoff is at 1 p.m. And remember, Cleveland beat Pittsburgh 27-24 on a Thursday night earlier this season. This is the rematch.

The Browns: what’s different this time.

Let’s take a closer look at this rematch, because the first meeting still stings. On a Thursday night in Cleveland earlier this season, the Browns beat the Steelers 27-24, and Pittsburgh dropped to 2-2. Since then, the Steelers have won three straight. And the Browns have kept winning too, including that comeback from 14-0 down in Tennessee on Sunday.

This time, the game is in Pittsburgh, on a full week of preparation, with the Steelers coming off a performance where they ran for 148 yards and held an opponent to 18. If the Steelers can carry that same physical identity into this game, it becomes a very different matchup from the first meeting, when the Steelers were still searching for an identity. And with Cleveland holding the tiebreaker right now, this is the Steelers’ chance to flip it.

McCarthy’s message: a four-game series.

McCarthy is already framing it the right way. After the game, he described this stretch as “a four-game series,” and said, “It’s important for us to go 4-0.” The Steelers are now 3-0 in that series. Win next week, and they head into the bye on a four-game winning streak and alone in first place in the AFC North.

And the players are ready. When Warren was asked about the Browns rematch, his answer was short: “We can’t wait.”

Trade block: does Paris change the plan?

Let’s talk about the deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

The run game’s success in Paris doesn’t erase the receiver problem. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are still on injured reserve, and the Steelers went 1-for-4 in the red zone. When the field shrinks, you need receivers who can win quickly against man coverage, and that’s still a weakness. So I still think a receiver is the top priority before the November 10th deadline, and Josh Downs in Indianapolis is still the name I like most, with the Colts expected to sell after firing their general manager.

But what Paris does tell us is that the Steelers don’t need a blockbuster to win. They need balance. If Khan can add a reliable target without giving up a premium pick, this offense becomes much harder to defend, because now opponents have to respect the run, too. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the deadline.

And on the defensive side, Paris makes the case even stronger for keeping Alex Highsmith, whose name popped up on a trade board this weekend. A defense that holds a team to 18 rushing yards doesn’t need to be broken up. It needs to be kept together.

What to watch this week.

So what’s next? Monday’s press conference with McCarthy will give us injury updates after the long trip. Keep an eye on Dowdle’s knee, Watt’s calf, and Highsmith’s groin. Watch whether Brandon Smith, who used his third practice squad elevation this season, gets signed to the active roster. And watch whether Marquez Valdes-Scantling gets his chance against the Browns after a full week of practice at home.

The bottom line.

So did the Steelers hit the jackpot in Paris? I think they did. They made history in the first NFL game in France. They found a running game that can carry them, with Warren going over 100 yards again and Dowdle chipping in on the ground and through the air. Their defense held the Saints to 18 rushing yards. Rodgers passed Peyton Manning. And the shocking decision, turning one of the league’s most pass-heavy offenses into a team that closed the game with 11 straight runs, might be the most important adjustment McCarthy has made all season.

It wasn’t perfect. The red zone has to be better, and the pass defense gave up too many completions. But the Steelers are 5-2, winners of three straight, and one game away from sitting alone in first place. That’s exactly where they want to be. A month ago, people were writing this team off after a Thursday night loss in Cleveland. Now the Steelers get a chance to settle that score at home, with a running game that finally matches their defense.
