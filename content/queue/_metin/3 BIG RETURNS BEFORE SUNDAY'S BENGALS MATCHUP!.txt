Steelers Nation, the final injury report for Sunday night is out, and for the first time in a long time, it’s a short one.

Pittsburgh heads to Cincinnati with three players returning to the picture at the perfect moment. A veteran receiver who caught a touchdown in the first meeting is back after missing three games. A starting safety who was limited on Wednesday finished the week with two full practices. And a young defensive tackle who made the play that won the September game is officially questionable to play.

Let me be clear right away about what “return” means for each of them, because it’s not the same thing. Two of these players are on track to play with no game status at all. The third depends on a roster decision the Steelers still have to make. I’ll explain exactly where each one stands, and why one of them deserves an extra look.

We’ll also go through what the Bengals’ injury report says, including a former Steeler who’s been ruled out. And as always, my board at the end, opinion only.

First place in the AFC North is on the line. Both teams are six and two. Let’s get into it.

THE FINAL INJURY REPORT AT A GLANCE

Here’s the big picture, according to the Steelers’ final report as summarized by Yahoo Sports and Athlon Sports.

Only one Steelers player has a game status: defensive tackle Derrick Harmon, listed as questionable with a foot injury.

Everyone else on the report is expected to play. That list includes receiver Ben Skowronek with a calf injury, safety DeShon Elliott with an ankle injury, and cornerbacks Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both coming back from hamstring issues. T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward and Darnell Washington were on the report for rest only, and all three practiced fully on Friday.

According to Athlon, every player on Pittsburgh’s report was a full participant on Friday. That’s about as clean as a mid-November injury report gets.

RETURN NUMBER ONE: BEN SKOWRONEK IS BACK

Let’s start with the player who’s officially ready to go.

Ben Skowronek has missed the last three games with a calf injury. This week, he was a full participant on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, and he has no game status. The transcript cites Steelers Now saying Skowronek confirmed he’s ready to return, and that he feels good and is excited about the second half of the season.

Now, some fans might shrug at this one. Skowronek isn’t a household name. But let me remind you what he did the last time Pittsburgh played Cincinnati, because it’s a lot more than most people remember.

WHAT SKOWRONEK DID IN SEPTEMBER

According to the Steelers’ official recap of the 30-27 win on September 27, Skowronek was involved in two of the game’s turning points.

The first came late in the opening quarter. With 52 seconds left in the first, Aaron Rodgers hit Skowronek for a 23-yard touchdown that put Pittsburgh up 14-7. The team noted it was his first touchdown since Week 1 of the previous season.

The second came in the fourth quarter, and it’s the one people forget. Skowronek recovered a muffed punt. The Steelers couldn’t turn it into a touchdown, but Chris Boswell’s 36-yard field goal off that turnover tied the game at 27 with 8:45 to play. Without that recovery, there’s no tie, and maybe no comeback.

So in a game decided by three points, a depth receiver produced seven points on offense and set up three more on special teams. That’s why his return matters.

WHAT SKOWRONEK BRINGS, AND WHAT HE DOESN’T

The transcript describes Skowronek as a physical receiver, a reliable depth option and an important special teams player. I’d agree with all three, and I’d put special teams first.

When a team is short on receivers, special teams suffer quietly. Coverage units lose a regular. Blocking assignments shift. The return game loses a body it trusts. Getting Skowronek back stabilizes those units at the exact moment Pittsburgh is heading into a hostile road environment where field position can swing a game.

But the transcript is honest about the limit here, and I want to be too. Skowronek doesn’t solve the receiver problem. DK Metcalf still needs help, and the passing game still needs more consistency from everyone. What Skowronek gives Rodgers is a trusted, experienced option on third downs and in the red zone. That’s valuable. It’s not a transformation.

And the other reinforcements are still a ways off. Roman Wilson, who caught a 38-yard touchdown in that same September game, can’t come off injured reserve until after this one. Michael Pittman Jr.’s practice window still hasn’t opened. So for Sunday night, Skowronek is the only receiver returning, and he’ll be lining up alongside Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard and the tight ends.

RETURN NUMBER TWO: DESHON ELLIOTT IS GOOD TO GO

Now let’s go to the back end of the defense.

DeShon Elliott popped up on the report this week with an ankle injury. He was limited on Wednesday, which got fans nervous. Then he practiced in full on Thursday and again on Friday, and the team gave him no game status. According to Athlon, he’s expected to play.

The transcript explains why that matters so much. Elliott helps organize the defense, communicates assignments and brings physicality to the secondary. Against Joe Burrow, communication is everything. Burrow is at his best when he catches a defense in a busted assignment or a late rotation. A veteran safety who can get everyone lined up correctly is one of the best defenses against that.

Remember how Cincinnati’s offense stayed in the September game. With 34 seconds left in the first half, after a Rodgers interception, Burrow hit Tee Higgins for a 28-yard touchdown that tied the score at 17. Plays like that are what the safety position is supposed to prevent.

And with Echols and Slaughter also fully healthy, the Steelers’ secondary looks as close to whole as it’s been in weeks. That’s a real boost against Ja’Marr Chase and Higgins.

RETURN NUMBER THREE: DERRICK HARMON IS QUESTIONABLE

And now, the one everybody’s been asking about.

Derrick Harmon has been on injured reserve with a foot injury. His 21-day practice window opened on Wednesday, and he took a clear step forward over the course of the week. Limited on Wednesday. Limited on Thursday. Then a full participant on Friday. The team listed him as questionable, and according to Athlon, a final decision on activating him is expected Saturday.

The transcript quotes Harmon saying he feels 100% and that he’s taking it day by day. That combination tells you he wants to play, but he’s respecting the process.

Here’s the catch, and the transcript explains it correctly. Harmon is still on injured reserve. To play Sunday, he has to be activated to the 53-man roster. And according to Yahoo Sports, Pittsburgh needs a corresponding move to make room. Nobody has reported who that would be, so I’m not going to guess.

If the Steelers decide to wait, that’s not bad news. The window gives them three weeks. They could choose to give Harmon one more week and target the Philadelphia game. But a full practice on Friday makes it look like he’s at least in the conversation for Sunday night.

WHAT THE ROSTER MOVE COULD LOOK LIKE

Let’s talk about how this actually works, without inventing names.

When a team activates a player from injured reserve and the 53-man roster is full, it has a few general options. It can release a player. It can move someone else to injured reserve. Or it can make some other transaction, like waiving a player with the hope of bringing him back on the practice squad if he clears waivers.

As the rules are generally understood, teams typically have until 4 p.m. Eastern the day before a game to finalize those moves. So for a Sunday night kickoff, the deadline is Saturday afternoon. That lines up with Athlon’s report that a decision is expected Saturday.

Here’s what I’d watch for. If the Steelers announce a transaction involving a defensive lineman or a special teamer on Saturday, that’s likely the corresponding move. If nothing is announced by Saturday afternoon, Harmon probably isn’t playing, and the team will try again next week.

Either way, I’d expect the coaching staff to make this decision based on medical clearance, not on the stakes of the game. A second-year first-round pick is too valuable to rush back for one night, no matter how big that night is.

A QUICK CORRECTION ABOUT HARMON

I need to fix something, and part of it is on me.

The transcript calls Harmon a first-round pick, and that’s correct. He was Pittsburgh’s first-round pick in the 2025 draft. That means he’s in his second NFL season, not his first. In a couple of recent videos, I described him as a rookie, and that was wrong. He’s a second-year player. My apologies for that one.

It doesn’t change anything about how important he is. If anything, it explains why the coaching staff trusts him in big moments. This isn’t a kid getting his first taste of a divisional game. He’s already made a game-winning play against this exact opponent.

WHY HARMON’S SACK MEANT SO MUCH

Let’s go back to that play one more time, because the details make it even bigger.

According to the Steelers’ recap, Harmon’s strip-sack of Burrow was Pittsburgh’s first sack of the entire game. Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered the fumble with 44 seconds left, and that sealed the 30-27 win.

Think about that. For more than 59 minutes, the Steelers couldn’t get Burrow on the ground. Then, on the play that mattered most, Harmon did it.

That’s the case for activating him now. Burrow gets the ball out quickly, and sacks against him are rare. You want the player who has already proven he can win that rep in that situation.

THE FIRST MEETING, PLAY BY PLAY

Since so much of this week’s story connects back to September, it’s worth walking through how that game actually unfolded. The Steelers’ official recap lays it out.

Pittsburgh struck first. With 1:50 left in the opening quarter, Rodgers hit Roman Wilson for a 38-yard touchdown. Cincinnati answered with a methodical drive to tie it. Then Skowronek’s 23-yarder put the Steelers back on top before the quarter ended.

The Bengals tied it again at 17 just before halftime, on the Higgins touchdown that followed a Rodgers interception.

In the third quarter, on 3rd-and-2 with 2:43 left, Rodgers found DK Metcalf for a 6-yard touchdown. It was Metcalf’s first score of the season, and it put Pittsburgh ahead 24-20.

Cincinnati went back in front in the fourth. Then came the Skowronek fumble recovery and Boswell’s 36-yarder to tie it at 27. With 2:15 left, Boswell hit again from 33 yards to make it 30-27. And with 44 seconds left, Harmon ended it.

Rodgers finished 19 of 34 for 292 yards, three touchdowns and one interception. Three of the key players in that sequence, Wilson, Skowronek and Harmon, have spent time on the injury list since. Two of them could be back for the rematch.

THE DEPTH THAT HELD THE LINE

The transcript makes another good point. Pittsburgh’s interior defensive line didn’t simply wait for Harmon to come back. Other players stepped up.

According to the transcript, Cam Heyward specifically credited several younger linemen during Harmon’s absence: Yahya Black, Keeanu Benton, Gabe Rubio and Kevin Jobity. I couldn’t find Heyward’s original comment, so treat the list as the transcript’s account. But the broader point checks out.

Yahoo Sports noted that Rubio’s role grew after Sebastian Joseph-Day went on injured reserve with a triceps injury. The AP reported that Jobity recorded the first sack of his career after Harmon went down. And Benton has been anchoring the interior next to Heyward all season.

That depth is a big reason the defense kept rolling. Adding a healthy Harmon on top of it doesn’t patch a hole. It sharpens a strength.

THE VETERAN REST DAYS

Now let’s address something that caused some unnecessary panic this week.

On Thursday, Watt was limited and Heyward didn’t practice. Both were listed for rest, not injury. The transcript handles this exactly right: rest designations are not new injuries, and nobody should suggest otherwise.

And the Friday report confirmed it. According to Yahoo Sports, Watt, Heyward and Washington were all full participants on Friday, and none of them received a game status.

This is just smart management. Watt and Heyward are two of the most important players on the team, and the Steelers are entering a stretch that includes three prime-time games in five weeks. Taking a day off their legs in mid-November is how you keep them fresh for December and January.

THE BENGALS’ INJURY REPORT

Here’s the other side of the ledger, and it includes a name Steelers fans know well.

Safety Kyle Dugger, who played for Pittsburgh before landing in Cincinnati, has been ruled out with a quad injury. He didn’t practice all week. According to Yahoo Sports, he aggravated the injury in Week 7 and has been listed as week-to-week. Steelers Depot also headlined the Friday report around the former Steeler being ruled out, though I’m only going by the headline on that one.

Then there are three Bengals who returned to practice from injured reserve this week, according to Steeler Nation: cornerback Jalen Davis, receiver Andrei Iosivas and guard Brian Parker II. All three were full participants. But on the final report, all three are listed as doubtful.

One detail jumps out. According to Steeler Nation, Davis was hurt in the Week 3 game against Pittsburgh and missed the second half. Iosivas went on injured reserve the day before that game. So Cincinnati has been without both of them since September.

One correction to the transcript here: it says Iosivas returned to full participation, which is true for practice. But being full in practice and being available on game day are two different things. With a doubtful tag, I wouldn’t expect to see him Sunday.

THE BENGALS WHO WILL PLAY

There’s good news for Cincinnati too, and it’s worth knowing.

Cornerback DJ Turner II, who has been dealing with a heel issue since Week 8, was limited on Wednesday and then full on Thursday and Friday. He has no game status, so he’s expected to play. He played through it in Madrid.

Guard Dylan Fairchild, who hurt his pectoral late in the win over Atlanta, was full on Thursday and Friday. Zac Taylor had said he was on track to play.

And defensive tackles Jonathan Allen and B.J. Hill were on the report for rest only.

So the bottom line: Cincinnati is missing a starting-caliber safety, and several returning depth players look unlikely to be activated. Pittsburgh, meanwhile, could be getting a key interior lineman back. On paper, the injury edge goes to the Steelers this week.

WHAT CINCINNATI BRINGS INTO THIS GAME

Before we get to the game plan, let’s give the Bengals their due, because this isn’t the same team Pittsburgh saw in September.

Cincinnati has won four straight, just like the Steelers. In their last game, the Bengals beat Atlanta 34-17 in Madrid. Burrow completed 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards and three touchdowns. Chase caught ten passes for 155 yards.

That’s an offense in rhythm. And the Bengals will have the home crowd behind them, in prime time, with the chance to take over first place and earn a season split.

So when the transcript says this game could change the AFC North race completely, it’s not exaggerating. A Pittsburgh win means a season sweep, the head-to-head tiebreaker, and sole possession of first place. A Cincinnati win evens the season series and puts the Bengals on top. There are no small games in this division in November, but this one is about as big as it gets.

HOW THE RETURNS SHAPE THE GAME PLAN

So how do these three returns fit into Sunday night?

On defense, the transcript lays out three priorities: pressure Burrow, control the middle of the field and prevent explosive plays. Harmon helps with the first. Elliott helps with the second and third. And healthy depth at cornerback lets Patrick Graham be more aggressive with his calls, because he isn’t hiding a backup in coverage.

On offense, the transcript wants Rodgers to take advantage of every opportunity, with Metcalf as a big part of the plan and Skowronek adding another option. I’d add that the run game is the real key. In September, according to the Steelers’ recap, Jaylen Warren had 176 total yards, including 127 rushing on 17 carries. Pittsburgh outgained Cincinnati 411 to 352 and converted 7 of 12 third downs.

The transcript also mentions Rico Dowdle, and that duo has been a problem for opposing defenses. If Warren and Dowdle can run the ball, Pittsburgh controls the clock and keeps Burrow, Chase and Higgins on the sideline. That’s the best defense against that offense: making sure it doesn’t get the ball.

And defensively, don’t forget Patrick Queen. He had a game-high 13 tackles in the first meeting. Cam Heyward added two quarterback hits. That interior pressure, plus Queen cleaning up behind it, is how you limit an offense that wants to get the ball out fast.

WHY DEPTH WINS IN NOVEMBER

The transcript’s closing point is one I really like: you don’t win a divisional battle just because you have bigger names. You win because your players execute, stay healthy and make plays under pressure.

That’s what this week’s returns are really about. None of these three players is a superstar headliner. Skowronek is a depth receiver and special teamer. Elliott is a veteran safety. Harmon is a second-year rotational lineman. But in the September game, two of them made plays that decided a three-point win.

And looking past Sunday, the schedule only gets harder. Next week, the Steelers visit Philadelphia. Then it’s a short week before the Black Friday home game against Denver. Then Houston at home on Sunday night and a Monday night trip to Jacksonville. Every healthy body the Steelers get back now is a body they’ll need in December.

The transcript also makes a fair warning: the Steelers can’t depend on injury updates alone. Coming off the bye, healthier than they’ve been in weeks, they need a complete performance on both sides of the ball. Healthy players give you a chance. They don’t give you a win.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for Sunday night. Remember: this is opinion, not a report.

Derrick Harmon: activate him. A full practice on Friday is the signal. Use him in a rotation of 20 to 25 snaps, mostly on passing downs. Let him and Heyward take turns collapsing the pocket.

DeShon Elliott: start him and let him run the back end. If the ankle flares up, don’t hesitate to pull him.

Ben Skowronek: give him three or four targets, especially on third down and in the red zone, where his size helps. And put him right back on the kick and punt units.

The run game: Warren and Dowdle should get at least 30 combined carries. Make Cincinnati prove it can stop the ground game.

Secondary: with Echols and Slaughter healthy, let Graham dial up pressure. The coverage behind it can hold up.

Long term: designate Roman Wilson to return next week, and keep Pittman on a slow, safe timeline.
