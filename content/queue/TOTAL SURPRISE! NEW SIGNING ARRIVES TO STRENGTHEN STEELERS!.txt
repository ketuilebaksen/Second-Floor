Steelers Nation, nobody saw this one coming. Six weeks ago, on cutdown day, the Steelers released a hard-hitting veteran linebacker, and he told people he was shocked. No deal to come back. No promise of anything. Omar Khan said the team was “certainly not closing the door.” And on Monday, that door swung wide open. Elandon Roberts is back in Pittsburgh. Mike McCarthy confirmed it himself, and what he said about him might be the best description of a football player I’ve heard all year: “This man just wants to win. Can’t have enough people like that.” So today we’re breaking down the surprise return, why it’s smarter than it looks, the one connection on the coaching staff that makes it a perfect fit, and then we’ll tackle the question every fan is asking this week: is a new receiver coming next? Because there’s a video going around about a very big name, and I need to set the record straight. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The surprise: how Roberts left, and how he came back.

Let’s go back to the start of September. Roberts had signed with Pittsburgh in the middle of August, late in training camp, and he made an impact right away. In the second and third preseason games, he played 73 snaps and racked up around 10 tackles. He was flying downhill, hitting everything that moved, the exact player Steelers fans remembered.

And then, on cutdown day, he was gone. McCarthy said special teams played a big part in the final roster decisions, and reports said the team decided to keep four quarterbacks, which squeezed a linebacker spot. Roberts was caught off guard. According to reports, he said there was no arrangement to come back, not even on the practice squad, and he simply wished the team well.

But Khan left a little crack of light. He called him “E-Rob,” praised his character, and said the team was “certainly not closing the door.” Most of the time, that’s just something general managers say. This time, it was real.

On Monday, McCarthy confirmed that Roberts has been re-signed to the practice squad. And listen to how the head coach talked about him. “I enjoyed him in training camp,” McCarthy said. “I think timing was a little bit to do with his opportunity.” And then the line that’s going to be on every Steelers fan’s lips this week: “This man just wants to win. Can’t have enough people like that.”

That’s not a coach doing a favor for a veteran. That’s a coach who wants a certain kind of person in his locker room.

Why Roberts matters: the resume.

If you’re a newer fan and you don’t know the name, let me tell you who Elandon Roberts is.

He’s 32 years old, 5-foot-11, 235 pounds, and he’s been in the NFL for 10 seasons. He started in New England, and he won two Super Bowls with the Patriots, Super Bowl 51 and Super Bowl 53. He’s played for the Patriots, the Dolphins, the Steelers, and the Raiders, and he’s piled up 694 career tackles.

And think about the stages he’s played on. Super Bowl 51 was the famous comeback against Atlanta, the first overtime game in Super Bowl history, a 34-28 win after New England trailed by 25 points. Super Bowl 53 was a 13-3 defensive slugfest against the Rams, and Roberts made a tackle in that game. He also played in Super Bowl 52 against the Eagles. Three Super Bowl appearances, two rings. You can’t fake that kind of experience, and young players notice when someone in the room has been there.

And his first run in Pittsburgh was special. In 2023, the linebacker room was hit by injury after injury, and Roberts was the glue that held it together. He made 101 tackles that year, with two and a half sacks. In 2024, he played next to Patrick Queen and a rookie named Payton Wilson, and in the playoff loss in Baltimore, he made 14 tackles. Over those two seasons, he played 33 games, made 147 tackles, and had 15 tackles for loss.

Then in 2025, he went to Las Vegas and started 16 games for the Raiders, with 90 tackles. That’s not a guy who’s done. That’s a guy who’s still producing.

And here’s the thing about Roberts that never shows up in a box score. He plays with a physical, downhill style that sets the tone for an entire defense. In August, he said, “I try not to change,” and added that he’s gotten “a little smarter,” but he’ll always play with the same physicality. When you’re trying to stop the run, that’s exactly the guy you want.

And here’s a little twist in this story. Back in the 2025 offseason, the Steelers decided not to re-sign Roberts, and they brought in Malik Harrison from Baltimore instead. Roberts went to Las Vegas, proved he could still start every week, came back to Pittsburgh in August, got cut on cutdown day, and now he’s back again. Some players would hold a grudge. Roberts picked up the phone. That tells you exactly what McCarthy meant when he said this man just wants to win.

The connection that makes this perfect: Patrick Graham.

Now here’s the detail that turns this from a nice story into a smart football move. Who was the defensive coordinator in Las Vegas last season when Roberts started 16 games and made 90 tackles? Patrick Graham. And who’s the defensive coordinator in Pittsburgh now? Patrick Graham.

Think about what that means. Roberts doesn’t need to learn a new defense. He already knows Graham’s calls, his checks, his adjustments, the way he wants the run fits played. He’s been inside that system for a full season. Most players who join a team in October need weeks to catch up. Roberts can walk into the meeting room and help teach it.

And that’s why the timing makes sense. Derrick Harmon is on injured reserve with his foot injury, so the defensive front is thinner. Roberts can be elevated from the practice squad on game days without using a spot on the 53-man roster. So if Graham needs a thumper for a specific game plan, especially against a team that wants to run the ball, he has one ready to go.

How the practice squad move works, and why it’s smart.

Let me explain the mechanics, because a lot of fans ask why he isn’t on the 53-man roster. Right now, the 53 is full, and with all the injuries, every spot matters. By signing Roberts to the practice squad, Pittsburgh keeps him in the building without cutting anyone from the active roster. On game days, the Steelers can elevate him for a specific matchup, and a practice squad player can be elevated up to three times in a season before the team has to make a bigger decision. So Khan gets a veteran insurance policy, Graham gets a run-stopping option, and nobody on the current roster loses a job. It’s flexible, it’s cheap, and it’s exactly the kind of quiet, efficient move this front office has been making all season.

Why this fits what we saw against the Colts.

Here’s why I love the timing. On Sunday, the Steelers beat the Colts 34-22, and the defense was the reason the game never got away. Jonathan Taylor, one of the best running backs in the league, finished with 45 yards on 15 carries. The Colts kicked five field goals and went 1-for-3 in the red zone.

That’s a defense that’s built on stopping the run and winning on early downs. And Roberts is a run defender first, second, and third. He’s not being brought in to cover slot receivers. He’s being brought in so that when a team tries to bully the Steelers up the middle, there’s a 235-pound veteran ready to meet them in the hole.

And there’s another layer. McCarthy has been talking about culture since the day he arrived. After the loss in Cleveland, he said the team had to clean its own house. After the Colts win, he said, “We redd it up.” Adding a two-time Super Bowl champion who “just wants to win” is a culture move as much as a football move. Veterans like Roberts teach younger players how to practice, how to prepare, and how to finish plays.

And here’s my favorite part of this whole story: Roberts and Payton Wilson, together again. In 2024, Wilson was a rookie trying to find his footing in the NFL, and Roberts was the veteran next to him in the middle of that defense. Two years later, Wilson is one of the best young linebackers in football. He had 10 tackles, two for a loss, and a huge pass breakup on fourth-and-17 against the Colts. Now the student and the teacher are back in the same meeting room. I don’t care what level you play at, having someone who knows exactly how you learned the game is valuable. Don’t be surprised if Wilson is the first guy at Roberts’ locker this week.

The win that set it all up.

And let’s not forget how good Sunday was, because this signing doesn’t happen in a vacuum. The Steelers are 3-2 after one of their best performances of the season. Aaron Rodgers threw for three touchdowns for the third straight week, completed his first 11 passes, and passed Ben Roethlisberger on the all-time wins list and Peyton Manning on the all-time touchdown list. Jaylen Warren exploded for 188 yards from scrimmage, with an 80-yard touchdown catch and a 50-yard touchdown run, something no NFL player had done in the same game since Herschel Walker in 1986. McCarthy called him “our feature player right now.”

When you’re winning, good veterans want to come play for you. That’s part of the story here, too.

And zoom out for a second, because this is part of a bigger pattern. In about a week, Khan has added cornerback Sean Murphy-Bunting off Tampa Bay’s practice squad, cornerback Terell Smith off Washington’s practice squad, defensive end KJ Henry for a third stint, and now Elandon Roberts. Four defensive additions, none of them flashy, all of them aimed at the same goal: making sure injuries don’t break this defense. And look at the result on Sunday. The Colts didn’t throw a single touchdown pass, and their star running back averaged three yards a carry. Khan’s approach isn’t about winning the press conference. It’s about winning in December, when the teams with depth are the teams still standing.

That’s also why I’m so positive about this move. Roberts doesn’t need to be a star. He needs to be ready. Maybe he plays 15 snaps in a game where the opponent goes heavy. Maybe he becomes Graham’s go-to run-down specialist on short-yardage and goal-line snaps, where a downhill hitter is worth his weight in gold. Maybe he just makes the practices tougher. Every one of those helps. And on a team that’s trying to win now with a 42-year-old quarterback, there’s no such thing as too much veteran toughness on the defensive side of the ball. Roberts gives this defense one more grown man who has seen everything this league can throw at him.

Now, the big question: is a new receiver coming?

This is where the excitement turns into honesty, because I know what a lot of you are hoping for. The Steelers’ receiver room is in rough shape. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot, and he has to miss at least four games. On Monday, McCarthy confirmed that Roman Wilson, with a dislocated finger, and Ben Skowronek, with a calf injury, won’t play against Tampa Bay. Reports say Wilson is getting a second opinion. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen as the healthy receivers on the 53-man roster.

So what’s the plan? McCarthy is backing his guys. About Bernard, he said, “Football comes easy to him.” About practice squad receiver Brandon Smith, who was elevated for the Colts game, he said Smith will “definitely get more opportunities,” and that he’s “definitely earned this opportunity.” There are also two more receivers on the practice squad, Isaiah Hodgins and Brandon Johnson, who could be elevated for the trip to Tampa without costing a roster spot. And of course, there’s the two-tight-end package with Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington. Freiermuth just caught two touchdowns against the Colts.

When McCarthy was asked about outside help, he said, “Those conversations are always going on.” So the Steelers are looking. But as of right now, no receiver has been signed.

And let’s be real about one more thing: Bernard is earning this. In his first NFL start against the Colts, he caught six passes for 80 yards, both career highs, including catches of 21 and 25 yards. Rodgers said he was “really proud of Germ the way he stepped in.” When the head coach says football comes easy to a rookie and the 42-year-old quarterback is publicly praising him, the front office has a good reason not to rush into a panic trade.

Let’s clear up the Ladd McConkey video.

Now, there’s a video making the rounds this week built around Ladd McConkey, the Chargers receiver out of Georgia, and how he’d look in Pittsburgh’s offense. It’s a long, fancy breakdown, but let me give you the facts it left out.

First, there is no report connecting McConkey to the Steelers. None. Second, McConkey is a key player for the Chargers, a team that drafted him in the second round in 2024 and leans on him heavily in its passing game. And third, he’s hurt himself. He was ruled out of the Chargers’ Week 4 game against Seattle with a foot injury, and Jim Harbaugh has called him week-to-week. So even in a fantasy world where the Chargers wanted to deal him, Pittsburgh would be trading for a receiver who can’t play right now. That’s not the fix this team needs this week.

I understand why people like the idea. McConkey is a precise route runner who wins with timing, and that’s exactly the kind of receiver Rodgers loves. But a fun idea and a real move are two different things.

The trade block: a realistic receiver target.

So here’s my trade block segment, and I want to be clear that this is my opinion, not a report. If Khan does make a receiver move before the November 10th deadline, the one name I keep coming back to is Calvin Ridley in Tennessee. He’s 31, he’s been an afterthought in a young Titans receiver room, and Pro Football Focus listed him as a trade candidate this month. He has the experience to step in right away, and he shouldn’t cost much.

And if Khan wants to aim higher, he still has the 2028 second-round pick from Dallas from the Joey Porter Jr. trade. That’s real currency. Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville would be the dream target, a 24-year-old who had 1,282 yards as a rookie. Jacksonville hasn’t wanted to move him, but if that ever changes before the deadline, Pittsburgh has the ammunition.

But here’s the honest truth: Khan’s first move this week wasn’t a receiver. It was a linebacker. And that tells you something about how this front office thinks. The Steelers didn’t panic about the receivers after one bad injury day. They addressed a depth need on defense first, and they’re giving Bernard, Smith, and the tight ends a chance to show they can carry the passing game for a couple of weeks.

What this means for Tampa Bay.

The Steelers head to Tampa Bay on Sunday, October 18th. And this is a game where a player like Roberts could matter. Kenneth Gainwell, who signed with the Buccaneers this offseason after his time in Pittsburgh, will be on the other side. Tampa will want to test a Steelers defense that just shut down Jonathan Taylor. Having a physical veteran ready to be elevated gives Graham another tool for that matchup.

There’s also good news coming on defense. DeShon Elliott’s 21-day practice window is open, and McCarthy said, “It’s going to go as he goes this week.” Jamel Dean has been targeting the Tampa game for his return, and Tampa is where his career began. Sean Murphy-Bunting, who came over from the Bucs’ practice squad, spent the first month of the season practicing against that offense. So the Steelers will walk into Raymond James Stadium with a lot of inside knowledge.

And on offense, the good news keeps coming. Rico Dowdle is close to returning from his toe injury. If he’s back, Warren gets a partner, and the Steelers can lean on a two-back running game while the receivers heal.

The bottom line.

So let me wrap it up. Yes, the Steelers made a new signing this week, and it was a genuine surprise: Elandon Roberts, the two-time Super Bowl champion, the 101-tackle linebacker from 2023, a player who knows Patrick Graham’s defense better than almost anyone, is back in Pittsburgh. McCarthy loves his mindset. Khan kept his word about not closing the door. And the defense gets a little tougher right when the schedule heads on the road.

No, the Steelers haven’t signed a new receiver, and there’s no report that Ladd McConkey or any other star is coming. But the conversations are happening, the trade deadline is less than a month away, and Khan has the draft capital to make a move if he needs to.

This team is 3-2, it just played its best game of the season, and it’s adding the kind of people who make a locker room stronger. That’s a good week in Pittsburgh.
