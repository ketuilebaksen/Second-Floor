Steelers Nation, this week Pittsburgh made a decision that tells you exactly how this front office plans to attack the rest of the 2026 season, and it wasn’t the one most people expected. With three receivers hurt, everybody assumed Omar Khan would rush out and sign a wide receiver. Instead, the Steelers stood firm. They backed the guys already in the building, brought back a veteran linebacker who knows Patrick Graham’s defense inside out, and set themselves up with a running back returning from injury. And at the same time, there’s a quarterback rumor going around that has Steelers fans fired up: Jalen Hurts in black and gold. So today we’re breaking down the decision Pittsburgh actually made, why it could change the direction of this season, what it might mean for Patrick Queen, the real story behind the Hurts noise, and the biggest decision of all, the one waiting at quarterback after Aaron Rodgers. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Part one: the decision Pittsburgh made this week.

Let’s start with the facts. After the 34-22 win over the Colts, the Steelers’ receiver room got wiped out. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with his foot. Roman Wilson dislocated a finger, and Ben Skowronek hurt his calf covering a punt. McCarthy has already ruled both of them out for Sunday in Tampa. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen as the healthy receivers on the 53-man roster.

Most teams would panic. They’d sign the best available veteran off the street and hope for the best. The Steelers didn’t. When McCarthy was asked about outside help, he said, “Those conversations are always going on.” But then he made it clear where his trust is. About practice squad receiver Brandon Smith, he said he’s “definitely earned this opportunity,” and, “We feel very good about Brandon moving forward.” About Bernard, he said, “Football comes easy to him.”

That’s a decision. It says: we’re not going to overpay for a band-aid. We’re going to trust our young players, lean on our running game, and keep our draft capital for the right move before the November 10th trade deadline.

And the only outside addition Khan did make this week tells you even more. On Monday, the Steelers brought back linebacker Elandon Roberts on the practice squad. The two-time Super Bowl champion was cut on cutdown day, and now he’s back. McCarthy put it perfectly: “This man just wants to win. Can’t have enough people like that.” Roberts started 16 games for the Raiders last season under defensive coordinator Patrick Graham, the same Patrick Graham who now runs Pittsburgh’s defense. So he knows the system before he even walks into the meeting room.

Why this decision could change everything.

Here’s why I think this matters so much. In past seasons, Steelers fans have watched this team chase quick fixes. This time, Khan is playing the long game while still trying to win now.

Think about what Pittsburgh has right now. A quarterback in Rodgers who just threw three touchdowns for the third straight week, completed his first 11 passes against the Colts, and passed Ben Roethlisberger on the all-time wins list and Peyton Manning on the all-time touchdown list. A running back in Jaylen Warren who put up 188 yards from scrimmage, with an 80-yard touchdown catch and a 50-yard touchdown run, the first player to do that in one game since Herschel Walker in 1986. And another running back, Rico Dowdle, a two-time 1,000-yard rusher who practiced on Wednesday and has “a good chance” of returning against Tampa, according to McCarthy.

When you have that, you don’t need to panic at receiver. You build the game plan around your strengths: two backs, two tight ends in Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington, play action for Rodgers, and quick throws to Bernard and Metcalf. That’s not a weakness. That’s an identity. And honestly, it’s the kind of identity that travels. Running the ball, controlling the clock, and playing good defense works in the rain in Pittsburgh in December, and it works in the heat in Tampa in October. Teams built that way don’t need everything to go perfectly to win. They just need to stay true to who they are.

And by holding onto the 2028 second-round pick from Dallas, the one Pittsburgh got in the Joey Porter Jr. trade, Khan keeps the flexibility to make one real move before the deadline if he needs to. That’s the difference between a desperate team and a disciplined one.

Part two: what the Roberts signing could mean for Patrick Queen.

Now, here’s where it gets interesting. Some Steelers writers believe the Roberts signing could be the first step toward another decision: the future of Patrick Queen.

Let’s be fair to Queen. In Week 3 against Cincinnati, he played maybe his best game as a Steeler and earned a game ball. Gerry Dulac of the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette called it possibly his best game in Pittsburgh. But a week later, in the Thursday night loss in Cleveland, he had a rough night, and the defense had miscommunication problems all over the field. Dulac was blunt afterward: “I see a lot of criticism out there about Patrick Queen and it’s deserved.”

Queen is in the final year of his contract, and one report puts his cap hit at around 17 million dollars this season. He’s set to become a free agent in 2027. Steelers Wire suggested this week that the Steelers “could be nearing a trade decision” after signing Roberts, pointing to a linebacker group with Payton Wilson playing at a high level, Cole Holcomb as a serviceable option, and Carson Bruener contributing on special teams.

And look at what Payton Wilson just did against the Colts. Ten tackles, a team high, two of them for a loss, plus a pass breakup on fourth-and-17 that helped the defense hold Indianapolis to five field goals. Jonathan Taylor finished with just 45 yards on 15 carries. When your young linebacker is playing like that, and you just added a veteran thumper who knows the defensive coordinator’s system, the pressure on the expensive veteran in the room naturally goes up. That’s not a knock on Queen as a person. It’s just how roster building works in the NFL. Production and cost always get weighed against each other, especially in the last year of a contract.

Let me be very clear: there is no report that the Steelers are actively shopping Queen. This is speculation. But here’s my opinion. If Pittsburgh could get real value for a linebacker who’s leaving after the season, and Roberts can fill the run-stuffing role on early downs, it’s at least a conversation worth having before November 10th. On the other hand, the Steelers are 3-2 and in the hunt. Trading a starter in the middle of a playoff push sends a message to the locker room. If I’m Khan, I listen to offers, but I only move Queen if the return is clearly worth it.

Part three: the Jalen Hurts rumor, and the truth behind it.

Now let’s get to the story that’s lighting up social media. Some videos are saying the Steelers are a “potential destination” for Jalen Hurts. Let’s look at where that actually came from.

On October 7th, The Spun reported on a post from Steelers writer Nick Martin, who wrote that he’d “just received a disturbing premonition where the Eagles move on from their QB.” That’s it. A premonition. Not a report. Not a source. Not a phone call. And the reaction from Steelers fans was brutal. Joey Bray of Behind the Steel Curtain replied, “I really just don’t have the heart to watch 17 games of Jalen Hurts, man.” One fan wrote, “I’d rather keep Rodgers until he’s fifty before I watch Hurts play a snap in Pittsburgh.”

So why is Hurts even being talked about? Because the Eagles are in trouble. Philadelphia is 2-3 after three straight losses. On Sunday in London, they lost 19-17 to Jacksonville on a 57-yard field goal as time expired, after Ameer Abdullah returned a kickoff 53 yards. They were missing Saquon Barkley and DeVonta Smith. Lane Johnson retired. And the Eagles traded two-time Pro Bowl center Cam Jurgens.

And Hurts himself has struggled. Through four games, he completed 59.6 percent of his passes for 713 yards, seven touchdowns, and three interceptions. Against the Rams, he threw for just 93 yards. Pro Football Focus had his passing grade at 58.6 through Week 4. In London, he went 21 of 40 for 220 yards, a touchdown, and an interception.

And the problems in Philadelphia go deeper than one player. Pro Football Talk’s Mike Florio pointed out that the Eagles’ offense gained fewer than 250 yards in two straight games and went 2-for-21 on third down in back-to-back losses. Analyst Fran Duffy said before the London game, “They have not converted on third down since the first half of Week 3.” With Johnson retired and Jurgens traded, Florio estimated the Eagles had lost about 40 percent of their offensive line, and he wrote that it looks like Philadelphia is giving up on 2026, while making clear it isn’t tanking in the classic sense. That’s a team in transition, but it doesn’t mean the quarterback is going anywhere.

But here’s what those videos don’t tell you. Trading Hurts is close to impossible right now. In 2023, he signed a five-year, 255 million dollar extension with 180 million guaranteed, and that deal includes a no-trade clause, the first in Eagles history. According to the report, trading him next offseason would leave Philadelphia with 85.2 million dollars in dead money, and the Eagles realistically can’t get out of the contract until after 2027. Even the article that spread the rumor said the Eagles remain committed to Hurts and that nobody should expect a deal.

And remember, this is a 28-year-old who was the MVP of Super Bowl 59, when the Eagles beat the Chiefs 40-22. Philadelphia isn’t going to give that up because of one bad month.

So no, the Steelers haven’t made any decision about Hurts, and there’s no report that they’re interested. If you see a thumbnail saying otherwise, it’s selling you a premonition.

And even if Hurts were somehow available, would he fit here? Let’s think about it honestly. Hurts is at his best as a dual threat, a quarterback who wins with his legs and with a dominant offensive line and run game around him. When those pieces fall apart, as they have in Philadelphia this year, his passing numbers drop fast. Pittsburgh’s offense under McCarthy is built on timing, rhythm, and getting the ball out on schedule. That’s a very different style. And financially, taking on a contract that size would tie up a huge chunk of the cap for years. For a team that’s been so disciplined about guaranteed money, just look at how the Porter contract talks ended, it’s hard to see Khan going there.

Part four: the real AFC North news hiding inside the Eagles story.

Now, here’s the part of the Eagles story that actually does affect Pittsburgh, and almost nobody is talking about it. Where did Cam Jurgens go? To the Baltimore Ravens.

According to Pro Football Talk, the Eagles sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. So while everyone was arguing about Hurts, a division rival quietly added a two-time Pro Bowl center in the middle of the season. That’s the kind of move that matters in the AFC North race.

And it’s a reminder of why Khan’s patience has a limit. The teams around the Steelers are making moves. Pittsburgh doesn’t have to match every one, but it can’t fall asleep either. That’s why I keep saying the Dallas second-rounder could become a weapon before November 10th.

And here’s one more thing to think about. Baltimore made that trade in the middle of a season, which tells you the Ravens believe they’re contenders. The AFC North is going to be a dogfight in November and December. Every win now, like the one over the Colts, matters even more when you know your rivals are loading up.

Part five: the trade block, my opinion.

So if the Steelers do make a move before the deadline, what should it be? Here’s my opinion, not a report.

At receiver, a cheap veteran like Calvin Ridley in Tennessee still makes the most sense. He’s 31, he’s barely been used, and Pro Football Focus listed him as a trade candidate this month. He could help right away without costing the Dallas pick. And if Khan wants to aim higher, Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, with 1,282 yards as a rookie, remains the dream target, even though the Jaguars haven’t wanted to sell.

And on defense, if a team calls about Queen with a strong offer, I’d listen. But I wouldn’t give him away. A 3-2 team chasing the playoffs needs as many good players as it can keep.

Part six: the biggest decision of all, life after Rodgers.

Now let’s talk about the decision that really will change everything, because it’s coming whether we like it or not.

Rodgers has made it clear that 2026 is his last season. Before the Cleveland game, Al Michaels asked him where he’d be a year from now, and Rodgers said, “I’ll either be tending to my pregnant wife or I’ll be taking care of my newborn.” That’s a man who’s at peace with this being his final chapter.

So what’s next? Pittsburgh is carrying four quarterbacks right now: Rodgers, Mason Rudolph, Will Howard, and rookie Drew Allar. That’s actually part of why Roberts was cut back in September, because the team decided to keep all four. And that decision tells you something too. The Steelers want to keep developing young quarterbacks behind Rodgers, so they can evaluate them before the 2027 draft.

And then there’s Mason Rudolph, the veteran who knows this organization better than almost anyone in the quarterback room. He’s in the final year of his contract, and some writers have even listed him as a possible trade candidate because the team is carrying four quarterbacks. But as long as Rodgers is 42 years old, having a reliable backup who’s started games in this league is insurance you don’t give away lightly.

Howard and Allar get to sit in meetings with a four-time MVP every week. They get to watch how a 42-year-old future Hall of Famer prepares, reads defenses, and manages a game. That’s a priceless education, even if neither one plays a snap this season.

Will one of them be the answer? I don’t know. Maybe the answer is in the 2027 draft. Maybe it’s a veteran who becomes available next offseason. But I’ll tell you what it probably isn’t: a quarterback with a no-trade clause and 85 million dollars of dead money attached to his name.

What it all means for Sunday in Tampa.

Let’s bring it back to this weekend, because the most important decision is the next game. The Steelers visit Tampa Bay on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern, and they’ve lost five straight on the road, including that 27-24 loss in Cleveland. That’s the streak that has to end.

The good news is that help is on the way. Dowdle was limited on Wednesday, and McCarthy said there’s a good chance he plays. Jamel Dean was limited with his ankle, and Tampa is where his career began. DeShon Elliott was limited, in the second week of his 21-day window off injured reserve, and McCarthy said the team would like to get him back “sooner rather than later.” The one new concern is defensive tackle Keeanu Benton, who missed practice with an ankle injury. With Derrick Harmon already on injured reserve, that’s where Roberts could come in handy if he’s elevated.

And there are storylines everywhere. Sean Murphy-Bunting, who came over from Tampa’s practice squad, spent the first month of the season practicing against that Bucs offense. Kenneth Gainwell, who left Pittsburgh for Tampa this offseason, will be on the other sideline.

If the Steelers win this game with three receivers out, Khan’s decision to stay patient will look brilliant. They’ll be 4-2, with a healthier roster coming, and real momentum heading toward the bye.

The bottom line.

So here’s the honest summary. The Steelers didn’t trade for Jalen Hurts, and there’s no report they’re even thinking about it. That story started as a writer’s premonition. But Pittsburgh did make a real decision this week: trust its young receivers, lean on Warren and Dowdle, bring back a veteran who knows Graham’s defense, and keep its best trade ammunition for the right moment. And the Roberts signing might even be the first domino in a bigger linebacker decision down the road.

Does that change everything? It changes the way this team is being built. Patient, physical, and smart. And if Sunday goes well in Tampa, a lot of people around the league are going to start paying attention.
