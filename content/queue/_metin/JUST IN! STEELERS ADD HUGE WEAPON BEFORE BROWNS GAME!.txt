Steelers Nation, circle a window on your calendar. Not a date. A window. Because the most plugged-in Steelers insider out there just told us roughly when Pittsburgh’s next weapon could arrive, and it’s sooner than a lot of people think.

Mark Kaboly, on Kaboly and Mack Live: “I would say early to middle of next week will be the sweet spot for a move to get done.” And then: “It will not last until the weekend. I would imagine they get something done in the coming days.”

Now let me be crisp about what that is and what it isn’t. That’s a prediction from a reporter who covers this team every day. It is not a signing. It is not a trade. Nobody has been added to the roster as of this morning, and Kaboly didn’t name a player in those comments. But when the guy who’s around this building constantly tells you to keep your phone alerts on, you listen.

So today I’m giving you the countdown. What happens Sunday against Cleveland, what happens in that early-next-week window, and how the bye week sets up the perfect landing spot for a new receiver. Plus the latest from T.J. Watt, Troy Fautanu, and Mike McCarthy before kickoff. I’m James, this is Second Floor. Let’s run the clock.

THE KABOLY WINDOW, EXPLAINED

Here’s the full logic Kaboly laid out, because it’s smarter than a typical rumor.

His point was about timing more than names. “If you acquire somebody during the bye week span, it would be the ideal time to get him acclimated to everything.” And then the line every Steelers fan should take seriously: “Keep your alerts on early next week.”

Think about the calendar. Pittsburgh plays Cleveland on Sunday, November 1. Week 9 is the bye. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern. If the Steelers wait until deadline day, a new receiver lands right before a Sunday night game in Cincinnati on November 15 with almost no practice time. If they move early next week, that player gets the entire bye to learn the playbook, sit in the quarterback room, and catch a few hundred passes from Aaron Rodgers before he ever steps on the field.

That’s the difference between a player who’s useful in December and a player who’s lost in November.

WHO FITS THE WINDOW?

Kaboly didn’t name anybody in those comments, so I’m not going to put words in his mouth. But we know who the reporting points to.

The hottest name is Colts receiver Josh Downs. He requested a trade on Wednesday, per ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler. Jordan Schultz reported interest from multiple AFC teams, naming Pittsburgh. Then Evan Sidery reported that the Steelers and Chiefs have both offered Day 3 draft compensation and wrote that “a Downs deal is expected to go down after this weekend,” with Indianapolis likely to fetch “a 4th or 5th-round pick.”

Read Kaboly’s window next to Sidery’s timeline. One says early to middle of next week. The other says after this weekend. Those two lines point at the same few days. That doesn’t prove Downs is the guy, and it doesn’t prove Pittsburgh wins the race over Kansas City. But the calendars match.

The other name in the background is Jacksonville’s Brian Thomas Jr. ESPN’s trade candidate list ranked him second overall and named Pittsburgh as one of his fits. But Kaboly said earlier this week that the Steelers “were” interested and he hasn’t “heard anything recently about it.” That’s a quieter trail.

And there’s one more Kaboly line worth knowing. Steelers Depot ran a headline this week saying Kaboly isn’t buying reported trade interest in a cornerback, with the phrase “It’s WR or nothing.” I couldn’t access the full piece, so I’m only working from the headline. But if that’s his read, it tells you where the front office’s attention is.

WHY THE RECEIVER ROOM NEEDS IT

Let’s be honest about the depth chart. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are both on injured reserve. And Kaboly himself said back on October 16, “Yeah, it doesn’t sound good for Pittman,” which hints that his absence could stretch longer than fans hoped.

Ben Skowronek is questionable for Sunday with his calf after three straight limited practices. Behind DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard, the active roster is thin, and the practice squad has been doing a lot of heavy lifting. Brandon Smith has used all three of his elevations. Marquez Valdes-Scantling is waiting for his shot, and Rodgers said this week, “It would be nice to get him up.”

Bernard has been the bright spot. He’s earned Rodgers’ trust fast, including that overtime touchdown in Tampa. But a rookie and a star shouldn’t be the whole plan for a team with Super Bowl ambitions. That’s why the window matters.

And listen to how Bernard describes playing with Rodgers, because it tells you what any new receiver would be stepping into. On Cam Heyward’s podcast, Bernard said, “There’s no stress when you’re out there with him because he doesn’t have any stress.” He also said, “For Aaron to trust me and rely on me in a crucial moment like that, it means everything.” That’s a quarterback who can make a newcomer comfortable quickly, if the newcomer can learn fast. Two weeks of bye-week reps with a quarterback like that is a real advantage.

A QUICK WORD ON THE PAST

The transcript I worked from brought up the history at this position, and it’s fair context. Pittsburgh traded George Pickens to Dallas after acquiring Metcalf from Seattle in 2025. Fans expected a dynamic duo. Instead, the Steelers have spent two seasons trying to find a reliable second and third option.

This year, they added Pittman from Indianapolis in March, drafted Bernard in the second round, and took Kaden Wetjen in the fourth. Then injuries hit. That’s the cycle Omar Khan is trying to break, and a mid-season addition that actually sticks would be the first step.

But there’s a balance. The Steelers have big money tied up in Metcalf, and they made a significant commitment to bring in Pittman. Adding another expensive long-term contract isn’t realistic. That’s why a cheap rental like Downs, who can become a free agent in March, fits the budget better than a blockbuster.

SUNDAY FIRST: THE BROWNS DECIDE A LOT

Before any trade, there’s a game. And this one might shape how aggressive Khan gets.

Both teams are 5-2. Cleveland won the first meeting 27-24 in Week 4 and owns the head-to-head edge for now. A Pittsburgh win evens the series and puts the Steelers alone in first in the AFC North heading into the bye. A loss gives Cleveland a sweep and a commanding position.

If the receivers struggle on Sunday to separate from Cleveland’s man coverage, which the Browns used on 73.3 percent of their snaps in the first meeting, according to Steeler Nation, the case for a trade gets louder by Monday morning. If Metcalf, Bernard, and the tight ends dominate, maybe Khan stays patient on price. Either way, Sunday is the final audition.

WATT’S PRIORITIES FOR SUNDAY

T.J. Watt met with the media on Friday, and he kept it simple. His number one priority? “Explosives.” He explained, “I think obviously you think about the deep, the deep pass. I think there’s a couple.” And then: “limiting explosives, continuing to stop the run game.”

That’s exactly the formula that has worked during this winning streak. In Paris, the Saints had 18 rushing yards on 17 carries. Against the Colts, Jonathan Taylor was held to 45 yards on 15 attempts. When you take away the run and the deep ball, you force an offense to grind, and most offenses can’t do that for sixty minutes.

Watt also stressed discipline: “you need to be very disciplined on who’s doing what.” And he added the line that sums up every pregame week: “it doesn’t matter unless you do it on Sunday.”

Asked about the trash talk from Cleveland, Watt wasn’t interested. “I don’t hear any of that stuff,” he said.

WATT ON THE DEFENSE FINDING ITSELF

Watt also offered a revealing look inside Patrick Graham’s defense. Remember, back in Cleveland, Watt grabbed a headset on the sideline in frustration. That was a low point in the relationship between the veterans and the new scheme.

On Friday, he described a very different picture. “I think PQ has a great feel for what to get us in with PG,” he said, meaning Patrick Queen working with Patrick Graham on the calls. “They have great conversations, and that just comes back to the vets having a say in what we’re doing on defense.” And then: “it takes a little bit to build that trust on both ways, and I think we’re finally getting to a good spot.”

That’s a big statement. The defense that looked disjointed in Week 4 now sounds like a unit where the coordinator and the veterans are on the same page. If that’s real, Cleveland is walking into a much tougher room than last time.

THE MATCHUP TO WATCH: WATT VERSUS A BACKUP

Here’s a detail that could swing the game. Cleveland right tackle Tytus Howard is in the concussion protocol and listed as questionable. He missed three straight days of practice. Head coach Todd Monken said, “We’ll have a better idea in the morning,” with another practice on Saturday.

If Howard can’t go, Dawand Jones is expected to start at right tackle, and he’d see plenty of T.J. Watt. Jones said the chance would be “pretty dope,” and that he’s “just trying to be ready to go when my name’s called.” He called Watt “a veteran, just real smart,” with “good get-off and technique.” And here’s the interesting part: according to Pro Football Focus, Jones held his own against Watt as a rookie, allowing no sacks, hits, or pressures in their first meeting.

So Jones has some confidence. But Watt has a long memory. If Watt wins that matchup early, Deshaun Watson is going to have a long afternoon.

FAUTANU’S ANSWER TO VERSE

On the other side of the line, Jared Verse has been the loudest voice in this rivalry. After the Week 4 game, he complained about the Steelers double-teaming, sliding, and chipping him, and called the line dirty. Cam Heyward already fired back on The Rich Eisen Show.

Troy Fautanu took a different approach. “I don’t know what he’s talking about honestly,” he said. “I got no comment, I’m just excited to play this game.” And: “We’re gonna go out there and continue to play our game.”

Asked if Verse’s words fired him up: “Personally not really. It’s just another game, I’m gonna approach it as such, and it’s a big game because it’s the next game.”

I love that. No bulletin board material, no drama. Just a young tackle who wants to let the tape do the talking.

MCCARTHY ON THE RED ZONE

Mike McCarthy addressed the elephant in the room too. Pittsburgh’s red-zone offense ranks 27th in the league, per ClutchPoints. In Paris, it was one touchdown on the trips that counted.

McCarthy’s response, via a video posted by Chris Adamski of the Tribune-Review: “I think, like everything, you look at the attempts. Obviously, you want to get down there.” Then: “But yeah, the running pass, we could definitely score more touchdowns.” And he said the team “feel[s] really good about this week’s package.”

Translation: the coach knows it’s a problem, and he believes they’ve schemed something up. Against a divisional opponent that beat you by three points, the difference between a field goal and a touchdown inside the 20 could decide first place.

THE SECONDARY QUESTION

One more pregame issue. Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both with hamstring injuries, are out for Sunday. Echols got hurt in Tampa and missed Paris. Slaughter got hurt in Paris.

Pittsburgh’s starters are healthy. Asante Samuel Jr. and Jamel Dean on the outside, Jalen Ramsey at nickel, Jaquan Brisker at safety. But the backups thin out fast. Sean Murphy-Bunting is next behind Samuel. Daylen Everette and Terell Smith are behind Dean. And the Steelers could elevate a corner from the practice squad, with Sebastian Castro, Darius Rush, and Car’lin Vigers among the options, according to SI.

That’s why Watt’s “limit explosives” message matters. With limited depth on the back end, one injury in the secondary could expose the Steelers to exactly the kind of deep shots Watt warned about.

SATURDAY’S DECISION: THE WEAPON YOU MIGHT SEE SUNDAY

Before any trade talk heats up, there’s a smaller roster decision coming today. Teams make their practice-squad elevations on Saturday afternoon, and Pittsburgh has a real choice to make at receiver.

Brandon Smith can’t be elevated again. He used all three of his elevations in Weeks 5, 6, and 7, so the only way he plays on Sunday is if the Steelers sign him to the 53-man roster. Valdes-Scantling still has elevations available, and his quarterback is openly lobbying for him. Skowronek is questionable, and if he’s cleared, the need for a call-up shrinks.

There’s also the cornerback angle. With two defensive backs out, the coaching staff might want to use an elevation on a corner instead. That’s the tension: every elevation spent on defense is one fewer receiver for Rodgers.

So watch the transaction wire this afternoon. If MVS gets the call, it tells you McCarthy wants a vertical threat against Cleveland’s man coverage. If a corner gets the nod instead, it tells you the staff is worried about the back end. Either way, that’s the first real hint at the game plan.

WHAT A NEW RECEIVER WOULD WALK INTO

Let’s imagine the window opens and the Steelers do add a receiver early next week. Here’s the gauntlet he’d be preparing for during the bye.

Sunday night, November 15, at Cincinnati. Then a trip to Philadelphia on November 22, a late-afternoon national game. Then Denver at home on Black Friday, November 27, on Prime Video. Then Houston at home on Sunday Night Football on December 6. Then Monday night in Jacksonville on December 14. Then Baltimore at home on December 20.

That’s three standalone national-TV games in four weeks, a road trip to Philadelphia, and two division games still to come against Cincinnati and Baltimore. Whoever arrives isn’t walking into a soft landing. He’s walking into the stretch that decides the division.

That’s exactly why the bye week timing matters so much. The two weeks between Cleveland and Cincinnati are the only real classroom a new player gets before the schedule turns brutal.

THE RIVALRY BACKDROP

One more bit of context for Sunday. The Steelers lead the all-time series with Cleveland 83-66-1, per ClutchPoints, and historically Pittsburgh has been a tough place for the Browns to win. But Cleveland has had its moments here too, including the playoff game after the 2020 season, and the Browns already have one win over this team in 2026.

That’s why I don’t buy any talk that this is an easy home game. Cleveland is 5-2, confident, and talking. Pittsburgh is 5-2, healthier than it’s been in weeks, and finally sounds like a unified defense. This one has the feel of a playoff game in November.

THREE KEYS BEFORE THE WINDOW OPENS

One. Win the trenches early. Warren and Dowdle need to get rolling in the first quarter, especially if Cleveland’s right side is shuffled with Howard questionable.

Two. Protect against the deep ball. With Echols and Slaughter out, Graham can’t afford a coverage bust. Watt said it himself: limit explosives.

Three. Finish in the red zone. McCarthy says he likes this week’s package. Show it. Touchdowns, not field goals.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s where I give you my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

If Kaboly’s window is right, I want Khan to move early next week, before the bye clock runs down. The best acquisition is the one who gets the most practice time.

My priority list hasn’t changed. Number one, Josh Downs at a fourth or fifth-round price. A slot receiver who wins quickly against man coverage, a rental through Rodgers’ last season, and a price that won’t touch the 2028 second-round pick Pittsburgh got from Dallas for Joey Porter Jr.

Number two, if Downs goes to Kansas City, don’t panic and overpay for a bigger name. Brian Thomas Jr. would likely cost more, and the trail has gone cold. A smaller veteran addition or a promotion for Valdes-Scantling might be smarter than spending a premium pick.

And number three, keep an eye on cornerback depth even if Kaboly says it’s “WR or nothing.” With Echols and Slaughter both hurt, a cheap depth corner might be worth a late-round pick. But I’ll defer to the reporting: the focus seems to be on receiver.
