Steelers Nation, Omar Khan has done it again. And depending on who you ask, that sentence is either a compliment or a complaint.

Here’s what happened. The NFL trade deadline closed Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern, and for the second year in a row, Pittsburgh’s general manager let it pass without a deadline-day trade. No receiver. No cornerback. No pass rusher. No veteran shipped out for picks. The six-and-two Steelers walked out of deadline week with the same roster they walked in with.

The source material for today’s video was recorded before the deadline, so it framed this as a decision still to come. It’s not anymore. Khan made his final decision, and it was to trust the team in the building, plus two key players who are close to coming back from injury.

So that’s the angle for today. We’ll look at why “again” is the right word, by comparing this deadline to Khan’s last two. We’ll go through what Mike McCarthy said the day before the deadline, because it told you exactly where this was heading. We’ll dig into the two internal reinforcements who might matter more than any trade. And we’ll connect it all to Sunday night in Cincinnati, where six and two meets six and two. My board at the end, opinion only.

WHY “AGAIN” IS THE RIGHT WORD

Let’s start with a little history, because this isn’t the first time Khan has played it this way.

Go back to the 2024 deadline. That year, Khan was aggressive. He traded a 2025 fifth-round pick to the New York Jets for receiver Mike Williams, and a 2025 seventh-round pick to Green Bay for pass rusher Preston Smith. The Steelers were trying to push for a deep run. And then, as Khan himself admitted later, the team lost five straight games to end the year.

In February of 2025, Steelers Now asked Khan if he regretted those moves. His answer was firm: “I think both of those guys helped us. If I had to do it again, I would do it again.” But he also added the line that probably shaped everything since: “But at the end of the day, the result was we lost five straight games and that’s not good enough.”

Then came the 2025 deadline. According to ESPN’s Brooke Pryor, Pittsburgh made no deadline-day trades. The only in-season trade was a late-round pick swap for safety Kyle Dugger the week before. The team was in talks for receiver Jakobi Meyers but got outbid by Jacksonville. And Pryor wrote that the Steelers “held on to valuable draft capital with an eye on the 2026 draft.”

And now, 2026. Same approach. No moves. According to DK Pittsburgh Sports, the Steelers pursued two receivers but stood pat, with “a reluctance to make moves that wouldn’t benefit them beyond 2026.”

So when you hear “Khan has done it again,” understand the pattern. After the 2024 deadline didn’t produce results, he’s now gone two straight years protecting draft capital rather than buying rentals. That’s a philosophy, not an accident.

WHAT DULAC SAID BEFORE THE DEADLINE

The transcript this video is built on leaned heavily on Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette, and his reporting turned out to be spot on.

According to the transcript, Dulac said the Steelers weren’t interested in selling their current players and weren’t looking to make an expensive investment for the second half. On November 4, Dulac said on 102.5 WDVE: “They’re not looking to make a move, and they’re certainly not looking to get rid of one of their players.”

The transcript also says other teams called Pittsburgh about some of its defensive players, and that the Steelers weren’t interested in breaking up the group. I couldn’t confirm which players drew calls, so I’ll leave it there. But the outcome matches the reporting: nobody left.

Dulac also raised the fit question on Josh Downs, writing that at five-foot-nine and 171 pounds, Downs “does not appear to be the type of receiver Mike McCarthy appears to favor.” Downs ended up getting traded to the Kansas City Chiefs.

WHAT McCARTHY SAID THE DAY BEFORE

Here’s the part I think a lot of fans missed. Mike McCarthy basically told everybody what was going to happen at his Monday press conference, one day before the deadline.

According to Heavy, McCarthy said: “Roster additions are definitely a part of it.” That sounds like he’s open to a trade. But then he followed it with this: “We just want to get our guys healthy.” And then: “I’m worried about the ones who are here.”

Read those three sentences together. That’s a head coach telling you the priority is the players already on the roster.

He also gave Khan and the front office credit for doing their homework. According to Athlon Sports on Yahoo, McCarthy said: “I know [General manager Omar Khan] and his staff are doing their due diligence.”

Due diligence. Not desperation. That’s exactly what we got.

THE INTERNAL TRADES: PITTMAN AND HARMON

Now let’s get to the part of this story that should actually make you feel better about the deadline: the two players who could return soon.

On Monday, McCarthy was asked about receiver Michael Pittman Jr. and defensive lineman Derrick Harmon. His answer, according to Steelers Now: “They’re getting close. I really can’t project anything for Cincinnati yet.” And on Pittman specifically: “I think Michael is getting close. I think his workout was, I want to say Friday.”

That’s not a guarantee. McCarthy was careful not to promise either player for Sunday. But “getting close” from a head coach the day before the deadline is a meaningful signal. If those two come back healthy for the stretch run, it’s like adding two starters without giving up a single pick.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: THE RECEIVER PITTSBURGH ALREADY TRADED FOR

Let’s talk about Pittman first, because in a lot of ways he’s the receiver the Steelers already acquired.

Pittsburgh brought him in from Indianapolis earlier this year to be a reliable, experienced target alongside DK Metcalf. But the foot injury has wrecked his first season in black and gold. According to Heavy, he hasn’t played since October 1. He’s appeared in only three games this season.

He’s been honest about how frustrating it’s been. In his words, according to Steelers Now: “It’s been tough, the most injuries I’ve had to deal with in my seven years.” And he’s been clear that he won’t rush it: “It’d be great to be back after (the) bye week, which is what I’d like, but I can’t go out there if I’m not effective.”

That’s the right mindset. A receiver playing at seventy percent on a bad foot doesn’t help anybody. A receiver at full strength in December and January could be a big piece of a playoff run.

Think about what a healthy Pittman means. Defenses already have to deal with Metcalf on the outside. Rookie Germie Bernard has carved out a role. Add Pittman, a big, physical receiver who wins on intermediate routes, and suddenly opposing coordinators have three receivers to account for, plus tight ends Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington. That’s the kind of depth the trade deadline was supposed to provide, and Pittsburgh might get it from within.

And here’s the thing about Pittman’s size. Remember Dulac’s point that McCarthy prefers bigger receivers? Pittman fits that mold. He’s exactly the type McCarthy wants. If he’s healthy, he may be a better fit for this offense than any slot receiver the Steelers could have traded for.

DERRICK HARMON: THE DEFENSIVE BOOST

Now let’s talk about Harmon, because his return could matter even more against Cincinnati.

Harmon went on injured reserve on October 5 with a foot injury, according to Steelers Now, after leaving the Week 4 loss to Cleveland in the second quarter. He was carted off the field.

Before the injury, according to the transcript, he had twelve tackles, a sack and a half and a forced fumble in his first four games. I couldn’t confirm that exact line, but I can confirm the biggest play he made this year. In the Week 3 win over the Bengals, it was Harmon who strip-sacked Joe Burrow in the final minute, and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered the fumble to seal Pittsburgh’s 30-27 victory.

Think about that. The last time the Steelers saw Burrow, Harmon ended the game. That’s why getting him back for the rematch would be so meaningful.

Defensive line coach Domata Peko has been high on him from the start. Back in September, he said: “He’s a guy that, man, has a very, very high ceiling.”

And the defensive line needs him. Sebastian Joseph-Day is also on injured reserve with a triceps injury. Rookie Gabriel Rubio and undrafted rookie Kevin Jobity Jr. have filled in admirably, but a healthy Harmon would give the front another disruptive body to throw at a quarterback who punishes you when he has time.

THE OTHER RECEIVERS IN THE PIPELINE

Pittman isn’t the only receiver who could help down the stretch.

According to the transcript, Ben Skowronek is expected back, and Steelers.com’s Bob Labriola had already mentioned that Skowronek was practicing and could be in the mix for Cincinnati. The transcript also says Roman Wilson could become available later in November. Reports on Wilson’s timeline have varied, so I’d treat that as a possibility rather than a promise.

And veteran Marquez Valdes-Scantling has been on the practice squad since October, after the Chargers released him. He has a long history with Rodgers and McCarthy from Green Bay, which makes him an easy elevation if the room gets thin.

So when you add it all up, the Steelers’ receiver plan for the second half looks like this. Metcalf and Bernard now. Pittman when he’s ready. Skowronek and Valdes-Scantling for depth. And Wilson, maybe, later. That’s not flashy, but it’s not empty either.

THE METCALF QUESTION

One more note on the receivers, because it’s been a topic all week.

According to Athlon Sports on Yahoo, Metcalf had just three receptions over the last two games. Nick Farabaugh of PennLive wrote that McCarthy “always looks at himself first when he sees a lack of production from his top weapons.” That’s Farabaugh’s characterization, not a direct McCarthy quote, but it fits the coach’s approach. When the production dips, the first question is about the scheme, not just the player.

That’s another reason the Pittman return matters. If defenses have to respect a healthy Pittman, Metcalf should see more favorable coverage. And if the staff gets Metcalf involved early with easier throws, he can get back into rhythm.

THE DEFENSE THAT MAKES STANDING PAT POSSIBLE

Let’s be real about something. The only reason Khan can afford to stand pat is that the defense has been this good.

According to the transcript, the Steelers have twenty-six sacks this season, tied for third in the NFL. I couldn’t confirm that exact ranking, but I can tell you they had eight sacks in the last game alone against Cleveland, with T.J. Watt getting two, Patrick Queen and Cam Heyward a sack and a half each, and Nick Herbig and Jobity one apiece.

The transcript also says McCarthy recently praised the communication between Heyward, Watt and the younger defensive linemen, and that the Steelers recorded only one sack in the first meeting with Cincinnati. Again, I couldn’t confirm those exact details, but the one sack in that game we know about was the one that mattered most, Harmon’s strip-sack at the end.

Here’s what has clearly changed since September. Before the Week 5 game against Indianapolis, defensive coordinator Patrick Graham moved from the coaches’ booth to the sideline. McCarthy said, “It needed to be different.” Since then, the Steelers haven’t lost. Four straight wins. The defense held Jonathan Taylor to forty-five yards in that first game after the change and held Cleveland to thirty-four rushing yards in the last one.

A front office that sees that kind of improvement from within is going to be less tempted to trade for help from the outside.

WHAT OTHER TEAMS DID

To be fair, let’s look at what the competition did, because some teams went all in.

Jacksonville made the biggest splash, acquiring safety Jessie Bates III from Atlanta for two draft picks. The transcript says it was a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth. The Jaguars also added defensive tackles Davon Godchaux and Jordan Davis. Buffalo traded for Amani Hooker, the league’s interception leader, from Tennessee. And Kansas City landed Josh Downs.

The transcript makes an important point here, and I agree with it. Pittsburgh doesn’t have to copy Jacksonville’s strategy. The Steelers have to make the right move for their own roster, not just make a move because another team grabbed headlines.

Still, I won’t pretend this doesn’t create pressure. When Buffalo, Jacksonville and Kansas City all get better and Pittsburgh doesn’t, the Steelers have to prove that their in-house plan was enough.

HOW A RETURN FROM INJURED RESERVE ACTUALLY WORKS

Since so much of this hinges on Pittman and Harmon, it’s worth explaining how the injured reserve process works, because “getting close” can mean a few different things.

Under NFL rules, a player on injured reserve has to miss at least four games. Both Pittman and Harmon are well past that. But they can’t just walk back onto the field. First, the team has to designate them to return, which opens a twenty-one-day window for them to practice. During that window, the team decides whether to activate them to the active roster. If it doesn’t, they revert to injured reserve for the rest of the season.

That’s why this week’s practice reports are so important. If either player is designated to return and practices fully on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, that’s a strong sign he’s ready. If he’s limited, the team may use more of that window before activating him. And remember, Labriola noted earlier that the bye week did not count toward Pittman’s required absence, so the timeline has been longer than some fans expected.

So when McCarthy says “getting close,” the next step to watch is simple: a designation, then practice participation, then an activation. Those three moves, more than any quote, will tell you whether either player suits up in Cincinnati.

WHAT TO WATCH ON THE PRACTICE REPORTS THIS WEEK

Let me give you a short checklist for the next few days.

First, Pittman. Is he designated to return? Is he a full participant by Friday? If yes, he probably has a role on Sunday night, even if it’s limited.

Second, Harmon. Same questions. And if he’s cleared, watch how many snaps he gets. A player coming back from a foot injury might be on a pitch count in his first game.

Third, the rest of the defensive line. With Joseph-Day still out, Rubio and Jobity will keep playing meaningful snaps. How they hold up against a Bengals offensive line protecting Burrow matters a lot.

Fourth, Metcalf. Any sign of a limited practice or a new injury would change the receiver picture overnight, which is exactly why the depth question hung over this deadline.

WHAT McCARTHY’S WORDS REVEAL ABOUT THE PARTNERSHIP

One more thing about those McCarthy quotes, because I think they reveal something important about how this organization works now.

Around the league, there are plenty of deadlines where it’s hard to tell whether a coach and a front office want the same thing. This time, the head coach and the general manager sounded completely aligned. McCarthy publicly backed Khan’s process, saying his staff was doing its “due diligence.” And McCarthy’s own priority, getting his guys healthy and worrying about the ones who are here, matched the final outcome exactly.

That alignment matters. When a coach and a general manager are on different pages at the deadline, you sometimes get panic moves or mixed messages to the locker room. Here, the message is consistent: we believe in this roster, and we believe in the guys coming back.

Is that the right message? We’ll find out. But at least it’s a clear one.

THE BIG GAME: SIX AND TWO VERSUS SIX AND TWO

That brings us to Sunday night.

The Steelers visit the Bengals on Sunday, November 15. Both teams are six and two. Both have won four straight. First place in the AFC North is on the line.

And Cincinnati is rolling. In Madrid last Sunday, the Bengals beat Atlanta 34-17. One correction to the transcript: it says 34-7, but the final was 34-17. Joe Burrow completed 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions. Ja’Marr Chase had ten catches for 155 yards and a touchdown.

So the formula for Pittsburgh is clear. Pressure Burrow without having to blitz. Disciplined coverage that keeps Chase and Tee Higgins in front of you. A run game that controls the clock and keeps Burrow on the sideline. And an offense that turns red-zone trips into touchdowns instead of field goals.

If Harmon is back, the pressure gets better. If Pittman is back, the offense gets another target. Those two “internal trades” could decide whether Pittsburgh sweeps Cincinnati or loses its grip on first place.

THE CASE THAT KHAN GOT IT RIGHT

Let me make the strongest case for Khan’s decision, because I think it deserves one.

One: the price. The Steelers’ 2027 fourth-round pick was already gone from the Broderick Jones trade. Matching what the Colts wanted for Downs meant giving up future picks for a rental.

Two: the quarterback future. Aaron Rodgers has said this is his final season. Pittsburgh is going to need every premium pick it can find to replace him.

Three: the 2024 lesson. Khan bought at the deadline two years ago, and the team lost five straight to close the year. Buying doesn’t guarantee anything.

Four: the reinforcements. Pittman and Harmon are getting close. That’s real help, at no cost.

Five: the results. This team is six and two and hasn’t lost since early October.

THE CASE THAT HE DIDN’T

And now the strongest case against.

One: the window. If Rodgers is retiring, this is the last chance to win with him. Every season is one chance, and this one is now.

Two: the competition. Kansas City, Buffalo and Jacksonville all got better. Pittsburgh didn’t.

Three: the injury risk. “Getting close” isn’t the same as “back.” If Pittman or Harmon has a setback, the depth the deadline could have provided simply isn’t there.

Four: the reaction. Steelers Wire’s Andrew Vasquez graded the deadline a D-minus, and Steelers On SI’s Jacob Punturi wrote that “Yet in the most Steelers fashion ever, the team did nothing.”

Both cases are fair. Where you land probably depends on how much you trust the health of the players coming back.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

The deadline is closed, so here’s a post-deadline board. Opinion, not a report.

Michael Pittman Jr.: don’t rush him. If he’s not effective, as he said himself, he shouldn’t play. If he’s ready for Cincinnati, great. If not, aim for the following week.

Derrick Harmon: if he’s cleared, get him on the field against Burrow. He ended the first game. Let him try to do it again.

Germie Bernard: keep him heavily involved. He’s earned that role, and Pittman’s return shouldn’t take it away.

DK Metcalf: easier throws early. Rebuild the rhythm before the deep shots.

The core: untouchable. Watt, Highsmith, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier.
