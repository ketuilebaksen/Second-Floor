Two-time thousand-yard rusher. That's who the Pittsburgh Steelers could be getting back for the Colts game. Not through a blockbuster trade. Not by spending a draft pick. Rico Dowdle, the running back they signed this offseason after back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons, has what NFL Network's Ian Rapoport called a "pretty good chance" to return from his toe injury against Indianapolis next Sunday. And he might not be the only reinforcement walking through that door. Veteran safety DeShon Elliott, the guy who's been called the leader of this secondary, is now eligible to come off injured reserve. For a team that just lost a heartbreaker in Cleveland and watched its best young cornerback get traded to Dallas, that's the best news Steelers Nation has heard in weeks.

Let me be upfront with you before we go any further, because I know how titles work on YouTube. The Steelers did not make a big trade this weekend. No new superstar arrived. What we're talking about is Pittsburgh adding key players back into the lineup, players who were expected to be major pieces of this team before injuries took them away. And honestly? That can matter more than a trade. Getting your own guys back costs you nothing, and they already know the playbook.

Let's start with the big one on offense. Rico Dowdle.

Here's why Dowdle matters. In 2024, with the Dallas Cowboys, he ran for over 1,000 yards. Dallas didn't bring him back. In 2025, with the Carolina Panthers, he did it again, running for 1,076 yards and catching 39 passes. And again, his team let him walk. Pittsburgh scooped him up this offseason, and the plan was simple: give this offense a reliable, physical lead back to pair with Jaylen Warren and take pressure off a 42-year-old quarterback.

And here's the part a lot of fans forget. Mike McCarthy knows Rico Dowdle better than almost anyone. Dowdle went undrafted out of South Carolina in 2020 and signed with Dallas, the same year McCarthy took over as the Cowboys' head coach. McCarthy watched him grind through special teams, injuries and limited roles for years, and he was the head coach in 2024 when Dowdle finally broke out with that first 1,000-yard season. So this isn't a random free agent. This is a back McCarthy trusts, a back who already knows how he wants to run the football. That connection matters when you're trying to get a player up to speed after an injury.

Then the season started, and nothing went according to plan. In Week 1 against Atlanta, Dowdle had eight carries for 15 yards. In Week 2 at New England, seven runs for 22 yards and two catches for 22 more. Then, going after a fumble in that Patriots game, he sprained his toe and was carted off. He's missed the last two games. So far this season, he's at 15 carries for 37 yards. That's not the Rico Dowdle anybody signed up for.

But here's the key detail. The Steelers never put him on injured reserve. Rapoport described the toe injury as day-to-day more than anything else. That tells you the team always expected him back relatively quickly. And with the ten-day break after Thursday night's game, he's had extra time to heal.

Now, you might be thinking, "James, Jaylen Warren has been great. Why do we even need Dowdle?" And you're right that Warren has been outstanding. Against Cincinnati, he ran 17 times for 127 yards and added three catches for 49 more, a career-high 176 yards from scrimmage. Against Cleveland, he led all rushers again with 93 yards on 17 carries plus 33 receiving yards. Warren has been the engine of this offense.

But that's exactly the point. Warren has been carrying the entire backfield on a shoulder that was so sore before the Bengals game that he wasn't catching passes in the walkthrough. Travis Homer and Lew Nichols have chipped in a little, but this has basically been a one-man show. Over a seventeen-game season, that doesn't work. Running backs break down. If the Steelers ride Warren at this pace, they risk losing him by November.

With Dowdle back, McCarthy can split the load. Dowdle as the early-down, between-the-tackles hammer. Warren as the explosive, do-everything back who catches passes, makes guys miss, and picks up blitzes. That's a thunder-and-lightning duo that can wear down a defense over four quarters.

And McCarthy has done this before. In Dallas, he leaned on a two-back system for years, pairing Ezekiel Elliott with Tony Pollard, a power runner and an explosive change-of-pace guy who could also catch out of the backfield. That's basically the Dowdle and Warren blueprint. McCarthy's offenses have always been at their best when the running game gives the quarterback easy play-action looks. If you've watched Rodgers over the years, you know how dangerous he is when the defense has to respect the run and he gets to fake a handoff and push the ball downfield.

And there's a second reason this matters so much, and it's about the quarterback. Aaron Rodgers has been sacked eleven times in the last three games. Four against New England, two against Cincinnati, five against Cleveland. He's 42 years old, and he's told us this is his final season. The best way to protect a quarterback isn't just better blocking. It's a running game that keeps the offense ahead of the sticks. When it's second-and-four, the defense can't just pin its ears back and rush. When it's second-and-eleven, Rodgers is a sitting duck. A healthy Dowdle-Warren combination keeps the Steelers in manageable downs, and that directly protects Rodgers.

Remember the Bengals game? Pittsburgh ran for 138 yards, converted seven of twelve third downs, and scored 30 points. That's what this offense looks like when the run game works. Now imagine that, with two backs instead of one.

Okay, now let's talk about the defensive weapon who could come back. DeShon Elliott.

Elliott is a veteran safety, and if you've followed this team, you know what he brings. Physicality. Communication. Leadership on the back end. Last season, he suffered a season-ending knee injury in Week 8. He worked his way back, said he was 100 percent healthy in the preseason, and even played 20 snaps in a preseason game against the Jets, earning a 93.6 grade from Pro Football Focus. Then, on August 30th, at the 53-man roster deadline, the Steelers placed him on injured reserve with a designation to return. Nobody outside the building really knows exactly why. But the rule meant he had to miss at least the first four games.

Those four games are now done. Atlanta, New England, Cincinnati and Cleveland. Elliott is eligible to return, and his practice window could open as early as this week. Mike McCarthy has praised his dedication to rehab, saying he's made considerable progress, even though he needed extra recovery time. Steelers reporters have noted there hasn't been a lot of chatter about his status, but that he's been around the team and isn't missing in action.

Here's how the return process works, so you know what to watch for. A player on injured reserve with a designation to return can have his practice window opened once he's served the minimum time. Once that window opens, the team has 21 days to activate him to the 53-man roster. So the first sign will be Elliott back on the practice field. If he practices this week and the team feels good about him, he could be activated in time for the Colts. If they take it slower, he could be targeted for Tampa Bay or the following week. Either way, the clock is now running.

Now let me tell you why this matters so much right now. Look at the state of this secondary. Joey Porter Jr. was traded to Dallas on Wednesday night for a 2028 second-round pick and a conditional sixth. Jamel Dean went down with an ankle injury in the second quarter against Cleveland and was in a walking boot. Jalen Ramsey is playing with a broken right wrist. Brandin Echols just came out of the concussion protocol. Asante Samuel Jr. was benched in the Bengals game. And rookie Daylen Everette has been thrown into heavy snaps.

The numbers tell the story. Against Cleveland, the Steelers missed 14 tackles and gave up 183 yards after the catch. Watson's rookie receivers combined for 153 yards. T.J. Watt was so frustrated he asked for a coach's headset in the middle of the game and called the final drive "way too easy" afterward.

A healthy Elliott changes that room. He gives defensive coordinator Patrick Graham a true safety who can play deep, which means Ramsey can move closer to the line or into the slot where his versatility shines, instead of being stretched thin all over the field with a broken wrist. He brings a veteran voice to a group full of moving parts and young players. And he brings a physical tackler to a defense that just missed fourteen tackles. When people talk about adding a "weapon" on defense, this is what they mean. Not just a good player. The right player for exactly what's broken.

Now, I want to be honest about the uncertainty. Neither Dowdle nor Elliott is guaranteed to play against the Colts. "Pretty good chance" isn't a guarantee. "Eligible to return" doesn't mean "activated." We'll learn a lot when the Steelers release their first injury report and practice participation this week. But if both guys are on the field on October 11th, Pittsburgh will look like a noticeably different team than the one that lost in Cleveland.

And there's one more addition worth mentioning, even if it's smaller. After the Porter trade, the Steelers signed cornerback Doneiko Slaughter to the 53-man roster. He's a 24-year-old former undrafted free agent who'd been elevated from the practice squad three times, and since teams can only do that so often, Pittsburgh had to sign him to keep him available. He's mostly a special teams player so far, but with this many injuries at corner, every healthy body counts.

So let's put it together. Here's what a fully reinforced Steelers team could look like against Indianapolis. On offense, Rodgers with two quality running backs, DK Metcalf coming off a 115-yard game, Roman Wilson with touchdowns in back-to-back weeks, and Darnell Washington and Pat Freiermuth as weapons at tight end. On defense, Watt, Highsmith and Herbig on the edge, and a secondary that finally has a veteran safety to anchor it. That's not a 2-2 team. That's a team that can go on a run.

Now, let's talk about the other side of the roster conversation, because it connects directly to all of this. On Saturday, Jonathan Jones of The Athletic named Alex Highsmith on a list of players he's keeping an eye on ahead of the November 10th trade deadline. To be clear, Jones said this isn't an imminent trade. It's a watch list. The logic is that Pittsburgh is paying three edge rushers big money, with T.J. Watt at around 41 million a year and Nick Herbig on a new four-year, 100 million dollar deal, and a move would likely only happen if the Steelers fall out of the playoff race.

And that's why getting Dowdle and Elliott back matters for Highsmith too. The healthier and better this team plays over the next few weeks, the less likely it is to become a seller. Every win makes the trade conversation quieter. Every loss makes it louder. And the AFC North isn't waiting. The Browns are already 3-1. Baltimore and Cincinnati both came into this weekend at 2-1, playing Tennessee and Jacksonville on Sunday. If both win, Pittsburgh wakes up Monday in last place in the division without even playing.

And now let's flip it, because if Pittsburgh does make a deadline move, I'd much rather see them add a weapon than sell one. Two names keep popping up in conversations around this team, so let's talk about both. And as always, this is opinion and analysis, not a report that any deal is happening.

First, Brian Thomas Jr. from Jacksonville. Two years ago, as the 23rd overall pick, he had one of the best rookie seasons for a receiver in recent memory: 87 catches, 1,282 yards, 10 touchdowns and a Pro Bowl. Since then, his production has dropped off. Through three games this season, he has just seven catches for 88 yards and no touchdowns, and his role in that offense has shrunk. He's 23 years old and still on his rookie contract. That combination, elite talent, reduced role, cheap deal, is exactly why his name is everywhere. Jonathan Jones put him on the same watch list as Highsmith. ESPN's Dan Graziano said teams like the Chiefs, 49ers and Raiders are likely to test the Jaguars before the deadline. And Bleacher Report's Kristopher Knox named Pittsburgh as a landing spot, calling him a potential WR1 at a budget cost.

But let me give you the reality check. Jacksonville's general manager said back in March the team had no interest in disrupting the momentum with Thomas, and there were reports during the offseason that the Jaguars turned down significant offers. The price that's been floated is in the range of a first-round pick. And the Jaguars came into this weekend at 2-1, so they're not exactly in sell mode. For Pittsburgh, pairing Thomas with DK Metcalf would give Rodgers two true outside threats in his final season, and that's a dream. But it would cost real capital, and the Steelers would have to decide whether a receiver is a bigger need than the secondary. Fun to talk about. Hard to pull off.

Second, Trevon Diggs. And this one is actually more realistic than people think, for one big reason: it might not even require a trade. Diggs was released by Dallas last December, claimed by Green Bay for the end of the season, and signed with Seattle in August. According to recent reports, he's on the Seahawks' practice squad. In the NFL, any team can sign a player off another team's practice squad to its own active roster without giving up anything in a trade. So if Pittsburgh wanted Diggs, it could simply offer him a spot on the 53-man roster.

And here's the connection that makes this so interesting. Mike McCarthy coached Diggs for five seasons in Dallas. He was there in 2021 when Diggs picked off 11 passes and made first-team All-Pro. Just like with Rico Dowdle, McCarthy knows exactly what he's getting. Diggs is 28, he's dealt with serious knee injuries in recent years, and he's always been more of a ball hawk than a lockdown corner, so he's not a perfect player. But look at this secondary. Dean in a boot, Ramsey with a broken wrist, a rookie learning on the fly, and Porter in Dallas. A low-risk move for a former All-Pro who knows the head coach could be exactly the kind of depth this team needs. Again, nothing's been reported here. But if I'm Omar Khan, I'm at least making that phone call.

Here's my take, and it's opinion, not a report. You don't sell Alex Highsmith while you're getting your own reinforcements back. The front office should give this team a chance to show what it looks like with Dowdle and Elliott healthy before making any decision about selling. And honestly, if they want to make a deadline move, it should be to add a cornerback, not subtract a pass rusher. Edge depth is insurance for a soon-to-be 32-year-old T.J. Watt. A healthy secondary is what fixes the defense.

And speaking of Watt, here's a fun little detail. He turns 32 on October 11th. The same day as the Colts game. What a birthday present it would be to have Elliott back behind him and a healthy running game making Rodgers' life easier on the other side of the ball.

Let me also give you the other storylines to watch this week. Derrick Harmon's foot is the big one. He was carted off in Cleveland and has been in a walking boot, and we're still waiting on a clear timeline. If he's out for a while, the defense loses its best young interior lineman. Roman Wilson got hurt late in the Browns game, and we'll need an update on him. Dean's ankle will tell us how desperate the cornerback situation really is. And down in Dallas, Joey Porter Jr. could make his Cowboys debut this Sunday against Houston, which will keep the trade conversation alive no matter what.

And for some extra context, the Colts haven't exactly been a brick wall on defense this year. Baltimore put 41 points on Indianapolis back in Week 1. Now, a lot can change in a month, and the Colts will come into Pittsburgh with their own game plan, but that tells you a balanced Steelers offense with two running backs and a healthy Rodgers has a real chance to move the ball. The key is avoiding the self-inflicted mistakes that killed them in Cleveland.

And then there's the Colts game itself. It's at home, at Acrisure Stadium, after a ten-day break. Michael Pittman Jr. faces the team that traded him to Pittsburgh this offseason. A win gets the Steelers to 3-2 before a trip to Tampa Bay. A loss drops them to 2-3, and suddenly every trade rumor becomes front-page news. So this isn't just another game. With these reinforcements, it's a chance to reset the season.

So what has to go right? Here's my checklist for the Colts. Number one, get Dowdle and Warren both involved early, establish the run, and keep Rodgers upright. Number two, if Elliott is active, let him quarterback the back end and free Ramsey to roam. Number three, clean up the penalties. Fourteen flags in Cleveland cannot happen again. Number four, tackle. Fourteen missed tackles cannot happen again either. And number five, finish. The Steelers have been in every game this year. They need to start closing them out.

And let's not overlook the emotional side of this. Getting Elliott back would give this secondary a familiar voice after a week where the room lost Porter in a trade and watched Dean leave the game and end up in a walking boot. Players talk about energy all the time, and sometimes fans roll their eyes. But when a respected veteran walks back into a meeting room after months of rehab, it lifts everybody. Same with Dowdle in the running back room. Warren has been carrying the load by himself. Having a partner back changes the vibe of the whole offense. After a week as rough as this one, the Steelers need that kind of jolt.

So Steelers Nation, here's where I land. The Steelers didn't need a blockbuster trade to add weapons this week. They might be getting a two-time thousand-yard rusher and their veteran safety back at the exact moment they need them most. That's not a fix-everything solution. The pass protection still needs work, the secondary is still thin, and the division is brutal. But it gives this team something it hasn't had in a while: options. And with options, a 2-2 team with a Hall of Fame quarterback in his final season can still make some noise.
