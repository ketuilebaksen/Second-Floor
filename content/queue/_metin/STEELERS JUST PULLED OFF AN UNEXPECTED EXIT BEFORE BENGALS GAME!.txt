Steelers Nation, I checked the transaction wire before I sat down to record this, and I want you to hear the result from me before you hear it from a thumbnail. As of today, there is no surprise release. No player walked out of the building. No trade sent anyone packing. The Steelers’ official transaction log shows no moves at all since October 24, when DeShon Elliott was activated off injured reserve.

So why are people talking about an “exit”? Because there are three different exits floating around this team right now, and they all matter heading into the November 15 trip to Cincinnati. One is an exit a lot of you are afraid of. One is an exit that could actually help Pittsburgh. And one is an exit that has nothing to do with the roster and everything to do with how this offense throws the football.

Let’s walk through all three, keep the facts separate from the speculation, and then I’ll put my trade-deadline board on the table, as opinion. The deadline lands Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

EXIT NUMBER ONE: THE ONE YOU’RE WORRIED ABOUT

Let’s start with the name that has the comment section on fire: Alex Highsmith.

Here is exactly what happened. Heavy published a trade pitch that sends Highsmith to Kansas City in return for a 2027 second-round pick. That’s it. A writer’s idea. The logic behind it is that Pittsburgh would be dealing from a deep spot on the roster, and Kansas City needs edge help badly enough to pay for it.

The source material I’m working from goes a step further and calls a second-rounder “a great return,” and it argues that because Kansas City is an AFC contender, Pittsburgh should charge what it calls a “rival tax.” It also claims several teams have already shown interest in Highsmith. I haven’t seen any reporting that backs up that last part, so I’m not going to tell you there’s a line forming outside Omar Khan’s office. There isn’t one on the record.

Now let’s talk about why this exit isn’t happening.

Highsmith is in year three of the four-year, sixty-eight million dollar deal he signed in 2023, so whoever has him also has him for next season. Through eight games he’s got eighteen tackles and three and a half sacks, after a nine-and-a-half-sack season a year ago. Those aren’t numbers you dump in November.

More importantly, the people who actually cover this team have shut the door. Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette said the Steelers, sitting in first place, aren’t going to move one of the players who got them there, and he singled out Highsmith by name. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler went even broader, saying he doesn’t expect Pittsburgh to move anyone.

Here’s my angle on the “rival tax” argument. It actually cuts the other way. The fact that Kansas City is a real AFC threat is the best reason not to deal with them at all. Picture a January game at Arrowhead where Highsmith is lined up across from Pittsburgh’s right tackle. No second-round pick in the world makes that picture okay.

And remember what this pass rush did against Cleveland. Eight sacks in one afternoon. Thirty-four rushing yards allowed. When a group is playing like that, you add to it, you don’t subtract from it.

So exit number one, Highsmith leaving, is a proposal and nothing more. Put it to rest.

THE PATIENCE ARGUMENT

The source also leans on ESPN’s Brooke Pryor, saying her deadline goal for the team can be summed up as: don’t mortgage the future for the present. It adds that Pryor pointed to the Browns win as the team’s most well-rounded performance of the year, and that she sees the long-term quarterback search as the top priority. I wasn’t able to pull up her original piece, so I’m treating that as a summary of her view rather than a verbatim quote.

The idea behind it, though, is hard to argue with. The source mentions young quarterbacks on the roster in Will Howard and Drew Allar, and it suggests the answer could already be in the building. Maybe, maybe not. But either way, protecting high draft picks keeps every door open. Spend late-round capital on targeted help. Keep the premium picks for the day the franchise needs to go get its next quarterback.

That’s the backdrop for exit number two.

EXIT NUMBER TWO: THE ONE THAT COULD HELP PITTSBURGH

This is the interesting one. It’s not about someone leaving Pittsburgh. It’s about a young cornerback possibly leaving Washington.

His name is Mike Sainristil. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated put him on the radar this week. According to the transcript, Breer wrote that Washington isn’t in full seller mode, since the Commanders are still in range in the NFC East, but that the team has been fielding calls, and that Sainristil is one player he’s heard could be available. In the version I was able to verify, Breer wrote: “Sainristil could have a market with teams that liked him coming out of the draft.”

And here’s the Pittsburgh hook. Joe Whitt Jr., in his first season as the Steelers’ secondary coach, was Washington’s defensive coordinator for Sainristil’s first two NFL seasons, 2024 and 2025. That’s not a scout who watched film on him. That’s the coach who built a game plan around him every single week.

One correction before we go further. The transcript at one point describes Sainristil’s college transformation as going from wide receiver to “playmaking quarterback.” That’s wrong. At Michigan, he moved from receiver to defensive back, and that’s where he became a star.

So what does the résumé look like? The source credits him with thirty-four games, thirty starts, twenty-six passes defensed and six interceptions across those first two seasons under Whitt. I couldn’t independently confirm every one of those numbers, so let’s call them approximate. In college, the 2023 season stands out: Michigan went fifteen and oh and won the national title with Jesse Minter running the defense, and the source credits Sainristil with six interceptions, two of them returned for touchdowns, during that run. Before the 2024 draft, NFL.com’s Lance Zierlein reportedly described him as a reliable, tough nickel with upside, praising his awareness in zone coverage.

WHY HE’S AVAILABLE, AND WHY THAT’S AN OPPORTUNITY

Players with that kind of track record don’t usually show up on the trade market. So what changed?

According to the source, which cites Pro Football Focus, Sainristil played mostly outside corner as a rookie, then shifted toward the slot in 2025, and this year he’s been moved around the Commanders’ secondary without finding a home. The usage tells the story. The source says he played more than eighty percent of the defensive snaps in each of Washington’s first two games, then less than half every week since, and that against Philadelphia this past Sunday, he only played special teams. It credits him with eighteen combined tackles and one tackle for loss in seven games.

I’d treat those snap figures as reported rather than confirmed, but the direction is clear. Washington has moved on from him as a core piece. And when a team moves on, the price drops.

For Pittsburgh, the fit is about the nickel spot. Jalen Ramsey has been excellent there this year, but he’s also being asked to cover safety and outside corner depending on the week. And with Brandin Echols dealing with an injury, the slot depth behind Ramsey is thinner than you’d like. A true nickel option would let the staff use Ramsey wherever he’s most dangerous, instead of wherever the injury report sends him.

There’s competition, too. Breer reportedly connected Sainristil to two head coaches who mentored him at Michigan: Mike Macdonald and Jesse Minter. That’s natural interest from people who know him well. The source also floats the idea of grabbing him to keep him away from Baltimore, but I haven’t seen any reporting that ties the Ravens to him, so I’d file that under speculation.

So exit number two is an exit Pittsburgh could take advantage of. A young corner leaving a situation that isn’t working, landing with the coach who knows him best, at a price that shouldn’t hurt.

EXIT NUMBER THREE: THE ONE THAT’S ABOUT THE PLAYBOOK

Now let’s get to the third exit, and it’s the one I think matters most for the Bengals game.

It isn’t about cutting DK Metcalf. That’s not happening. It’s about whether the Steelers exit a habit that hasn’t been working: the high-difficulty shots to Metcalf that keep falling incomplete.

First, the play everybody keeps talking about. Against Cleveland, Rodgers threw a deep ball down the middle to Metcalf. It went through his hands and turned into an interception. That’s the moment that reignited the conversation.

Then came the mailbag. The source says Gerry Dulac was asked how the Steelers could fix Metcalf, and that his reply was: “You don’t. He is what he is.” I haven’t been able to find the original mailbag, so I’m passing that along as a reported quote, not one I’ve verified. If it’s accurate, it’s about as blunt as it gets.

What’s more interesting is the second part of what Dulac reportedly said. According to the source, he wants Rodgers to stop forcing the so-called fifty-fifty balls: the end-zone fades and the deep sideline throws. To him, those look more like twenty-five, seventy-five. They might be fifty-fifty balls for an elite contested-catch receiver, he argues, but that’s not what Metcalf is right now.

Here’s what we know for sure from Steelers.com. In Bob Labriola’s November 3 Asked and Answered, he noted that Metcalf had a team-high fifty-eight targets heading into the Browns game, and then had six targets and one catch for four yards against Cleveland. Labriola also made the point that Metcalf’s issue isn’t effort, and that the team will need him later in the season.

So the honest version is this. Metcalf stays. But the way the Steelers use him could, and probably should, change. Fewer coin flips. More throws where he catches the ball in rhythm and gets to use that size after the catch. If the bye week produces that kind of adjustment, it’s the most valuable exit this team could make.

And Rodgers has been honest that the offense needs to be better. His words after the Browns game: “Defense played excellent. Special teams did fine. But offense should have had a lot more points.”

THE ROOKIE WHO SHOULD GET MORE OF THE BALL

Now, the source closes that section with a name, and I owe you a correction of my own.

In an earlier video, I said I couldn’t match a receiver named “Jeremy Bernard” to Pittsburgh’s roster. That was my mistake. The player is Germie Bernard, the Steelers’ second-round pick, forty-seventh overall, in the 2026 draft out of Alabama. He’s very real, and he’s been one of the bright spots of the passing game.

Here’s his story so far. Bernard started the year as the fourth receiver. Then Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson went down, and the rookie got thrown into a much bigger role. Through six weeks, he had twenty-four catches, second among rookie receivers, with seventeen of those coming in a two-game stretch. That run included an eleven-catch, eighty-seven-yard game against Tampa Bay in which he caught the overtime game-winning touchdown. Against the Colts, he had six catches for eighty yards, thirty-nine of them after the catch.

And Rodgers has noticed. He said: “There comes a time when the confidence starts to match the potential, and I think we’re reaching that time with Germie.” That’s not something a veteran quarterback says lightly about a rookie.

Bernard’s side of it is just as telling. Back in June, he said of Rodgers: “He’s just pouring into me as much as he can.” And in October, he explained what he’s been focused on: “Just learning as much as I can, getting him to trust me.”

So why does the source say he’s barely been in the game plan lately? The recent production has dipped. He had a quiet game against the Saints, and against Cleveland in Week 8 he caught all five of his targets, but for only thirty yards. Five-for-five is efficient. Thirty yards is not a featured role.

That’s the opportunity coming out of the bye. Bernard can make people miss. He’s sure-handed. And he hasn’t really been used as a vertical threat yet. If the staff wants to move away from contested deep balls to Metcalf, scheming the rookie open on crossers and option routes is an obvious place to start.

WHAT THE BYE WEEK SHOULD PRODUCE

Let me get specific about what I’d want to see from the offense when the Steelers take the field in Cincinnati, because “use Metcalf better” is easy to say and harder to put on a call sheet.

First, early touches. Get Metcalf two or three catches in the opening drives on throws he can catch in stride: slants, quick outs, a crosser off play-action. Confidence for a receiver usually starts with the easy ones.

Second, fewer fades. When the Steelers are in the red zone, the default doesn’t need to be a one-on-one jump ball to the corner. Use Metcalf as a decoy to pull coverage, and let Warren, a tight end or Bernard work underneath.

Third, a defined role for the rookie. Bernard should have plays designed for him every week, not just targets that find him when the first read is covered. A screen, a jet motion, a route that gets him the ball on the move. Make the defense account for him.

Fourth, rhythm for Rodgers. The offensive coordinator talked about the timing of the quarterback’s footwork. If the drops and the breaks line up, the ball comes out on time, and the pass rush becomes less of a factor. That’s the whole ballgame against a team that knows you as well as Cincinnati does.

None of that requires a trade. It just requires the bye week to do its job.

HOW A DEADLINE DEAL WOULD ACTUALLY COME TOGETHER

One last practical note on Sainristil. Deadline deals for players like him tend to happen late, often on the final day, because the selling team wants to see who else calls. Washington has every reason to wait until Tuesday to find out whether a better offer appears. That means Pittsburgh doesn’t need to rush, but it does need to have its number set and be ready to move when the market settles.

WHY THE BENGALS GAME RAISES THE STAKES

Let’s put this all in context.

The Steelers are six and two, first in the AFC North. The run game, led by Jaylen Warren, has kept the offense ahead of the chains and helped Pittsburgh control time of possession. The defense has been nasty. This team is better on both sides of the ball than it was a year ago.

But the next game is on the road in Cincinnati on November 15, against a Bengals team that’s five and two and sitting right behind Pittsburgh in the division. The Steelers won the first meeting back in Week 3, but a road game in a division race is a completely different animal.

Here’s something worth remembering: Pittsburgh has already seen how quickly a division game can flip. Earlier this season, they lost a Thursday night game to Cleveland, 27-24, before getting their revenge with the 27-6 win. Division opponents know you well. The rematch is never the same game twice.

That’s why these three exits matter. Keep Highsmith, and you keep a pass rush that can rattle any quarterback. Add a corner like Sainristil, and you get depth for a game where Cincinnati will try to attack the slot. Fix the way the passing game uses Metcalf and Bernard, and you give Rodgers more than one way to move the ball when Warren gets bottled up.

MY TRADE BLOCK BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board, and please treat it as opinion, not a report. Nothing here should be read as an active negotiation.

Alex Highsmith to Kansas City for a 2027 second: no. Not to a conference contender, not in November, not for that price.

Mike Sainristil: yes, at a fifth-round price. I’d go a bit higher because of the Whitt connection, but there’s a catch. Pittsburgh’s 2027 fourth-round pick already went to Dallas in the Broderick Jones deal, so a fourth would mean getting creative with 2028 picks. If Washington insists on a third for a player who’s been limited to special teams, I walk away.

Receiver help: still on the list, but I’m a little less desperate than I was a week ago, because Bernard deserves a bigger share of the targets. A one-year veteran at a late Day 3 price, like the Darnell Mooney idea we’ve talked about, is still sensible. Anything that costs real capital, I pass.

DK Metcalf: stays. Change the usage, not the roster.

Core players: untouchable. Highsmith, Watt, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. Nobody exited the Steelers this week. The roster is the roster. But three exits are worth watching: the Highsmith exit that isn’t happening, the Sainristil exit from Washington that could open a door for Pittsburgh, and the exit from a passing-game habit that hasn’t been paying off.

Get two of those right, and this team heads to Cincinnati in great shape. Get all three right, and the Steelers might just take control of the division on November 15.
