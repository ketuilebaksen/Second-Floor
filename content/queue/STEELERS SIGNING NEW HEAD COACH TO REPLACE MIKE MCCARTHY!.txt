Steelers Nation, let me get this out of the way right at the top, because I know what you saw in the title.

The Steelers are not signing a new head coach. Mike McCarthy has not been fired. Nobody is replacing him. There’s no search, no interview, no report of anything like that from anyone who covers this team. McCarthy will be on the sideline in Philadelphia on Sunday, coaching a 6-3 team that’s still in the thick of the AFC playoff race.

So why are we talking about head coaches at all? Because two real stories collided this week, and together they’ve got people asking questions about the headset in Pittsburgh.

The first is a former NFL general manager putting the Cincinnati loss squarely on McCarthy and his coordinators. The second is Mike Tomlin, the man McCarthy replaced, being asked on a national conference call whether he’ll ever coach again, and giving an answer that didn’t quite close the door.

Neither of those is a coaching change. But both of them matter. So let’s break them down honestly, and then get to the injury report that came out Friday for Sunday’s game.

Let’s go.

STORY ONE: THE CRITICISM

Let’s start with what was said, because the words matter.

After the 38-18 loss in Cincinnati, former NFL general manager Doug Whaley went on WPXI’s Black and Gold Zone and didn’t hold back. According to Steeler Nation, here’s what he said:

“People will say this game was lost on the field. This game was lost the beginning of the week, during game planning.”

He kept going: “The coordinators for the Cincinnati Bengals out-coached, out-classed the coordinators and the head coach for the Pittsburgh Steelers.”

And then the line that got everyone’s attention: “The way they moved the ball, it looked like the Steelers gave them a practice script of defenses they were going to run.”

He finished with this: “Everybody’s going to start talking about the players. I think it starts right at the top with the coaches.”

WHO DOUG WHALEY IS

That criticism carries weight because of who’s saying it.

Whaley is a former general manager of the Buffalo Bills. And as I understand it, before that, he spent years in the Steelers’ scouting department, so he knows this organization from the inside. He’s not a random voice on social media. He’s someone who has sat in draft rooms and evaluated coaching staffs for a living.

That doesn’t make him automatically right. But it means his opinion deserves to be taken seriously, even if you disagree with it.

WHAT HE DID NOT SAY

Now, an important point that the video I’m working from today handles well.

When Whaley said it looked like the Steelers “gave them a practice script,” he was not accusing anybody of cheating. There’s no evidence the Bengals had Pittsburgh’s game plan. It’s a figure of speech. He was saying the Steelers’ defense looked so predictable that Cincinnati seemed to know what was coming before the snap.

That’s a criticism of preparation and play-calling, not an accusation of wrongdoing. And that distinction matters, so I want to be clear about it.

THE PART OF HIS CRITICISM THAT STICKS

Here’s why Whaley’s point is hard to dismiss.

The Steelers came into that game off a bye week. They had extra time to prepare. The Bengals, meanwhile, had played the week before in Madrid against Atlanta. On paper, Pittsburgh had the advantage in preparation and rest.

And yet the game looked like this. Joe Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns. Ja’Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards. Cincinnati put up 484 yards to Pittsburgh’s 222, according to the Northeast Times. And the Steelers were down 24-3 at halftime.

When a team has two weeks to prepare and still looks that far behind, it’s fair to ask what happened during those two weeks. That’s Whaley’s point. And honestly, McCarthy seems to agree with part of it. This week he said, according to the Northeast Times: “Being on the road, there are some things mechanically and operational that we were not on top of.”

Defensive coordinator Patrick Graham said, simply: “We got punched in the mouth.”

So the coaches aren’t hiding from it. They’re owning it.

THE PART OF HIS CRITICISM I’D PUSH BACK ON

But I also think the full picture matters.

This is the same coaching staff that took a team many people doubted to a 6-2 record before the bye. The same staff that turned a 2-2 start into a four-game winning streak. The same staff that built one of the best run games in the league around Jaylen Warren.

According to the Northeast Times, even after Cincinnati, this defense ranks seventh in points allowed and third against the run. The Steelers are plus-five in turnover margin. And they’re 4-0 at home.

So yes, the staff got beaten badly in one game. But “out-coached for one night” and “the wrong coaches for this team” are two very different conclusions. One bad game, even a really bad one, doesn’t erase nine weeks of evidence.

THE COORDINATORS IN THE CROSSHAIRS

Whaley didn’t just point at McCarthy. He pointed at the coordinators too, so let’s look at them.

On defense, Patrick Graham has already been through one adjustment this season. After the Thursday night loss in Cleveland in Week 4, McCarthy moved Graham out of the booth and down to the sideline, and the defense took off. The Steelers won four straight after that. So Graham has shown he can adapt. What Cincinnati showed is that a great quarterback with two elite receivers can still find the holes when the pressure doesn’t get home.

On offense, it’s a little more complicated, because McCarthy calls the plays and Brian Angelichio is the coordinator. According to the Northeast Times, Angelichio said the offense has struggled to find a rhythm early, especially on the road. That’s an honest admission, and it lines up with what we saw: Rodgers opened the game 0-for-8 in Cincinnati, the first time in his career he’s started a game that way.

So when Whaley says the coordinators were out-coached, he’s not pulling it out of thin air. The question is whether this staff can fix it in a week. Graham did it once already this season. That gives me some confidence.

WHAT A FIRST SEASON USUALLY LOOKS LIKE

Here’s some perspective that I think gets lost.

First seasons for new head coaches are rarely smooth, even for great ones. Tomlin’s first year in Pittsburgh, 2007, ended with a division title and a home playoff loss. McCarthy’s first year in Green Bay, 2006, was an 8-8 season. Both went on to win Super Bowls with those franchises.

McCarthy is 6-3 in his first year in Pittsburgh, with a new defensive coordinator, a new system and a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season. That’s not a coach on the hot seat. That’s a coach with a good team that just had a bad night.

WHY NO COACHING CHANGE IS COMING

And here’s the bigger point, the one that answers the title directly.

The Steelers don’t fire coaches midseason. They barely fire coaches at all. From 1969 until this year, this franchise had exactly three head coaches: Chuck Noll, Bill Cowher and Mike Tomlin. McCarthy is the fourth. He’s in his first season. He’s 6-3. He’s leading a team that’s a game out of first place in the division.

There’s no version of reality where the Rooney family replaces a first-year coach with a winning record in November. It’s not how this organization works, and it’s not something any credible reporter has even hinted at.

What is real is pressure. McCarthy has to show that Cincinnati was a stumble, not a pattern. And the next chance to prove that comes Sunday in Philadelphia.

THE FAIR QUESTIONS MCCARTHY HAS TO ANSWER

The video I’m working from asks three questions I think are completely fair.

How does a team with extra preparation time look so unprepared? How does an offense struggle to find any rhythm? And how does a defense let a quarterback keep attacking the same weak spots without finding an answer?

Those questions don’t have to be answered in a press conference. They have to be answered on the field. If the Steelers come out in Philadelphia looking sharp early, with a clear plan on both sides of the ball, Whaley’s criticism becomes a footnote. If they come out flat again, the criticism gets louder, and it’ll be deserved.

The video also makes a smart point about film. When an opponent finds something that works, the rest of the league studies it. Every defensive coordinator and every offensive coordinator left on the schedule will watch what Cincinnati did. So the Steelers’ coaches don’t just have to fix things for Sunday. They have to fix them for the rest of the season.

STORY TWO: MIKE TOMLIN

Now let’s talk about the other half of this, because this is where the “new head coach” talk really comes from.

Mike Tomlin stepped away from the Steelers after last season, following the Wild Card loss to Houston. He spent 19 seasons in Pittsburgh. The video I’m working from today lists his record: 193 regular-season wins, 13 playoff appearances, a Super Bowl title after the 2008 season, and not a single losing season. That’s an incredible run of consistency.

But it also ended with years of playoff frustration. The Steelers’ last playoff win came after the 2016 season, at Kansas City. That’s a big part of why the franchise decided it was time for a new voice.

Since then, Tomlin has been working as a studio analyst on NBC’s Football Night in America. And by all accounts, he’s enjoying it.

WHAT TOMLIN ACTUALLY SAID

Here’s what set off the conversation. On November 11, during an NBC conference call, Tomlin was asked about a possible return to coaching.

According to Awful Announcing, his answer was: “Haven’t given one iota of thought about coaching.” And then: “I’ll cross that bridge when I come to it.”

He also described his TV job like this: “I’m having a lot of fun.” He called it “part-time work, full-time pay.” And he said he’s “Really excited about my new role and working to get better each and every week.”

So let’s read that honestly. He’s not planning a comeback. He’s not talking to teams. But he’s also not saying “never.” He’s saying he’ll deal with it when it comes up. And for a coach of his stature, that’s enough to get the whole league talking.

WHAT PEOPLE CLOSE TO THE SITUATION ARE SAYING

There’s some useful reporting on top of that.

According to The Comeback, the Post-Gazette’s Gerry Dulac reported earlier this month that Tomlin had already told the Steelers he doesn’t intend to coach anymore. Dulac added: “We’ll see.” And Cam Heyward, who played his entire career under Tomlin, said around the same time that Tomlin wouldn’t return to an NFL sideline.

So the people closest to him lean toward him staying in television. The Athlon piece syndicated by Yahoo Sports came to the same conclusion, comparing him to Bill Cowher and Tony Dungy, two coaches who were linked to job after job once they left and never went back.

THE CONTRACT ANGLE: COULD THE STEELERS GET COMPENSATION?

Now here’s the part that connects back to Pittsburgh, and it’s genuinely interesting.

According to Dallas Sports Journal on Yardbarker, citing Front Office Sports, the Steelers announced a three-year extension for Tomlin in June 2024 that reportedly ran through the 2026 season, with a team option for 2027. Front Office Sports reported it’s unclear whether that option was exercised. If it was, a team that wants to hire Tomlin could have to compensate the Steelers.

But there’s an important counterpoint. According to The Comeback, Dulac reported that the Steelers are free of any compensation if Tomlin returns to coaching after this season.

So we have two reports pointing in different directions. One says it’s unclear. The other says Pittsburgh wouldn’t get anything after this season. I’m not going to pretend I know which is right. What I’ll say is this: nobody should be counting on draft picks from a Tomlin hire. The video I’m working from today says the same thing, and it’s right.

THE SEAN PAYTON PRECEDENT

The reason people even ask is Sean Payton.

Payton stepped away from New Orleans after the 2021 season and spent 2022 as a FOX studio analyst while still under contract with the Saints. When Denver hired him in January 2023, according to Dallas Sports Journal via Yardbarker, the Broncos sent their first- and second-round picks to New Orleans and got a third-round pick back.

So there’s a precedent: a coach under contract goes to TV, and the team that holds his rights gets paid when he returns. But every contract is different. Without knowing the exact terms of Tomlin’s deal, you can’t assume Pittsburgh would be in the same position New Orleans was.

WHERE TOMLIN COULD LAND, IF HE EVER RETURNS

And just for completeness, here’s where the outside speculation is pointing.

According to Dallas Sports Journal, Pro Football Talk’s Mike Florio listed the Jets, Colts, Eagles and Panthers as teams to watch, and said it was a list off the top of his head. He named Carolina as the one to watch most closely, pointing out that Panthers owner David Tepper is a former Steelers part-owner. Front Office Sports also reported that some people in the league see the Jets and Commanders as possible pursuers if they make changes.

And one detail I found interesting: an unnamed high-ranking official with one NFL team told Front Office Sports, “He will clearly be at the top of the list for any team that has an opening.”

Notice what’s not on any of those lists: Pittsburgh. The Steelers made their choice. They hired McCarthy. Tomlin returning to the Steelers isn’t part of anyone’s reporting.

TOMLIN’S PRICE TAG

There’s also the money, and it isn’t a small detail.

According to Dallas Sports Journal, Florio estimated the top of the coaching market at about $25 million a year, and said it would probably take $30 million or more per year to pull Tomlin out of the studio. Those are Florio’s estimates, not reported offers. And the same report notes that the Giants’ hiring of John Harbaugh was reportedly a five-year deal worth about $100 million.

Front Office Sports also reported that Tomlin’s NBC deal is believed to be lucrative, and that while he can’t jump to another network during it, he’s allowed to return to coaching. So the door is technically open. But it’s an expensive door, and Tomlin himself says he hasn’t even looked at it.

THE IRONY OF SUNDAY

Here’s a small irony for you. One of the teams on Florio’s list is Philadelphia, which is exactly where the Steelers are headed Sunday.

I want to be careful here. That’s Florio’s speculation about the coaching market in general, not a report about the Eagles’ current staff. But it gives you a sense of where this week’s conversation is. A 2-7 Eagles team that’s lost seven straight. A Steelers team coming off its worst game of the year. And the ghost of Mike Tomlin hanging over both of them in the national conversation.

The best way for McCarthy to end all of this talk is simple. Win the game.

NOW, THE FRIDAY INJURY REPORT

Let’s get to the football, because the final injury report came out Friday and it matters.

According to Steelers.com, Friday was a walkthrough, so the report is an estimate of what a full practice would have looked like. And here are the game designations for Sunday.

Questionable: Patrick Queen, hand. Michael Pittman Jr., foot. Roman Wilson, hand. DeShon Elliott, knee. Nick Herbig, hip and ribs. And Gennings Dunker, knee.

Nobody was ruled out.

Pittman, Wilson and Dunker are all still on injured reserve, so they’d have to be activated by Saturday afternoon to play. Queen and Elliott were estimated as full on Friday. Herbig was the only player listed as not participating.

Jalen Ramsey, Darnell Washington, T.J. Watt and Cam Heyward were all estimated as full, and none of them got a game designation.

WHAT THE PLAYERS SAID

Steelers.com also had some good quotes from the guys involved.

Roman Wilson said he spent his time on injured reserve watching film: “The biggest advice I got from teammates and coaches was become a film junkie. Be a film rat.” He added: “It wasn’t ideal, but it was valuable time in my life to take four or four to five weeks to just watch football.”

Spencer Anderson, stepping in at left guard for Mason McCormick, said: “(I’m) as ready as I’m going to be.” And about McCormick’s leadership role: “He’s our vocal leader, so obviously I’ve got to take ownership of that.” Then: “At the end of the day it’s next man up. It stinks for Mason.”

And rookie Gennings Dunker, honest as can be: “It felt pretty good.” But also: “I don’t know where I am skill-wise because this was my first practice.”

I love that honesty from Dunker. It tells you exactly why he probably isn’t starting on Sunday. He knows he’s not there yet.

THE EAGLES’ FINAL REPORT

On the other side, according to Steelers.com, Philadelphia ruled out two players: receiver Dontayvion Wicks, with an ankle and foot injury, and linebacker Zack Baun, with a back injury. Linebacker Smael Mondon is doubtful. And running back Will Shipley is questionable.

DeVonta Smith was a full participant Friday, so expect him to play. And Joe Milton starts at quarterback.

Losing Baun matters. He’s one of the key pieces of that defense. And against a Steelers offense built on the run, a thinner linebacker group is exactly what Pittsburgh wants to see.

WHAT SUNDAY NEEDS TO LOOK LIKE

So let’s tie these stories together.

If Whaley is right that the Steelers lost the Cincinnati game during the week, then the proof of a good week is a good start on Sunday. That means a clean opening drive. A defense that doesn’t give up chunk plays to DeVonta Smith. And a run game that gets Warren going early against a defense without Baun.

The video I’m working from today puts it simply: it wants a defense that communicates better, adjustments when opponents start exploiting a weakness, and an offense that sustains drives instead of putting the defense back on the field. That’s not asking for perfection. It’s asking a professional team to look prepared.

I agree with every word of that.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Opinion, not a report.

Head coach: McCarthy stays, period. No change is coming, and none should. Judge him over 17 games, not one Sunday night.

Whaley’s criticism: fair for one game. Not a verdict on the season. The answer has to come on the field.

Tomlin: enjoy him on NBC. Don’t count on draft compensation. And don’t expect him back in Pittsburgh.

Roman Wilson: activate. He’s been full all week and sounds ready mentally too.

Michael Pittman Jr.: activate with a snap count.

Gennings Dunker: activate only if there’s room, but no start. Let him get his feet under him, like he said himself.

Spencer Anderson: starts at left guard and takes on the vocal role.

Nick Herbig: if he can’t go, Watt, Highsmith and Sawyer carry the edge.

Game plan: start fast. Run the ball at a defense without Zack Baun. Keep DeVonta Smith in front of you.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers are not signing a new head coach. That headline isn’t happening, and you deserve to know that up front.

What is happening is a real test of a first-year coach. A respected former GM says McCarthy got out-coached. The man he replaced is on national TV, keeping the door open just a crack. And the team plays a 2-7 opponent in a building where it hasn’t won since 1965.

That’s pressure, but it’s normal pressure. The kind every Steelers coach has had to handle. And the answer is always the same: you prepare better, you play better, and you win.

Sunday in Philadelphia is McCarthy’s chance to do exactly that.
