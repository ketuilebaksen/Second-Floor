Steelers Nation, the dust has settled on Paris, and the more you look at what happened, the better it gets for Pittsburgh. The 16-10 win over the Saints was not pretty, but it came with a wave of good news. The Steelers are 5-2 for the first time this season. Their biggest division rival in Baltimore just collapsed. Their rookie class is starting to carry real weight. Their running game finally showed up. And according to Steelers Depot’s injury recap, the team came out of the trip with good news on the health front. So today, we’re going through all the good news from this win, the rookies who are changing this roster, and the schedule ahead, including the second half stretch that will decide this season. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Good news number one: a win that travels.

Let’s start with the game itself, because the way the Steelers won matters as much as the result. There were no fireworks in Paris. No comeback, no wild finish. Just cold, physical football. Chris Boswell opened the scoring with a field goal. The Saints tied it. Aaron Rodgers found Darnell Washington for a short touchdown. New Orleans answered right before halftime. And after that, the Saints’ offense never scored again.

Boswell added a field goal at the end of the first half and another in the third quarter. Payton Wilson picked off Tyler Shough at the Steelers’ 11-yard line to end New Orleans’ best drive of the second half. And Pittsburgh closed it out with a long, clock-killing drive built entirely on the ground.

That’s the kind of win that travels. It doesn’t depend on a hot shooting night from the quarterback or a fluky bounce. It depends on owning the line of scrimmage, protecting the football, and making one big defensive play at the right moment. Good teams collect those kinds of wins in October, and they become the foundation for December. Three weeks ago, this team couldn’t buy a road win. Now it has two in a row, one in Florida and one in France.

How far this team has come.

To really appreciate the good news, you have to remember the road. The Steelers opened the season with a 20-13 win over the Falcons. Then came a loss in New England. They bounced back to beat the Bengals 30-27, then lost 27-24 in Cleveland on a Thursday night to fall to 2-2. At that point, plenty of people were ready to write them off.

Since then, it’s been three straight wins: 34-22 over the Colts, a 26-19 overtime comeback in Tampa, and now 16-10 in Paris. Each win has looked different. The Colts game was an offensive explosion. The Tampa game was a defensive stand followed by a late rally. And the Paris game was a ground-and-pound grind. That variety is good news too, because it shows this team can win in more than one way.

Good news number two: the run game is real.

The biggest positive from Paris was the ground game. The Steelers ran 30 times for 148 yards. Jaylen Warren led the way with 101 yards on 18 carries, and Rico Dowdle added 41 more on nine carries, despite being questionable all week with a knee injury.

And remember where this offense was just a week earlier. In Tampa, the Steelers ran only 22 times and Rodgers threw 49 passes. Coming into Paris, according to NFL.com, Pittsburgh had the second-highest pass rate in the league. This time, the plan was clear from the start. As McCarthy put it, “That was the goal to get the rush attempts up.”

That matters for the rest of the season. An offense that can run the ball late in games can protect leads, keep its defense fresh, and take pressure off a 42-year-old quarterback. The offensive line looked like a group that can wear opponents down in the fourth quarter. When you can do that on the road, in a foreign country, after an overseas flight, that tells you something about this team’s toughness.

The supporting cast stepped up too.

It wasn’t just the running backs. DK Metcalf made the biggest play of the day for the offense, a 51-yard catch that set up the touchdown. That’s the kind of explosive play that becomes possible when a defense has to respect the run. Darnell Washington, the big tight end, finished that drive by catching a fade from Rodgers in the end zone, his second touchdown in as many weeks after scoring in Tampa too.

And Chris Boswell bounced back in a big way. A week after missing a 48-yarder in Tampa, he went three-for-three on field goals in Paris, including a 47-yarder as time expired in the first half that gave the Steelers a lead they never gave back. In a 16-10 game, those nine points from Boswell were the difference.

Warren’s breakout season.

And let’s take a moment for Jaylen Warren. In Week 5 against the Colts, he ran for 101 yards, caught six passes for 87 yards, scored twice, and won AFC Offensive Player of the Week. In Week 6, he carried the load late in the overtime comeback. And in Paris, he went over 100 rushing yards again. Rodgers said the team is “almost expecting Jaylen to have 100 yards” at this point, and called him “so dynamic.” For a player who came into the league as an undrafted free agent, this is the season he’s been working toward.

Good news number three: the defense shut down the run.

On the other side of the ball, the Saints ran 17 times for 18 yards. That’s just over a yard per carry. And the Steelers did it with two interior defensive linemen, Derrick Harmon and Sebastian Joseph-Day, on injured reserve.

That’s balance. Stop the run, make the offense one-dimensional, and then win the big moments through the air. Shough did complete a lot of passes, and Chris Olave had a 49-yard catch. But when the Saints got close, the defense came up with the plays. T.J. Watt had a sack that forced a field goal. Wilson had the interception. Patrick Queen had a third-down sack in the fourth quarter that forced a punt. That’s how you win in December and January: limit the run, generate pressure, and create a turnover when it matters most.

Good news number four: Rodgers keeps making history.

Rodgers didn’t have to be Superman, and he never tried to be. He went 19 of 30 for 212 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions. He took what the defense gave him, let the run game and the defense do the heavy lifting, and even scrambled for a first down late in the fourth quarter to help kill the clock.

And that touchdown to Washington moved him past Peyton Manning into sole possession of third place on the NFL’s all-time regular-season touchdown passes list. He’s also, according to CBS Sports, the first quarterback to throw touchdown passes in both Ireland and France. At 42, he’s not just managing games. He’s still adding to his legacy.

Good news number five: the rookies are delivering.

Now here’s the part I think doesn’t get enough attention. This 2026 draft class is already making a real impact. Let’s go through it, because some of these names are going to be important for years.

The first-round pick, at 21st overall, was offensive tackle Max Iheanachor out of Arizona State. He took over at right tackle earlier this season, and he’s looking more and more like a long-term starter every week. On a day when the Steelers ran for 148 yards, the young right tackle was part of a line that controlled the game.

The second-round pick, at 47th overall, was receiver Germie Bernard out of Alabama. With Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve, Bernard has gone from a developmental piece to a key part of the offense. He caught 17 passes in his first two career starts, including the game-winning touchdown in overtime in Tampa. In Paris, he nearly had a 20-yard catch before it was wiped out by a penalty. Rodgers trusts him, and that’s not something you can say about a lot of rookie receivers.

The Day 2 rookies on defense.

In the third round, the Steelers took quarterback Drew Allar out of Penn State, cornerback Daylen Everette out of Georgia, and guard Gennings Dunker out of Iowa. Everette has already made his mark. In Tampa, after a rough start that included a pass interference flag, he bounced back to intercept Jalon Daniels for the first pick of his career, and that turnover helped spark the fourth-quarter comeback.

Allar is developing behind Rodgers, Mason Rudolph, and Will Howard. He’s part of the long-term conversation at quarterback, even if he’s not playing right now.

The late-round finds.

And the late-round picks are contributing too. Receiver Kaden Wetjen, a fourth-round pick out of Iowa, has been one of the few healthy receivers on the active roster during this stretch of injuries. Defensive end Gabriel Rubio, a sixth-round pick out of Notre Dame, has had to step into a bigger role with Harmon and Joseph-Day out. And then there’s safety Robert Spears-Jennings, a seventh-round pick out of Oklahoma, who has improved every week and played well enough in sub-packages that the coaches want to keep him involved even with DeShon Elliott back.

And don’t forget the rest of the class. Gennings Dunker, the Iowa guard taken in the third round, gives the offensive line young depth. Riley Nowakowski, a fifth-round fullback out of Indiana, adds a physical piece for a team that suddenly wants to run the ball 30 times a game. And Eli Heidenreich, a seventh-round pick out of Navy who can play running back or receiver, is part of the backfield depth behind Warren and Dowdle. Not every rookie is starting, but almost every one of them has a role.

McCarthy said it himself last week: “I’m proud of our rookie class.” And when a team can win while its rookies are playing meaningful snaps, that’s how you compete now without sacrificing the future.

Good news number six: DeShon Elliott is back.

Speaking of Elliott, his activation was another piece of good news this weekend. The veteran safety missed the first six games after the knee and hamstring injuries that ended his 2025 season. Now he’s back on the active roster, giving Patrick Graham another physical, experienced voice in the secondary.

Graham’s approach to the safety rotation has been to “let everything happen organically.” That means Elliott, Jaquan Brisker, and Spears-Jennings can all contribute. Depth at safety is exactly what you want heading into the second half of the season, when injuries pile up and offenses start game-planning specifically for your defense. And with the Browns, Bengals, and Eagles coming up, the Steelers are going to face some of the best receiving groups in the league. Having three safeties they trust gives Graham the freedom to disguise coverages and rotate players without losing communication in the back end.

Good news number seven: the division shifted.

And here’s some good news that came from somewhere else. While the Steelers were winning in Paris, the Ravens were collapsing at home. Baltimore led the Bengals 13-0 at halftime and lost 35-13. That drops the Ravens to 4-3 and 0-2 in the division, which puts them in last place in the AFC North.

The Browns won in Tennessee, 27-24, after falling behind 14-0. They’re 5-2 and 2-0 in the division, so they hold the tiebreaker for now. The Bengals are 4-2. And the Steelers are 5-2 and tied for first.

That means the Steelers control their own destiny. Beat the Browns next Sunday, and they’ll be alone in first place heading into the bye.

The road ahead: the Browns.

Let’s look at the schedule, because it gets interesting fast. Next up is the Cleveland Browns at Acrisure Stadium on Sunday, November 1st, at 1 p.m. Both teams are 5-2. The Browns beat the Steelers 27-24 on a Thursday night in Cleveland in Week 4. This is the rematch, and it’s for first place.

McCarthy has been calling this stretch “a four-game series,” and he said, “It’s important for us to go 4-0.” The Steelers are 3-0 in that series. Warren summed up the locker room’s mood in three words: “We can’t wait.”

Why the Browns game is bigger than one win.

There’s more riding on next Sunday than just first place for a week. If the Steelers win, they split the season series with Cleveland and improve to 2-1 in the division, which would match the Browns at 2-1. That makes the tiebreaker picture much more even heading into the second half. If they lose, Cleveland sweeps the season series and goes up two games on Pittsburgh in the division, which would be very hard to overcome.

And there’s the momentum factor. Going into the bye on a four-game winning streak means two weeks of confidence, rest, and preparation before that Sunday night trip to Cincinnati. Going into the bye after a loss means two weeks of questions. McCarthy knows that, and so does this locker room.

After the bye: the gauntlet.

Then comes the Week 9 bye, and after that, the schedule gets serious. In Week 10, the Steelers go to Cincinnati for Sunday Night Football on November 15th. In Week 11, they travel to Philadelphia for a 4:25 p.m. game on November 22nd. Those two road games will tell us a lot about where this team really stands.

Then they come home for Denver on Black Friday, November 27th, at 3 p.m. on Prime Video. Then Houston at home on Sunday Night Football on December 6th. Then a Monday night trip to Jacksonville on December 14th. And then the Ravens come to Pittsburgh on December 20th. The season wraps up with Carolina at home, a trip to Tennessee on January 3rd, and the regular-season finale in Baltimore.

That’s three prime-time games in a five-week stretch, plus a Black Friday showcase. The whole country is going to be watching this team in November and December. A win over the Browns would make every one of those games feel even bigger. The second half of the schedule is not soft by any means. But if the Steelers keep playing like they have the last three weeks, physical, complementary, and tough in the trenches, they’re going to be in every one of those games.

What still needs work.

Now, I don’t want to oversell this. The Steelers aren’t perfect, and nobody is saying they are. The red zone is still a problem, and it’s the one area that could cost them against a team as good as Cleveland. They went just 1-for-4 inside the 20 in Paris, settling for field goals instead of putting the game away. The passing game still needs to be more consistent against man coverage, according to NFL.com. And the secondary is going to be tested more as the season goes on and teams build their game plans around attacking it.

There’s also the strength-of-schedule question. NFL.com noted that the Steelers’ only win so far over a team with a winning record came against the 4-2 Bengals. That’s why the Browns game, and then the Bengals and Eagles road trips, matter so much. Those are the games that will prove whether this is a real contender.

Trade block: what the good news means for the deadline.

Let’s talk about the November 10th trade deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

The good news from Paris doesn’t erase the biggest need on this roster. With Pittman and Wilson on injured reserve, a red zone that went 1-for-4, and a rookie receiver already carrying a big load, adding a reliable veteran target still makes a lot of sense. I keep coming back to Josh Downs in Indianapolis, with rival teams expecting the Colts to sell after firing their general manager.

What I wouldn’t do is touch the defense. Alex Highsmith’s name popped up on a trade board this weekend, but a unit that just held a team to 18 rushing yards doesn’t need to be broken up. And the rookie class is proof that Khan’s long-term plan is working, so I wouldn’t trade away premium picks that could become the next Iheanachor or Bernard. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the deadline. If they are, I’d expect a targeted move for a receiver, not a blockbuster. That’s been the Khan approach all season: fix the specific problem, protect the core, and keep the future intact.

The bottom line.

So what’s the good news after the Saints game? Pretty much all of it. The Steelers are 5-2 and riding a three-game winning streak. The run game is real, with Warren over 100 yards again. The defense held New Orleans to 18 rushing yards. Rodgers passed Peyton Manning. DeShon Elliott is back. The rookies are contributing in a big way. The Ravens just fell to last place. And the team came out of the Paris trip with good news on the injury front.

Now the Browns come to town with first place on the line, and Acrisure Stadium is going to be loud. If Pittsburgh can bring that same physical brand of football home, the rest of the AFC is going to have to deal with a very dangerous Steelers team.
