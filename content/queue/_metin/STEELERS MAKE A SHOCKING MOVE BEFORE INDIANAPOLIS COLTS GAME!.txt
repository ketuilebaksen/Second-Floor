Steelers Nation, one week after Pittsburgh traded Joey Porter Jr. to Dallas, another Steelers defensive back's name just landed in the trade rumor mill, and this time it's a three-time first-team All-Pro. On Wednesday, ESPN's Jeremy Fowler, writing about the Detroit Lions' struggling defense, asked a question that set Steelers Twitter on fire: "Is it worth calling Pittsburgh for Jalen Ramsey?" And then he added the part a lot of people skipped: "Not sure he's available." So today, we're going to break this down properly. What Fowler actually said, why Detroit is desperate, what Ramsey's contract really looks like, what it would mean for a Steelers secondary that's already hanging by a thread, and my honest verdict on whether Omar Khan should even pick up the phone. This is James with Second Floor, and this is a big one.

Before we start, I need to clear out some junk, because I've seen a version of this story floating around that's frankly a mess. It said Mike Tomlin is building the defense. He isn't, Mike McCarthy is the head coach. It said Kenny Pickett has shown flashes at quarterback. Kenny Pickett isn't on this team. Aaron Rodgers is the quarterback. It listed Patrick Peterson and Minkah Fitzpatrick as foundation pieces in this secondary. Peterson isn't here, and Fitzpatrick was literally the player Pittsburgh sent to Miami to get Ramsey in the first place, back in the 2025 offseason. And it talked about Joey Porter Jr. like he's still developing in Pittsburgh. He's in Dallas. If you're going to talk about trading a Steelers cornerback, at least know who's on the roster. Okay. Let's do this right.

Part one: what Fowler actually said.

This matters, because the headlines ran way ahead of the reporting. Fowler was writing about teams that need to make moves before the November 10th trade deadline, and he said Detroit needs cornerback help. Then he floated two possibilities. One was Kansas City's Kristian Fulton, who's been in and out of the Chiefs' lineup, and Fowler even suggested the teams could split Fulton's guaranteed salary to make a deal work. The other was Ramsey. His exact words: "Is it worth calling Pittsburgh for Jalen Ramsey? Not sure he's available, but the Lions' defense seems far from settled."

So let's be crystal clear. This is not a report that the Steelers are shopping Ramsey. This is not a report that Detroit has called. It's a well-connected insider saying, in effect, "if I'm Detroit, I'd at least ask." That's a big difference, and you should keep it in mind every time you see a thumbnail that says "RAMSEY TRADE."

Part two: why Detroit is desperate.

Now, Fowler's logic on the Lions side is airtight. Detroit is 2-2, and its pass defense is dead last in the NFL, allowing about 304 passing yards per game. They've given up 30 or more points in three of their four games. Dan Campbell said it himself: "It's not good enough right now."

And the injuries in that secondary are brutal. Avonte Maddox, who started at safety, needed season-ending foot surgery. Brian Branch has been working back from an Achilles injury, and Detroit just opened his practice window. Kerby Joseph has been out as well and is expected back later. Rock Ya-Sin left the Carolina game with a hamstring injury. So Detroit has a contending roster, an offense that can score with anybody, and a secondary held together with tape. Sound familiar? It's basically Pittsburgh's problem, just in a different conference.

And here's a connection most people missed. The Lions were in the mix for Joey Porter Jr. before Pittsburgh sent him to Dallas. Dallas made the only serious offer, so Porter went there. That tells you Detroit has been actively shopping for a cornerback for weeks. Now they're circling back to Pittsburgh's secondary again, just for a different player.

Detroit also has the money. The Lions have roughly 34.5 million dollars in cap space, the second most in the league. Taking on a contract like Ramsey's wouldn't be a problem for them. And they've got a bye coming up after their next game against Arizona, which means a new player would get extra time to learn the defense. From Detroit's point of view, it all lines up.

But notice something about Fowler's other suggestion, because it matters for Pittsburgh's leverage. Kristian Fulton has played in only three games for Kansas City this season, and five million dollars of his 9.75 million dollar base salary is guaranteed. That's why Fowler floated the idea of the Chiefs and Lions splitting the money. In other words, Detroit has a cheaper, lower-profile option on the table. If the Lions can patch the hole with Fulton for a late pick and some shared salary, they're not going to overpay for a 32-year-old with a broken wrist. That cuts both ways. It means Pittsburgh shouldn't expect a big offer, and it also means Pittsburgh has no reason to accept a small one.

Part three: who Jalen Ramsey is right now.

Let's talk about the player, because his résumé is not in question. Ramsey is a three-time first-team All-Pro, and he was voted to the Pro Bowl Games again last season in his first year in Pittsburgh, alongside T.J. Watt and special teamer Ben Skowronek. That's one of the best cornerbacks of his generation.

But here's the 2026 reality, and it's important. Ramsey turns 32 later this month. He opened training camp on the physically unable to perform list with a knee injury from minicamp. He worked his way back and has started all four games. And right now, he's playing with a broken wrist in a cast. In Cleveland, he missed only one snap, which tells you everything about his toughness. But it also tells you why he didn't look like himself. He had one tackle and got lost in space on the final drive. On Wednesday, he didn't practice, although the expectation is that he plays through it again on Sunday.

And here's the detail that's going to matter for a trade. Ramsey isn't playing outside corner for Pittsburgh. The Steelers have used him in the slot and at free safety, moving him around the formation. For Detroit, with two starting safeties banged up, that's not a negative. That's exactly the profile they'd want. A veteran who can line up at nickel on one snap and play deep safety on the next is a Swiss Army knife for a secondary missing multiple starters.

And if you want to understand where Ramsey's trade value is right now, look at the price tags on his career. In 2019, Jacksonville traded him to the Los Angeles Rams for two first-round picks and a fourth-rounder, and he won a Super Bowl ring with the Rams. In 2023, the Rams sent him to Miami for a third-round pick and a tight end. Then in the 2025 offseason, Miami moved him and tight end Jonnu Smith to Pittsburgh in a deal built around Minkah Fitzpatrick. Two first-round picks, then a third, then a player swap. That's the arc of an aging star's market. It's not an insult to Ramsey. It's just how the league values corners in their thirties. And it tells you why nobody should expect Detroit to offer a king's ransom now.

Part four: the contract, and this is where it gets interesting.

Ramsey is on a four-year deal worth about 84.7 million dollars that runs through 2028, with void years after that. He counts about 19.5 million dollars against Pittsburgh's cap this season. And here's the key: according to contract breakdowns from earlier this year, essentially all of his guaranteed money is tied to 2026. After this season, his salary is not protected the same way, and his cap number climbs significantly in the later years of the deal. Some reports put it in the 30-million range down the line.

So what does that mean in plain English? It means Ramsey's contract is very team-friendly for whoever holds it after this season. If Pittsburgh keeps him, they can decide next offseason whether to keep him, restructure him, or move on, with limited long-term pain. And it means a team like Detroit could trade for him, use him for a playoff run, and have the same flexibility next spring. That's actually what makes him tradeable. It's also exactly why Pittsburgh doesn't have to rush into anything.

Part five: the case for Pittsburgh trading him.

I want to give you both sides, because there is a real argument here. Some Steelers writers have already floated it. The case goes like this. Ramsey is about to turn 32, he's playing hurt, and his contract gets more expensive in the years ahead. Pittsburgh has already shown this fall that it's willing to move defensive backs when the long-term math doesn't work. Some speculation has put his trade value around a mid-round pick, maybe a fourth-rounder. And if the Steelers stumble over the next month and the season starts to slip, getting something for a veteran before his value drops further is a defensible business decision.

There's also a cap argument. If Pittsburgh was already likely to move on from Ramsey in the 2027 offseason, trading him now gets them a pick instead of nothing. That's the same logic that sent Porter to Dallas. Get value while you still can.

And moving his salary would have one more benefit that fans don't always think about. Under NFL rules, unused cap space rolls over into the next season. So whatever portion of Ramsey's 2026 money Detroit absorbed would turn into extra room for Pittsburgh in 2027, an offseason where this team will have big decisions to make with Rodgers retiring and a quarterback question looming. That's a real, long-term reason a front office might listen. It's just not a strong enough reason to do it in October of a season you're still trying to win.

Part six: the case against, and this is where I land.

Now here's my honest take, and this is opinion, not a report. Trading Jalen Ramsey right now would be a disaster for the 2026 Steelers, and I don't think it's close.

Look at the cornerback room. Porter is gone. Jamel Dean has an ankle injury and didn't practice Wednesday. Sean Murphy-Bunting just signed off a practice squad on Monday after missing all of last season. Daylen Everette is a rookie about to make his first real starts. Asante Samuel Jr. is the only corner listed as a starter on the depth chart. In that situation, Ramsey isn't a luxury. He's the most experienced, most versatile defensive back you have, and he's the one who can move to wherever the biggest leak is on any given Sunday.

Trading him now would be like knocking out the last load-bearing wall in a house that already has two walls missing. It doesn't save you money that matters this season, and it guarantees the secondary gets worse in a stretch where you're playing the Colts, the Buccaneers, the Saints in Paris, and the Browns again before the bye.

And think about who would actually replace him. If Ramsey left, the slot would go to someone like Brandin Echols or Everette, a rookie who's already being asked to play outside. The deep safety snaps he's been taking would fall to Robert Spears-Jennings, Jaquan Brisker, or Rayshawn Jenkins, who was benched in Cleveland after a 25.6 grade from Pro Football Focus. Every single one of those options is either younger, less experienced, or coming off a rough game. You'd be asking three different players to absorb one veteran's job, in the middle of a season, in a secondary that has already had to reshuffle twice in eight days.

And there's a message in it, too. Pittsburgh brought back a 42-year-old Aaron Rodgers for one last ride. It extended T.J. Watt. It extended Nick Herbig. You don't do all of that and then trade a three-time All-Pro in October while you're one game out of first place. Trading Porter could be explained as a contract philosophy decision. Trading Ramsey at 2-2 would be read, by the locker room and by the fan base, as waving the white flag on the season. Even one Steelers writer who discussed the idea admitted it would signal that the team had given up on 2026.

And finally, the return. If the realistic price is a fourth-round pick for a player who just made the Pro Bowl, Pittsburgh shouldn't be interested. That's not value. That's a fire sale.

So where does that leave the Lions' phone call? Here's my read. If Detroit calls, Omar Khan should listen, because that's his job. But unless the Lions are offering a real Day 2 pick, or the Steelers are 3-5 or worse at the bye, Ramsey stays. And frankly, with how thin this secondary is, I'd be surprised if Pittsburgh even entertained it before November.

And if Detroit does call, here's what I'd want to hear, and again, this is my opinion. Not a fourth-round pick. Not a pick two years from now. Either a real Day 2 pick in next year's draft, or a younger player who helps right now, ideally a corner or safety on a rookie contract. Swapping a 32-year-old for a 24-year-old who can start in Pittsburgh for the next four years is a deal that helps both teams. Swapping him for a late pick helps only Detroit. And remember, the Lions are the ones with the urgent problem. They're the team giving up 304 passing yards a game. Pittsburgh is allowed to set the price.

Now, while we're on trades, let's flip the script one more time, because the conversation this week has been all about the Steelers selling defensive backs, when the real need is to add one. Pittsburgh still has an open spot on its 53-man roster after signing Murphy-Bunting. Trevon Diggs is still sitting on Seattle's practice squad, with five years of history with McCarthy and three with secondary coach Joe Whitt Jr. Signing him would cost nothing but a roster spot. My opinion: if anyone in this conversation should be changing teams, it's a cornerback coming into Pittsburgh, not one going out.

Part seven: the injury report heading into the Colts game.

Let's finish with what actually matters this week, because the Ramsey rumor is noise compared to Sunday. Wednesday's practice report had three Steelers sitting out: Cameron Heyward, getting a veteran rest day, Jamel Dean with his ankle, and Ramsey with his wrist. The Dean situation is the one to watch. He didn't practice, but he also wasn't wearing a boot. If he can't go, it's Samuel and Everette on the outside, with Murphy-Bunting as the backup.

There's good news on offense. Rico Dowdle was limited in practice, which is real progress. He's missed the last two games after dislocating his toe twice against New England. Mike McCarthy said he's still in the rehab group but will ramp up this week. If Dowdle can go, Pittsburgh finally has the thunder to go with Jaylen Warren's lightning, and that could be a big deal after Warren only got 20 touches in Cleveland. If he can't, Lew Nichols could be elevated from the practice squad.

Michael Pittman Jr. was also limited with a foot issue. He missed Week 2 with it, and he was seen after practice riding a scooter through the locker room with his foot propped up. That's worth watching, especially since Sunday is his first game against the Colts, his old team.

And DeShon Elliott is still on injured reserve. His 21-day practice window apparently wasn't opened Wednesday, and McCarthy didn't have an update. So the safety help everyone's waiting for may not arrive this week.

Thursday and Friday's reports will tell us a lot more, especially on Dean and Dowdle. But the big picture is simple. This team is banged up in the secondary, it's getting healthier on offense, and it has a home game against Indianapolis on Sunday, on T.J. Watt's birthday, that could swing the entire tone of the deadline conversation.

So here's the bottom line on Ramsey. Fowler asked a smart question from Detroit's point of view. The Lions should call. But from Pittsburgh's point of view, the answer right now should be no. Ramsey is hurt, he's 31, and his contract has an expiration date built into it. All of that is true. But he's also the glue in a secondary that's already lost two starters in a week. You don't remove the glue when the thing is already falling apart.

And honestly, Ramsey has the best possible answer available to him this Sunday. Play through the wrist again, line up all over the formation against the Colts, make a couple of plays on the ball, and help this defense get off the field on third down. Nothing shuts down a trade rumor faster than a veteran reminding everybody why he's the most important piece in the room. He's done it his whole career. I wouldn't bet against him doing it again.

If the Steelers are 3-5 at the bye, we'll revisit it. If they're 5-3, nobody will be talking about trading Jalen Ramsey. They'll be talking about who Pittsburgh is adding for Rodgers' last run.
