Steelers Nation, the biggest wide receiver trade of the season is happening right now, and Pittsburgh has every reason to be in the middle of it. On Saturday, ESPN’s Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport reported that the Arizona Cardinals are finalizing a deal to trade Marvin Harrison Jr., the fourth overall pick in the 2024 draft. He’s been ruled inactive for Arizona’s game today. The destination hasn’t been announced yet, and reports say both teams want to wait until Sunday’s games are over. And on the same day, the Steelers put Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve. So today I want to answer three questions. Is Pittsburgh in this race? Should it be? And if not Harrison, what’s Omar Khan’s best move at receiver? This is James with Second Floor, and as I record this, the deal isn’t official, so let’s get into what we know.

First, I have to clean up one video going around this week, because it’s one of the most confused things I’ve ever seen. It talked about Marvin Harrison Jr. like he’s already a Steeler, a fan favorite in Pittsburgh, the “cornerstone” of this franchise. He’s not. He’s never played a snap for the Steelers. He’s been with Arizona since the Cardinals drafted him. It also called Minkah Fitzpatrick and Najee Harris core pieces of this team. Fitzpatrick was traded to Miami before last season, and Najee Harris isn’t here either. And it talked about finding “Ben Roethlisberger’s successor.” Steelers Nation, the quarterback is Aaron Rodgers, and he’s 42. If you saw that video, forget it. Here’s what’s actually happening.

Part one: what’s real right now.

Let’s start with the reporting, piece by piece, because there’s a lot of it.

On Saturday, Schefter and Rapoport reported that Arizona is finalizing a trade for Harrison. Rapoport added that nothing was final yet, “But he’s expected to be traded.” The Cardinals decided to make him inactive for their game against Detroit today. Jordan Schultz reported that both sides prefer to keep the destination quiet until Week 5’s games are over.

So where is he going? The name that keeps coming up is Houston. Schefter reported that the Texans have been involved in talks, although a team source said they “can’t confirm or deny” interest. Mike Florio at Pro Football Talk reported that Houston is the likely destination, which would reunite Harrison with C.J. Stroud, his former Ohio State teammate. Florio also reported that the Texans offered a 2028 second-round pick, while Arizona has pushed for a 2027 second-rounder. The Colts did their homework on him too, but multiple reports said Indianapolis won’t be the landing spot.

And here’s the price, according to NFL Network’s Tom Pelissero: league sources say Arizona would want “a second-round pick and more” to deal him. Pelissero also reported that the Cardinals have received several calls, and that general manager Monti Ossenfort wasn’t eager to move Harrison because of how much the team invested in him.

Now, where are the Steelers in all this? Let me be honest with you. No report has the Steelers in active talks for Harrison. Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox named Pittsburgh one of the two best landing spots for him back on October 2nd. Other Steelers coverage this week said Pittsburgh probably isn’t the team, because giving up a second-round pick and more would be an aggressive move for Omar Khan. So, as I record this, the smart money says Houston. But until the deal is announced, nothing is done. And there’s even a report that a late offer from some other team could still change things.

And here’s why this matters for Pittsburgh even if the Steelers never pick up the phone. Houston is an AFC team. If the Texans land Harrison and pair him with Stroud, his old college teammate, that’s a contender in our conference adding a young, talented receiver while the Steelers stand still. In the AFC playoff race, seeding can come down to one game. You never want to watch a rival get stronger at the exact position where you just lost a starter to injured reserve.

Part two: why Harrison is available at all.

This is the part that surprises people. Harrison was the fourth overall pick in 2024, the son of a Hall of Famer, and one of the most hyped receiver prospects in years. So how does a player like that end up on the trade block in Year 3?

The production hasn’t matched the hype. As a rookie in 2024, he caught 62 passes for 885 yards and eight touchdowns. Solid, not spectacular. Through his first two seasons combined, he had 1,566 receiving yards and 12 touchdowns in 32 games. And this year, under new head coach Mike LaFleur, he’s barely been used. Through four games, he has just five catches for 85 yards and a touchdown on 13 targets. He’s behind Kendrick Bourne in the pecking order. One AFC executive called him “a third receiver with upside.” That’s a long way from the fourth overall pick.

But there’s another side to it. His targets this season have traveled an average of 17.7 yards downfield, according to Pro Football Focus. He’s being used as a deep shot, not as a featured weapon. And there’s an old saying in this league: sometimes the player isn’t the problem, the situation is.

Think about how rare this is. Top-five picks almost never get traded in their third season. Teams usually give them every chance, because trading them means admitting the pick didn’t work out. For Arizona to move him now, with a new head coach and Harrison sitting behind Kendrick Bourne, tells you the Cardinals have decided he doesn’t fit what they’re building. That’s exactly the kind of situation where a smart buyer can find value, because a change of scenery has turned around plenty of careers in this league. The question is never whether the talent is real. The question is whether you’re the team that can unlock it, and at what price.

Part three: the case for Pittsburgh.

So let me make the case for the Steelers, and this is my opinion, not a report.

Reason one: the need just got real. On Saturday, Pittsburgh officially placed Pittman on injured reserve. That means at least four games without him. The receiver room is now DK Metcalf, Roman Wilson, rookie Germie Bernard, Ben Skowronek, and practice squad receiver Brandon Smith, who was elevated for today’s game. Metcalf is carrying a huge share of the load, and Wilson has stepped up. But there’s no second big-time outside threat on this roster right now.

Reason two: Pittsburgh has the exact currency. When the Steelers traded Joey Porter Jr. to Dallas, they got a 2028 second-round pick back. And look at what Houston reportedly offered: a 2028 second-rounder. That’s the same kind of pick sitting in Khan’s pocket right now, and it isn’t even one of Pittsburgh’s own picks. Athlon’s Neal Coolong made exactly this point this week: the Steelers could cover the price without touching their own selections.

Reason three: the contract. Harrison is still on his rookie deal through 2027, with a fifth-year option after that. Pelissero reported that about 6.3 million dollars in guaranteed money remains on the deal. For a former top-five pick at 24 years old, that’s not much of a financial risk. Khan wouldn’t just be adding a receiver for Rodgers’ final run. He’d be adding a receiver for whoever comes after Rodgers.

Reason four: the vertical element. With Metcalf on one side and Harrison stretching the field on the other, defenses couldn’t roll every coverage toward Metcalf anymore. That opens up Wilson underneath, Pat Freiermuth over the middle, and the run game for Jaylen Warren. One more deep threat changes the math for everybody.

Reason five: the bye-week timing. Pittsburgh’s bye comes in Week 9, and the trade deadline is the Tuesday right after it, November 10th. But a receiver doesn’t learn an offense in a week. If Khan wants a new receiver to actually matter in December and January, the earlier he makes the move, the better. A player who arrives in mid-October gets three or four games to build timing with Rodgers, then the bye to catch up on the playbook. A player who arrives on November 10th starts from zero with half the season gone. That’s why the Harrison situation matters even if he ends up in Houston: it tells you the receiver market is moving right now, not at the deadline.

Part four: the case against, and it’s a strong one.

Now, the other side, because you always get both from me.

First, the production. “A third receiver with upside” is not what you pay a second-round pick and more for in the middle of a season with a 42-year-old quarterback. Pittsburgh’s window is right now. Spending premium draft capital on a project is a risky use of a short window.

Second, the fit. One Steelers-focused column made a really interesting point: Pittsburgh’s offense has been facing man coverage on more than half of its snaps, and over his first two seasons, Harrison was far more productive against zone than against man coverage. That’s a real concern. If defenses are daring the Steelers’ receivers to beat them one-on-one, you want a receiver who wins one-on-one. That column concluded, quote, “the Steelers should look elsewhere for help on offense, regardless of what the price may be in a trade.”

Third, the timing. If the trade is basically done and Houston is the destination, Pittsburgh may simply be too late. And Khan has never been the kind of GM who jumps into a bidding war just because a big name is available.

So what would a realistic Steelers offer have looked like? Again, this is my opinion, not a report. I’d start with that Dallas 2028 second-round pick, and I’d stop there. No first-round pick. No Roman Wilson, because you don’t fix a receiver shortage by trading a receiver. No Germie Bernard. If Arizona really wants “a second-round pick and more,” the “more” for me would be a late-round pick swap, not a starter. And here’s the thing that makes the money easy: unused cap space rolls over to next season in this league, so taking on Harrison’s rookie salary wouldn’t hurt Pittsburgh long term. The cost is the draft pick. That’s the whole decision.

And let me be clear about why I’d stop there. Khan has built this roster by being disciplined, and the moment you start chasing a player with your own premium picks, you’re paying for the name on the jersey, the fourth overall pick and the Hall of Fame father, more than the player who’s actually on the field this season. If the price goes past the Dallas pick, you let Houston have him and you move on to the next option.

Part five: Harrison or Brian Thomas Jr.?

And this is where it gets really interesting, because Harrison isn’t the only 2024 first-round receiver who’s been buried on a depth chart. We talked about this yesterday. Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville is in almost the exact same situation.

Let’s put them side by side. Both were first-round picks in 2024. Harrison went fourth overall to Arizona. Thomas went 23rd overall to Jacksonville. Both are 24 years old. Both are on rookie contracts that are cheap for a team like Pittsburgh. And both have been pushed down their team’s depth chart this season.

But here’s the difference. Thomas’s rookie year was the better one by far: 87 catches for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. Harrison’s was 62 catches for 885 yards and eight touchdowns. Thomas has already shown what he looks like as a true number one. Harrison hasn’t yet. Thomas has nine catches through four games this year as Jacksonville’s third receiver. Harrison has five.

The catch with Thomas? Jacksonville hasn’t been in a hurry to sell. Jaguars GM James Gladstone has called the trade rumors “fraudulent,” and The Athletic’s Jeff Howe reported that Jacksonville has turned down significant offers for him. Arizona, on the other hand, is clearly selling Harrison right now.

So if you’re Khan, here’s how I’d think about it, and again, opinion, not a report. If Harrison is gone to Houston, the Thomas conversation becomes even more important, because there’s one less young receiver on the market and Pittsburgh’s need hasn’t gone away. And if Harrison’s deal falls apart for some reason, Khan should at least make the call, with that Dallas 2028 second-rounder as the starting point and nothing more.

And there’s a third name in this conversation that some of you keep bringing up: Tyreek Hill. He’s a free agent, he’s 32, and he’s coming off knee ligament tears. His agent said he expected Hill to sign within days, and the Chiefs have been in contact. There’s still no report connecting him to Pittsburgh. Hill would cost no draft pick at all, but he’s a short-term rental with health questions. Harrison and Thomas are long-term pieces with a price tag. Those are two very different bets, and that’s exactly the kind of choice Khan has to make before the deadline.

Part six: what the Pittman roster move tells you.

Here’s a detail that tells you a lot about Khan’s thinking. When Pittman went to injured reserve on Saturday, it opened a spot on the 53-man roster. And Pittsburgh used it on defensive back Doneiko Slaughter, signing him back from the practice squad. They also elevated running back Lew Nichols and receiver Brandon Smith for today’s game.

So the open spot went to the secondary, not to an outside receiver, and not to Trevon Diggs either, for those of you who’ve been asking. That tells you two things. One, the front office still sees the secondary as the most urgent problem, with Jamel Dean out today and DeShon Elliott still on injured reserve. And two, if Khan does make a receiver move, it’s more likely to be a trade before the November 10th deadline than a quick free-agent signing this weekend.

And Lew Nichols being elevated is worth watching too. It could be insurance in case Rico Dowdle, who’s listed as questionable, isn’t ready. We’ll find out at inactives.

Part seven: today’s game.

Because let’s not forget, there’s a football game today. The Colts come to Acrisure Stadium at 1 p.m. Eastern, both teams at 2-2, and Indianapolis hasn’t won in Pittsburgh since November of 2008.

With Pittman on IR, the passing game runs through Metcalf and Wilson. I want to see Bernard get real, designed targets. And I want to see the Steelers lean on Jaylen Warren, attack the edges instead of running into DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart, and get the ball out quickly with the Colts missing pass rusher Arden Key.

And don’t sleep on this Colts team. They’ve won two straight, including a 30-13 win over Washington last week where Taylor ran 20 times for 95 yards and two touchdowns. Jones completed 19 of 34 passes for 143 yards in that game, so the Colts didn’t need him to do much. That’s their formula: run the ball, play complementary football, and let Jones avoid mistakes. Keenan Allen is questionable, along with Charvarius Ward on defense, and tight end Tyler Warren is someone the linebackers have to account for on every snap.

On defense, it’s Jonathan Taylor and Daniel Jones. Stop Taylor on early downs, and make Jones beat you. Jones has seven turnovers through four games. Pittsburgh’s defense lives on takeaways. With Ramsey expected to play through his wrist injury and Dean out, the secondary needs to be sharp, but the pass rush can make their job a lot easier.

And here’s the twist: Harrison’s trade could be announced right after today’s games. So you might be watching the Steelers try to beat the Colts while finding out where the biggest receiver trade of the season landed. If Pittsburgh wins and the receivers struggle, the pressure on Khan to make a move before November 10th gets even louder. If Pittsburgh wins and Wilson and Bernard step up, maybe the front office feels it can be patient.

One more thing to keep in mind today. If the Steelers’ receivers struggle against a Colts defense that’s missing pieces, you’re going to see a lot of noise online about Harrison, Thomas, Tyreek Hill, everybody. Don’t overreact to one Sunday. Watch the trend over the next four weeks. If Pittsburgh is consistently struggling to get anything from a second outside receiver, that’s a roster problem, and that’s when a deadline move becomes a must. If Wilson and Bernard keep developing, Khan can afford to be patient and keep his picks.

So let me bring it home. Marvin Harrison Jr. is not a Steeler, no matter what some videos tell you. But he is being traded right now, and Pittsburgh has the need, the cap room on a rookie contract, and the exact draft pick other teams are offering. The reports point to Houston, and I’d be surprised if Khan jumped in at this stage. But the bigger picture is clear. With Pittman on injured reserve, the Steelers need another outside receiver. Whether it’s Harrison, Brian Thomas Jr., or someone else, I expect Omar Khan to be on the phone before November 10th.
