Steelers Nation, the trade deadline is November 10th, and the rumor mill is already spinning so fast it's starting to fly off the rails. This week I've seen videos telling you the Steelers could sell off Patrick Queen, DK Metcalf, Asante Samuel Jr., Jaquan Brisker, Brock Hoffman, a rookie offensive lineman, and, I'm not making this up, that Pittsburgh should consider trading for Aaron Rodgers. The same Aaron Rodgers who is already the Steelers' starting quarterback. So today, we're doing something I think this fan base needs right now. We're putting every name on the so-called trade block through a truth test. Contract, role, cap math, and whether a deal actually makes sense. At the end of each one, I'll give you my verdict: real possibility, long shot, or not happening. This is James with Second Floor, and let's separate the noise from the news.

First, let's set the table, because none of this makes sense without context. The Steelers are 2-2. Cleveland and Baltimore are 3-1. Cincinnati is 2-2, and Pittsburgh already beat the Bengals head to head. So this is a team one game out of first place in early October, with a home game against the Colts on Sunday. That is not a seller's profile. Not yet. Teams don't start a fire sale at 2-2 with a 42-year-old future Hall of Fame quarterback on his final ride. The selling conversation only becomes real if this team drops a few more games over the next month.

And look at what's coming. Colts at home on October 11th. Then at Tampa Bay. Then the Saints in Paris on October 25th, a Steelers home game played overseas. Then the Browns at Acrisure on November 1st. Then the bye. And the deadline lands the Tuesday after that bye, November 10th. So by the time Omar Khan has to decide, Pittsburgh will have played eight games. If they're 5-3 or 4-4, they're buyers or they stand pat. If they're 2-6 or 3-5, the conversation changes. Everything I'm about to say lives inside that window.

Okay. Let's start the truth test.

Name number one: Aaron Rodgers.

I have to start here because it's the wildest one. One video I saw floated the idea of Pittsburgh trading for Aaron Rodgers, calling him a "long shot" because of his contract. Steelers Nation, Rodgers is already here. He's in his second season in Pittsburgh. He's the starting quarterback. He's 42 years old, it's his 22nd NFL season, and he has said publicly that this is his final ride. Pittsburgh is not trading for him, and frankly, Pittsburgh is not trading him either. Nobody is moving a retiring 42-year-old quarterback at the deadline for a meaningful return, and the Steelers didn't bring him back to flip him in November.

Verdict: not happening. In either direction.

Name number two: DK Metcalf.

This is the one that gets the most clicks, so let's be careful with it. The claim is that Metcalf carries a hefty contract and has struggled early, so Pittsburgh might move him. Let's look at the facts. Pittsburgh traded for Metcalf in March of 2025 and gave him a four-year, 132 million dollar extension. In 2025, he had 850 receiving yards and six touchdowns. This August, the Steelers restructured his deal, converting base salary into signing bonus and creating nearly 18 million dollars in cap space for this season.

And that restructure is the key detail everyone skips. When you convert salary into bonus, you spread that bonus money over future years. If you trade the player, all of that remaining bonus money accelerates onto your cap as dead money. So the Steelers literally just made Metcalf more expensive to trade, not less. Teams don't restructure a player in August that they're planning to dump in November.

As for "struggling"? In Cleveland he had five catches for 115 yards and made big plays on both late touchdown drives. Coming into that game, he was running a route on every single pass play, with a 22 percent target share. Is the passing game perfect? No. But Metcalf isn't the problem. If anything, the problem was the night Rodgers missed him running wide open deep.

Verdict: not happening.

Name number three: Patrick Queen.

Now we're getting into a real conversation. This one actually has some substance, so let's give it the respect it deserves.

Queen is in the final year of the three-year, 41 million dollar contract he signed with Pittsburgh in 2024. He counts about 17.2 million dollars against this year's cap. And there was real trade interest this offseason. Multiple teams called, including the Dallas Cowboys. But the Steelers let what was essentially a soft deadline pass in March, when they paid his 2.5 million dollar roster bonus. Back in May, Queen called the whole trade rumor cycle a "charade." He said, "Some of it was true, some it was just extended truth." And on an extension, he said, "If they do wanna extend, cool. If not, cool. The better I play, the better we both can be."

On the field, it's been uneven. He had 126 tackles last season, second on the team behind Payton Wilson. But in Cleveland, he made five tackles and missed too many, and he struggled against the run and in space. That's not a great look for a linebacker who's playing for his next contract.

So here's the math. If Pittsburgh traded Queen now, the savings are around 10.8 million dollars with about 6.4 million in dead money. Not nothing. And he's an expiring contract, which is exactly the kind of player sellers move at the deadline. But here's the problem: Derrick Harmon just went on injured reserve, the defensive line got reshuffled, and the run defense already has issues. Subtracting a starting linebacker right now makes the Harmon hole worse, not better.

And there's one more wrinkle with Queen that rarely gets mentioned. When a team lets a good veteran walk in free agency, it can end up with a compensatory draft pick the following year, depending on how the league's formula shakes out. So if Pittsburgh keeps Queen, lets the season play out, and he signs a big deal elsewhere next spring, the Steelers might get a pick back anyway, without weakening the defense this year. That means any deadline offer has to beat that alternative. A late-round pick probably doesn't. A meaningful Day 2 pick might.

Verdict: real possibility, but only if the Steelers are clearly losing by early November. At 2-2, he stays. At 3-5, he's the first name I'd expect Khan to shop.

Name number four: Asante Samuel Jr.

The claim here is that Samuel has regressed, his contract is expiring, and Pittsburgh should get something for him before he walks. Some of that's true. His contract does expire. The Steelers re-signed him in March on a one-year, four million dollar deal. He's 26 years old, and he came back from spinal fusion surgery, played six games last season with an interception, and earned that new deal.

But here's what the trade-him crowd is missing. Right now, Asante Samuel Jr. is the only cornerback listed as a starter on Pittsburgh's depth chart for the Colts game. Joey Porter Jr. is in Dallas. Jamel Dean has an ankle injury and is expected to miss multiple weeks. Sean Murphy-Bunting just walked in the door from Tampa's practice squad. Daylen Everette is a rookie. You don't trade your last healthy veteran starting corner in the middle of a cornerback crisis to save a few million dollars on a one-year deal. And realistically, a modest one-year contract for a corner who's been "serviceable, not spectacular" isn't bringing back much more than a late-round pick.

Verdict: not happening. Not with this depth chart.

Name number five: Jaquan Brisker.

One video I watched called Brisker a "recent draft pick" and talked about how hard it would be to trade "homegrown talent." So let's fix that. Brisker wasn't drafted by Pittsburgh. He was a second-round pick by the Chicago Bears in 2022, out of Penn State, and spent four seasons in Chicago. The Steelers signed him this offseason on a one-year, 5.5 million dollar deal. Now, he is a hometown guy in a different sense. He's from Wilkinsburg and went to Gateway High School, right here in the Pittsburgh area. So "homegrown" fits geographically. It just doesn't fit the draft history.

On the field, it's been mixed. He made seven tackles in Cleveland, but Steelers Depot's grades said he "misses far too many tackles" and "plays out of control at times." That's the concern with Brisker. The effort and physicality are there. The finishing isn't always.

Could he be moved? He's on a one-year deal, so technically he's an expiring asset. But the trade value of a safety on a 5.5 million dollar, one-year contract who's been inconsistent is minimal. And with the secondary already thin, Pittsburgh needs bodies back there more than it needs a seventh-round pick.

Verdict: long shot. Possible only in a full-blown sell-off, and even then the return would be tiny.

Name number six: Brock Hoffman.

This one surprised me. Hoffman is a versatile interior lineman who can play center or guard. He signed with Pittsburgh in March on a one-year, 2.5 million dollar deal, and the connection is obvious: he played three seasons in Dallas under Mike McCarthy. In 2025, he played six games at center and one at left guard for the Cowboys.

So think about why he's here. McCarthy brought him in because he knows him and trusts him as interior depth. He's on a cheap, one-year deal. And Pittsburgh's offensive line is already dealing with injuries. Starter Spencer Anderson has been playing through a broken bone in his lower leg since Week 2. You don't trade the versatile interior lineman your head coach specifically wanted while your line is held together with tape. And even if you did, a one-year, 2.5 million dollar interior lineman doesn't bring back anything meaningful.

Verdict: not happening.

Name number seven: Gennings Dunker.

This one is a strange inclusion. Dunker is a rookie. Pittsburgh took him in the third round of this year's draft, 96th overall, out of Iowa. Analyst Brian Baldinger called him the best third-round pick in the 2026 draft. Offensive line coach James Campen praised him, saying, "He is an aggressive kid. Good football player. They're so well coached at Iowa."

But here's the reality. On September 26th, the Steelers placed Dunker on injured reserve with a sprained MCL. The estimated recovery was four to six weeks, with no surgery needed. He'd also been inactive for the first two games, and he had struggled with pass protection in training camp after moving from tackle to guard.

So the idea of trading him is backwards. Teams don't trade a rookie third-round pick on injured reserve in his first season. His value to Pittsburgh is in the future. His value to another team right now is close to zero, because they can't even see him play. When he comes back, he's a developmental guard with a real run-blocking upside. That's a guy you keep.

Verdict: not happening.

Name number eight: Alex Highsmith.

And here's the name that, funny enough, those videos didn't lead with, even though he's the one with actual reporting behind him. The Athletic's Jonathan Jones put Highsmith on his deadline watch list, alongside a handful of names from around the league. That's not a report of an imminent deal. But it's the only Steelers player in this conversation who has been named by a national insider.

We covered Highsmith in depth on this channel already, so I'll keep it short. He's a productive starting edge rusher on a contract that runs through 2027, with a cap hit north of 20 million dollars. Pittsburgh also pays T.J. Watt and Nick Herbig like starters. Some projections put his value around a Day 2 pick. In Cleveland, he had a quiet night, one tackle, one tackle for loss, and a missed sack in the red zone. But with Harmon on injured reserve, trading a pass rusher right now would weaken the exact part of the defense Pittsburgh is built around.

Verdict: real possibility only if the season slides. At 2-2, you keep him. That said, of all the names on this list, he's the one most likely to get serious phone calls.

So let's put the scoreboard together. Rodgers: not happening. Metcalf: not happening. Samuel: not happening. Hoffman: not happening. Dunker: not happening. Brisker: long shot. Queen: real possibility if the season slips. Highsmith: real possibility if the season slips, and he's the only one with actual reporting behind his name.

That's two real names out of eight. And notice the pattern. The two real candidates are veterans on significant contracts who have value to contenders. Everyone else on that list is either cheap, injured, essential depth, or the starting quarterback. That's how you spot a lazy trade rumor. If the player's contract wouldn't bring anything back, or the team would be crippled without him, it's not a trade block. It's a filler list.

So here's a quick guide for you, so you can run your own truth test the next time one of these lists pops up in your feed. Rule one: check the contract. If the player is on a cheap one-year deal, he won't bring back much, so there's little reason to move him. If he was just restructured or extended, the team is almost certainly planning to keep him. Rule two: check the depth chart. If trading the player leaves a hole the team can't fill, especially at a position already hit by injuries, it's not happening. Rule three: check the source. Is there a named reporter, like Jonathan Jones, actually saying the player is being discussed? Or is it just a video narrator saying "don't be surprised if"? If you run every name through those three questions, you'll filter out most of the trade noise you see between now and November 10th.

Now, let me flip the script, because I think the more realistic conversation for the Steelers isn't about selling at all. It's about buying, and we've been tracking that all week.

Pittsburgh signed Sean Murphy-Bunting off Tampa's practice squad on Monday and still has one open spot on the 53-man roster. Trevon Diggs is sitting on Seattle's practice squad, and he played five seasons under McCarthy in Dallas and three under secondary coach Joe Whitt Jr. Signing him wouldn't cost a single draft pick. Steelers insider Mark Kaboly has called him an option, and he's right. And on the trade market, names like Houston's Kamari Lassiter have been floated, although, as I said yesterday, a young corner like that is going to cost much more than people think and will come with the same contract questions that sent Joey Porter Jr. to Dallas. My opinion, and this is opinion, not a report: if Pittsburgh is 4-4 or better at the bye, the Steelers should be adding a corner, not subtracting starters.

And there's a bigger philosophical point here. In the last few months, Pittsburgh has traded Broderick Jones and traded Joey Porter Jr. It extended Watt, extended Herbig, signed Jamel Dean, and kept Rodgers for one more year. That's a franchise trying to win now while protecting its long-term cap. You can argue with that approach. But it's not a franchise that's going to blow it up at 2-2 because a few YouTube videos told it to.

So what would actually turn Pittsburgh into sellers? Here's my honest checklist. One: losing to the Colts at home and to Tampa on the road, falling to 2-4. Two: continued injuries in the secondary, with Dean's ankle lingering and no answer at corner. Three: the offense still sputtering, with Rodgers taking five sacks a game and the run game underused. If all three happen, then yes, the Queen and Highsmith conversations become very real by November. And even then, I'd expect Khan to start with expiring veterans before he touches anyone else.

But if Pittsburgh beats the Colts, competes in Tampa, and gets Dean, DeShon Elliott and Rico Dowdle back healthy, this whole trade-block conversation flips. Instead of "who are we selling," it becomes "who do we add for Rodgers' last run."

And honestly, that's what this week is really about. On Sunday, against Indianapolis, this team gets to decide which conversation we're having. Win, and everybody stops writing sell-off lists. Lose, and the phones in the front office start ringing a lot more.

And the storylines on Sunday are perfect for a statement game. Michael Pittman Jr. gets his first shot at the Colts, the team he left this offseason. T.J. Watt plays at home on his birthday. And a secondary that's been patched together all week gets its first real test with Murphy-Bunting in the building. A clean, convincing win would do more to kill the sell-off chatter than anything Omar Khan could ever say at a podium. That's how it works in this league. The scoreboard writes the trade rumors.
