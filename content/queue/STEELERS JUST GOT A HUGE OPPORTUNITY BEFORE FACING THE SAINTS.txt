Steelers Nation, it’s game day in Paris, and the Steelers made their final moves before kickoff against the Saints. The headline is DeShon Elliott. After missing the first six games, the veteran safety is back on the active roster. On top of that, two receivers were elevated from the practice squad, and there’s one roster detail in these moves that tells you a lot about what Omar Khan could do next. So today, we’re breaking down every move, what each player brings, why this is a huge opportunity for the Steelers against a 2-4 Saints team, and what to watch when the first regular-season game in Paris kicks off. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The big one: DeShon Elliott is back.

Let’s start with the move everyone was waiting for. The Steelers activated DeShon Elliott from injured reserve. He was placed on injured reserve back on August 30, and he returned to practice on October 8, which opened a 21-day window for the team to make a decision. Pittsburgh had two choices: activate him, or leave him on injured reserve for the rest of the season. The decision is made. Elliott is back.

And this is more than just another name on the depth chart. Elliott has started 69 of the 77 games he’s played in his career. He’s made more than 400 tackles, with 25 passes defensed, five interceptions, six forced fumbles, five fumble recoveries, and four and a half sacks. He set a career high with 108 combined tackles in 2024, his first season in Pittsburgh.

Those numbers describe a safety who’s been involved in everything: tackling in space, competing for the ball, forcing turnovers, and even getting after the quarterback. And for a defense that depends on communication, a familiar veteran voice in the back end can be just as valuable as the stats.

Why Elliott matters so much for this defense.

Think about what a safety has to do on every snap. Read the formation. Recognize what the offense is trying to do. Adjust after a shift or motion. Help in coverage, then come downhill against the run. One wrong read and a 10-yard completion becomes a 60-yard touchdown.

That’s where Elliott’s experience comes in. Early this season, a lot of the mistakes this defense made were communication mistakes, two players seeing the same formation and expecting different responsibilities. A veteran who has seen just about every look an offense can throw at him helps clean that up. Mike McCarthy summed it up this week: “It’s been a long road back, but he’s a big part of what we’re about in Pittsburgh.”

The long road back.

It’s worth remembering how long this road has been. Elliott’s 2025 season ended in a game against the Green Bay Packers, when he hyperextended his knee and tore his hamstring, an injury that required surgery. He was limited at the start of training camp this summer, worked his way up to full participation, and even started a preseason game. But the Steelers still placed him on injured reserve before the season, and he’s watched the first six games from the sideline.

While he was gone, the defense found its footing. The last two weeks have been the best stretch of the season, with the Steelers holding the Colts to 4-of-15 on third down and the Buccaneers to 6-of-19. Now Elliott gets to join a unit that’s already rolling, instead of being asked to rescue one that’s struggling. That’s the best possible situation for a player coming back from a long injury.

But keep expectations realistic.

Now, let’s be honest about something. Being activated doesn’t mean Elliott walks right back into his old workload. Last season, he played just five games before the knee and hamstring injuries ended his year, and he hasn’t played a regular-season snap since. The coaching staff will have to judge where he is physically and how much he can handle safely.

My expectation is a defined role, not 70 snaps. Jaquan Brisker has taken on a bigger role while Elliott was out, and rookie Robert Spears-Jennings has improved every week. Patrick Graham said there could be competition for snaps, and his philosophy was simple: “Let everything happen organically.” I’d expect a rotation on Sunday. A return to the roster is a big step forward, but the Steelers need Elliott healthy in December and January, not just in Paris.

The receiver elevations.

Now to the offense. The Steelers elevated two wide receivers from the practice squad for the Saints game: Brandon Johnson and Brandon Smith. These are temporary moves. After the game, both are expected to go back to the practice squad.

That’s important context, because the receiver room is thin. Ben Skowronek was ruled out with a calf injury. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are on injured reserve. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen as the active receivers, so Johnson and Smith give McCarthy the bodies he needs to run his offense and cover special teams.

Who is Brandon Johnson?

Let’s start with Johnson, because he’s actually got some production on his résumé. He signed with the Denver Broncos as an undrafted free agent out of UCF after the 2022 draft, and he spent time on both their practice squad and active roster. His best season came in 2023, when he played 13 games for Denver and caught 19 passes for 284 yards and four touchdowns.

For his career, he’s played in 23 games with three starts, catching 26 passes for 335 yards and five touchdowns. He also spent time on Tampa Bay’s practice squad before coming to Pittsburgh, and he’s been with the Steelers organization since 2024. He was elevated for games against the Jets and Giants that year, and he was elevated for last week’s game in Tampa too.

He’s also familiar with what this coaching staff wants, because he’s been through game weeks with the team before. That matters on a trip like this, when there’s no time to teach a newcomer the playbook. Five career touchdowns on 26 catches tells you something. When Johnson gets chances near the end zone, he’s made the most of them. In a game where the Steelers want to finish drives better than they did in Tampa, that’s not nothing.

Who is Brandon Smith?

Brandon Smith has taken a longer road. He played college football at Iowa, and his professional journey has included stops with the Patriots’ practice squad, the Cowboys, the Cardinals, and the DC Defenders in the UFL. He spent parts of 2024 and 2025 with the Jets, appearing in three games. Pittsburgh signed him to a reserve/future contract at the end of last season.

This season, Smith was elevated for the Week 5 game against the Colts and the Week 6 game in Tampa, and now again for the Saints. And that’s where the detail I mentioned at the top comes in.

The roster detail that matters.

Under NFL rules, a player can only be elevated from the practice squad three times in a season. Smith was elevated in Week 5, Week 6, and now Week 7. If my count is right, that’s his third elevation. That means if the Steelers want him active for another game this season, they’d have to sign him to the 53-man roster.

We’ve already seen that happen this year. Running back Lew Nichols had used up his elevations, and the Steelers signed him to the active roster before the Tampa game. So keep an eye on Smith after Paris. If the receiver injuries continue, he could be the next player to get that call.

And here’s the other piece. With Rayshawn Jenkins released and Sebastian Joseph-Day on injured reserve, the Steelers had two open spots on the 53-man roster heading into the weekend. Elliott’s activation fills one of them. Practice squad elevations don’t count against the 53. So unless there’s a move we haven’t seen yet, Khan still has an open spot to work with. That gives him flexibility heading into the Browns game and the trade deadline.

Why not Marquez Valdes-Scantling?

A lot of you are going to ask about Marquez Valdes-Scantling. The two-time Super Bowl champion rejoined the practice squad this week, and McCarthy said he “looked like he stepped right in, like he never left.” But he wasn’t one of the two receivers elevated for the Saints game.

That makes sense when you think about it. MVS arrived in the middle of a week that ended with an overseas flight, and the team’s Friday walkthrough at the Stade de France was canceled because of weather. Johnson and Smith have been in the building and in the game plan for weeks. My guess is that MVS’s bigger chance comes next week, after a full week of practice at home, against the Browns.

And when he does play, he’ll bring a long résumé. He was a fifth-round pick of the Packers in 2018, the 174th overall pick, and spent four seasons there with Aaron Rodgers. He then played two seasons in Kansas City, followed by stops in Buffalo, New Orleans, and San Francisco. For his career, he’s played in 119 games with 70 starts, catching 221 passes for 3,708 yards and 21 touchdowns.

A week of quiet, smart moves.

Step back and look at the whole week, and you can see a plan. On Tuesday, the Steelers signed MVS to the practice squad. On Wednesday, they released Rayshawn Jenkins, a 32-year-old safety who had been limited to special teams. On Thursday, Sebastian Joseph-Day went on injured reserve with a triceps injury. And on Saturday, Elliott was activated and Johnson and Smith were elevated.

None of those moves made national headlines. But each one had a purpose. Clear a spot for a returning starter. Protect the receiver room. Keep enough depth on the roster to survive a long trip. That’s how a front office keeps a 4-2 team on track when injuries pile up, and it’s the same approach Khan has taken all season, from claiming two cornerbacks off practice squads to bringing back a veteran who already knows the quarterback.

The opportunity in front of the Steelers.

Now let’s talk about the bigger picture, because this game is a real opportunity. The Steelers are 4-2 and riding a two-game winning streak. The Saints are 2-4. A win in Paris would put Pittsburgh at 5-2 and make it three straight wins, with the Browns coming to Acrisure Stadium next week.

And the injury situation is better than it looked a few days ago. T.J. Watt was added to the injury report on Thursday with a calf issue, but McCarthy said he didn’t expect it to affect Watt’s availability, and Watt had no game designation. Alex Highsmith, Jalen Ramsey, and Cam Heyward were all expected to play. The Steelers are getting healthier at exactly the right time. And remember, Ramsey is coming off an AFC Defensive Player of the Week performance, with 10 tackles, four tackles for loss, and two sacks while playing through a broken wrist. Having him, Watt, and now Elliott on the field together gives Graham more options than he’s had at any point this season.

The Saints have their own issues. Guard Cesar Ruiz and defensive lineman Nathan Shepherd were listed as questionable. Alvin Kamara was limited all week with a back issue, though he didn’t receive a game designation. Their first-round receiver, Jordyn Tyson, is on injured reserve with a hamstring injury.

Why the safeties will be tested.

There’s also a matchup reason Elliott’s return matters. The Saints have receivers who can hurt you if the back end breaks down, including Chris Olave. Quarterback Tyler Shough practiced fully this week despite a left hand injury. If the Steelers’ pass rush gets home, the coverage behind it has to be disciplined, because the one way an offense beats a pressure-heavy defense is with a big play over the top when someone loses their assignment. That’s exactly the kind of play a veteran safety is supposed to prevent.

What to watch on defense.

So what should you watch for on defense? First, how the safeties rotate. Does Elliott play in the base defense, in sub-packages, or both? How do Brisker and Spears-Jennings fit around him? Second, the interior line. With Harmon and Joseph-Day both on injured reserve, Heyward, Keeanu Benton, Yahya Black, and Gabriel Rubio have to hold up against the run on early downs.

And third, the pass rush. If the Steelers force the Saints into third-and-long, Watt, Herbig, and Highsmith can take over, like they did in Tampa, when the defense finished with five sacks and two interceptions and held the Buccaneers to 6-of-19 on third down.

What to watch on offense.

On offense, the first thing I’m watching is the start. In Tampa, the Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters. Rodgers said afterward that once they got Metcalf involved, “the offense just shifted,” and that they could get that going “a little earlier next time.” So does Metcalf get the ball early?

Second, the backfield. Rico Dowdle was questionable with a knee injury. If he’s limited, Jaylen Warren could carry the load, and after this week’s debate about the Warren and Dowdle split, that’ll be one of the biggest storylines of the game.

And third, the depth receivers. Do Johnson or Smith get involved on offense, or is their value mostly on special teams? Elevated players don’t come with a guaranteed number of snaps, but if one of them makes a big play, it could change how the Steelers handle the receiver spot heading into the deadline.

Don’t forget special teams.

Elevated receivers often earn their spot on special teams before they ever see a target, and that matters a lot this week. With Brandin Echols out with his hamstring, the Steelers lost a core special teamer who worked on the punt return unit. Johnson and Smith can help fill those coverage and return roles. And in a game played in an unfamiliar stadium, field position can swing everything.

Just look at last week. Chris Boswell missed a 48-yarder in Tampa, but he also hit from 52, from 58 to tie the game late in the fourth quarter, and from 20 to put the Steelers ahead. In close games, the third phase decides a lot. If the Steelers’ depth players can win the hidden yardage on punts and kickoffs, they make life easier for Rodgers and the defense.

What a win would mean.

And let’s be clear about the stakes. The Steelers, Browns, and Ravens all entered this week at 4-2, with Cleveland holding first place on its division record. Every win right now matters for tiebreakers and for seeding. A win over the Saints sets up a home game against the Browns with a chance to grab control of the AFC North before the bye. A loss would put real pressure on that game. In a division this tight, this is exactly the kind of game a contender has to win.

Trade block: what these moves tell us.

Let’s finish with the deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

These moves tell me Khan is managing the receiver room week to week, and that’s not sustainable all season. Pittsburgh is relying on practice squad call-ups while Pittman and Wilson are out, and Smith may now be out of elevations. That’s why I still think a receiver is the top priority before November 10th. Josh Downs in Indianapolis is still the name I like most, with the Colts expected to sell after firing their general manager. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the deadline.

And with that open roster spot, Khan has the room to make a move without cutting anyone. That flexibility is exactly what a front office wants heading into the final two weeks before a deadline.

A historic stage.

Before we wrap up, let’s take a second to appreciate the moment. This is the first regular-season NFL game ever played in Paris. Despite the unrest in France this month, the league has said it’s confident in its security plans, and Art Rooney II flew in this week saying that, with heightened security in place, “they don’t seem to be concerned.” Europe also turned its clocks back overnight, so the time difference with Pittsburgh dropped from six hours to five on game day. McCarthy’s message to the team was simple: it’s a business trip. Kickoff is at 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh time.

The bottom line.

So what’s the huge opportunity? The Steelers get a veteran safety back, they’ve got enough healthy receivers to run their offense, the pass rush is healthy, and they’re facing a 2-4 team with a chance to make it three straight wins before a big division game against Cleveland. DeShon Elliott’s return is the headline, but the details matter too: Brandon Smith may have used his final elevation, Marquez Valdes-Scantling is waiting in the wings, and Khan still has a roster spot to play with heading into the deadline.

No single move decides a game. But together, these moves give the Steelers more options, more depth, and more flexibility. Now it’s time to go win in Paris and make a little history while they’re at it.
