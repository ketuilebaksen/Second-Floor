Steelers Nation, the updates are coming in waves this week, and the newest one might be the most important. The price on Josh Downs is dropping. Pittsburgh already has an offer on the table. And one NFL reporter now says a deal is expected to happen after this weekend.

That’s update number one, but it’s not the only one. Cam Heyward just explained, in a way only Cam can, why Aaron Rodgers gets to change plays at the line when almost no other quarterback in this league would be trusted to. A rookie receiver revealed the exact words Rodgers used in the huddle when he took over in Tampa. Heyward also wants to rewrite part of the rule book. And Thursday’s practice report gave this team a serious shot of good health before the biggest game of the season.

One quick correction before we start: if you saw this framed as a Tampa Bay week, that game is long done. Pittsburgh beat the Buccaneers 26-19 in overtime back on October 18. This week is Cleveland, at Acrisure Stadium, Sunday at 1 p.m., with first place in the AFC North on the line.

I’m James, this is Second Floor, and we’re going through every update, one wave at a time. Let’s go.

UPDATE ONE: THE DOWNS PRICE IS FALLING

Here’s the full context, because the details matter.

On Wednesday, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that Colts receiver Josh Downs had requested a trade. The same day, Jordan Schultz reported interest from multiple AFC teams, naming the Steelers.

On Thursday, Adam Schefter said on his podcast that the Colts “have told teams they’re looking for third-round value.” And then he added, “Nobody’s willing to do that right now.”

And then came the post that shifted the whole story. NFL reporter Evan Sidery wrote that the Chiefs and Steelers “have both offered Day 3 draft compensation to the Colts for Josh Downs.” He continued: “After requesting a trade this week, a Downs deal is expected to go down after this weekend. Indianapolis will likely fetch a 4th or 5th-round pick for Downs after originally seeking” a higher price.

So let’s put those pieces together. The Colts asked for a third. Nobody bit. Pittsburgh and Kansas City put Day 3 offers on the table. And now the expectation, according to Sidery, is a fourth or a fifth, with the deal coming after Sunday.

That’s a big deal. It means the Colts’ leverage is collapsing in real time. Nothing is finalized, nobody has confirmed anything, and the Steelers could still lose out. But the market is moving toward Pittsburgh’s number, not away from it.

UPDATE TWO: IT MIGHT BE A THREE-TEAM RACE

Kansas City is the name everybody’s talking about, and for good reason. The Chiefs have been hit hard at receiver, with Tyquan Thornton and Rashee Rice both dealing with injuries. Fowler says Kansas City appears poised to add a receiver.

But there’s a third name worth watching. Athlon Sports pointed out that New England is a natural landing spot, because Downs played with Patriots quarterback Drake Maye at North Carolina in 2022. Old college connections can matter, especially for a team hoping to keep a player who can become a free agent in March.

ESPN gave Downs a 45 percent chance of being traded. And Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox projected the price at a 2027 fifth-round pick. That lines up neatly with what Sidery is hearing.

So the competition is real. Pittsburgh isn’t the only team at the table, and it isn’t guaranteed to win.

UPDATE THREE: WHY THE COLTS ARE CAVING

Look at how the Colts have operated this week, and you’ll understand why the price is coming down.

On Tuesday, October 27, they traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth. Hours later, they traded DeForest Buckner to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth. Indianapolis is 2-5 after a 16-10 loss to Minnesota, and it fired general manager Chris Ballard about a week before that.

When a team is selling an eight-time Pro Bowl guard for a third and a fifth, it’s hard to justify asking for a third for a slot receiver who has 26 catches for 307 yards and no touchdowns this season. Downs’ best year was 2024, with 72 catches for 803 yards and five touchdowns, so the talent is real. But he’s on the last year of his rookie deal, he asked out, and everyone around the league knows it.

There’s also an interesting connection here. Athlon noted that Pittsburgh already acquired Michael Pittman Jr. from Indianapolis back in March. These front offices have done business before.

UPDATE FOUR: WHAT DOES DULAC’S SKEPTICISM MEAN NOW?

Earlier this week, Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette said, “They are not adding talent on the outside,” and that he didn’t expect a move unless there’s a significant injury.

Some fans are reading the Sidery report as a contradiction. I don’t think it is. First, Downs is primarily a slot receiver, not an outside guy. Second, there’s a difference between a big splash and a modest offer. Making a Day 3 bid isn’t the same as entering an expensive bidding war. Pittsburgh may simply be testing how far Indianapolis will drop.

And here’s what SI’s Jacob Punturi wrote: a fourth-round pick could be acceptable, but Omar Khan reportedly won’t give up a third. That’s a front office with a number in mind and the discipline to stick to it.

UPDATE FIVE: HEYWARD EXPLAINS RODGERS’ FREEDOM AT THE LINE

Now let’s move from the front office to the field.

Cam Heyward was on The Rich Eisen Show this week, and Rich brought up something people have been asking about: how much freedom Aaron Rodgers has to change plays at the line of scrimmage. Heyward’s answer was honest, and a little funny.

“Well, if you had a younger quarterback, and Aaron is not a younger quarterback, you’d be very worried,” he said. But then: “When he’s checking plays and he’s making sure everybody’s accountable, you feel as comfortable as you can.”

He explained where that trust comes from. “Aaron prepares as soon as that game’s done on to the next game.” And when Rodgers is directing things at the line: “You feel like he’s going to lead you to the right destination just because he is prepared.”

Think about what that means in practice. When a quarterback checks out of a play, every player on the field has to hear it, understand it, and execute the new assignment. If the right tackle misses the protection change, the quarterback gets drilled. If a receiver runs the old route, it’s an interception. A young quarterback making those calls would make a veteran defensive tackle nervous. A 42-year-old Rodgers, who studies the next opponent before the plane even lands, earns the benefit of the doubt.

And Cam had a perfect line when asked about Rodgers’ age: “Every old man has his day.”

UPDATE SIX: BERNARD REVEALS WHAT RODGERS SAID IN TAMPA

This connects directly to the comeback in Tampa. Remember, Pittsburgh trailed 16-3 heading into the fourth quarter before winning 26-19 in overtime.

Rookie receiver Germie Bernard went on Heyward’s Not Just Football podcast and shared what happened in the huddle. According to Bernard, Rodgers told the offense: “Listen guys, I’m going to take a little bit of control. I’m going to start calling some of the plays.”

And Bernard explained what it’s like to play with that kind of quarterback. “There’s no stress when you’re out there with him because he doesn’t have any stress.” He described Rodgers as “so poised and resilient, it makes you not rush.” And about the overtime touchdown, his first in the NFL: “For Aaron to trust me and rely on me in a crucial moment like that, it means everything.”

Heyward clearly sees it too. On Eisen’s show he said Bernard is “so mature for his age,” adding, “That’s the type of rookie you want on your team.”

Now, let’s keep this in perspective. Rodgers taking more control during a comeback isn’t the same as him calling every play. McCarthy is still the head coach, and according to reporting on that game, he acknowledged afterward that he should have called the plays that were working earlier. But it tells you something about this relationship. When the offense needs a spark, McCarthy trusts his quarterback to provide it.

UPDATE SEVEN: THE THURSDAY HEALTH WAVE

Now the update that affects Sunday the most.

Jalen Ramsey practiced fully on Thursday after not practicing on Wednesday with his wrist. That’s huge for the secondary. T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward, and Darnell Washington, who rested Wednesday, were also full participants.

Ben Skowronek was limited for a second straight day with his calf. He’s the first of the injured receivers who has a real shot to return, ahead of Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson, both on injured reserve. Alex Highsmith was limited again with his groin.

The concern is still in the secondary depth. Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both with hamstrings, didn’t practice Wednesday or Thursday. We’ll see Friday’s final designations soon.

And there’s the Marquez Valdes-Scantling question. Rodgers said Wednesday, “It would be nice to get him up,” and called MVS “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with.” Brandon Smith can’t be elevated anymore after using all three of his elevations, so Pittsburgh has to choose: sign Smith to the 53, elevate MVS, or both. That decision will come Saturday.

UPDATE EIGHT: HEYWARD WANTS TO CHANGE THE RULE BOOK

This one’s just fun. On the same Eisen appearance, Heyward made a pitch to get rid of kneel-downs entirely.

“I want no more kneel downs,” he said. “Get rid of that. Get rid of it all.” His idea: “Just make sure it’s an active play that both teams are trying to win.” He even took aim at spiking the ball, suggesting teams should “throw it out of bounds” instead. He admitted Rodgers would not be thrilled with him: “He’s gonna kill me for it.”

Funny timing, too. The Steelers’ final red zone trip in Paris ended with a kneel-down, which is why McCarthy’s “one in four” red zone stat was really more like one in three. So Cam’s own offense is part of the reason he’s sick of watching kneel-downs.

He also said he wants replay to play a bigger role on roughing-the-passer calls so penalties don’t decide games. That’s a defensive lineman talking, but he has a point.

UPDATE NINE: CAM’S MESSAGE TO CLEVELAND

And finally, the Browns. Heyward didn’t hold back.

On the Week 4 loss: “They got a W. That’s a W. But that has no indication on what the next game is, and the next game is them.”

On Jared Verse complaining about the Steelers double-teaming, sliding, and chipping him: “I’m checking the rule book real quick. Those are legal.” And then: “I got the bruises to prove it, but we don’t whine about it, baby. It’s part of the game.”

On the division: “The AFC North is on fire, and I’m glad I’m in the kitchen ’cause I like it that way.”

And the closer, borrowing a phrase from Mike Tomlin: “We’re in it. So bring it on.”

He was respectful, though. He praised Deshaun Watson, tight end Harold Fannin, running back Quinshon Judkins, and called Cleveland’s defense “electric.” He also brushed off the Myles Garrett storyline: “Everybody likes to talk about the Myles Garrett. That does not matter with this team.” Garrett, remember, is now with the Rams after the June trade that brought Jared Verse to Cleveland.

BONUS UPDATE: THE COLTS AREN’T DONE SELLING

Here’s one more wrinkle that could affect the timing. Indianapolis is not finished.

According to Athlon’s roundup, Jonathan Taylor’s name is still floating around, with ESPN’s Fowler and Matt Bowen naming Seattle, Washington, New Orleans, Denver, and Minnesota as fits. But Schefter reported that the Colts’ owner reportedly doesn’t want to trade him, and Taylor himself said, “I haven’t given any thought.” FOX Sports’ Ben Arthur named defensive tackle Grover Stewart as the Colts’ top trade candidate, with a $14.25 million cap hit and only about $2 million in dead money if he’s moved.

Why does that matter for Pittsburgh? Because the Colts play at Jacksonville on Sunday, November 1, and host Dallas on November 8, and the deadline is 4 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10. Indianapolis is juggling multiple deals at once. Sidery’s timeline, a Downs trade after this weekend, fits that picture. The Colts want to see who’s left standing after Sunday, both on their roster and in the bidding.

And for the Steelers, waiting until after Sunday isn’t a bad thing. Khan gets to see his team against Cleveland, see whether Skowronek comes through healthy, and then decide how badly he needs another receiver for the stretch run.

THE DIVISION STAKES IN ONE BREATH

Let’s quickly frame what Sunday decides, because every update this week points back to it.

Cleveland is 5-2 and already 2-0 inside the AFC North. Pittsburgh is 5-2 and 1-1 in the division. Cincinnati sits at 4-2 after crushing Baltimore 35-13. The Ravens are 4-3 and 0-2 in the division, in last place.

If the Steelers win Sunday, they split the season series with Cleveland, the division records even out, and Pittsburgh takes sole possession of first place heading into the bye. If they lose, the Browns sweep, lock up the head-to-head tiebreaker, and Pittsburgh’s path to the division title gets a lot steeper with road trips to Cincinnati and Philadelphia right after the break.

That’s why a Downs deal “after this weekend” makes so much sense. The Browns game will tell Khan whether he’s buying for a division leader or for a team that has to chase.

THE OFFENSE STILL HAS HOMEWORK

Let’s be honest about one more thing before the trade block. The updates are good, but this offense isn’t finished.

In Paris, Rodgers went 19 of 30 for 212 yards and a touchdown. The Steelers ran for 148 yards on 30 carries, but they converted just one red zone trip into a touchdown. Three straight wins, yes. But against a Browns team that beat them a month ago, field goals won’t be enough.

Kurt Warner made the same point on The Rich Eisen Show this week, saying he doesn’t think this team is “gonna go very far” because it struggles to create explosive plays. I think that’s too harsh for a 5-2 team that’s figured out its identity. Jaylen Warren put it best on Thursday: “We’re popping off runs and you can’t ask for much more than that.” But Warner’s warning about the passing game is worth hearing.

That’s why the Downs conversation matters. It isn’t about replacing DK Metcalf or Germie Bernard. It’s about giving Rodgers a dependable target in the middle of the field when the defense sells out to stop Warren and doubles Metcalf on the outside.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, here’s where I take off the reporter hat. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Earlier this week I said my ceiling for Downs was a fourth-round pick plus a late-round sweetener, and that I wouldn’t pay a straight third. Now that Sidery is reporting the price could land at a fourth or a fifth, my answer is simple: make the deal.

At a fifth, it’s an easy yes. At a fourth, it’s still a yes. Downs is 25, he’s productive when the offense around him works, and he fits exactly what Pittsburgh lacks: a quick slot receiver who can beat man coverage on third down. Cleveland played man on 73.3 percent of its snaps in the first meeting, per Steeler Nation. That’s the kind of defense Downs is built to beat.

What I wouldn’t do is let Kansas City drag the price back up. If the Chiefs suddenly offer a third, wish them luck. Don’t touch the 2028 second-rounder from the Joey Porter Jr. trade. And don’t treat Downs like a long-term piece. He’s a rental who can become a free agent in March. That’s fine in Rodgers’ last season.

And if Pittsburgh loses the race? Skowronek is close, Pittman is eligible to return after the bye, and MVS is waiting. The Steelers are winning without Downs. They’d just be harder to stop with him.

SUNDAY: WHAT I’M WATCHING

The first fifteen plays. If McCarthy comes out running again, it’s a signal the Paris plan is the new normal.

Rodgers at the line. Heyward told us he trusts the checks. Watch how many times Rodgers waves people around before the snap against Cleveland’s man looks.

Ramsey’s usage. With a full practice Thursday, I expect him all over the field, from the slot to deep safety.

Finishing. Heyward has talked all week about a finish mentality. The last time these teams met, Cleveland drove for the winning field goal in the final seconds. This time the defense has to close.

PREDICTION AND YOUR TURN

I’m staying with my pick: Steelers 23, Browns 17. Pittsburgh wins the line of scrimmage, gets one big defensive play, and heads into the bye alone in first place.
