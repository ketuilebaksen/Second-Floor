Steelers Nation, let’s be upfront about the headline. Aaron Rodgers didn’t make a bombshell decision this week. He isn’t retiring early. He isn’t demanding a trade. He isn’t changing anything about his plan for this season, which he said back in May will be his last.

What he did do is something quarterbacks rarely do in public. He went out of his way to name a practice squad receiver who hasn’t played a single regular-season snap, and he said he wants to throw him the football. Then his head coach did almost the same thing in the same week.

The player is Max Hurleman. He wears number 84, the same number Antonio Brown made famous in this city. And if you don’t know his story, it’s one of the most unusual paths to an NFL roster you’ll ever hear: a running back at a small school, a walk-on cornerback at a national powerhouse, and now a receiver the quarterback is asking for by name.

So today, we’ll go through exactly what Rodgers and Mike McCarthy said, who Hurleman really is, how the practice squad rules affect his timeline, and where he could actually fit, because I think the source material oversells one part of this. And with the Eagles up next, my board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

WHAT RODGERS ACTUALLY SAID

Here’s the quote, from Rodgers’ Wednesday media session, as reported by Sports Illustrated’s Steelers site and Heavy.

Rodgers was asked about the receiver room. He started by saying the team has “raised up a couple of our P-squad guys who are talented.” And then he added: “Still would love to get some opportunities for Max at some point whenever he comes off.”

That’s it. Two sentences. But think about who said them.

Rodgers is a four-time MVP and a Super Bowl champion. He’s famously particular about the receivers he trusts. He didn’t have to mention any individual practice squad player. He chose to name one, and he chose a guy who’s been on the injured list for nearly two months.

When a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season goes out of his way to campaign for a player, that’s not small talk. That’s a preference.

MCCARTHY SAID IT TOO

And it wasn’t just Rodgers.

According to the transcript, McCarthy brought Hurleman up the same day, a few hours earlier, while talking about the players getting healthy. He mentioned Michael Pittman Jr. getting close, then named Roman Wilson and Hurleman in the same breath.

Then he got personal. Steelers Depot ran a headline quoting McCarthy: “How do you not love Max?” I couldn’t open that article, so I’m going by the headline. The transcript adds that McCarthy described Hurleman as someone who comes in every day, works hard and “rips up” the GPS data. I couldn’t find that GPS line in the coverage I could access, so treat it as the transcript’s account.

What I can confirm is what SI reported on November 12. McCarthy said: “I felt that he truly performed to a level that he deserved a 53-man roster spot.” And then: “So, unfortunate that he got hurt when he did.”

That’s a head coach telling you, on the record, that the only reason this player isn’t on the 53 is an injury.

WHAT “RIPPING UP THE GPS” MEANS

The transcript does a nice job explaining the GPS comment, so let me pass that along, because a lot of fans don’t know what it refers to.

Every NFL team tracks its players in practice with wearable GPS devices. Those systems measure top speed, total distance, number of sprints and overall workload. When a coach says a player rips up the GPS numbers, he means that player is consistently near the top of the team in speed and effort.

Earlier this season, SI reported McCarthy praising Hurleman’s “incredible workload capacity,” which fits the same picture. It’s objective data, not a feeling. And it’s exactly the kind of thing that gets a coach’s attention when he’s deciding who to trust.

HURLEMAN’S REACTION

And how did Hurleman take all this?

According to SI, he said: “Haven’t played a game yet, but it’s awesome to hear they trust me and see me as an option.”

I love that answer. No bragging, no promises. Just a guy who’s been waiting a long time for a chance, hearing that the two most important people in the building want him to get one.

WHO IS MAX HURLEMAN?

Now let’s get into the story, because it’s a great one, and it starts close to home.

Hurleman is from Wyomissing, Pennsylvania, about an hour west of Philadelphia. According to his Wikipedia profile, he played running back and defensive back at Wyomissing Area High School.

From there, he went to Colgate, an FCS school in the Patriot League, where he spent four seasons from 2020 to 2023. He was a running back and return specialist. Over those four years, he ran for 814 yards and caught 80 passes for 841 yards. In 2023, he was named first-team All-Patriot League as a special teamer.

Then came the twist. For his final college season in 2024, he transferred to Notre Dame as a walk-on, and switched to cornerback. He also became the Irish punt returner, with 23 returns for 138 yards, plus 13 tackles and a fumble recovery on special teams.

That 2024 Notre Dame team made a run all the way to the national championship game, where it lost to Ohio State. And here’s a fun detail: Ohio State’s quarterback in that game was Will Howard, who’s now in the Steelers’ quarterback room. So two guys who were on opposite sidelines in a national title game are now teammates in Pittsburgh.

THE NUMBER 84

Let’s talk about that jersey number for a second, because the transcript opens with it.

Number 84 belonged to Antonio Brown during his years in Pittsburgh, when he was one of the most productive receivers in the league. Since then, it’s been passed around. According to the NFL Jersey Numbers account, when Hurleman was given 84, the last player to wear it was Kevin Foelsch.

I’m not going to pretend a jersey number means anything about a player’s future. Hurleman isn’t Antonio Brown, and nobody should expect him to be. But there’s something fitting about a player who has had to fight for every snap wearing a number with that much history. Every time he walks onto the practice field, there’s a reminder of what’s possible at that position in this city.

HOW HE GOT TO PITTSBURGH

After Notre Dame, Hurleman went undrafted in 2025. According to Steelers.com, he earned his contract through a tryout at the Steelers’ rookie minicamp. Imagine that: a walk-on cornerback with a Patriot League résumé, trying out with no guarantee, and earning a spot.

When he signed, he thanked the organization. According to SI, he said: “I can’t thank them enough, Coach Tomlin, Mr. Rooney, Mr. Khan, for just affording me this opportunity.”

The Steelers moved him to receiver. And in the 2025 preseason opener against Jacksonville, according to his Wikipedia profile, he caught two passes for 29 yards and a touchdown. I mentioned that touchdown in an earlier video as unconfirmed, and I’m glad to say it checks out.

He was waived on August 25, 2025, and re-signed to the practice squad. He spent the entire 2025 season there without playing in a game, according to Steelers.com. Then, after the playoff loss to Houston, he signed a reserve/future contract in January.

THE POSITION QUESTION

Here’s where it gets a little confusing, so let’s lay it out.

The transcript says Hurleman was listed as a running back at one point, switched to receiver, and also took some cornerback snaps trying to make the roster. Wikipedia says that when he signed his reserve/future deal in January, he was moved back to running back. But by this season, SI and the Steelers’ own transactions list him as a receiver.

So he’s moved around a lot. Running back in high school and at Colgate. Cornerback at Notre Dame. Receiver, then running back, then receiver again in Pittsburgh.

One correction to the transcript: at one point it says Hurleman would take quarterback snaps if needed. I think that’s a slip. The context makes clear it meant cornerback snaps, not quarterback.

The bigger point stands, though. McCarthy has said repeatedly that unless you’re a quarterback, you need to be able to do multiple things to make his roster. Hurleman is the definition of that. Running back, receiver, defensive back, returner. A guy like that is hard to cut.

A PITTSBURGH TRADITION: THE UNDRAFTED GUY

Before we get to the injury, let’s put Hurleman’s path in context, because this franchise has a long history with players like him.

The Steelers have built some of their best rosters with players nobody drafted. James Harrison went undrafted and became a Defensive Player of the Year. Willie Parker went undrafted and became a Super Bowl starter.

And you don’t have to go back that far. Jaylen Warren, the best offensive player on this team right now, also went undrafted in 2022. He came in, did the dirty work on special teams and in pass protection, and turned himself into one of the most productive backs in the league.

That’s the blueprint for a player like Hurleman. Nobody expects him to become Warren. But Warren is proof that, in this building, an undrafted player who works hard and does multiple jobs can earn a real role. And McCarthy has made it clear he values exactly those traits.

WHY HE’S BEEN INVISIBLE ALL SEASON

So if the coaches love him this much, why hasn’t he played?

The answer is the injury. According to Steelers.com, the team placed Hurleman on the practice squad injured list on September 19, ahead of the Patriots game. The team didn’t disclose the injury.

That matters because of how the practice squad works. The transcript explains this well. Teams can elevate practice squad players to the game-day roster when injuries pile up, without making a permanent move. That same week, for example, the Steelers elevated running back Travis Homer and defensive back Doneiko Slaughter for the Patriots game.

But a player on the practice squad injured list can’t be elevated, because he can’t play. So while other practice squad players, like Brandon Smith, have been getting game-day opportunities, Hurleman has been stuck on the sideline.

According to SI, he’s now eligible to return. Once he’s healthy and back on the regular practice squad, he becomes an option again. And based on what Rodgers and McCarthy said, he may be the first option they reach for.

THE PRACTICE SQUAD CLOCK

There’s another reason this matters, and it’s about the rules.

As the rules are generally understood, a practice squad player can only be elevated to the game-day roster a limited number of times in a season. After that, if the team wants to keep using him, it has to sign him to the 53-man roster.

There’s also a risk on the other side. While a player sits on the practice squad, other teams can sign him to their own 53-man rosters. So if Hurleman comes back healthy and starts looking like the player Rodgers and McCarthy described, the Steelers will have a decision to make sooner rather than later.

That’s one reason public praise can be a double-edged sword. When a head coach says a practice squad player “deserved a 53-man roster spot,” other teams hear that too.

WHERE THE TRANSCRIPT OVERSELLS IT

Now, the transcript makes a bold claim: that Hurleman could be the Steelers’ number three receiver as soon as he returns.

I’d pump the brakes on that, and here’s why.

The transcript says Ben Skowronek, Roman Wilson and Pittman are all still out. But that’s outdated. Skowronek practiced in full all week and had no game status for Sunday night in Cincinnati. So he’s back.

Then there’s rookie Germie Bernard, who’s been part of the rotation next to DK Metcalf. Roman Wilson becomes eligible to come off injured reserve after the Bengals game, and he’s caught a long touchdown against Cincinnati this season. And Pittman, who SI says is dealing with plantar fasciitis, is working off to the side and getting closer.

So when everyone is healthy, Hurleman is probably not your number three receiver. More realistically, he’s competing for the fifth or sixth spot, along with a role on special teams.

THE MORE REALISTIC PATH: SPECIAL TEAMS

And honestly, that’s the most likely way he gets on the field first.

Look at his résumé. All-Patriot League as a special teamer. Notre Dame’s punt returner during a run to the title game. The transcript itself points out that special teams is how a lot of Steelers players make the roster.

So if I’m McCarthy and I want to get Hurleman involved, I start with a game-day elevation where he covers kicks, maybe returns a punt, and gets a small package of offensive snaps. That’s how a practice squad player earns a bigger role. Not by jumping straight into the receiver rotation, but by being useful in three phases.

The transcript does make a good point on this front: a player returning from injury usually needs a few practices to get his conditioning and timing back. I’d expect the team to ease him in, exactly as the transcript suggests.

His return résumé runs deep, too. According to Steelers.com, across five college seasons, Hurleman appeared in 51 games with 23 starts, and he returned 49 punts for 353 yards. That’s a lot of experience catching a ball with defenders bearing down on him, which is exactly the skill a punt returner needs at this level.

WHAT HE BRINGS AS A RECEIVER

When he does get offensive snaps, what kind of receiver is he?

He’s listed at five-foot-eleven and 200 pounds, according to Wikipedia. That’s sturdy for his height. The transcript describes him as a physical player who can stretch the field, catch short passes and turn them into long gains with yards after the catch.

And his running back background matters. At Colgate, he caught 80 passes out of the backfield. That kind of player understands how to find soft spots in zone coverage, how to make the first defender miss, and how to block in the run game. In an offense built around Jaylen Warren, a receiver who can block and run after the catch has real value.

His defensive experience matters too. As the transcript points out, a player who has lined up at cornerback knows how a defender thinks. That can make a receiver smarter about leverage and route timing.

THE RODGERS TRUST FACTOR

Let’s come back to Rodgers, because the transcript is right that this is the most important part of the story.

The transcript claims Rodgers didn’t play in the preseason, so Hurleman never got live game reps with him, and that most of their work together came in training camp. I couldn’t confirm Rodgers’ preseason snap count, so take that as the transcript’s account. But even if it’s only partly true, the point stands: Hurleman earned Rodgers’ trust in practice, not in games.

And we’ve seen that Rodgers’ trust carries weight. He publicly backed Brandon Johnson earlier this season, saying he’s “always been a fan.” He’s talked up Brandon Smith. He’s mentioned Marquez Valdes-Scantling, his old Green Bay teammate, who’s on the practice squad.

But here’s the difference. Smith and Valdes-Scantling are veterans Rodgers has played with before, and Johnson has years of NFL snaps on his résumé. Hurleman is an undrafted second-year player who’s never been on a regular-season field. Rodgers vouching for him isn’t loyalty. It’s evaluation.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE NEXT STRETCH

So why does any of this matter in mid-November?

Because the Steelers are about to enter the toughest part of their schedule. Next week, they visit Philadelphia on November 22. Then it’s a short week before the Black Friday home game against Denver. Then Houston and Jacksonville in prime time.

Over that stretch, injuries will happen. Receivers will get banged up. And when that happens, the Steelers will reach into the practice squad. Rodgers and McCarthy have just told you whose name is at the top of that list.

There’s a roster angle, too. Pittsburgh chose not to add a receiver at the trade deadline. Part of the bet was on players returning from injury: Wilson, Pittman, and yes, Hurleman. If Hurleman can provide even a few useful snaps on offense and special teams, that bet looks smarter.

WHAT TO WATCH THIS WEEK

So how will you know if Hurleman’s moment is coming? Here’s what I’d watch over the next few days.

First, the transaction wire. If the Steelers move Hurleman off the practice squad injured list, that’s step one. It means he’s healthy enough to practice and be considered for elevation.

Second, the practice reports and the media sessions. If McCarthy mentions him again, or if reporters at practice note him working with the offense, that’s a sign he’s in the plan.

Third, Roman Wilson. If Wilson’s practice window opens next week, that could affect how many receiver snaps are available. Hurleman’s role may depend partly on how quickly Wilson gets back to full speed. According to SI, Wilson had missed four straight weeks with a finger injury as of November 12.

Fourth, the game-day elevations. The Steelers announce those the day before kickoff. If Hurleman’s name shows up on Saturday, he’s about to make his NFL debut.

THE EAGLES GAME

Whatever the scoreboard said in Cincinnati, the trip to Philadelphia is next. Kickoff is Sunday, November 22, at 4:25 p.m. Eastern, according to CBS Pittsburgh’s schedule.

For Hurleman, there’s a fun subplot: Wyomissing is about an hour from Lincoln Financial Field. If he’s healthy and elevated for that game, it would be a debut in front of a lot of family and friends from the area.

I’m not predicting that he’ll play. The team still has to activate him from the injured list, and the coaches will want to see him practice. But if the timing lines up, it would make a great story.

A QUICK WORD ON THE EAGLES

Philadelphia will be a tough test regardless of who’s on the field.

The Eagles made one of the biggest moves of the trade deadline, sending defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-rounder, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer. That’s a sign of a front office willing to make bold decisions, and it changes the look of their defensive interior.

For Pittsburgh’s offense, that could matter. A run game built around Warren will want to test whatever the Eagles do in the middle without Davis. And if the Steelers are moving the ball on the ground, that’s when a versatile receiver who can block and run after the catch becomes useful.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Max Hurleman: activate him from the practice squad injured list as soon as the medical staff clears him. Give him a full week of practice before any elevation.

First elevation: special teams first. Kick coverage and a chance at punt returns. Then a handful of offensive snaps, ideally on screens and quick routes where his yards-after-catch ability shows up.

Receiver rotation: Metcalf, Bernard and Skowronek for now. Roman Wilson designated to return next week. Pittman when he’s ready.

Rodgers: keep doing what he’s doing. A quarterback publicly backing a young player is good for the whole locker room.

Long term: if Hurleman shows anything in the next month, sign him to the 53 before another team grabs him.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. There was no Aaron Rodgers bombshell this week. What there was is a quarterback and a head coach both putting their reputations behind an undrafted, unproven, injured practice squad receiver. That doesn’t happen by accident.

Max Hurleman has gone from Colgate running back to Notre Dame walk-on cornerback to a rookie minicamp tryout to the Steelers’ practice squad. Now, the most demanding quarterback of his generation is asking for chances to throw him the ball.

He still has to get healthy. He still has to earn snaps. But this is a name you’ll want to remember.
