Steelers Nation, I’ve watched a lot of Steelers football, and that was one of the wildest afternoons I can remember. Down 16-3 heading into the fourth quarter in Tampa. An offense that had done almost nothing for three quarters. A head coach who spent halftime in the medical tent. And somehow, the Steelers walked out of Raymond James Stadium with a 26-19 overtime win, their first road win of the season, their first winning streak of the season, and a 4-2 record. Aaron Rodgers led a 12-play, 70-yard drive in overtime. A rookie scored the first touchdown of his career to win it. A former Buccaneer came back to haunt his old team. And Jalen Ramsey, playing with a broken wrist, ended the game with a sack. So today we’re breaking down the comeback, the heroes, the good news that came out of it, and what it means heading to Paris. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

First, how bad it looked.

Let’s not pretend the first three quarters were pretty, because they weren’t. Tampa kicked a 43-yard field goal in the first quarter. Early in the second, undrafted rookie quarterback Jalon Daniels, starting again for the injured Baker Mayfield, hit Emeka Egbuka for a two-yard touchdown. Chris Boswell missed a 48-yard field goal wide left. Germie Bernard was stopped short on a fourth-and-3. At halftime, it was 13-3 Tampa.

And then it got stranger. Mike McCarthy had his foot stepped on near the sideline. By the end of the half, he was limping badly and needed help from a staff member to get to the medical tent. He spent the entire halftime there instead of the locker room, while assistant head coach Joe Whitt took on extra responsibilities. After the game, McCarthy joked, “I don’t recommend it. It’s really hot in there.” And Rodgers said, “Glad he’s okay. He’s from Pittsburgh. He toughed it out.”

Tampa added another field goal in the third quarter to make it 16-3. Through three quarters, Pittsburgh’s offense was sputtering, DK Metcalf had only two catches for 11 yards in the first half, and the Steelers’ five-game road losing streak looked like it was about to become six.

Then came the fourth quarter.

The comeback.

According to CBS Sports, it was historic: the biggest comeback of its kind by a team trailing entering the fourth quarter since 1992. And Pro Football Talk laid out just how dominant Pittsburgh was in that final quarter: 230 yards of offense, two interceptions of Daniels, and scoring drives of 68, 24, 37, and two yards.

It started with Rodgers. He led an eight-play, 68-yard drive that ended with a five-yard touchdown pass to tight end Darnell Washington. Now it was 16-10, and you could feel the game change.

Then the defense took over. Rookie cornerback Daylen Everette, who’s been growing every week, intercepted Daniels after Cam Heyward pressured him. It was the first interception of Everette’s NFL career. Boswell, who had missed earlier, came back with a 39-yarder to make it 16-13.

And then, with under two minutes to go, Boswell lined up from 58 yards. His first attempt was wiped out by the two-minute warning, so he had to kick it again. He drilled it. 16-16, with 1:55 left. Fifty-eight yards, twice, with the game on the line. That’s Chris Boswell.

The Jamel Dean moment.

Now here’s the play everybody in Pittsburgh is talking about. Jamel Dean, who missed the Colts game with an ankle injury and spent the first part of his career in Tampa, was back on the field against his old team. On the Bucs’ next possession, Daniels tried to hit Egbuka, and Dean jumped the route. Interception. He returned it 40 yards, all the way to the Tampa 3-yard line, before Daniels pushed him out of bounds.

Nick Herbig summed it up perfectly: “That’s like a storybook ending for that to happen in your own place that drafted you.” And Dean wasn’t done. He finished the game with six passes defended. Six. That’s a cornerback telling everyone he’s back.

The Steelers couldn’t punch it in from the 3, but Boswell kicked a 20-yard field goal to give Pittsburgh its first lead of the day, 19-16, with 1:21 left. Boswell’s fourth-quarter line: three field goals, including the 58-yarder. After a miss in the first half, he answered when it mattered most.

The gut punch, and the response.

But Tampa wasn’t done. Chase McLaughlin, who had already made field goals from 43, 47, and 52 yards, hit a 53-yarder with eight seconds left. 19-19. Overtime.

That’s the moment where a lot of teams fold. You just came all the way back, you finally took the lead, and the other team ties it at the buzzer. On the road, in the heat, after five straight road losses. This is where the old version of this team might have collapsed.

Not this time.

The overtime drive.

Tampa won the overtime coin toss and chose to kick off, which meant Rodgers got the ball first. And he went to work. Twelve plays. Seventy yards. Six minutes and 45 seconds off the clock.

He hit Metcalf. He found Jaylen Warren, who had 25 yards on the drive, including a 12-yard catch-and-run that moved Pittsburgh into field goal range. He gave it to Rico Dowdle, back from his toe injury, who picked up the drive’s final first down. And then, from six yards out, Rodgers threw to rookie Germie Bernard in the end zone. Touchdown. The first touchdown of Bernard’s NFL career. 26-19, with 3:15 left.

It was Rodgers’ seventh career overtime win. And that touchdown pass was his 539th in the regular season, tying Peyton Manning for third in NFL history. It was also the fourth time in his career he’s won a game when trailing by 13 or more entering the fourth quarter.

Rodgers by the numbers.

Let’s appreciate what Rodgers did in this game, because the box score only tells part of the story. He finished 33 of 49 for 253 yards, two touchdowns, and one interception, and he did it while being sacked five times behind an offensive line that was under pressure all afternoon. In the first three quarters, nothing came easy. In the fourth quarter and overtime, he was surgical. He spread the ball to Metcalf, Bernard, Washington, Warren, and Dowdle. He managed the clock. He didn’t panic.

And remember the context. This is a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season, with two of his top three receivers on injured reserve, throwing to a rookie and a receiver fighting an illness. Three weeks ago, people were debating his sideline behavior in Cleveland. Now he’s tied Peyton Manning on the all-time regular-season touchdown list and led one of the best comebacks of his career. That’s what Hall of Famers do.

The final stop.

Tampa had one last chance. And on fourth-and-8, with 1:06 left, Patrick Graham dialed up a blitz. Jalen Ramsey, the same Jalen Ramsey who has been playing for weeks with a broken wrist in a cast, came free and sacked Daniels. Game over.

After the game, Ramsey said, “As long as there’s time on the clock, we’re going to continue fighting no matter what the score is.” And Dean put it even more simply: “The game doesn’t end until it’s over with.”

The good news, part one: the defense is becoming elite.

Now, let’s talk about the good news that came out of this game, because there’s a lot of it.

Start with the defense. Tampa finished with just 224 total yards. The Bucs went 6-of-19 on third down. Bucky Irving, one of the most dangerous running backs in the NFC, was held to 46 yards on 19 carries. Daniels completed 22 of 43 passes for 164 yards, with one touchdown and two interceptions. The Steelers sacked him five times: two each for Ramsey and Nick Herbig, and one for Yahya Black. Payton Wilson led the team with 11 tackles.

Think about the last two weeks. Against the Colts, Jonathan Taylor was held to 45 yards, and Indianapolis didn’t score a passing touchdown. Against the Bucs, Irving was held to 46 yards, and the defense created two turnovers and five sacks. This unit is becoming the backbone of the team.

And don’t overlook Nick Herbig. Two sacks in this game, including one on Tampa’s final drive of regulation. With T.J. Watt and Alex Highsmith drawing so much attention on the edges, Herbig keeps finding ways to make plays. He’s the kind of young pass rusher every contender needs, and he’s showing up in the biggest moments.

And Payton Wilson continues to be everywhere. After 10 tackles against the Colts, he led the team again with 11 against Tampa. Sideline to sideline, against the run and in coverage. He’s quietly becoming one of the best young linebackers in football.

The good news, part two: Germie Bernard is for real.

Remember when Rodgers said before the season that young receivers usually take “a year or two”? Well, Germie Bernard is on a faster schedule. In his first career start against the Colts, he had six catches for 80 yards. Against Tampa, he had 11 catches for 87 yards and the game-winning touchdown in overtime.

Rodgers put it beautifully after the game: “There comes a time when the confidence starts to match the potential, and I think we’re reaching that time with Germie.” And McCarthy said, “I just love everything he’s doing. He’s accepted the challenge.”

With Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve, this is the best possible news. Pittsburgh may have found a real number two receiver right when it needed one.

The good news, part three: DK Metcalf showed up when it counted.

Metcalf was a late addition to the injury report with an illness, and he barely factored in early, with two catches for 11 yards in the first half. But he finished with 11 catches for 93 yards. Rodgers said it plainly: “Once we got DK involved, the offense just shifted.” Two receivers with 11 catches each in the same game, with the receiver room this depleted. That’s impressive.

The good news, part four: Jamel Dean is back.

We covered the interception, but this deserves its own spot. Dean missed the Colts game, practiced on a limited basis all week, and was listed as questionable. Then he came out and had six passes defended and the biggest defensive play of the game, against the team that drafted him. When Dean is healthy, he’s a true number one corner, and Pittsburgh’s secondary looks completely different.

The good news, bonus: Chris Boswell’s redemption.

I have to give Boswell his own moment. In the first half, he missed a 48-yarder wide left, and in a game this tight, every point matters. A lot of kickers would let that get in their head. Not Boswell. He came back to make a 52-yarder before halftime, a 39-yarder early in the fourth, and then the two biggest kicks of the game: the 58-yarder to tie it, which he had to make twice because of the two-minute warning, and the 20-yarder to take the lead. That’s mental toughness. That’s why he’s one of the most trusted kickers in the league.

The good news, part five: McCarthy is okay.

And of course, the coach is fine. McCarthy said he had his foot stepped on, and he returned to coach the second half from the sideline. He even praised the staff, saying, “They didn’t miss a beat.” And he summed up the whole afternoon in one line: “We won our first away game. We stacked two in a row.” He also gave a shout-out to the traveling fans: “Just seeing the Terrible Towels show up, especially at the key parts of the game in the fourth quarter and overtime, that is special.”

What still needs work.

Now, I always give you both sides. This wasn’t a perfect performance. Rodgers was sacked five times, and he threw an interception on a deflection: Yaya Diaby tipped the pass, it bounced off Darnell Washington’s hands, and A’Shawn Robinson caught it as he fell. The Steelers ran the ball only 18 times, just eight after halftime, so the Warren and Dowdle combination we talked about all week didn’t get much of a chance. Boswell missed a 48-yarder. And the offense couldn’t score a touchdown after Dean’s interception at the 3-yard line.

Those are things McCarthy will want to clean up before Paris. But here’s the thing: good teams find ways to win when they don’t play their best. This was one of those wins.

The running game question.

One thing I want to watch going forward is the backfield. All week, we talked about Warren and Dowdle finally being together. In this game, though, the Steelers fell behind early and had to throw, so they ran the ball only 18 times. But look at how the backs were used when it mattered. In overtime, Warren caught a pass and turned it into a 12-yard gain that moved the Steelers into field goal range, and he had 25 yards on that drive. Dowdle, in his first game back since dislocating his toe in Week 2, picked up the last first down before Bernard’s touchdown. So even in a game where the run game wasn’t the focus, both backs made winning plays in the biggest drive of the day.

In Paris, if the Steelers can play with a lead, I expect to see a lot more of that one-two punch. That’s the formula that makes this offense the hardest to stop.

And here’s one more name that deserves credit: Pat Freiermuth. He was a late addition to the injury report with a calf issue, but he played. And Darnell Washington, the big tight end, caught the touchdown that started the comeback. The tight ends keep showing up for this offense, week after week.

Let’s clear up the noise.

And a quick correction, because I’ve seen some recaps going around that got this game badly wrong. One video credited Mike Tomlin and Matt Canada with the play calling, and said the Steelers moved to 3-3. That’s not this team. Tomlin and Canada aren’t the coaches. McCarthy is the head coach, and the Steelers are 4-2. That same video also said Baker Mayfield played. He didn’t. He’s still out with his thumb injury, which is why Daniels started. If you want the real story, it’s all right here.

Why this win matters so much.

Let’s step back and think about the bigger picture. A week ago, the Steelers were 3-2, coming off a big home win against the Colts but carrying a five-game road losing streak. The question everyone was asking was simple: can this team win away from Acrisure Stadium? Now we have the answer. They won on the road, in the heat, against a team that had just beaten Dallas, after trailing by 13 entering the fourth quarter, with their head coach hobbling around on an injured foot.

That’s the kind of win that builds belief inside a locker room. Young players like Bernard and Everette now know they can make plays in big moments. Veterans like Rodgers, Ramsey, and Dean showed the young guys what it looks like to keep fighting. And McCarthy’s first season, which looked shaky after Cleveland, suddenly has real momentum: back-to-back wins, a 4-2 record, and a team that’s finding different ways to win every week.

And in the AFC North, every win matters. Baltimore just traded for Cam Jurgens to strengthen its offensive line. The division race is going to be tight all season. Stealing a road win when you didn’t play your best for three quarters could be the difference between a home playoff game and a wild-card trip in January.

What it means for the trade deadline.

So does this win change Omar Khan’s plans? Here’s my opinion, not a report. I think it reduces the urgency at receiver, but it doesn’t eliminate it. Bernard and Metcalf combined for 22 catches. That’s great, but you can’t ask two receivers to carry the whole passing game every week. With Pittman and Wilson out until after the bye at the earliest, a bridge veteran still makes sense.

The name I like most is still Jauan Jennings of the Vikings. Pro Football Talk reported that he could be traded after Minnesota signed Odell Beckham Jr. to its practice squad. There’s no report connecting him to Pittsburgh, but he caught nine touchdowns last season and could be had for a late-round pick. Darius Slayton, who the Colts released on Friday, is another option as a free agent, though his knee is a question. And Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline. After a win like this, Khan can shop from a position of strength.

Next up: Paris.

Now the Steelers head overseas to face the New Orleans Saints in Paris in Week 7. They’ll go at 4-2, on a two-game winning streak, with a defense that’s playing at an elite level, a rookie receiver who’s emerging, and a quarterback who just reminded everyone why he’s one of the greatest to ever do it. DeShon Elliott could also be closer to returning from injured reserve.

Two weeks ago, people were asking if this team was falling apart. Now it’s 4-2, and it’s finding ways to win. That’s the good news, Steelers Nation, and it might just be the start of something bigger.
