Steelers Nation, this is the moment. Not next week. Not “sometime before the deadline.” Right now. The NFL trade deadline closes Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern, and the Pittsburgh Steelers are staring at the biggest receiver decision of Omar Khan’s season with roughly a day and a half left on the clock.

Let me tell you exactly where things stand before we break it all down. No trade has been completed. No receiver has been acquired. But three names have been circled by ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler as the receivers in play around the league, Pittsburgh has been linked to all of this for weeks, and the Colts, who own the player Steelers fans want most, said they would revisit their offers after Sunday’s game against Dallas. That game is now behind them. Which means the decision window is officially open.

So here’s how we’re going to do this today. I’m going to walk you through the decision the way a front office would: one fork in the road at a time. Brian Thomas Jr., Josh Downs and Calvin Ridley, each with the case for, the case against and the price. Then we’ll handle the rest of this week’s news: Jaquan Brisker’s reported fine, the injured players who could be coming back, the real numbers behind Pittsburgh’s offense, and what all of it means for the trip to Cincinnati on November 15. And at the end, my board, labeled as opinion, not a report.

THE SETUP: WHY THIS DECISION CAN’T WAIT

Let’s start with the problem the Steelers are trying to solve.

Pittsburgh is six and two, first place in the AFC North. The defense has been suffocating. The run game has been one of the most efficient in football. But the passing attack has been the part of this team that still makes fans nervous, and the injury report hasn’t helped.

Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with a foot injury. He’s played in only three games this season. Roman Wilson is on injured reserve as well. That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard and a thin supporting cast to carry the receiving load for a forty-two-year-old quarterback who wants to get the ball out fast.

Look at the Browns game, the 27-6 win that sent Pittsburgh into the bye. According to Steelers.com, the Steelers piled up 361 total yards. Aaron Rodgers went 13 of 25 for 172 yards. His biggest completion was a 58-yard catch-and-run by tight end Darnell Washington that set up a Jaylen Warren touchdown. The transcript this video is built on also says Rodgers threw no touchdown passes and one interception, and that Pittsburgh converted just four of eleven third downs. I couldn’t confirm the third-down number in the team’s recap, so take that one as reported. But the general picture is clear: the ground game did the heavy lifting, and the passing game was more of a supporting actor.

Rodgers knows it. After the game, he said: “Defense played excellent. Special teams did fine. But offense should have had a lot more points.”

That’s the backdrop. A team good enough to win now, with one obvious hole. And a deadline that won’t wait.

FORK NUMBER ONE: THE HOME-RUN SWING — BRIAN THOMAS JR.

Let’s start with the biggest name and the biggest price tag.

Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jacksonville Jaguars is the most explosive talent of the three. He’s a vertical threat who can flip a game with one route. And according to Fowler, there’s a sense around the league that his time in Jacksonville is running out. Executives believe a move is possible as the deadline approaches.

The transcript notes that Jacksonville has other receivers it can lean on, naming Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers. That’s part of why a trade even makes sense from the Jaguars’ side.

Here’s the catch. According to Fowler and Tom Pelissero, Jacksonville wanted to hold on to Thomas at least through its Week 9 game against Baltimore. That means the Jaguars were in no rush, and teams that aren’t in a rush usually don’t take discounts.

And the numbers this season have been rough. Through seven games, Thomas had ten catches for one hundred seventeen yards and two touchdowns. NBC Sports’ trade tracker mentioned this weekend that he’s been talking openly about a rough stretch. That’s not the production of a player you’d normally pay a premium for. But teams aren’t buying the 2026 version. They’re buying the talent.

So what’s the case for? If you believe a change of scenery unlocks the player he was as a rookie, he could be a true number-one receiver for years. And he’s still on his rookie contract, which makes him valuable beyond this season.

And the case against? The cost. Thomas would almost certainly require Day 2 draft capital, the kind of pick Pittsburgh has every reason to protect with a quarterback decision looming after this season. Kansas City has also been linked to him, and Pro Football Talk’s Mike Florio reported earlier this month that Downs is actually the Chiefs’ “Plan B, if they can’t get Thomas away from the Jaguars.”

Read that again. If Kansas City lands Thomas, the competition for Downs gets lighter. If Kansas City misses on Thomas, it could come hard after Downs. Pittsburgh’s decision might depend on what Kansas City does first.

FORK NUMBER TWO: THE SMART SWING — JOSH DOWNS

Now let’s talk about the name that has dominated Steelers conversations for two weeks.

Josh Downs requested a trade out of Indianapolis, according to Fowler. The Colts are rebuilding on the fly. They fired general manager Chris Ballard, then traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth, and sent DeForest Buckner to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth. Downs is twenty-five, in the last year of his rookie deal, and set to become a free agent after the season.

Here’s the timeline. On November 1, according to Yahoo’s tracker, Adam Schefter reported the Colts wanted a third-round pick for Downs. On November 2, Jordan Schultz named three teams to watch: the Steelers, the Chiefs and the Commanders. On November 5, Ian Rapoport put the Colts’ preference at a third or a fourth. And on November 7, Schefter and Rapoport reported the Colts had told other teams they’d received a fourth-round offer, without naming the team. NBC’s tracker summarized it simply: the Colts want more than a fourth.

That’s the market in a sentence. There’s a fourth on the table. Indianapolis is hoping someone blinks and goes higher.

What about the player? Downs has thirty catches for three hundred thirty yards and a touchdown through eight games this season, according to Heavy. His best year was 2024, with seventy-two catches for eight hundred three yards and five touchdowns. He works from the slot, he gets open quickly, and he catches the ball in traffic. That’s precisely the skill set Rodgers needs on third down when the defense rolls coverage toward Metcalf.

As for Sunday’s game, the Colts hosted Dallas, and the box score I checked late in the fourth quarter showed the Cowboys in control. I couldn’t confirm the final score or Downs’ stat line before recording, so I won’t throw out a number. But whatever happened, the Colts’ path is the same: a team at the bottom of its division with a receiver who asked to leave.

So, the case for? He’s productive, cheap to acquire, cheap to pay for the rest of the year, and a natural fit. Local insider Mark Kaboly wrote that Downs “seems like a perfect fit in terms of availability and price.”

And the case against? He’s a rental unless Pittsburgh can extend him. If the Steelers give up a meaningful pick and he walks in March, that’s a lot to pay for ten weeks of football. And there’s the pick problem: Pittsburgh’s own 2027 fourth-round pick already belongs to Dallas from the Broderick Jones trade. So matching a “fourth” means a 2028 pick or some creative swapping.

FORK NUMBER THREE: THE SAFETY NET — CALVIN RIDLEY

The third name is the one nobody gets excited about, but every front office keeps in its back pocket.

Calvin Ridley of the Tennessee Titans is a veteran with a long track record. But his role in Tennessee has shrunk. Through this point in the season, he’s caught just six passes for ninety-one yards.

Fowler put it plainly: Ridley “is considered a backup plan for a few teams in the receiver market.” And he added that “the Titans would need a quality Day 3 pick to part with Ridley.”

So the case for Ridley is that he’s experienced, he knows how to run a route tree, and he might cost less than a premium pick. If the Steelers miss on Downs, he’s a player who could step in and contribute without a long learning curve.

The case against is just as real. His production this year has been minimal, he’s on the back end of his career, and his remaining contract money could complicate a deal. For a team that wants to protect its future picks and its cap flexibility, that’s a lot of baggage for a backup plan.

THE NAME THAT’S OFF THE BOARD: GARRETT WILSON

Quick note for anyone still hoping for Garrett Wilson. According to NBC Sports’ tracker, the Jets reportedly aren’t open to trading him, and Wilson has said he wants to stay. Combine that with the concussion protocol he was in earlier this week, and you can take that name off your list.

THE DECISION TREE

So let’s put it all together the way Omar Khan probably is right now.

Branch one: Is Brian Thomas Jr. available at a price that doesn’t touch Day 2 capital? If yes, he becomes a long-term option worth a serious look. If no, and that’s the most likely answer, move on.

Branch two: Can Pittsburgh land Josh Downs for a fourth-round-level pick, ideally from 2028? If yes, do it. That’s the sweet spot. If the Colts demand a third, ask for a pick back. If they won’t budge, walk away.

Branch three: If both fall through, is Calvin Ridley available for a late-round pick with Tennessee covering some of the money? If yes, it’s a reasonable insurance policy. If not, stand pat.

And branch four, the one nobody likes: do nothing. Trust Warren, trust the defense, trust the development of Germie Bernard, and wait for Pittman and Wilson to return. That’s not a disaster. It’s a choice. But it does mean the passing game would have to grow on its own.

THE BRISKER STORY: A REPORTED FINE

Now let’s move to the rest of the news, starting with Jaquan Brisker.

According to the transcript, the NFL fined Brisker seventeen thousand nine hundred eleven dollars for his hit on Deshaun Watson in the Browns game. I couldn’t find the league’s fine report confirming it, so let’s treat that as reported. What I can tell you is that the amount lines up with the standard figure for a first roughing-the-passer offense this season. A Yardbarker report on a different player earlier this year cited that exact number for the same type of penalty.

Here’s the play. On Cleveland’s third snap of the game, Brisker hit Watson high and was flagged for roughing the passer, giving the Browns a fresh set of downs. Watson was evaluated for a concussion, passed and returned for the next drive.

Brisker was not happy about the call. According to ClutchPoints, he said: “Babying the quarterback. He’s over there crying or whatever he’s doing. It is what it is.”

Look, I understand the frustration. Brisker plays fast and physical, and that’s part of what makes this defense scary. But here’s the balance. A fifteen-yard penalty on the opening drive is a gift to the offense. Against a team like Cincinnati, gifts like that turn into points. The Steelers don’t need Brisker to play softer. They need him to stay aggressive while keeping his aim low.

THE INJURY WATCH: WHO COULD BE COMING BACK

Now, the injury report, because the bye week was supposed to be a chance to get healthier.

Let’s start with Gennings Dunker. The transcript spells his name “Jennings,” and even some reports have used both spellings, so let me clarify: it’s Gennings Dunker, the rookie guard out of Iowa, taken in the third round. The Steelers placed him on injured reserve on September 26 with an MCL sprain suffered in practice, and the expected recovery was four to six weeks. We’re now well beyond that window, which means he could be close. Getting him back would add depth to an offensive line that’s been playing well together.

Next, Derrick Harmon. The young defensive lineman is on injured reserve, and his return would be a real boost to the front. Jack Sawyer reportedly sounded excited about getting Harmon back during a radio appearance.

And then there’s Sebastian Joseph-Day, who went on injured reserve on October 22 with a triceps injury. According to the transcript, Sawyer’s comments suggested Joseph-Day might not return this season. One correction: the transcript calls Sawyer a rookie. He isn’t. He’s a second-year player, drafted in the fourth round in 2025. And there’s been no official word from the team that Joseph-Day is done for the year. Until the Steelers say otherwise, his status is uncertain.

The broader point the transcript makes is the right one. Players coming off injured reserve have to be medically cleared and activated before they can play. Eligibility isn’t the same as availability. But if even two of these guys come back for the stretch run, that’s like adding players at the deadline without spending a pick.

THE OFFENSE: WHAT THE NUMBERS ACTUALLY SAY

Let’s go deeper on the offense, because this is the heart of the receiver decision.

The run game is elite. Jaylen Warren ran for 143 yards on 17 carries against Cleveland, tying his career high, and scored a touchdown. Rico Dowdle added 56 yards, including a 9-yard touchdown run. Warren goes into the second half of the season with 998 scrimmage yards, second in the NFL, according to Steelers.com. Pittsburgh ranks third in the league at 4.9 yards per carry.

The pass game is a different story. Rodgers’ 172 yards against the Browns came largely on a few big plays, including Washington’s 58-yard catch-and-run. Metcalf had one catch for four yards on six targets in that game, according to Steelers.com’s Asked and Answered. Bernard caught all five of his targets, but for only thirty yards.

And this matters for the playoffs. The transcript makes a smart point: eventually, the Steelers will face a team that can stop the run. When that happens, the passing game has to be able to carry the offense for a drive, a quarter, maybe an entire half. Right now, it’s not clear that it can.

That’s why this receiver decision matters so much. It’s not about adding flash. It’s about adding a reliable option for the moments when Warren gets bottled up.

WHAT THE BYE WEEK SHOULD HAVE FIXED

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio said before the bye that much of the passing game’s inconsistency comes down to timing. His words: “A lot of it is within the time clock of the quarterback’s feet.” In other words, Rodgers’ drop and the receivers’ breaks need to line up perfectly.

The bye week was the staff’s chance to work on exactly that. Whether a new receiver arrives or not, I’d expect to see more quick-rhythm throws, more designed touches for Bernard, and more situations where Metcalf catches the ball in stride rather than fighting for fifty-fifty balls.

If a new receiver does arrive, the ramp-up would have to be quick. A few days of practice before Cincinnati. A small package of routes. A bigger role by December.

THE BENGALS PREVIEW: WHY NOVEMBER 15 IS SO BIG

Now let’s look ahead to Cincinnati.

The Steelers visit the Bengals on Sunday, November 15. The transcript bills it as a Sunday Night Football matchup. I’d double-check the official kickoff time before you set your alarm, but a prime-time spot would fit what’s at stake: first place in the division against the team right behind it.

Pittsburgh won the first meeting back in Week 3. But the Bengals have been dangerous offensively. Before the Steelers’ bye, Cincinnati beat Tennessee in overtime in a shootout, and Joe Burrow was involved in six touchdowns while throwing for more than four hundred seventy yards. That’s the kind of offense that can put pressure on Pittsburgh to score more than it did against Cleveland.

So what does Pittsburgh need to win?

First, pressure. This defense had eight sacks against the Browns. T.J. Watt had two, Patrick Queen and Cam Heyward had a sack and a half each, and Nick Herbig and Kevin Jobity Jr. had one apiece. If the Steelers can make Burrow uncomfortable, they control the game.

Second, discipline. No free yards. No opening-drive roughing penalties. Cincinnati is too good to give extra chances to.

Third, a passing game that can finish drives. Against a team that can score in bunches, field goals won’t be enough. The Steelers will need to turn red-zone trips into touchdowns. And that’s where a new receiver, or a better-used Metcalf and Bernard, could make all the difference.

HOW A DEADLINE MOVE CHANGES THE CINCINNATI MATCHUP

Let’s imagine two versions of the Steelers walking into that game.

Version one, no trade. The Steelers lean on Warren and Dowdle, mix in play-action, and hope Metcalf and Bernard can win enough one-on-one matchups. It can work. It worked against Cleveland. But against a better offense, the margin for error shrinks.

Version two, Downs arrives. Now Cincinnati’s nickel corner has a real problem in the slot. The safety can’t cheat toward Metcalf as freely. Bernard sees softer coverage. And Rodgers has an answer on third-and-medium that doesn’t require a perfect throw into tight coverage. Even with only a few practices, Downs could take a handful of key snaps and change how the Bengals have to defend.

That’s the difference a smart deadline move can make. Not a revolution. A few extra first downs in the moments that decide close games.

WHAT TO WATCH IN THE NEXT 30 HOURS

Let’s keep this practical.

Monday: Schultz’s timeline said the Colts would revisit offers after the Dallas game, with deals possible Monday or Tuesday. If Pittsburgh is serious about Downs, Monday is when the calls get more serious.

Kansas City’s move: if the Chiefs land Brian Thomas Jr., the path to Downs might get easier for Pittsburgh. If they miss on Thomas, expect them to push hard for Downs.

Washington: the Commanders were also named as a team to watch on Downs. They’re another bidder that could drive the price up.

Tuesday afternoon: deadline deals often come together in the final hours. If the news doesn’t drop Monday, don’t panic.

MY TRADE BLOCK BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board, and remember: this is opinion, not a report. I’m not claiming any of these conversations are happening.

Josh Downs: yes, for a 2028 fourth-round pick. Yes, for a fifth if it’s somehow possible. If Indianapolis insists on a third, only if a pick comes back the other way. A straight third for a player on an expiring deal, I walk.

Brian Thomas Jr.: pass at a Day 2 price. Too much capital with a quarterback decision coming. If his price somehow falls into Day 3 territory in the final hours, I’d at least pick up the phone.

Calvin Ridley: backup plan only. A late-round pick, and Tennessee covers some of the salary. Otherwise, pass.

Garrett Wilson: off the board. The Jets aren’t selling.

Jaquan Brisker: no change in role. Keep the aggression, lower the target.

The core: untouchable. Highsmith, Watt, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers haven’t made their receiver decision yet, but they’re going to have to make it in the next day and a half. Brian Thomas Jr. is the home-run swing that probably costs too much. Calvin Ridley is the safety net nobody wants to use. And Josh Downs sits right in the middle, the smart swing that fits the roster, the timeline and the budget.

Whatever Khan decides, it’ll shape the November 15 trip to Cincinnati and the rest of this season. A first-place team with an elite run game and a nasty defense is one reliable receiver away from being a really tough out in January.
