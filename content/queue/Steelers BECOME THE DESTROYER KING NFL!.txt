Steelers Nation, there’s a word I keep coming back to after watching Sunday’s game in Tampa, and that word is destroyer. Not the offense. Not yet. I’m talking about a Pittsburgh defense that sacked the quarterback five times, took the ball away twice, held the Buccaneers to 224 total yards, and refused to let a 16-3 deficit turn into a loss. This is a unit that is starting to wreck game plans, and it’s doing it in back-to-back weeks. Now, let me be honest with you right at the top. The Steelers are not the king of the NFL today. They’re 4-2, tied with Cleveland and Baltimore in the AFC North, and there’s real work to do on offense. But if you’re asking which part of this team is starting to play like royalty, the answer is obvious. So today we’re going to break down why this defense has become the destroyer, the players leading the charge, what Mike McCarthy said on Monday about the offense, and why the next two weeks could decide who rules the division. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The destroyer numbers.

Let’s start with what the defense actually did to Tampa Bay, because the numbers are loud. Jalon Daniels completed just 22 of his 43 passes for 164 yards, and once you take away the yardage lost on five sacks, the Bucs’ net passing total was barely over 120 yards. Bucky Irving, Tampa’s lead back, carried the ball 19 times and managed only 46 yards. The Bucs went 6-for-19 on third down. And the defense came up with two interceptions, both in the fourth quarter, both turning directly into points.

That’s not a defense hanging on. That’s a defense taking something away from you on every snap. And remember, this came one week after the same group held the Colts to 4-of-15 on third down and didn’t allow a single passing touchdown. Two straight weeks, two straight offenses that couldn’t stay on the field. When you hear people say a defense is trending, this is what it looks like.

The only real leak on Sunday was Daniels himself. When he pulled the ball down and ran, he hurt Pittsburgh, picking up 39 yards on just four carries. That’s the one thing Patrick Graham will circle in the film room this week. When everybody does the coverage part right and the quarterback still escapes the pocket, you’re giving back yards you already earned. Rush lanes have to stay disciplined, especially against a mobile passer.

Jalen Ramsey: the destroyer-in-chief.

Now let’s talk about the man at the center of all of it. Jalen Ramsey, playing through a broken wrist, delivered one of the best all-around games of his Pittsburgh career. According to the box score, he finished with 10 tackles, nine of them solo, two sacks, four tackles for loss, and two quarterback hits. And then he finished the game himself, coming on a blitz on fourth-and-8 in overtime and taking Daniels down to end it.

Think about how unusual that stat line is for a defensive back. Four tackles for loss is a big day for an edge rusher. Ramsey did it while also handling coverage responsibilities in the slot, with a cast on his wrist. That versatility is exactly why Graham can be so creative with this defense. When the offense doesn’t know whether Ramsey is dropping into coverage or coming off the edge, the protection has to account for him on every single snap. That’s a nightmare for any offensive coordinator.

And the bigger point is this: Ramsey isn’t just making plays, he’s setting the tone. When a veteran with his resume is willing to play through an injury like that and still throw his body at running backs and quarterbacks, the rest of the defense notices.

Nick Herbig: finishing the job.

Right next to Ramsey on the stat sheet is Nick Herbig. He finished with five solo tackles, two sacks, three tackles for loss, and two quarterback hits. And here’s what I love about Herbig’s game on Sunday. Pressure is nice, but pressure that doesn’t end in a sack often ends with a quarterback throwing the ball away and trying again on the next down. Herbig finished. He put the Bucs behind the chains, and that’s a big reason Tampa’s third-down numbers looked the way they did.

With Ramsey, Herbig, and Black combining for all five sacks, offenses can no longer slide their protection toward one guy and feel safe. That’s how a pass rush becomes a destroyer. You can’t take everybody away.

The secondary that turned the game.

Then there’s the back end. Jamel Dean, playing his first game since his ankle injury against the team that drafted him, broke up six passes. According to the Steelers’ official site, that’s a franchise record. He also came up with the 40-yard interception return to the Tampa 3-yard line late in the fourth quarter. McCarthy gave him a game ball on Monday, and Dean served as a captain for the week. McCarthy explained why those moments matter: “It goes a long way in the locker room.”

It’s also worth remembering how the playing time worked out for Dean. He was coming back from an ankle problem and was testing it early in the game, so the rotation with rookie Daylen Everette wasn’t just a coaching preference. There was a health factor involved, and that matters when you evaluate the snap counts.

And speaking of Everette, his afternoon tells you a lot about this young defense. Tampa Bay went right after him when he came in. He was flagged for pass interference and had a couple of rough moments in coverage. Plenty of rookies would have let that snowball. Instead, Everette came back and picked off Daniels for the first interception of his career, after Cam Heyward got pressure on the throw. That turnover led to a field goal and helped flip the momentum of the entire fourth quarter. Is there still stuff to clean up? Absolutely. But a young corner bouncing back like that is exactly what you want to see.

The linebackers did their part too. Payton Wilson led the way with 11 tackles and was all over the field against the run. Patrick Queen added three tackles. When the linebackers are flowing to the ball and the edge rushers are winning, a running back like Irving has nowhere to go. That’s why Tampa’s rushing attack never got going.

The veteran glue.

And don’t overlook the role Cam Heyward played in all of this. His pressure set up Everette’s interception, and that one play changed the fourth quarter. Heyward doesn’t need a sack to wreck a drive. When he collapses the pocket from the inside, the quarterback can’t step up, and that pushes him right into the path of edge rushers like Herbig. That’s how a pass rush works as a unit. One player creates the problem, and another player cashes it in.

That’s also why this defense feels different from the version we saw in September. Back then, Pittsburgh would get pressure and still give up the big play because somebody in coverage was a step late. Now the pressure and the coverage are working on the same clock. The quarterback has to hold the ball, the rush gets there, and the turnover follows. When that happens three or four times a game, you don’t just slow an offense down. You break it.

Why this defense could keep getting better.

Here’s the part that should get Steelers Nation excited. This is Graham’s first year running this defense, and the communication mistakes that hurt this group early in the season are shrinking week by week. The results against Indianapolis and Tampa weren’t flukes built on one or two lucky bounces. They were built on pressure, tackling, and disciplined coverage over four full quarters, and in Tampa’s case, an overtime period too.

And there may be reinforcements coming. DeShon Elliott is still on injured reserve, and his 21-day practice window is nearly up, so the Steelers have to make a decision on him soon. When McCarthy was asked about Elliott on Monday, he said: “I hope so. I thought he had a great week last week.” If Elliott comes back, you’re adding a hard-hitting veteran safety and a strong communicator to a defense that’s already rolling.

The injury the destroyer has to absorb.

Now the bad news. Brandin Echols injured his hamstring while working on the punt return unit late in the first half and didn’t return. As of Monday, the team hasn’t released a timeline, and one report suggested he could be out through the Week 9 bye. Echols has been the backup slot corner, the dime cornerback, and a core special teamer, so his absence touches a lot of different units.

That’s where the depth Omar Khan built comes in. Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith, the two corners claimed off practice squads earlier this month, were both inactive in Tampa. If Echols is out, at least one of them could be in uniform in Paris. A destroyer defense doesn’t stay that way by accident. It stays that way because the next man is ready.

The offense: what McCarthy said on Monday.

Now, as great as the defense was, we have to be honest about the other side of the ball, because a defense this good shouldn’t have to play from 16-3 down. And McCarthy didn’t dodge it on Monday.

He said the Steelers left roughly four or five sets of downs on the field against Tampa Bay, and he made it clear that third down is a priority this week. In his words: “We left some out there. That’s where we’ll actually start a little early tonight. We have to convert on third downs.” According to Athlon Sports, Pittsburgh ranked 28th in the league in third-down conversion rate heading into the week. That has to improve.

He also called running the ball more “clearly a focus.” And when you look at Sunday’s numbers, it’s easy to see why. Jaylen Warren carried 14 times for 50 yards, including a 16-yard run. Rico Dowdle carried just seven times, but he picked up 40 yards, almost six yards per carry. Pittsburgh finished with only 22 rushing attempts while Aaron Rodgers threw the ball 49 times behind a line that allowed five sacks.

That balance has to change. Not because running fixes everything, but because you can’t ask a 42-year-old quarterback to keep throwing into pressure while you have a back giving you almost six yards a carry. Give Dowdle five or six more touches. Make the defense prove it can stop him. And McCarthy clearly still loves his starter, saying: “I think Jaylen’s one of the hottest players in the National Football League as we stand here.”

The goal-line debate.

And that brings us to the sequence everybody’s still talking about. After Dean’s interception set Pittsburgh up at the Tampa 3-yard line late in the fourth quarter, the Steelers threw incomplete to Darnell Washington, got Warren closer on a run, threw incomplete again, and settled for Boswell’s 20-yard field goal. That made it 19-16, but it left the door open for Tampa to tie it with a 53-yarder with eight seconds left.

A lot of fans wanted another handoff after the run got the Steelers that close. McCarthy defended the calls on Monday, saying: “I get the post evaluation, but those are solid plays.” And Rodgers has said the problem was execution, not the calls.

Here’s my take. Both of those things can be true. The plays may have been sound on paper. But when your running game just moved you closer to the goal line and your defense has been dominant all day, there’s a strong case for making the opponent stop the run one more time. The Steelers won anyway, but in a playoff race this tight, those points matter.

Protection is the other piece. Rodgers was sacked five times, and several analysts singled out the tackle play, including Troy Fautanu, as an area that needs more consistency. I’m not calling for a lineup change from one game. Some sacks come from coverage, some from the quarterback holding the ball, some from missed assignments. But if the protection doesn’t improve, the coaches will have to look honestly at every option, including Dylan Cook, who has stepped in at tackle before and held his own.

The bright spots on offense.

Now, I don’t want the positives to get lost, because there were real ones. Once the fourth quarter arrived, Rodgers and his receivers found a rhythm. DK Metcalf finished with 11 catches for 93 yards, including a huge third-and-13 conversion in overtime. Darnell Washington caught a touchdown. Warren added two catches for 28 yards. And Rodgers threw his 539th career regular-season touchdown pass, tying Peyton Manning for third all-time.

And then there’s Germie Bernard. The rookie also caught 11 passes, for 87 yards, and scored the game-winning touchdown in overtime, the first regular-season touchdown of his career. McCarthy gave him a game ball too, and said: “That speaks volumes about where he is on his climb and his contribution to the offense.”

Am I handing Bernard Offensive Rookie of the Year after two big games? No. But if he keeps producing like this, he’ll put himself in that conversation. And that creates an interesting decision once Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson return from injured reserve. My view is simple: if Bernard is catching the ball and moving the chains, he keeps his snaps. You find room for production. You don’t take it away.

Trade block: what could make the destroyer even scarier?

Let’s turn to the trade deadline. This is my opinion, not a report.

The trade deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday after the Week 9 bye. On defense, I don’t think Khan needs a major move. The pass rush is producing, the secondary has depth with Murphy-Bunting and Smith, and Elliott could be back soon. If anything, a veteran slot or dime corner could make sense if the Echols injury lingers, but that’s a depth move, not a blockbuster.

The offense is where the real question is. With Pittman and Wilson out until after the bye at the earliest, a reliable veteran receiver could keep Rodgers from forcing the ball to Metcalf and Bernard. Jauan Jennings is the name I keep coming back to after Pro Football Talk reported Minnesota could move him. There’s no report connecting him to Pittsburgh. Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland has also been floated by NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala, though a trade inside the division would be rare. And Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the deadline. Again, none of this is confirmed. But a defense playing at this level deserves an offense that can match it.

The race for the AFC North crown.

Now let’s talk about the crown, because this is where the title gets real. After Week 6, the Steelers, Browns, and Ravens are all 4-2. Cleveland beat Baltimore on a field goal as time expired on Sunday, which gave the Browns a 2-0 division record and sole possession of first place for now. Pittsburgh sits second at 1-1 in the division. Baltimore is third. Cincinnati is 3-2 after its bye.

So when someone says the Steelers are the king of the NFL, here’s the honest answer: they’re not even king of the AFC North yet. But they control their own path. And the schedule is about to give them a chance to take the throne.

Paris, then Cleveland.

First up is the New Orleans Saints in Paris on Sunday, October 25th, at the Stade de France. Kickoff is at 9:30 in the morning Pittsburgh time. The Steelers will fly out after Thursday’s practice. McCarthy, who has never coached an international game, explained the decision: “We looked at both options, and we clearly feel that this one, leaving Thursday, is the best one for us.”

There’s also been concern about protests in France. The NFL said last week that “preparations continue as scheduled for NFL events and the game at Stade de France on October 25,” and that it’s working with local authorities on security. So as of now, the game is on.

And then, seven days later, the Browns come to town. A team that beat Pittsburgh earlier this season, now sitting atop the division. If the Steelers can handle the travel, take care of New Orleans, and then beat Cleveland, they could be sitting alone in first place heading into the bye. That’s the two-week stretch that could turn this destroyer defense into the foundation of a division champion. And with an early kickoff, jet lag, and a hungry Browns team waiting at home, nothing about it will be easy.

The bottom line.

So, did the Steelers become the destroyer of the NFL? The defense is earning that name. Five sacks, two interceptions, a franchise-record six passes defended from Dean, a monster day from Ramsey, and two straight weeks of shutting down third downs. That’s a unit that can carry a team through January.

But a king needs a complete team. That means more runs for Dowdle and Warren, cleaner protection for Rodgers, better third-down execution, and finishing drives when you’re sitting at the 3-yard line. McCarthy said it himself: they left some out there. If the offense catches up to this defense, watch out.
