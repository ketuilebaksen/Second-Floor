Steelers Nation, Mike Tomlin has finally told us why he walked away. Not through a press release. Not through a statement from the team. He told Cam Heyward, on Cam’s own podcast, and what he said is one of the most honest things a head coach has ever said about leaving a job.

Now, let me be straight with you about the headline. There’s no new “bombshell decision” from Tomlin this week. He made his decision back in January, the day after the playoff loss to Houston. What’s new is the explanation. For the first time, Tomlin walked through the exact moment he knew, the reasons behind it, and who he was thinking about when he made the call. And the answer to that last part is the two men who still anchor this defense: Cam Heyward and T.J. Watt.

The timing makes it even more interesting. Tomlin now works for NBC, on the studio show that previews Sunday Night Football. And this Sunday night, the game is Steelers at Bengals.

So today, we’re going to go through everything Tomlin said, and the emotional moment Heyward shared from that January morning. We’ll look at Tomlin’s full legacy with the right numbers, because the transcript gets one of them badly wrong. Then we’ll get to the rest of the week: Heyward’s response to the quiet trade deadline, and a young receiver Aaron Rodgers just mentioned by name who almost nobody is talking about. And my board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

WHERE TOMLIN SAID IT

The conversation happened on “Not Just Football,” the podcast Cam Heyward hosts. Steeler Nation dates the episode to Thursday, November 12. Several outlets picked it up the same day, including Sportsnet Pittsburgh and For The Win.

Think about that setting for a second. This wasn’t a sit-down with a national reporter. It was a former head coach talking to the captain he coached for more than a decade, a player who’s still in the locker room he left behind. That’s why it feels so different from a typical farewell interview. Tomlin wasn’t explaining himself to the public. He was explaining himself to one of his guys.

THE MOMENT HE KNEW

Let’s start with the timeline.

The Steelers’ season ended with a 30-6 wild-card loss to the Houston Texans. According to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, Tomlin told Heyward that he was ready to resign “by the time I pulled in my driveway.”

That’s a stunning detail. Nineteen years in the job, and the decision crystallized in the length of a drive home. The transcript describes it the same way: it came together very quickly. And Steeler Nation reports that he stepped down the next day.

Now, quick decisions sometimes get described as emotional or impulsive. But listening to Tomlin’s reasoning, it doesn’t sound impulsive at all. It sounds like the final step in something he’d been thinking about for a long time, and the Houston loss simply removed any doubt.

“19 YEARS OF BAGGAGE”

Here’s the heart of his explanation.

Tomlin talked about the weight that builds up over a long tenure. According to Steeler Nation, he said: “There’s a cumulative component to coaching.” And according to For The Win, he described himself as carrying 19 years of “baggage” by the end.

What did he mean by baggage? Not personal problems. He meant the accumulated history every long-tenured coach drags into the building. Every playoff loss. Every narrative. Every “same old Steelers” headline. Tomlin’s point, as For The Win summarized it, is that players end up helping a coach carry all of that, whether they want to or not.

And he didn’t want his players carrying it anymore. Sportsnet Pittsburgh reports that he believed the team could still compete for a title, but that his “bags might slow that down.”

He also said something I think every coach in this league should hear: “Football, man, is bigger than all of us, and I never took myself so seriously that I thought that I was immune from that.” And then, the line that sums up the whole decision: “I never wanted to stay too long.”

THE TAMPA LESSON

There’s a piece of Tomlin’s own history that explains a lot of this, and it’s one most fans don’t know.

Tomlin got his NFL start as a defensive backs coach in Tampa Bay under Tony Dungy in 2001. When Jon Gruden replaced Dungy in 2002, the Buccaneers kept Tomlin. And Gruden won the Super Bowl in his very first season.

Tomlin saw that up close. A talented veteran roster, a new voice, and an immediate championship. On the podcast, according to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, he said: “The optimism that comes with new regimes, I saw it in Tampa.”

And then he said he wanted that for his players, “even if it means it doesn’t happen with me.”

That’s the selfless part of this story. Tomlin isn’t saying the Steelers couldn’t win with him. He’s saying he’d watched a coaching change unlock a veteran team once before, and he wanted Heyward and Watt to have that same chance, even if it meant he wouldn’t be the one holding the trophy.

“I FEEL LIKE I FAILED YOU”

Now, here’s the moment from the podcast that hit me hardest. And it came from Heyward, not Tomlin.

According to Steeler Nation, Heyward described his reaction the day after the wild-card loss, when Tomlin told the team he was stepping down. Heyward said: “No doubt, I just remember one thing I said to him, and I was like, ‘I feel like I failed you.’”

Read that again. The coach is leaving because he feels his baggage might hold the players back. And the captain’s first instinct is to apologize to the coach for not winning enough for him.

Steeler Nation reports that Tomlin, on the same podcast, expressed the mirror image of that feeling. He felt he had let the veterans down through the lack of playoff success. So you had two men, each convinced he’d failed the other. That tells you everything about the relationship between them, and about how much those playoff losses weighed on everyone inside that building.

I’ll be honest, that exchange changes how I think about January. From the outside, a coaching change can look cold, like a business decision. This one wasn’t. It was two people who cared about each other trying to do right by each other.

TOMLIN’S LEGACY, WITH THE RIGHT NUMBERS

Let’s talk about the record, because the transcript has a typo here that I want to fix.

The transcript says Tomlin finished with a “193-14-2” regular-season record. That’s not right. The correct record is 193 wins, 114 losses and two ties. Those 193 wins tie Chuck Noll for the most in franchise history, according to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, and NBC’s announcement ranked them ninth all-time in the NFL.

Here’s the rest of the résumé, according to NBC Sports. Nineteen seasons, from 2007 through 2025. Thirteen postseason appearances. Never a losing season, a streak NBC describes as unmatched in NFL history. And a 27-23 win over Arizona in Super Bowl XLIII, which made him the youngest head coach ever to win a Super Bowl, at age 36.

The transcript also credits him with eight AFC North titles and two Super Bowl appearances. The second Super Bowl was the loss to Green Bay after the 2010 season. I’d treat the division-title count as the transcript’s number, since the NBC release I relied on doesn’t list it.

And then there’s the other side of the ledger. The Steelers’ last playoff win came in January 2017. According to Sportsnet Pittsburgh, they didn’t win a playoff game in any of Tomlin’s last nine seasons.

That’s the tension that defined the end of his tenure. The floor was always high. The ceiling, in the postseason, kept getting lower. And Tomlin himself seems to have recognized that the second part had become the story.

DID TOMLIN READ THE ROOM CORRECTLY?

So here’s the question everyone wants to ask: was he right?

Ten weeks into the McCarthy era, the early evidence is encouraging. The Steelers are six and two, alone in first place in the AFC North heading into Sunday night. The defense has been one of the best in football since Patrick Graham moved down to the sideline before the Week 5 game against Indianapolis. And according to the AP, Pittsburgh leads the league in sacks.

There are also signs of exactly the kind of fresh energy Tomlin was talking about. In the Browns game, according to the AP, Rodgers urged McCarthy to go for a touchdown from the 1-yard line at the end of the first half instead of kicking the field goal. McCarthy went for it, and Pat Freiermuth scored. Right guard Brock Hoffman credited the coaches for “showing belief” in the players. That’s what a new regime can feel like.

But let’s be fair. A 6-2 start isn’t new for this franchise. According to the AP, the Steelers have started 6-2 eight times since 2002. The real test of Tomlin’s theory isn’t November. It’s January. If this team wins a playoff game, Tomlin’s decision will look even wiser. If it doesn’t, the debate the transcript mentions, about whether his departure came at the right time, will keep going.

TOMLIN ON SUNDAY NIGHT

Now, here’s the subplot I can’t stop thinking about.

In April, NBC Sports announced that Tomlin was joining “Football Night in America” as a studio analyst. According to NBC’s press release, part of his job is to preview that night’s Sunday Night Football game. When he signed on, he said: “I’m humbled and honored to come alongside the best group in sports.” NBC’s Sam Flood put it simply: “When Mike Tomlin talks, people listen.”

This Sunday’s game is Steelers at Bengals.

I can’t tell you exactly what NBC has planned, or how much airtime Tomlin will get. But if the show follows its usual format, there’s a good chance Tomlin will be on national television breaking down his former team just days after explaining why he left it. That’s must-see TV, and I’ll be watching for every word.

THE TWO VETERANS TOMLIN HAD IN MIND

Let’s spend a minute on the players at the center of Tomlin’s reasoning, because both are playing like men who took his words personally.

T.J. Watt entered the bye with seven and a half sacks, tied for the league lead, according to the AP. That’s not a player winding down. That’s a player who looks re-energized, which is exactly what Tomlin hoped a new staff would bring out of him.

Heyward, meanwhile, is still the voice of that room. After the Browns win, he explained why this defense feels different: “When multiple guys can add to the party, it leads to ‘this can be sustainable.’” Pittsburgh’s sack total, by the AP’s count, came from Watt plus ten other players. That spread is a sign of a defense that isn’t relying on one star to survive.

And there’s a nice symmetry in who replaced Tomlin. Sportsnet Pittsburgh noted that the move positioned Mike McCarthy, a Pittsburgh native, to land his dream job. So the coach who left wanted a fresh start for his veterans, and the coach who arrived grew up in the city those veterans play for. If you were writing it as a movie, you’d probably think that was too neat.

HEYWARD’S DEADLINE MESSAGE

Let’s move from the past to the present, because Heyward made headlines this week for something else too.

The trade deadline came and went on Tuesday with no moves from Pittsburgh. The transcript lists several receivers fans had connected to the Steelers, including Garrett Wilson, Brian Thomas Jr., Josh Downs and Keenan Allen. Some of those were serious reported possibilities. Others were mostly fan and media speculation, so I’d treat the full list carefully.

Two of those names did move. Downs went to Kansas City. And according to the San Francisco Standard, the 49ers acquired Allen from Indianapolis for a 2027 sixth-round pick. Allen had 40 catches for 351 yards in eight games with the Colts.

That’s the backdrop for Heyward’s now-famous line, reported by ESPN’s Brooke Pryor: “Trades are for Madden.”

What I like about the quote, in light of the Tomlin podcast, is the consistency. Tomlin left because he believed this roster was good enough to compete. Heyward is now saying the exact same thing about the roster, from the other direction. The coach who left believes in the guys in the room. The captain who stayed believes in the guys in the room. That’s not a coincidence. That’s a culture.

The transcript also says Rodgers told reporters that, after so many years in the league, he’s learned not to hold his breath at the trade deadline. I couldn’t find that quote in its original form, so take it as the transcript’s paraphrase. But it would fit his tone all season: steady, unbothered, focused on who’s already here.

THE NAME ALMOST NOBODY IS TALKING ABOUT: MAX HURLEMAN

And that brings us to the most unexpected part of the week.

On Wednesday, Rodgers was asked about the receiver room. And instead of talking about the trade targets that didn’t arrive, he mentioned a player who has never appeared in a regular-season NFL game.

According to Heavy and Yahoo Sports, Rodgers said: “We’re obviously raised up a couple of our P-squad guys who are talented.” And then he added: “Still would love to get some opportunities for Max at some point whenever he comes off.”

Max is Max Hurleman. The transcript spells his name a few different ways, but it’s Hurleman.

Here’s who he is. According to Heavy, he’s an undrafted free agent who joined the Steelers after the 2025 draft. He’s a receiver and running back in his second NFL season. And according to Sports Illustrated’s Steelers site, he’s currently on the practice squad’s injured list and eligible to return.

SI also notes that Hurleman nearly made the 53-man roster in both the 2025 and 2026 training camps, and that his undersized frame may be what kept him off it. The transcript mentions a preseason touchdown against Jacksonville in 2025. I couldn’t confirm that touchdown, but SI does show him playing in that August 9, 2025 preseason game against the Jaguars.

WHY THE COACHES LOVE HIM

Here’s what makes this more than one quarterback being nice to a teammate. Mike McCarthy is just as high on him.

According to SI, McCarthy called Hurleman a hard worker with an “incredible workload capacity.” And he said Hurleman “truly performed to a level that he deserved a 53-man roster spot.” Steelers Depot ran a headline quoting McCarthy as saying, “How do you not love Max?” I wasn’t able to open that article, so I’m going by the headline only.

And Hurleman himself told SI he’s “been itching to play.”

So you’ve got a head coach who thinks he earned a roster spot, a four-time MVP quarterback asking for opportunities for him, and a player who’s dying to get on the field. That’s a combination worth watching.

RODGERS AND THE DEPTH GUYS

The transcript makes a good point here: this isn’t the first time Rodgers has gone to bat for a depth receiver.

Back in October, according to ClutchPoints, Rodgers said: “I’ve always been a fan of Brandon Johnson.” He also expressed belief in Brandon Smith, whom he knew from their time with the Jets. And earlier this week, he mentioned Marquez Valdes-Scantling, his old Green Bay teammate, who’s on the practice squad and hasn’t been elevated yet.

There’s a pattern. Rodgers trusts guys who show up and do the work, regardless of their résumé. That’s a valuable trait in a veteran quarterback, especially on a team that chose not to add a receiver at the deadline.

THE REALITY CHECK ON HURLEMAN

Now, let’s not get carried away, and the transcript is careful about this too.

Hurleman has to get healthy first. He’s on the practice squad’s injured list, so he can’t do anything until he’s activated. Then, even after he’s back, he’d most likely need to be elevated from the practice squad to play in a game. As the rules are generally understood, a practice squad player can only be elevated a limited number of times per season before the team has to sign him to the 53-man roster.

And the receiver room is about to get crowded. Roman Wilson becomes eligible to come off injured reserve after the Bengals game. Michael Pittman Jr. is working his way back, and McCarthy said Monday he’s “getting close.” DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, Ben Skowronek and the tight ends are already carrying the load.

So the honest answer is that Hurleman might not get a real role this season. But when your quarterback and head coach both single out an undrafted player by name, he’s a name worth knowing.

THE WEEK’S INJURY PICTURE

Before we get to Sunday, here’s a quick snapshot of where the roster stands, because it’s improving.

On defense, Derrick Harmon, the rookie who forced the late fumble in the first Bengals game, has had his practice window opened and was limited Wednesday and Thursday. He’s still on injured reserve, and according to Yahoo Sports, the team will need to clear a 53-man roster spot to activate him.

Safety DeShon Elliott moved from limited on Wednesday to full on Thursday with his ankle, according to Yahoo Sports and CBS Sports. Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter were full both days coming off hamstring injuries. Watt and Heyward were held out for rest on Thursday, which is routine.

So when we talk about Tomlin wanting Heyward and Watt to have their best shot at a title, here’s the encouraging part: both of them look healthy heading into one of the biggest games of the season.

SUNDAY NIGHT IN CINCINNATI

And that brings us to the game the transcript closes on.

Both teams are six and two. First place in the AFC North is on the line. Cincinnati is coming off a 34-17 win over Atlanta in Madrid, where Joe Burrow went 24 of 29 for 271 yards and three touchdowns. Pittsburgh won the first meeting, 30-27, back in Week 3.

The transcript’s closing line is a good one: talk is cheap in the NFL. Heyward said trades are for Madden. Rodgers said he trusts the guys in the room. Tomlin said this roster can win a championship. Sunday night, in front of a national audience, with their old coach likely watching from the NBC studio, they get to prove it.

WHAT I’LL BE LISTENING FOR

If Tomlin does break down this game on NBC, here are three things I’ll be listening for.

First, how he talks about Heyward and Watt. After that podcast, any comment about them will carry extra weight.

Second, whether he picks Pittsburgh. Analysts are supposed to be neutral, and Tomlin has always been careful with his words. But fans will read into every phrase.

Third, what he says about the offense. Tomlin coached against Burrow for years. Nobody on that set knows better how Cincinnati likes to attack Pittsburgh, and how Pittsburgh needs to respond.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Remember: this is opinion, not a report.

On Tomlin: I think he made the right call, for the right reasons, and I respect how openly he explained it. Leaving before you’re asked to leave is rare in this league.

On Heyward: “Trades are for Madden” and “I feel like I failed you” in the same week tells you who this guy is. He takes responsibility, and he backs his teammates. That’s a captain.

On Max Hurleman: activate him from the practice squad injured list as soon as he’s healthy. Give him reps on special teams and a package of three or four touches in the passing game. He’s earned a look.

On the receivers: Roman Wilson designated to return next week. Pittman on a slower track. No rush on either.

On Sunday night: feed Jaylen Warren, protect Rodgers, and if Harmon is activated, let him chase Burrow on obvious passing downs.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. There was no new Tomlin decision this week. But there was something better: the truth about the one he already made. He didn’t leave because he’d stopped believing in this team. He left because he believed in it so much that he was willing to get out of the way.

And the strangest part is that the man he was thinking about most, Cam Heyward, is now the one telling everybody that this roster is good enough. Tomlin believed it. Heyward believes it. Rodgers believes it. Sunday night in Cincinnati, we find out if they’re right.
