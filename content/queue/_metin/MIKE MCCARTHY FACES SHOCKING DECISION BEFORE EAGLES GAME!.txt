Steelers Nation, there’s a clock ticking in Pittsburgh right now, and Mike McCarthy is the one holding it.

By Saturday afternoon, this coaching staff has to settle a stack of questions that will shape Sunday in Philadelphia, and maybe the rest of the season. Who plays left guard. Which receivers come back. How a defense that just got shredded responds. And how a team that looks great at home and shaky on the road fixes the way it travels.

Let me be straight with you before we start. There’s no single shocking announcement out of the building today. Nobody got cut, nobody got benched, nobody got traded. The “shocking decision” here is really a set of them, and the deadline is coming fast. So today I’m going to walk you through the decision clock, day by day, and tell you where I’d land on each one.

And yes, at the end, the board. Opinion, not a report.

Let’s go.

FIRST, A QUICK CORRECTION ON THE SCORE

The video I’m working from today says the Steelers lost 38-8 in Cincinnati. That’s not right. The final was 38-18. It felt worse than 18, I’ll give you that, but let’s keep the record clean.

The yardage gap is real, though. According to the Northeast Times scouting report, Pittsburgh finished with 222 total yards, its lowest of the season. Cincinnati put up 484. That’s a 262-yard gap in a division game.

Joe Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns. Ja’Marr Chase caught 11 balls for 231 yards. And the Steelers were down 24-3 at halftime. When the score looks like that at the break, the second half isn’t really a football game. It’s damage control.

So that’s the starting point for every decision we’re about to talk about. McCarthy isn’t making these calls from a position of comfort. He’s making them a week after the worst performance of his first season in Pittsburgh.

WHY THERE’S A DEADLINE AT ALL

Here’s something worth explaining, because it’s why this week feels so urgent.

As it’s generally understood around the league, teams have until 4 p.m. Eastern the day before a game to finalize roster moves, things like activating a player off injured reserve or elevating someone from the practice squad. For a Sunday 1 p.m. kickoff, that means Saturday afternoon.

So every decision involving a player coming off IR has to be made by then. McCarthy can’t wait until Sunday morning to see how Roman Wilson’s hand feels in warmups. He has to decide based on what he sees Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.

That’s the clock. Now let’s go through it.

WEDNESDAY: THE DECISIONS ALREADY MADE

Some of the decisions are already behind us.

On Wednesday, McCarthy opened the 21-day practice windows for four players: receivers Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr., rookie guard Gennings Dunker, and practice squad receiver Max Hurleman. That starts the clock on each of them. Under the IR rules as generally understood, a team then has three weeks to activate that player, or he stays on injured reserve.

The other Wednesday decision was Mason McCormick going on injured reserve. And that one came with real relief, because NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported McCormick’s ankle was dislocated but not fractured, with no surgery needed and a possible return in the playoffs, according to WPXI.

That’s important for how McCarthy thinks about the rest of the week. He isn’t replacing McCormick for good. He’s bridging a gap. And when you’re bridging a gap, you make different choices than when you’re rebuilding.

DECISION ONE: WHO PLAYS LEFT GUARD

This is the biggest football decision of the week, and it’s the one the video I’m working from spends the most time on.

The video calls McCormick an important part of the unit, and that’s an understatement. He’d started every game this season before Cincinnati. He was a leader. And now there’s a hole on the left side of an offensive line that Aaron Rodgers depends on.

The obvious answer right now is Spencer Anderson. He finished the Bengals game at left guard after McCormick was carted off. The video says McCarthy has expressed confidence in Anderson, and that’s consistent with everything we heard earlier this week. Anderson is versatile, he knows the system, and he’s been around long enough to handle the speed of an NFL game.

But here’s the honest counterpoint. Jacob Punturi of Steelers On SI wrote this week that it’s unclear whether Anderson will be the starting left guard going forward. And he made the case for Dunker, writing: “Dunker isn’t a guarantee, but he does provide optimism and excitement.”

THE DUNKER CASE, AND THE RISK

Let’s be fair about what Dunker is and isn’t.

According to Punturi, Dunker hasn’t played a regular-season NFL snap. He played tackle in college at Iowa. He went on injured reserve before Week 3 with an MCL sprain suffered in practice. And in the preseason, he struggled against the pass rush but stood out as a run blocker.

That’s the whole trade-off in one sentence. If you want to keep running the ball the way this team wants to run it, Dunker’s strength matches the need. If you’re worried about Rodgers getting hit by Philadelphia’s pass rush, Dunker’s weakness is exactly where you’d worry.

And remember, he just got back on the practice field Wednesday. He’s been out about two months. Asking a rookie to go from rehab to starting in an NFL game inside four days is a big ask.

So my read is this: Anderson starts Sunday. Dunker gets real practice reps this week, and if he looks sharp, he gets a package or a rotation role. The full handover, if it happens, comes after Denver, not before Philadelphia.

WHY THE LINE MATTERS SO MUCH THIS WEEK

The video makes a point I want to build on. When protection breaks down, the quarterback has less time. When the running lanes disappear, the run game gets predictable. And when both happen together, the whole offense looks disconnected.

That’s exactly what Cincinnati looked like. Jaylen Warren, who’s been one of the best backs in football this season, ran seven times for 15 yards, according to the Northeast Times. Seven carries. For a player with 719 rushing yards on 126 carries this season, a 5.7 average, that’s not a game plan. That’s a game script that got away from you.

And the sacks matter too. Pittsburgh has allowed 26 sacks this season, which ranks in the bottom third of the league, according to the same report. Rodgers is 42. Every hit he takes counts.

So whoever plays left guard isn’t just filling a spot. He’s protecting the one player this season can’t afford to lose.

THURSDAY: THE RECEIVER DECISIONS

Now the receivers, which the video calls potentially massive news.

Let’s start with what McCarthy actually said. On Monday, according to EssentiallySports, he was asked about Wilson and Pittman and said: “Working through that. Hopeful. Yeah, hopefully those guys can, we can get them out there and get them going.” He added that the team would have answers as it got ready for Wednesday’s practice.

And on Wednesday, the answers started coming. Wilson was a full participant, according to Yahoo Sports. That’s the best sign you can get on day one of a practice window.

DECISION TWO: ACTIVATE ROMAN WILSON

The video gets Wilson’s numbers right. Before he got hurt, he had 14 catches for 188 yards and two touchdowns in five games, according to EssentiallySports. He hurt his left hand against the Colts on October 11 and went on injured reserve on October 17.

The video makes a good point here: don’t judge him by raw numbers alone. His value is that he can move around, work against man coverage and create chances downfield. And man coverage is exactly what beat this offense in Cincinnati. Rodgers said so himself after the game.

Here’s the stat that tells you how badly they need him. In Cincinnati, Steelers receivers combined for five catches and 69 yards, according to EssentiallySports. DK Metcalf had 54 of those yards and a touchdown. Rookie Germie Bernard was held without a catch on three targets.

Five catches. From the whole receiver room. In a game where the team was chasing points all night.

That’s why Wilson’s activation isn’t really a hard call if he keeps practicing fully. The harder call is how much to use him. A player coming back from a hand injury after five weeks might not be ready for 60 snaps. But he doesn’t need 60 snaps to change how a defense lines up. He just needs to be a threat.

DECISION THREE: WHAT TO DO WITH PITTMAN

Pittman is the harder call.

According to EssentiallySports, he went on injured reserve on October 10 with a foot injury and has missed the last five games. He’s played in only three games this season, with seven catches for 89 yards. The good news is that he’s been running on a treadmill and outside in cleats, and stretching and warming up with the main group.

And he’s clearly frustrated. Last week, he said: “It’s been tough, the most injuries I’ve had to deal with in my seven years.” He also said he’s trying to get back as fast as possible and “prove to this team that I came here to better it.”

You have to respect that. He came over from Indianapolis in March to be part of this, and he’s barely gotten to play.

But here’s the decision McCarthy faces. Pittman’s injury is in the foot, and it’s already been aggravated once this season. Rush him back for Philadelphia, and you risk losing him again for the stretch run. Wait a week, and you get him for Denver and beyond.

And there’s a scheduling wrinkle that makes this even more interesting.

THE SHORT WEEK NOBODY’S TALKING ABOUT

After the Eagles game on Sunday, the Steelers host Denver on Friday, November 27, according to the Northeast Times. That’s the Black Friday game. Sunday to Friday is a short turnaround.

So this isn’t just about Sunday. Every snap a returning player takes in Philadelphia is a snap his body has to recover from in five days. That’s why I think McCarthy’s smartest move with Pittman is patience. Get Wilson up for Philadelphia. Target Denver for Pittman, if the foot cooperates. And manage everybody’s workload knowing a second game is coming fast.

DECISION FOUR: MAX HURLEMAN’S ROLE

One smaller decision, but a fun one.

Max Hurleman, the undrafted receiver Rodgers publicly lobbied for, had his practice window opened on Wednesday too. He’s been on the practice squad’s injured list since September.

He’s not going to rescue the receiver room. But he’s a returner by background, and he’s earned a lot of trust from this staff. A practice squad elevation for special teams is the realistic path. I’d love to see it, but I wouldn’t force it this week if the coaches want one more week of practice first.

FRIDAY: THE DEFENSIVE RESPONSE

Now the defense, which is where the video gets most pointed. And honestly, I agree with its tone.

You cannot give up 409 yards and four touchdowns through the air and feel okay about it. Defensive coordinator Patrick Graham didn’t pretend otherwise. According to the Northeast Times, his assessment was: “We got punched in the mouth.”

That’s honest. And I like that he said it out loud.

But here’s the thing I want you to remember, because it’s easy to forget after a game like that. Through Week 10, this defense is still allowing 20.2 points per game, seventh in the league, according to the Northeast Times. It’s allowing 83.2 rushing yards per game, third in the NFL. It has 27 sacks, tied for fifth. And it has 13 takeaways.

So Cincinnati wasn’t who this defense has been. The question McCarthy and Graham have to answer is whether it was an outlier, or whether Burrow and Chase just found the blueprint everybody else is going to copy.

DECISION FIVE: HOW TO HANDLE THE NEW INJURIES

That defensive response gets harder because of Wednesday’s injury report.

According to Yahoo Sports, outside linebacker Nick Herbig did not practice with a hip and ribs issue, and linebacker Patrick Queen was limited with a hand injury. Safety DeShon Elliott, who was hurt in warmups last week, was limited with his knee. And cornerback Jalen Ramsey missed practice for a personal reason, which isn’t something I’m going to speculate on.

I’m not going to guess how serious Herbig’s injury is. But I’ll tell you what the decision looks like. If Herbig can’t go, Graham has to generate pressure in other ways. That means more blitz looks, more movement, more snaps for the young edge rushers. And against Joe Milton, who according to the Northeast Times was sacked seven times in his only start this season, pressure should be there for the taking.

If Queen plays through a hand injury, that’s a decision about protection and padding. Linebackers do it all the time. The question is whether it affects his tackling. He had 14 tackles in Cincinnati, so he’s clearly not shy about contact.

DECISION SIX: THE SECONDARY

The other defensive decision is in the back end.

The Steelers have been looking at defensive backs this week. On Tuesday, according to NFL insider Jordan Schultz as reported by BVM Sports, they worked out former Chiefs cornerback Nazeeh Johnson. They’d already signed AJ Finley to the practice squad the same day.

That tells you the front office is thinking about depth. It doesn’t tell you anyone’s losing their job. And with Elliott and Ramsey both on the injury report Wednesday, depth matters more than usual this week.

My view: no major lineup shake-up in the secondary for Philadelphia. The Eagles’ passing game, with Milton at quarterback, isn’t the Bengals’ passing game. This is a week to clean up communication and get healthy, not to bench starters after one bad night.

SATURDAY: THE ROAD PROBLEM

Now here’s the decision I think matters most, and it’s one the video only touches on.

According to the Northeast Times, the Steelers are 4-0 at home and 2-3 on the road. Philadelphia will be their sixth road game.

And McCarthy has already identified this as a problem. His words, from the Northeast Times: “Being on the road, there are some things mechanically and operational that we were not on top of.”

That’s a coach admitting the team isn’t handling the travel side of the job well. Things like communication, the snap count, getting plays in on time, starting fast in a hostile building. Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio said the same thing in his own way, according to that report: the offense has struggled to find a rhythm early, especially on the road.

DECISION SEVEN: HOW TO START FAST IN PHILADELPHIA

So what does McCarthy actually decide here?

He has to script an opening that gets Rodgers comfortable early. In Cincinnati, Rodgers started 0-for-8, the first time in his 22-year career he’d opened a game that way. You can’t let that happen again, especially on the road.

That probably means easier throws early. Quick game. Play action, which has been the most efficient part of this passing offense all season. And a commitment to the run that doesn’t disappear the first time the team falls behind.

And here’s where the matchup helps. According to the Northeast Times, Philadelphia allows 126.1 rushing yards per game, 25th in the league. The Steelers average 4.8 yards per carry, tied for fourth. If there was ever a week to give Warren 20 carries, this is it.

WHAT THE EAGLES BRING

Let’s be fair to Philadelphia, because “2-7” doesn’t mean “easy.”

The Eagles have lost seven straight. They’re scoring 16.4 points per game, tied for 30th, according to the Northeast Times. They’ve allowed 30 sacks, 29th in the league. And they have just five takeaways all season, last in the NFL. Joe Milton is starting at quarterback with Jalen Hurts out for the year.

But they’re coming off a bye. They’ve had two weeks to prepare. And they’re at home, where the Steelers haven’t won a regular-season game since 1965, according to Steelers.com.

Desperate teams coming off a bye are dangerous. That’s exactly the kind of game a team like Pittsburgh can sleepwalk into, especially a week after a gut punch.

THE NUMBERS THAT SHOULD GIVE YOU CONFIDENCE

Let me give you the other side, because this week has been heavy.

Through ten weeks, according to the Northeast Times, the Steelers are plus-five in turnover margin, tied for sixth. Rodgers has 15 touchdowns and six interceptions with 2,014 yards. Warren has 30 catches for 320 yards on top of his rushing numbers. T.J. Watt has 7.5 sacks and a pick-six. Payton Wilson has 88 tackles. Jamel Dean has forced three fumbles.

This is still a 6-3 team. It’s still in the AFC playoff picture. One awful night in Cincinnati doesn’t erase nine weeks of football.

THE TIGHT END QUESTION

One more offensive decision worth flagging. With the receivers banged up, this offense has leaned heavily on its tight ends, according to EssentiallySports. Pat Freiermuth has five touchdown catches this season and Darnell Washington has three, according to the Northeast Times. That’s eight of Rodgers’ 15 touchdown passes going to tight ends.

If Wilson comes back, McCarthy gets to decide how much to shift that balance. My answer: not much. Keep the tight ends as the red zone weapons, and let Wilson stretch the field so those tight ends see lighter boxes and softer coverage underneath. You add a receiver without taking anything away from what already works.

THE LEADERSHIP DECISION

There’s one more decision that doesn’t show up on an injury report.

The video says McCarthy has to get this team focused, and that there’s no time for a week of excuses. I agree. And the way McCarthy handles this week will tell us a lot about him in Year 1.

He could overreact. Shake up the lineup, change the play-calling, make a statement move just to show he’s doing something. Or he could stay steady. Fix the operational problems, get healthy bodies back, lean on the identity that got this team to 6-2.

Everything we’ve heard from him this week points to the second path. He talked about getting players back. He identified specific road problems. He didn’t throw anyone under the bus. That’s a coach who believes his team is closer to the 6-2 version than the Cincinnati version.

WHY THIS GAME MATTERS SO MUCH

The video says this season isn’t over because of one loss, but the loss matters. That’s exactly right.

Here’s why Philadelphia matters more than a 2-7 opponent usually would. If the Steelers win, they’re 7-3 heading into a short week at home against Denver, with receivers returning and a chance to stack wins in December. If they lose, they’re 6-4, they’ve dropped two in a row, and suddenly every road game the rest of the way becomes a referendum.

And the AFC North race doesn’t wait. Cincinnati is 7-2. The Steelers are a game back, and the Bengals now hold the season split. Every week matters.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s how I’d handle McCarthy’s decision clock. Again, opinion, not a report.

Left guard: Spencer Anderson starts. Dunker gets practice reps and, if he looks sharp, a small rotation package. Full evaluation after Denver.

Roman Wilson: activate him by Saturday if he keeps practicing fully. Three to five targets, at least one shot downfield.

Michael Pittman Jr.: don’t rush him. The short week makes patience smarter. Aim for Denver.

Max Hurleman: elevate for special teams only if the coaches feel he’s ready. No pressure this week.

Pass rush: if Herbig can’t go, blitz Milton early and often. Make a quarterback who was sacked seven times in his only start feel it again.

Secondary: no benchings. Clean up communication, and if Nazeeh Johnson signs, he’s a special teams addition first.

Road operations: script the first 15 plays for rhythm. Quick game, play action and Warren. Get Rodgers a completion on the first drive.

Workload: manage snaps for everyone coming back, because Friday is coming fast.

FINAL WORD

Here’s where I land. Mike McCarthy doesn’t face one shocking decision this week. He faces seven, and none of them is flashy. But together, they’ll decide whether Cincinnati was a stumble or the start of a slide.

Start Anderson. Activate Wilson. Be patient with Pittman. Attack Joe Milton. Run the football. And fix the road operation that McCarthy himself said wasn’t good enough.

Do that, and the Steelers walk out of Philadelphia at 7-3 with momentum heading into Black Friday. Don’t, and this week’s questions turn into next week’s crisis.
