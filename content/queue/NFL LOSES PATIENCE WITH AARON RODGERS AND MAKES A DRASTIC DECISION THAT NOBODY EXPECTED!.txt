Steelers Nation, the NFL has made its decisions from that Thursday night in Cleveland, and if you’ve seen a headline saying the league “lost patience” with Aaron Rodgers, I need you to hear this first: the league did not punish Aaron Rodgers. There’s no suspension. There’s no fine with his name on it. In fact, the biggest decision the league made about Rodgers this week went in his favor. The NFL went back to the tape of that 27-24 loss, and it fined the Browns defender who took him down with an illegal tackle. It also fined a Steeler, and it’s not who you’d expect. And while all of that was happening, Rodgers answered every critic in the only way that matters, on the field. So today I’m going to walk you through every league decision from that game, what each one really means, how Rodgers has responded since the cameras caught him yelling on the sideline, and what it all means heading into Sunday in Tampa. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Decision number one: the NFL protected its 42-year-old quarterback.

Let’s start with the one that should matter most to Steelers fans. On October 10th, the league released its fines from Week 4, and it was a big week. According to The Big Lead, the NFL fined a season-high 10 players for “major” fouls. One of them was Cleveland Browns defensive tackle Adin Huntington.

Here’s what happened. With 9:28 left in the third quarter, on third-and-5, Huntington brought Rodgers down for a three-yard loss. That sack ended the drive and forced Pittsburgh to punt from its own 45-yard line. No flag was thrown on the play. But the league reviewed the tape afterward and ruled it an illegal swivel hip-drop tackle, the technique where a defender wraps up a ball carrier and then drops his hips and body weight onto the runner’s legs. The NFL fined Huntington 5,620 dollars.

And some context on that rule. The NFL banned the swivel hip-drop tackle before the 2024 season, and it’s clearly enforcing it hard now. In that same batch of Week 4 fines, the league fined several players for hip-drop tackles, including former Steeler Minkah Fitzpatrick, now with the Jets, who was fined 17,911 dollars. Lions linebacker Jack Campbell was fined 15,930 dollars for one as well. So this wasn’t the league singling anyone out. It’s a clear message across the whole NFL: if you use that technique, you’re going to pay for it.

Think about what that means. That wasn’t just a sack. That was a dangerous tackle on a 42-year-old quarterback, the kind the league banned specifically because it can wreck ankles and knees. And the league agreed it shouldn’t have happened.

And that wasn’t the only shot Rodgers took that night. Earlier in the game, Browns defensive tackle Maliek Collins sacked him on one play and was flagged for roughing the passer on the next. The Browns were called for three penalties on that single play. For a first-time roughing-the-passer foul, the league’s fine schedule lists 17,911 dollars, and every roughing call gets reviewed.

So when people talk about Rodgers’ frustration that night, remember what was happening to him. He was getting hit, and some of those hits were illegal. That doesn’t excuse every word he said on the sideline. But it explains a lot.

Decision number two: Cleveland’s celebration cost them.

The league also handed out fines to three more Browns from that game, and these had nothing to do with football plays. Deshaun Watson was fined 14,926 dollars and Quinshon Judkins was fined 14,397 dollars for an illegal celebration, a smoking gesture after Watson’s touchdown pass to Judkins. Austin Barber was fined 6,944 dollars for a similar gesture.

That’s four Browns players fined from one game against Pittsburgh. So if you’re keeping score on who the league “lost patience” with after that Thursday night, it wasn’t the Steelers’ quarterback.

And here’s a detail that tells you how ugly that Week 4 game got. In one Thursday night game, the league fined four Browns players and one Steeler. That’s five fines from a single game. When you hear people say the Steelers “lost their composure” that night, remember that the other sideline had far more players getting letters from the league office.

Decision number three: the Steeler who did get fined.

Now, to be fair, one Steeler did get fined from that game, and it wasn’t Rodgers. It was DK Metcalf. The league fined him 11,941 dollars for taunting.

It happened on Pittsburgh’s very first possession. Metcalf caught a seven-yard pass on third-and-3. That should have been a big first down near midfield. But according to reporting from Alan Saunders, Metcalf tossed the ball at two Cleveland defenders, and he was flagged for unsportsmanlike conduct. The Steelers still got the first down, but they started the next play from their own 35 instead of the 50. Fifteen yards, gone, on the opening drive of a road game.

Metcalf was the only Steelers player fined for Week 4. And the lesson here is simple. Emotion is part of football, but when you’re on the road in a loud building, giving away free yards can cost you a close game. And Pittsburgh lost that one by three points.

And remember, there’s a bigger picture with these fines this season. Earlier this year, Cole Holcomb was fined 11,041 dollars for a blindside block on special teams in the Bengals game. Under the league’s tougher system this season, repeated fines for major fouls can eventually lead to a suspension. So these aren’t just numbers. Every flag and every fine matters a little more in 2026.

So where did the “lost patience” idea even come from?

Let’s be honest about the noise. That Thursday night, Prime Video’s cameras caught Rodgers and Mike McCarthy cursing at each other on the sideline in the first quarter, reportedly over late play calls in a loud stadium. Later, the cameras caught Rodgers getting heated with his receivers too. After the game, former Steelers offensive lineman Trai Essex, a two-time Super Bowl champion with this franchise, went on social media and called Rodgers’ game day behavior “despicable,” even while saying he might be a great locker room guy.

So yes, there were people losing patience. Some fans. Some alumni. Some national media voices. One national writer even suggested the Colts should come to Pittsburgh and “put them out of their misery.”

And let me be fair to Essex, because his voice deserves respect. He was a third-round pick of this franchise in 2005, spent seven seasons in black and gold, played all five offensive line positions, and won two Super Bowls here. When a player like that speaks, he’s speaking from the standard he helped build. He wasn’t attacking Rodgers as a person. He was saying that what fans saw on TV that night didn’t look like Steelers football. That’s a fair criticism of one night. But one night isn’t a season.

But the league? No. And the Steelers? Absolutely not.

How the Steelers responded: McCarthy chose his quarterback.

Here’s what happened inside the building. McCarthy owned his part right away. He said after the Cleveland game, “Any negative communication on my part, I wish I wouldn’t use those words.” And before the Colts game, he made it clear that he wasn’t going to panic. He said, “I’m a huge believer in preparation,” and, “When it doesn’t look right, I prepare more.”

Then he changed the routine. The team spent Tuesday of that week focusing only on itself, studying its own mistakes before turning to the Colts. McCarthy said afterward, “I think taking Tuesday and focusing on ourselves was the right call,” and, “The newness of how you do things in year one, this was clearly our best week.”

And after the game? McCarthy didn’t take a shot at his quarterback. He praised him. “I thought Aaron did a great job of staying on time,” he said. “I thought his pocket presence was excellent, his ability to move with his feet and make plays out of the pocket.”

That’s a coach and a quarterback who won a Super Bowl together in Green Bay, standing together when the outside world wanted them to split. Remember, Rodgers has said he thought 2025 might be his last year, but then McCarthy got hired, and his wife told him he could do one more year. He came back because of this relationship. One loud Thursday night wasn’t going to break it.

And let’s not forget where this partnership started. In 2025, Rodgers’ first season in Pittsburgh, he threw for 3,322 yards, 24 touchdowns, and only seven interceptions in what NFL.com described as a restricted offense. This year, with McCarthy running the offense, it has opened up. The tight ends are scoring. The backs are catching the ball in space. Rookies like Germie Bernard are getting real targets. You can see McCarthy’s fingerprints all over the way Rodgers is being used, and you can see why Rodgers came back for one more year to play for him.

How Rodgers responded: history.

And here’s the part that should end the debate. Even in that loss in Cleveland, with the hits and the noise, Rodgers threw three touchdown passes. And then he came home and was even better.

Against the Colts, he completed his first 11 passes. He finished 22 of 31 for 235 yards and three touchdowns, his third straight game with three touchdown passes. Over that three-game stretch, he’s completed 63 of 105 passes for 826 yards and nine touchdowns. No Steelers quarterback had ever thrown three touchdowns in three consecutive weeks of the same season. Not Terry Bradshaw. Not Ben Roethlisberger.

And the records kept coming. His 166th regular-season win as a starter moved him past Roethlisberger into fifth place all-time. He passed Peyton Manning for third in NFL history in touchdown passes, postseason included, with 580. And his 80-yard touchdown pass to Jaylen Warren tied him with Roethlisberger for the most 80-plus-yard touchdown passes in NFL history, with nine.

Steelers Nation, that is not a quarterback the league is losing patience with. That’s a quarterback the league is going to be celebrating for a long time.

And remember what he said about this season. In May, he announced, “This is it.” At training camp, when he was asked if he might change his mind, he said, “Zero debate.” And before the Cleveland game, he told Al Michaels that a year from now, “I’ll either be tending to my pregnant wife or I’ll be taking care of my newborn.” Every week of this season is part of a farewell tour. When you know it’s the last ride, you play with emotion. Sometimes that emotion boils over. The key is what you do next, and Rodgers has answered with three straight huge performances.

The real “drastic decision”: a front office that keeps building around him.

If you want to talk about decisions that actually change things, look at the front office. Since that Cleveland loss, Omar Khan has claimed two cornerbacks off other teams’ practice squads, Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith. He brought back pass rusher KJ Henry and linebacker Elandon Roberts. And this week, longtime Steelers insider Mark Kaboly posted that he’s “getting strong indications that the Steelers are going to be active heading into the NFL trade deadline set for Nov. 10.”

That’s not a team losing patience with its quarterback. That’s a team trying to give its quarterback more help.

My trade block opinion.

And here’s where I think that help should come from, and this is my opinion, not a report. With Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve and Roman Wilson and Ben Skowronek out for Tampa, the receiver room is down to DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen on the 53-man roster. Rodgers needs another target.

The most realistic option I see is Jauan Jennings of the Minnesota Vikings. Pro Football Talk reported this week that he could be traded after the Vikings signed Odell Beckham Jr. to their practice squad, and Ian Rapoport said the 49ers “make sense” as a suitor. There’s no report linking Jennings to Pittsburgh. But he caught 77 passes for 975 yards in 2024 and nine touchdowns in 2025, and much of his one-year deal has already been paid through his signing bonus. For a late-round pick, he could be a tough, physical third-down target for Rodgers the rest of the way.

And if Khan wants a bigger swing, there’s Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland, who NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala called “just an odd man out,” or Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville, with the 2028 second-round pick from Dallas as the centerpiece. Tyreek Hill is still a free agent, but his agent says “nothing is imminent,” and the Chiefs are the favorite.

Why this matters for the rest of the season.

Here’s why I spent so much time on this. The Steelers are entering the stretch of the season that will define McCarthy’s first year: Tampa this week, then the Saints in Paris in Week 7, then one more game before the Week 9 bye and the November 10th trade deadline. In that stretch, there are going to be more loud stadiums, more tough calls, and more moments when emotions run hot. What we saw after Cleveland is a team that knows how to reset. The coach owned his part. The quarterback answered on the field. The front office kept adding pieces. That’s how good teams handle adversity.

And if there’s one lesson from those Week 4 fines, it’s this: discipline wins on the road. The Browns gave away money with their celebrations. The Steelers gave away 15 yards with a taunting penalty. Both of those things are avoidable. Over a full season, the teams that avoid those mistakes are the ones still playing in January.

What it all means for Sunday in Tampa.

Now let’s bring it to this weekend. The Steelers are 3-2, and they visit Tampa Bay on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern. The Buccaneers are 1-4, and Baker Mayfield is likely out with a dislocated thumb, which means undrafted rookie Jalon Daniels will probably start for Tampa.

On Pittsburgh’s side, here’s the final injury report. Ruled out: Roman Wilson, Ben Skowronek, and DeShon Elliott, who isn’t being activated from injured reserve this week. Questionable: DK Metcalf with an illness after missing Friday’s practice, Jalen Ramsey with his wrist, Jamel Dean with his ankle, Rico Dowdle with his toe, and Keeanu Benton with his ankle. Dowdle and Benton both practiced fully on Friday, and Dowdle is expected to make his return after missing three games.

And here’s a storyline for you. Remember how this whole “lost patience” talk started on the road, in a loud stadium in Cleveland? Pittsburgh has lost five straight road games. Sunday is the chance to end that streak. If Rodgers can stay calm, get the ball out on time, and lean on Warren and Dowdle in the run game, the Steelers can win this game comfortably.

And don’t overlook Tampa’s young quarterback. Jalon Daniels threw for 148 yards with a touchdown and two interceptions in his first start, a 17-14 loss to Green Bay, and then completed 76 percent of his passes for 189 yards and a touchdown to beat Dallas last week as a 9.5-point underdog. The Steelers’ defense, which just held Jonathan Taylor to 45 yards and didn’t allow a passing touchdown against the Colts, needs to make him uncomfortable from the first snap. Sean Murphy-Bunting, who spent the first month of the season on Tampa’s practice squad, should know exactly what the Bucs like to do.

On offense, Jaylen Warren comes in as the AFC Offensive Player of the Week after his 188-yard performance against Indianapolis. If Dowdle is back alongside him, Rodgers won’t have to carry this team through the air with a thin receiver room. He can manage the game, hit the open man, and let the backs do the damage.

And here’s one thing I’ll be watching closely: discipline. After that Cleveland game, where a taunting penalty cost the Steelers 15 yards on the opening drive, I want to see a clean game. No free yards. No emotional flags. The best way to answer every critic is to win the right way.

The bottom line.

So let’s set the record straight. The NFL did not lose patience with Aaron Rodgers, and it did not make any drastic decision against him. The league fined the Browns defender who hit him with an illegal hip-drop tackle. It fined three Browns players for a celebration. And it fined one Steeler, DK Metcalf, for taunting.

The people who lost patience were some critics on the outside. The people who kept faith were the ones who matter most: his head coach, his teammates, and the front office. And Rodgers answered with three straight three-touchdown games and a pile of history. If that’s what happens when people lose patience with Aaron Rodgers, Steelers Nation should hope they keep doing it all season long.
