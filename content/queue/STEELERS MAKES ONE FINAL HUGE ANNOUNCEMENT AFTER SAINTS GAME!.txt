Steelers Nation, kickoff in Paris is only hours away, and the Steelers have made their last announcements before the first regular-season game ever played in France. DeShon Elliott is officially back from injured reserve. Two receivers have been called up. And while the team was finalizing its roster, a new trade deadline story popped up involving one of Pittsburgh’s most important defenders, Alex Highsmith. One analyst says the Steelers would only move him under one very specific condition. So today, we’re covering the final roster announcements, the full story of Elliott’s comeback, the receiver situation with Michael Pittman Jr. out, and whether a Highsmith trade makes any sense at all. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The final announcements before kickoff.

Let’s start with what’s official. On Saturday, the Steelers activated safety DeShon Elliott from injured reserve, and they elevated wide receivers Brandon Johnson and Brandon Smith from the practice squad for the game. Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who rejoined the practice squad on Tuesday, was not one of the two receivers called up.

Those moves address two different problems. On offense, the Steelers need bodies at receiver. On defense, they’re getting back a veteran starter who has been missing since the summer. Let’s take them one at a time.

Why the receiver room needs help.

The receiver problem starts with Michael Pittman Jr. The veteran was placed on injured reserve earlier this month after aggravating a foot injury that had already cost him a game earlier in the season. He came back from it once, but the problem flared up again, and the Steelers had to shut him down. According to reports, he had seven catches for 89 yards in three appearances before the setback. McCarthy said at the time that Pittman would miss multiple weeks.

Then Roman Wilson went on injured reserve with a finger injury, and Ben Skowronek was ruled out for Sunday with a calf injury. That leaves DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard as the two receivers carrying the passing game, with Kaden Wetjen behind them.

That’s why Johnson and Smith matter. Neither one is a star, but both have been around this offense. Johnson has five career touchdowns on 26 catches, mostly from his time in Denver. Smith has been elevated for each of the last three games. Their job is simple: know the assignments, block when the ball goes elsewhere, help on special teams, and be ready if Rodgers looks their way. Sometimes one catch on a key third down can change how a coaching staff sees a depth player.

Bernard’s growing role.

And the injuries have created a big opportunity for Bernard. The rookie has caught 17 passes in his first two career starts, including 11 catches for 87 yards and the game-winning touchdown in overtime in Tampa. McCarthy gave him a game ball and said his performance “speaks volumes about where he is on his climb and his contribution to the offense.”

With Pittman out, Bernard isn’t easing into the NFL anymore. He’s being asked to be a reliable second option next to Metcalf, in a road game, on another continent. How he handles that is one of the most interesting storylines of the day. Rookies who produce when the depth chart thins out tend to earn trust that lasts, even after the veterans come back. If Bernard keeps stacking games like Tampa, Pittman’s return later this season becomes a bonus rather than a rescue.

Elliott’s comeback: the full story.

Now let’s talk about the defensive headline, because DeShon Elliott’s road back is a story worth telling.

Elliott started his NFL career with the Baltimore Ravens, a sixth-round pick out of the University of Texas in 2018. That’s right, his career began on the other side of the Steelers’ fiercest rivalry. And the early years were rough. He missed his entire rookie season after fracturing his forearm, and a knee injury limited him to six games in his second year.

His breakthrough came in his third season, when he became a full-time starter after the Ravens moved on from veteran safety Earl Thomas. Elliott quickly built a reputation as a physical, hard-hitting safety who liked to play close to the line of scrimmage, finishing fourth on the team in tackles with two forced fumbles and two and a half sacks.

From Detroit to Miami to Pittsburgh.

Injuries hit him again in Baltimore, and after leaving, he signed one-year deals with the Detroit Lions and the Miami Dolphins. Those stops helped him prove he could still be a reliable starter, and that led him to Pittsburgh. In 2024, the Steelers signed him to a two-year deal worth about $6 million.

He was a bargain. Elliott started 14 of 15 games that season and finished second on the team with 108 total tackles, a career high. He added two forced fumbles, three fumble recoveries, six passes defended, and an interception. The Steelers rewarded him with a two-year extension worth $12.5 million.

Then came the setback. Early in 2025, he was off to another strong start, with an interception, a sack, and a forced fumble in his first five games. But in a game against Green Bay, he hyperextended his knee and tore his hamstring, an injury that required surgery and ended his season. This year, he opened on injured reserve and missed the first six games.

Now he’s back. For a player whose career has been interrupted by injury after injury, getting back on the field in Paris means a lot. And there’s something fitting about it. A player who started his career in Baltimore, fought his way back from setback after setback, and found a home in Pittsburgh now gets to make his return on one of the biggest stages the NFL has ever put together. Whatever his snap count looks like, that’s a moment worth appreciating.

What Elliott brings to this defense.

So what does Elliott add on Sunday? Physicality, first of all. He’s at his best coming downhill, supporting the run, and finishing tackles. That’s important for a defense that has had some issues with missed tackles in the middle of the field, where one missed tackle turns a four-yard run into a 20-yard gain.

His presence also helps the linebackers. Patrick Queen and Payton Wilson both have responsibilities against the run and in coverage. When there’s a safety they trust filling the alley behind them, they can play faster.

And it helps Jaquan Brisker. With Elliott out, Brisker has carried a heavier load in the secondary. Now Patrick Graham can rotate his safeties, keep Brisker fresh, and keep rookie Robert Spears-Jennings involved. Graham has said there will be competition, and his approach is to “let everything happen organically.”

But remember, this is his first game in a long time. Being activated doesn’t mean playing every snap. The coaching staff will be watching his conditioning and his timing, and I’d expect a measured role.

The Highsmith trade story.

Now let’s get to the story that popped up on Saturday, because it surprised a lot of people. According to Athlon Sports, Alex Highsmith showed up on a trade deadline big board from analyst Daniel Popper. But here’s the key detail: Popper believes Pittsburgh would only consider moving Highsmith in a player-for-player deal, not for draft picks.

That makes sense. The Steelers are 4-2 and tied for the top of the AFC North. This isn’t a team selling off starters for future picks. If Pittsburgh ever traded Highsmith, it would have to bring back a player who helps right now.

Popper named the Cleveland Browns and the Chicago Bears as the best fits. He also noted that Highsmith is under contract through 2027, with a projected cap hit of about $7.25 million for any team that acquires him. That’s a reasonable number for a proven edge rusher, which is exactly why other teams would be interested.

Who Highsmith is.

It’s easy to forget how long Highsmith has been a key piece in Pittsburgh. He came into the league as a third-round pick out of Charlotte in 2020, a small-school prospect most fans didn’t know much about. He worked his way into the starting lineup, and in 2022 he broke out with 14 and a half sacks, the best season of his career. That’s why the Steelers gave him a four-year, $68 million extension the next year.

He’s had some injury setbacks since then, including a groin injury in 2024 that cost him three games, and he’s been managing a groin issue again this week. But when he’s on the field, he’s been one of the most dependable players on this defense. He doesn’t always get the headlines that Watt does, but the coaches know exactly how much he does on every snap, against the pass and against the run.

Why Highsmith is too valuable to move.

But let’s look at what Highsmith has done this season, because it’s a lot. He leads the Steelers in quarterback pressures through six games. Still Curtain, citing Sumer Sports, put the number at 20. He has three sacks. And Pro Football Focus gives him a 77.6 run defense grade, which ranks seventh among 115 edge defenders.

That’s not a backup. That’s a starter who rushes the passer and sets the edge against the run. Pass rushers who can do both are hard to find, and teams don’t usually give them away in the middle of a winning season. And on Ramsey’s game-winning sack in Tampa, McCarthy specifically credited Watt and Highsmith for setting the edges that allowed Ramsey to get home.

Still Curtain’s Loyal Ricks Jr. put it bluntly this week, writing that “The Pittsburgh Steelers cannot afford to trade Alex Highsmith before the NFL Trade Deadline,” and that “Highsmith’s value extends beyond his career sack total.” I agree. He’s part of the reason this defense is allowing just 204.3 passing yards per game, seventh in the league entering this week.

What about the Herbig extension?

Some fans have asked whether the Nick Herbig extension makes Highsmith expendable. In June, the Steelers signed Herbig to a four-year extension worth up to $100 million. So yes, Herbig is clearly part of the long-term plan.

But having three quality edge rushers isn’t a problem. It’s an advantage. Offenses have to account for Watt, Highsmith, and Herbig. Every extra blocker sent to one of them creates a one-on-one somewhere else. And with Watt dealing with a calf issue this week and Highsmith managing a groin injury, depth on the edge isn’t a luxury. It’s essential.

And there’s one more problem with the trade idea. One of Popper’s two best fits was Cleveland. The Browns are tied with the Steelers at 4-2 and come to Pittsburgh next week. Trading a starting pass rusher to your biggest division rival in the middle of a tight race would be a very hard sell.

Why Cleveland is interested in pass rushers.

It’s not surprising that the Browns came up as a fit. Cleveland has been connected to a lot of edge defenders ahead of the deadline, according to reports this week. The Browns are 4-2, they’re in first place, and they want to push their chips in. That’s exactly why the Steelers should be careful. Helping the team you’re fighting for the division title is the last thing Khan wants to do, no matter what the return looks like.

The Bears are a different story. Chicago is in the NFC, so a trade there wouldn’t come back to haunt the Steelers twice a year. But even then, the return would have to be a player who makes an immediate impact, and it would have to be worth losing a starter in the middle of a playoff race.

What it would mean for Herbig and Sawyer.

Think about what a Highsmith trade would do to the rest of the edge group, too. Herbig would move into a full-time starting role, which he’s ready for. But behind him, the depth would get thin very quickly. Jack Sawyer would become the top backup, and if Watt or Herbig got hurt, the Steelers would be asking a young player to carry a big load in December. A pass rush is only as strong as its rotation, and this rotation is a big reason the defense has been so good late in games.

Trade block: what a player-for-player deal would have to look like.

So let’s play it out, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

If the Steelers ever traded Highsmith, the only thing that would make sense is a player who fixes the biggest weakness on the roster right now. And that’s wide receiver. With Pittman and Wilson on injured reserve, a starting-caliber receiver in return could, in theory, make the offense much more dangerous.

But here’s the thing. You’d be taking away from the strongest unit on your team to patch the weakest one. That’s robbing Peter to pay Paul. I’d much rather see Khan use draft capital, like the extra 2028 second-round pick from the Joey Porter Jr. trade, to add a receiver, and keep the pass rush intact. Josh Downs in Indianapolis is still the name I like most, with the Colts expected to sell after firing their general manager.

Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline. My expectation is that if they make a move, it’ll be adding a weapon, not subtracting a pass rusher.

Elliott and the run defense against New Orleans.

Let’s bring it back to Sunday. The Saints are 2-4, but they have weapons. Alvin Kamara was limited all week with a back issue but didn’t get a game designation, so he’s expected to play. Running back C.J. Donaldson was a full participant on Friday. If New Orleans tries to establish the run, Elliott’s physicality becomes even more important, especially with the Steelers missing Derrick Harmon and Sebastian Joseph-Day on the interior line.

The formula from the last two weeks still applies. Stop the run on early downs. Force third-and-long. Then let the pass rush, led by Watt, Highsmith, and Herbig, go to work. That’s how the Steelers held Tampa to 6-of-19 on third down and the Colts to 4-of-15.

Three players to watch in Paris.

Let me give you three players to keep your eyes on once the game starts.

Number one: DeShon Elliott. Watch how often he’s on the field, and where. Is he lining up deep, or closer to the line of scrimmage in run situations? If he’s flying downhill and making tackles early, that tells you he’s ready. If his role is small, that’s fine too. It’s a long season.

Number two: Germie Bernard. With Pittman, Wilson, and Skowronek all out, the rookie is going to see a lot of targets. After 17 catches in his first two starts, can he keep it going against a new defense in a new environment? If he does, the Steelers’ receiver problem looks a lot less scary.

Number three: Alex Highsmith. On a weekend when his name is floating around trade boards, he’s playing through a groin issue that kept him limited earlier in the week. A big game from him would be the best possible answer to the trade talk. And with T.J. Watt coming off a calf scare of his own, the Steelers may need Highsmith to carry a little more of the pass rush than usual.

The pass rush health check.

Speaking of Watt, the calf issue that showed up on Thursday turned out to be mostly a scare. He was a full participant on Friday’s estimated report, and McCarthy said he didn’t expect it to affect his availability. That’s a relief, because this pass rush is at its best when Watt, Highsmith, and Herbig are all on the field together. In Tampa, the Steelers finished with five sacks. If all three are healthy today, the Saints’ offensive line has its hands full, especially with guard Cesar Ruiz listed as questionable.

The opportunity in Paris.

The Steelers come into today 4-2, tied with the Browns and Ravens, with Cleveland holding first place on its division record. A win over the Saints would make it three straight, with the Browns coming to Pittsburgh next week. And they’d do it in front of a global audience, in the first regular-season NFL game ever played in Paris.

Kickoff is at 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh time at the Stade de France. Europe set its clocks back overnight, so it’s a five-hour difference today instead of six.

The bottom line.

So what are the final announcements before the Saints game? DeShon Elliott is officially back after a long and difficult road. Brandon Johnson and Brandon Smith are elevated to help a receiver room that’s missing Pittman, Wilson, and Skowronek. And Alex Highsmith’s name has shown up on a trade board, with an analyst saying Pittsburgh would only move him in a player-for-player deal.

My view on that last one is simple. Keep him. He leads the team in pressures, he’s one of the best run defenders at his position, and he’s part of a pass rush that’s carrying this team. If Khan wants to add a weapon before the deadline, there are better ways to do it than weakening the defense.
