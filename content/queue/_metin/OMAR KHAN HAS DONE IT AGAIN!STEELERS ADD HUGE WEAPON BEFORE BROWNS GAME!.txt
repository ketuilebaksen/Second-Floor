Steelers Nation, there is a race going on right now, and most people don’t even realize Pittsburgh is in it. On one side of the country, the Kansas City Chiefs are hunting for a receiver. In Pittsburgh, Omar Khan is hunting for one too. And according to a report that dropped Thursday, both teams have now put an actual offer on the table for the same player.

That’s the weapon story this week. Not a rumor about interest. Not a pundit’s wish list. An offer.

But hold on, because the weapon race isn’t only happening on the phone lines. It’s happening on the practice field too, where Thursday’s injury report brought some of the best news Pittsburgh has had all week. And it’s happening in the comment section, where so many of you are still asking me about Tyreek Hill.

So here’s the plan for today. I’ll walk you through the trade offer and the price fight with Indianapolis. I’ll give you the practice-field reinforcements heading into Sunday. I’ll give you an honest Hill check, short and clean. And then my opinion on what Khan should do before the deadline. I’m James, this is Second Floor, and the Browns are coming to town. Let’s work.

FIRST, A QUICK CLEANUP

Before we get to the new stuff, a quick reality check, because a video making the rounds this week is recycling the story from Saints week. It talks about Marquez Valdes-Scantling as if he’s about to suit up in Paris and as if the big question is whether he plays against New Orleans.

That game is over. Pittsburgh won it 16-10 at the Stade de France. And MVS did not play in it. He wasn’t elevated from the practice squad for Paris. Brandon Smith and Brandon Johnson got the call-ups instead. So any “weapon” framing built around MVS in Paris is a week old. The real MVS question is about Cleveland, and we’ll get there. Same goes for any clip that still says Skowronek has been ruled out. That was the Saints game. This week he’s back on the practice field, and that changes the receiver math for Sunday in a real way.

THE BIG ONE: STEELERS AND CHIEFS BOTH MAKE OFFERS FOR JOSH DOWNS

Let’s start with the news that actually moves the needle.

Quick timeline so you have the whole picture. On Tuesday, the Colts started dismantling the roster. Quenton Nelson went to Seattle. DeForest Buckner went back to San Francisco. On Wednesday, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that Colts receiver Josh Downs is requesting a trade, and Jordan Schultz reported Downs had interest from multiple AFC teams, naming the Steelers.

Then came Thursday. NFL reporter Evan Sidery posted this: “The Chiefs and Steelers have both offered Day 3 draft compensation to the Colts for Josh Downs.”

Read that one more time. Offered. Not inquired. Not monitored. Offered. Day 3 compensation means a pick in rounds four through seven. Neither team has a deal, and nothing has been confirmed by the Steelers, the Chiefs, or the Colts. But if Sidery’s report is accurate, Pittsburgh has moved past the window-shopping phase.

And think about who the competition is. Kansas City. The Chiefs have been short on healthy receivers for weeks. Tyquan Thornton suffered a dislocated ankle back in early October and is expected to miss most or all of the regular season, and Rashee Rice has dealt with a hamstring. Kansas City is also the team most often linked to Tyreek Hill. So the same franchise that dominates the Hill conversation is now bidding against Pittsburgh for the same slot receiver. That’s the race.

THE PRICE FIGHT: WHAT THE COLTS WANT VS. WHAT TEAMS WILL PAY

Now here’s where it gets interesting. Adam Schefter talked about Downs on his podcast Thursday, and he gave us the other side of the negotiation.

His words: “Josh Downs, the Colts have told teams they’re looking for third-round value.” And then the kicker: “Nobody’s willing to do that right now.”

So you’ve got the Colts asking for a third. You’ve got the Steelers and Chiefs reportedly offering something on Day 3. And you’ve got a gap in the middle.

Schefter also pulled out two comparable trades from last season to frame what a receiver like Downs is worth. Rashid Shaheed went from New Orleans to Seattle for a fourth and a fifth. Jakobi Meyers went from Las Vegas to Jacksonville for a fourth and a sixth. Then he asked the question everybody in a front office is asking: is Downs as good as Shaheed or Meyers?

That’s the right question. And here’s the leverage picture as I see it. Downs asked out. He’s on the final year of his rookie contract. The Colts already sold an eight-time Pro Bowl guard for a third and a fifth, and a former first-team All-Pro defensive tackle for a fourth. Every day closer to November 10, Indianapolis has less leverage, not more. If the Colts wait for a third and nobody blinks, they risk getting a fifth or nothing.

So time is on the buyer’s side. That’s why I’m not surprised the offers are coming in low.

WHY DOWNS MAKES SENSE FOR THIS OFFENSE

Let me remind you what Downs actually is, because he’s not a household name for a lot of fans.

He was a third-round pick out of North Carolina in 2023. His best season came in 2024: 72 catches, 803 yards, five touchdowns. This year, in an Indianapolis offense that has struggled, he has 26 catches for 307 yards and no scores through seven games, and he still leads the team in receiving yards.

He’s a slot receiver who separates quickly. And that’s precisely the skill Pittsburgh’s offense is missing right now. Think about how this offense wins. Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle pound the ball, the defense creeps closer to the line, and then Aaron Rodgers needs a target who can beat man coverage on third-and-medium before the pass rush gets home. Cleveland played man on 73.3 percent of its snaps in the first meeting, according to Steeler Nation. Rodgers said this week, “You got to win your one-on-ones.” A quick slot receiver is how you win those one-on-ones without asking a 42-year-old to hold the ball.

Even if a deal happened tomorrow, which no one is reporting, Downs would not play Sunday. He’d have to learn the offense. This is a bye-week and beyond conversation. But the decision could be made in the next eleven days.

WHAT DULAC AND THE LOCALS SAY

To be fair, the local voices are cautious. Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette said earlier this week, “They are not adding talent on the outside,” and that he didn’t expect a move “unless there is a significant injury.” His reasoning was that the Steelers are getting their receivers back and the secondary isn’t as bad as people think.

Note the wording, though. Dulac said talent on the outside. Downs is a slot guy. I’m not saying Dulac was wrong, and I’m not saying he was hinting at anything. I’m saying a Day 3 offer for a slot receiver isn’t necessarily in conflict with “no outside receiver.” Keep that nuance in your back pocket.

THE PRACTICE-FIELD WEAPONS: THURSDAY WAS A GOOD DAY

Now let’s talk about the weapons that could actually play on Sunday, because Thursday’s injury report was a big improvement over Wednesday’s.

Jalen Ramsey went from not practicing on Wednesday to a full practice on Thursday. That’s the headline. Ramsey has been playing with a broken wrist since Week 3, and he’s the Swiss Army knife of this secondary, playing slot, safety, and anything Patrick Graham dreams up. Full on Thursday is exactly what you want to see.

T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward, and Darnell Washington, who all had rest days Wednesday, were full participants Thursday too. The stars are fresh.

Ben Skowronek was limited for a second straight day with his calf. That’s progress, not a finish line. As one Yahoo write-up put it, “Skowronek is the first of that group that will have an opportunity to return to action,” meaning the first of the injured receiver trio with Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson. Friday’s report will tell us if he gets a game designation.

Alex Highsmith was also limited for the second day with his groin. And here’s the one concern: Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both with hamstrings, did not practice either day. That’s two depth pieces in the secondary, and the Friday statuses will matter.

THE MVS DECISION, ONE MORE TIME

Now, the receiver question for Sunday. Rodgers made his feelings clear Wednesday: “We’ll see what happens. It would be nice to get him up.” He also called Valdes-Scantling “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with.”

Steelers Depot ran a headline this week quoting McCarthy saying MVS is “doing a really good job,” framed around how the veteran can earn playing time. I couldn’t access the full article, so I’m only going on the headline there.

Here’s the roster logic. Brandon Smith has burned all three of his practice-squad elevations and reverted to the practice squad on Monday. In Paris, he played 29 snaps and caught both of his targets for 24 yards. If Pittsburgh wants him Sunday, he has to be signed to the active roster. MVS, on the other hand, still has fresh elevations. So the likely paths for Saturday are: elevate MVS, sign Smith, do both, or ride with Skowronek if he gets cleared.

We’ll know by Saturday afternoon. My gut says Rodgers gets his guy.

THE LOCKER ROOM IS TALKING LIKE A RUN TEAM

I love how this building is talking this week, because it sounds like a team that knows exactly who it is.

Jaylen Warren on Thursday: “We’re popping off runs and you can’t ask for much more than that.” And about the guys in front of him: “I wouldn’t want to run behind any other offensive line.”

Zach Frazier, the center, kept it short: “We love running the ball.”

McCarthy, still riding his favorite line of the week, called Warren “hot as a firecracker.” And about Paris, he was honest: “I’d like to win by 21 points every week, but you need these games.”

Then there’s the defensive side. Cam Heyward looked back at the Week 4 loss in Cleveland, where the Browns drove for the winning field goal in the final seconds, and said, “We can’t surrender a drive like that for them to win the game.” And then: “It all comes down to the fourth quarter, and you’ve got to have a finish mentality.”

And DeShon Elliott, back from injured reserve, just sounded grateful: “Just happy to be able to practice with my guys, play with my guys.”

That’s a team with a clear identity on offense and a chip on its shoulder on defense. I’ll take it.

THE BROWNS SIDE OF IT

Quick check on Cleveland. Jared Verse and center Elgton Jenkins both did not practice Wednesday, and Steelers Depot’s Thursday headline says both were upgraded. I couldn’t open the full report, so I’m not going to guess at the exact levels. But it sounds like Verse is trending toward playing, which makes his chirping back in Week 4, and Heyward’s “those are words that were said and they will be addressed,” that much more fun.

Patrick Graham summed up the Browns in two words on Thursday: “They’re talented.” Both teams are 5-2. Whoever wins Sunday at 1 p.m. on CBS walks away alone in first place in the AFC North.

THE TYREEK HILL CHECK: SHORT AND HONEST

I know. Every time I post, the comments fill up with the same name. So here’s the honest update on Tyreek Hill, and I’ll keep it tight.

He hasn’t signed anywhere. There is no report connecting him to Pittsburgh, and there hasn’t been all season. I haven’t found any meaningful new development in his free agency over the last week or so. The most recent substantive reporting is from mid-October.

What we know: his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said in mid-October, “Nothing is imminent at this time,” and that Hill is continuing rehab “with the goal of being on a team in the immediate future.” He’s 32 and coming back from a serious knee injury from September 2025. Hill also has an off-field legal matter that the NFL is reviewing under its personal conduct policy, and that’s all I’ll say about it. Jordan Schultz said on the Ross Tucker Podcast that in his opinion, “it’s more likely he’s suspended first before he signs.”

And here’s the part that ties it together. Kansas City has been the team most associated with Hill. And according to Sidery, the Chiefs just made an offer for Josh Downs. Earlier this month, The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones said the Chiefs continued to “sniff around” the receiver trade market, and that a trade for a speed receiver would be a negative development for a Hill reunion. If Kansas City lands Downs, that could cool Hill’s most likely landing spot even further.

So if you’re dreaming of Hill in black and gold, my honest answer is that the realistic weapon fight for Pittsburgh is Downs, not Hill. I’d rather win the race that’s actually happening.

THE DEADLINE CALENDAR: WHY THE NEXT ELEVEN DAYS MATTER

Let’s map the timing, because it shapes every decision Khan makes. After Sunday, Pittsburgh has its Week 9 bye. The trade deadline lands on Tuesday, November 10, right after that bye week. So the Browns game is the last snapshot the front office gets before it has to decide.

Win Sunday, and you’re 6-2, alone in first, and suddenly a modest move for a slot receiver looks like the final piece for a division champion. Lose Sunday, and you’re 5-3, swept by Cleveland, and the conversation could shift toward protecting the future instead of chasing the present.

And look at what comes right after the deadline. Sunday night in Cincinnati on November 15. A trip to Philadelphia on November 22. Denver at home on Black Friday. Houston at home on Sunday night football in Week 13. That’s a brutal stretch, and it’s exactly where a new receiver would be getting comfortable in the offense. If you’re going to add a weapon, you want him learning the playbook during the bye, not in December.

WHAT A DOWNS TRADE WOULD MEAN FOR THE ROOM

Here’s the other piece people skip. A trade isn’t just a player coming in. It’s a roster puzzle.

If Downs arrives, he’d join Metcalf and Germie Bernard as the top three. Skowronek would be the special-teams ace and blocker. Kaden Wetjen would keep developing. And then Pittman, who went to injured reserve before the Colts game, becomes eligible to return after the bye, with Roman Wilson a little later. All of a sudden a receiver room that was down to three healthy bodies in Paris could be the deepest Rodgers has had all season.

There’s also the contract. Downs is on the last year of his rookie deal, and asked about an extension before the season, he said, “If it came my way, it came my way, but eh.” That tells you he’ll want to test the market. So realistically, this is a rental for the rest of 2026 and the playoff run. That’s fine. Rodgers has called this his last season. If there was ever a year to rent a weapon, it’s this one.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, the reporter hat comes off. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Target one: Josh Downs. Go get him, but don’t panic-buy. Schefter laid out the comps. If Meyers went for a fourth and a sixth and Shaheed went for a fourth and a fifth, then a fair landing spot for Downs is a fourth-round pick plus a late-round sweetener. That’s my ceiling. I would not pay a straight third, and I would not touch the 2028 second-rounder Pittsburgh received from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. If Kansas City wants to overpay, let them. The worst outcome is losing a bidding war you never should have entered at that price.

Target two: an internal option. If the Downs price climbs, keep the picks. Sign Brandon Smith, elevate MVS, wait for Skowronek this week and Pittman after the bye. The offense ranks first in rushing EPA from Week 3 through Week 7, per Athlon. You don’t blow up a winning formula over one slot receiver.

Target three, the long shot I keep watching: Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville. Mark Kaboly said this week that Pittsburgh “were” interested, but he hasn’t “heard anything recently about it.” That’s a cold trail for now. Downs is the warm one.

FOUR KEYS FOR SUNDAY

One. Win the first quarter on the ground. Pittsburgh’s best drives start with Warren. Establish him early and make Cleveland’s front fight uphill all day.

Two. Ramsey’s role. With him full on Thursday, I want Graham moving him around to confuse Deshaun Watson, especially on third down.

Three. Finish. Heyward said it. The last time these teams met, Cleveland drove for the winner in the final minute. If Pittsburgh has a lead late, the defense has to close.

Four. Red zone. Field goals won in Paris. Field goals might lose Sunday. Get Darnell Washington and Pat Freiermuth involved inside the 20.
