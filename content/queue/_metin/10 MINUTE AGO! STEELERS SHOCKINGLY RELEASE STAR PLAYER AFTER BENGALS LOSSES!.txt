Steelers Nation, I know what the thumbnail said. “Ten minutes ago, the Steelers released a star player.” So let me give you the most up-to-date version of the truth.

The newest Steelers transaction isn’t a release at all. It’s a signing. On Tuesday afternoon, Pittsburgh added a defensive back to its practice squad, a player who was cut by Seattle just five days earlier. And the team didn’t have to release anyone to make room for him.

Meanwhile, the biggest story inside the building is still Mason McCormick. The starting left guard was carted off in Cincinnati, and on Tuesday, new details emerged about how the team plans to replace him, how McCormick is handling it, and a decision on a rookie lineman that’s expected before Wednesday’s practice.

So today: the Tuesday signing and why it makes sense after what Joe Burrow did to this secondary. The latest on McCormick. The two players competing to fill his spot, with new quotes from McCarthy and offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio. A few things the source video gets wrong. And what all of it means for Sunday in Philadelphia. My board at the end, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

THE NEWEST MOVE: AJ FINLEY SIGNS

Here’s the transaction, straight from Steelers.com.

On Tuesday, November 17, the Steelers signed defensive back AJ Finley to their practice squad. The team announced it simply: “We have signed DB AJ Finley to the Practice Squad.”

Finley is 25. According to Steelers.com, he went undrafted out of Ole Miss in 2023 and signed with the Los Angeles Chargers. Seattle claimed him off waivers in 2024. He missed the 2025 season with a knee injury, then re-signed with the Seahawks and appeared in three games this year.

For his career, he’s played 29 NFL games with one start, nine defensive tackles and 10 special teams tackles, plus a forced fumble. According to HEAVY, his first career start came on September 27 against Washington, and Seattle released him on November 12 to make room for a practice squad lineman.

At Ole Miss, according to Steelers.com, Finley was productive: eight interceptions, 21 passes defensed and 229 tackles.

A SUPER BOWL RING ALREADY

Here’s a detail most fans won’t know. According to Yahoo Sports, Finley earned a Super Bowl LX ring with Seattle, even though an injury kept him off the field for that 2025 season.

His 2026 path has been a roller coaster. Yahoo Sports reports he was waived in August, then bounced between Seattle’s active roster and practice squad before his final release on November 12. Pittsburgh is now his third NFL team.

That kind of journey doesn’t make a player a star. But it does make him a professional who knows how to prepare, which is exactly what you want from someone who may get called up on short notice.

WHY A DEFENSIVE BACK, AND WHY NOW

This is a small move, and Steeler Nation said exactly that. But the timing tells you what the coaches saw on film.

Cincinnati piled up 484 yards on Sunday night. Ja’Marr Chase had 231 receiving yards by himself. And the Steelers played that game without veteran safety DeShon Elliott, who hurt his knee in pregame warmups.

When your back end gets exposed like that, and your starting safety’s status is uncertain, you add depth. Finley has played mostly free safety on defense, according to Yahoo Sports, logging 148 regular-season defensive snaps. Most of his work has come on special teams.

That special teams experience matters. Steeler Nation suggests special teams coordinator Danny Crossman could use him if injuries pile up. And as Steeler Nation notes, practice squad players can be elevated to the game-day roster up to three times in a season.

Defensive coordinator Patrick Graham also set the tone for the week. According to Steeler Nation, he said: “Our toughness didn’t go away because we got beat.”

WHAT A PRACTICE SQUAD SAFETY ACTUALLY DOES

Some fans hear “practice squad” and assume it means nothing. That’s not quite right.

During the week, practice squad defensive backs often run the scout team, imitating the next opponent’s receivers or defenders so the starters can prepare. A smart veteran like Finley can make those reps more realistic, which helps the whole secondary.

On game days, he’s an emergency option. If Elliott can’t go, and another defensive back gets hurt during the week, Finley can be elevated in a hurry. And his special teams experience means he could fill a coverage-unit role right away, freeing up a starter.

So this isn’t a headline move. It’s the kind of quiet depth signing good teams make after a game exposes a weakness.

WHY NOBODY HAD TO BE CUT

Here’s the part that answers the headline directly.

According to Yahoo Sports, the Steelers had an open spot on their 17-man practice squad. So signing Finley didn’t require releasing anyone. No corresponding move was announced, and none was needed.

And looking back over the month, the only Steelers player waived in November was cornerback Terell Smith, on November 14, according to NFL.com’s waiver list. That was the move that cleared room for Derrick Harmon to come off injured reserve before the Bengals game.

So if you’re keeping score: one depth cornerback waived before the game, one depth safety added to the practice squad after it. No star released.

MASON MCCORMICK: WHERE THINGS STAND

Now to the injury that’s actually shaping this week.

According to Athlon Sports on Tuesday, McCormick still has no diagnosis or timetable. He’s reportedly a candidate for injured reserve. McCarthy’s update on Monday hadn’t changed: the team is “just working through the information gathering.”

One columnist at 93.7 The Fan wrote that the injury likely ends McCormick’s season. But the station’s own article makes clear that’s not confirmed. I’ll treat it the same way: a real possibility, not a fact.

And here’s a detail that clears up a number from my recent videos. ClutchPoints reported that McCormick had appeared in 43 straight games. Athlon says he had started 40 straight games before Sunday. Both can be true: 43 consecutive appearances, with 40 straight starts. The transcript I was working from last time mentioned 40 starts, and Athlon now backs that up.

THE LEADER WHO WON’T STOP LEADING

This might be my favorite detail of the week.

According to 93.7 The Fan, McCarthy called McCormick “clearly one of the leaders on the team” and confirmed he’s part of the team’s leadership group. And even after the injury, McCormick reached out to McCarthy twice during Monday’s offensive meetings.

Think about that. A player who was carted off the field less than 24 hours earlier was still checking in on the meetings. That’s not a guy who’s going to disappear from the building, whatever the diagnosis.

His teammates feel it too. According to 93.7 The Fan, Spencer Anderson called McCormick “our leader” and said he’s probably the most vocal player on the team. Troy Fautanu called his absence from the offense and the line room a “big loss.” Cam Heyward said McCormick had been a standout on the line all year.

And Pat Freiermuth, as the transcript mentions, called him the heart and soul of the offensive line room.

ONE IMPORTANT CORRECTION

Before we go on, I need to flag a mistake in the source video.

In its opening, the transcript says the injury involves “one of the most important players on Pittsburgh’s defensive line.” McCormick plays on the offensive line. He’s the left guard. Later in the video, the transcript gets it right, so it looks like a slip in the intro.

There’s a second correction. The transcript says Rodgers “completed just 15 of 29 passes” as if that were a first-half number. That was his full-game line. Rodgers finished 15 of 29 for 143 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions.

THE REPLACEMENT: SPENCER ANDERSON

So who plays left guard Sunday?

According to 93.7 The Fan and Athlon, Spencer Anderson is moving back into the starting lineup and is in line to start in Philadelphia.

Athlon gives some useful history. Anderson opened this season as the starting right guard, but the Steelers replaced him with Brock Hoffman after two games, amid struggles in the run game. Since then, Anderson has worked as an extra blocker and occasionally as an in-line tight end. By Athlon’s count, he’s started 11 of 34 games over the last two seasons.

McCarthy still believes in him. According to Athlon, he said: “He’s just a 360 football player.” In coach-speak, that’s a player who does a little bit of everything and knows the whole system.

That versatility is valuable. But Anderson lost a starting job earlier this season, and now he has to win one back in a hurry.

THE ROOKIE: GENNINGS DUNKER

The other option is rookie Gennings Dunker.

According to Athlon, the third-round pick has missed seven weeks on injured reserve with an MCL injury he suffered in practice. He’s eligible to return, but the team first has to open his practice window.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio made his feelings clear. According to Athlon, he said: “We have a ton of confidence in him.”

And according to Athlon, McCarthy expected to make a decision on Dunker before Wednesday’s practice. So by the time you’re watching this, that decision may already be public. Keep an eye on whether his window opens.

93.7 The Fan also reported that Dunker may return to practice and join the two-deep. That’s not the same as starting Sunday. But it would give the Steelers a real backup behind Anderson, which they badly need.

ATHLON’S “UNCOMFORTABLE CHOICE”

Athlon framed this as an uncomfortable choice, and I think that’s fair.

Option one is a veteran who knows the offense but already lost a starting job this season. Option two is a rookie who’s been out for seven weeks and hasn’t played a regular-season snap at guard. Neither one is McCormick.

My view: Anderson starts in Philadelphia, because he has the most recent game snaps and the trust of the staff. But if Dunker practices well, he should get a real shot over the next couple of weeks. The worst thing the Steelers could do is lock in one answer too early and ignore what they see in practice.

WHAT THE INJURY MEANS FOR THE ROSTER

There’s also a roster angle here, and it connects back to that “release” headline.

As the rules are generally understood, if the Steelers place McCormick on injured reserve, they open a spot on the 53-man roster. That spot could go to Dunker if he’s activated. Or it could go to one of the receivers coming back from injured reserve.

Remember, McCarthy said on Monday he was “hopeful” Roman Wilson could return for Philadelphia and optimistic about Michael Pittman Jr., according to PennLive’s Nick Farabaugh, as reported by CBS Sports. Each of those activations needs a roster spot.

So if a McCormick injured reserve move comes, it’s not just bad news. It’s also how the Steelers make room for reinforcements without cutting anyone. That’s the kind of “roster move” fans should actually be watching for this week.

THE LINE AROUND THE NEW GUARD

Whoever plays left guard won’t be doing it alone, and the players around him matter a lot.

Center Zach Frazier, who’s been one of the team’s best linemen all season, will be right next to him. On the other side of him is Troy Fautanu, who worked alongside McCormick on the left side all year, according to Steeler Nation. And Brock Hoffman stays at right guard, the job he took from Anderson earlier this season.

That’s a good thing. When one piece changes, the coaches want everything else to stay the same. Frazier making the line calls and Fautanu knowing the left-side combinations gives the new guard a lot of support.

THE OFFENSE HAS TO HELP, TOO

There’s a temptation to put everything on the offensive line. But the coaching staff and the quarterback can make the new guard’s job easier.

Rodgers can get the ball out faster. Angelichio can call more quick passes and play action that slows down the pass rush. And Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle can give the offense balance, as long as the Steelers don’t fall behind early again.

Warren had only seven carries in Cincinnati. Part of that was falling behind 17-0. If the Steelers can stay close early in Philadelphia, the run game becomes a weapon again, and that’s the best protection any lineman can get.

THE BENGALS GAME, ONE MORE TIME

The transcript spends a lot of time on the game itself, so here’s a quick summary in my own words.

Pittsburgh came into Cincinnati at 6-2 on a four-game winning streak. It left at 6-3, a game behind the Bengals. The final was 38-18. Cincinnati led 24-3 at halftime.

Burrow completed 35 of 44 for 409 yards and four touchdowns without an interception. Chase caught 11 balls for 231 yards. The Steelers didn’t get a first down until deep into the second quarter, and their two touchdowns, to DK Metcalf and Freiermuth, came in the fourth quarter after the game was out of reach.

The transcript sums it up well: the problem wasn’t one player. The line struggled, the passing game lacked rhythm, and the defense couldn’t slow Burrow down.

THE WATT-MIMS MATCHUP

The transcript also zeroes in on T.J. Watt, and this is one of the more interesting claims in the video.

It says Watt came into the game with seven and a half sacks, which matches the AP’s count after Week 8, and nine and a half tackles for loss, which I couldn’t confirm. Then it cites Next Gen Stats reporting that Bengals right tackle Amarius Mims allowed zero pressures on 23 pass-blocking snaps against Watt on Sunday, and zero on 45 snaps across both meetings.

I wasn’t able to find that Next Gen Stats data, so treat those numbers as the transcript’s claim. What I can confirm is the result: the Steelers sacked Burrow only once, on a Patrick Queen play, according to Steelers.com.

The transcript is careful not to blame Watt, and I agree. Every great pass rusher has nights when one tackle has his number. The fix is schematic: move him around, use stunts, and make the offense guess where he’s lining up.

HARMON BACK, ELLIOTT OUT

The transcript closes its defensive section with a good point: Pittsburgh got one key defender back and lost another on the same night.

Derrick Harmon, the 2025 first-round pick, was activated from injured reserve on Saturday after missing four games with a foot injury. That’s a real boost for the interior rotation.

But Elliott’s knee injury in warmups forced rookie Robert Spears-Jennings into a bigger role than anyone planned. That’s why the Finley signing makes sense. It’s not a replacement for Elliott. It’s insurance in case Elliott misses more time.

THE MESSAGE FROM THE TOP

It’s worth remembering how McCarthy handled the loss publicly, because it frames this whole week.

He didn’t make excuses. After the game, he said the Steelers were “beaten decisively,” according to the Tribune-Review, and that the performance was “clearly not to the standard of the Pittsburgh Steelers,” according to Sportsnet Pittsburgh. But he also said the team wasn’t “going to drive down the drama road.”

And on McCormick, his message on Monday was striking. According to Steelers.com, he said: “I don’t look at it as a loss. I look at it more as an adjustment.”

That’s the tone of a coach trying to keep the team focused on solutions. The Finley signing, the Dunker decision and the receiver windows all fit that approach: fix what you can, add depth where you need it, and get ready for the next game.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

Let’s step back for a second.

The Steelers are 6-3 with eight games left, one game behind Cincinnati in the AFC North. The Bengals have scored 30 or more points in five straight games, according to the Bengals, the longest such streak in franchise history.

And Pittsburgh’s schedule doesn’t ease up after Philadelphia. According to CBS Pittsburgh, the Steelers host Denver on Black Friday, November 27, then Houston on Sunday Night Football on December 6, and visit Jacksonville on Monday Night Football on December 14.

That’s why this week’s depth moves matter more than they look. A healthy, settled roster is how you survive a stretch like that.

WHAT TO WATCH ON WEDNESDAY

Wednesday is the first real practice of the week, and it should answer several questions.

First, does Dunker’s practice window open? McCarthy said a decision was coming before practice.

Second, do Wilson and Pittman practice? If their windows open, they’re on the road back.

Third, is Elliott on the field? His status will tell us whether Spears-Jennings needs to prepare to start.

Fourth, does McCormick go on injured reserve? That move would confirm a longer absence and open a roster spot.

And fifth, keep an eye on the depth chart. Steelers Depot ran a headline Tuesday about the team’s updated Week 11 depth chart. I wasn’t able to open it, so I can’t tell you exactly what it shows, but it’s worth checking who’s listed at left guard and safety.

THE EAGLES ARE WAITING

The transcript ends by pointing to the next challenge: Philadelphia, on Sunday, November 22.

Kickoff is at 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS, after the NFL flexed the game from its original 4:25 slot, according to Steelers Now. The Eagles are 2-7 and have lost seven straight, according to Yahoo Sports. They’re coming off a bye, and they’ll start Joe Milton at quarterback with Jalen Hurts out for the season.

The Eagles are making moves of their own this week. The Northeast Times ran a headline about Philadelphia promoting players for Steelers week and leaving two spots open on its 53-man roster. I couldn’t open that article, so I won’t go into the details.

And as always, there’s the history. The Steelers haven’t won a regular-season game in Philadelphia since 1965, according to Steelers.com.

The transcript asks the right questions for that game. Can the line protect Rodgers without McCormick? Can the run game take pressure off him? Can the defense get pressure? And can McCarthy get this team to respond? As the transcript puts it, one loss doesn’t destroy a season, but the response tells you a lot.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

AJ Finley: smart, low-cost depth. Elevate him for Philadelphia only if Elliott is out and the team needs another special teams body.

Left guard: Spencer Anderson starts. Open Gennings Dunker’s window on Wednesday and let him earn snaps in practice.

McCormick: if the medical news points to a long absence, place him on injured reserve and use the spot for Dunker or a returning receiver.

Receivers: activate Roman Wilson for Philadelphia if he practices fully all week. Michael Pittman Jr. can follow for Denver.

Secondary: if Elliott sits, give Spears-Jennings help over the top and keep Jalen Ramsey as the communicator in the back end.

Pass rush: line Watt up in different spots all game. Don’t let one tackle see him from the same angle twice in a row.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. Nobody released a star player ten minutes ago, or at any point after the Bengals loss. The latest move was the opposite: a depth safety signed to the practice squad, with no one cut to make room.

The real story is still the left side of the offensive line. Mason McCormick is hurt, but he’s still leading, checking in on meetings the day after being carted off. Spencer Anderson gets his job back for now. And a rookie named Gennings Dunker may be about to get his chance.

And the Finley signing is a reminder that the front office is paying attention to every weak spot the Bengals exposed, not just the obvious ones.

Wednesday’s practice should give us a lot of answers. Sunday in Philadelphia will give us the rest.
