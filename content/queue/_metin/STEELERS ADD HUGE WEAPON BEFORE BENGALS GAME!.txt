Steelers Nation, three days. That’s all that’s left before the NFL trade deadline closes at 4 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10. So let me give you the headline the honest way, before the rumor mill does it the other way. As of right now, the Steelers have not completed a trade. No new receiver has walked through the doors on the South Side. Nobody has signed a contract. If you saw “added” in a title and pictured a press release, that press release doesn’t exist yet.

But here’s why this weekend matters so much. The weapon this team has been linked to for two straight weeks is still on the board, his price has dropped, Pittsburgh is reportedly one of the teams that has already made an offer, and a respected local insider said this morning that the fit is close to perfect. On top of that, there’s a veteran on the Steelers’ own roster who could be heading out the other direction, meaning Omar Khan might be a buyer and a seller in the same week.

So let’s walk through it. The receiver. The price. The competition. The linebacker who could be moved. The pass rusher who isn’t going anywhere. A defensive line update you need to hear. And the weapon Pittsburgh already has, who might matter more than any trade. Then my board, labeled as opinion, not a report.

THE WEAPON EVERYONE IS TALKING ABOUT: JOSH DOWNS

Let’s start with the name at the top of every Steelers deadline conversation: Indianapolis Colts receiver Josh Downs.

The backstory matters here, so let’s set it up. The Colts’ season has gone sideways. They started two and five. General manager Chris Ballard was fired after a Week 6 loss to Tennessee, and Ed Dodds took over on an interim basis. Since then, Indianapolis has been moving veterans. All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson went to Seattle. DeForest Buckner went to San Francisco. And then, according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, Downs asked for a trade himself.

How did Downs handle it publicly? He didn’t confirm it, and he didn’t deny it. His words: “I’m open to whatever.” And then: “Whatever comes my way, comes my way and we go from there.” That’s a player who knows a change is coming and isn’t going to make a scene about it.

His head coach, Shane Steichen, took a similar tone: “You’re a professional. Obviously you requested a trade, but you’re on this football team.”

Now, the numbers. Through seven games, according to Yardbarker, Downs had twenty-six catches on forty-six targets for three hundred seven yards, and he was leading the Colts in receiving. The transcript this video is based on puts him at thirty catches, three hundred thirty yards and one touchdown. That could reflect another game since then, but I couldn’t confirm the updated line, so I’ll give you both and let you decide which one to trust.

Over his career, the profile is consistent. Downs came out of North Carolina as a third-round pick, seventy-ninth overall, in 2023. His best season was 2024, with seventy-two catches, eight hundred three yards and five touchdowns. And here’s the part that matters most for a deadline deal: he’s in the final year of his rookie contract, a four-year deal worth about five and a half million dollars. That means he’s cheap for the rest of this season, and he becomes a free agent after it.

WHY DOWNS FITS WHAT PITTSBURGH ACTUALLY NEEDS

Here’s where the fit gets interesting.

Downs is not a big, physical outside receiver. Pittsburgh already has one of those in DK Metcalf. Downs does his damage from the slot and underneath. He creates quick separation, he catches the ball in traffic, and he gives a quarterback an easy answer when the first read is covered. For Aaron Rodgers, who has been getting the ball out fast and living close to the line of scrimmage, that is the exact kind of player who keeps drives alive.

Think about what defenses have been doing. They roll coverage toward Metcalf. They sit on the shorter throws. And when the receiver room is thin, there aren’t enough reliable options to punish them. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with a foot injury and has played in only three games. Roman Wilson is on injured reserve as well. Rookie Germie Bernard has stepped up, but asking a rookie to carry a passing game through a division race is a lot.

Adding Downs wouldn’t make Pittsburgh’s receiver room elite overnight. It would make it complete. Metcalf outside, Downs in the slot, Bernard developing, and Pittman back at some point down the line. That’s a group that forces a defense to pick its poison instead of stacking the box against Jaylen Warren.

THE PRICE HAS DROPPED, AND THAT CHANGES EVERYTHING

Now let’s talk about money, because this is the part that makes Downs realistic rather than a fantasy.

At the start of the process, the reported asking price was a third-round pick. That’s a lot for a player in the final months of a rookie deal, and the market seemed to agree. Since then, by multiple accounts, the price has come down.

On October 30, NFL reporter Evan Sidery wrote: “The Chiefs and Steelers have both offered Day 3 draft compensation to the Colts for Josh Downs.” He added that he expected Indianapolis to end up with a fourth- or fifth-round pick. Remember, that’s one reporter’s information, and the deal he expected after that weekend hasn’t happened yet. But it’s a very specific claim, and it puts Pittsburgh directly in the bidding.

Then, on November 4, Colts insider Derek Schultz, speaking on 93.7 The Fan in Pittsburgh, went even further. “The Colts have no leverage right now. They have to sell,” he said. His suggested price: “If not a four, maybe a five and a pick swap, something along those lines.” He even pointed to the Buckner deal as evidence that Indianapolis is willing to accept less when it has to sell.

The transcript also cites a Sports Illustrated report from November 5 saying the compensation could fall in the third- to fourth-round range. I wasn’t able to locate that specific piece, so I’ll file it as reported but unconfirmed. Either way, the direction is the same. The price is coming down, not going up.

And then, this morning, Mark Kaboly of The Athletic weighed in. He said Omar Khan has explored the receiver market thoroughly, writing that every option “has been explored by Khan; you can be assured of that.” He named Brian Thomas Jr., Jauan Jennings, Calvin Ridley and Ladd McConkey among the names, and then he singled out Downs as the one that “seems like a perfect fit in terms of availability and price.”

When the Colts’ own insider says Indianapolis has no leverage, and the Steelers’ own insider says the fit is perfect, you can see why this is the name to watch over the next seventy-two hours.

THE COMPETITION: KANSAS CITY

But Pittsburgh isn’t alone. Kansas City wants Downs too.

According to Fowler, the Chiefs have been “calling around, at least, to see what they could potentially add.” And Sidery’s report puts Kansas City right alongside Pittsburgh with a Day 3 offer on the table.

That’s a meaningful detail. If the Steelers and Chiefs are bidding against each other, the Colts can play one off the other and try to squeeze a little more out of the deal. For Pittsburgh, it means the front office has to decide how badly it wants him. A late-round bid might not be enough if Kansas City is willing to bump its offer.

And there’s another layer to it. Kansas City is an AFC contender. Every receiver the Chiefs add is a receiver the Steelers might have to defend in January. Losing out on Downs to Kansas City would sting twice.

THE OTHER NAMES ON THE BOARD

Let’s quickly run through the alternatives, because Downs isn’t the only option.

Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville is a more explosive talent, but he would almost certainly cost Day 2 capital, and that’s a lot for a team trying to protect its future quarterback plan.

Calvin Ridley in Tennessee is the veteran fallback. Fowler said Ridley “is considered a backup plan for a few teams in the receiver market,” and added that “the Titans would need a quality Day 3 pick to part with Ridley.” He has just six catches for ninety-one yards this season, and his remaining contract money could complicate any deal.

Garrett Wilson of the Jets had been mentioned in earlier reports, but he was also in the concussion protocol this week, and his price would be much higher.

So when you line them up, Downs sits right in the sweet spot. Productive, young, cheap to acquire, cheap to pay and, apparently, available.

BUT WAIT: WILL THE STEELERS ACTUALLY DO ANYTHING?

Now, in the spirit of honesty, I have to give you the other side.

On November 4, Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette said on 102.5 WDVE that Pittsburgh isn’t planning a trade. His words: “They’re not looking to make a move, and they’re certainly not looking to get rid of one of their players.” He added that “right now they’re not actively thinking we need to make a trade,” though he left the door open if another team makes an offer.

So you have two respected voices pulling in slightly different directions. Dulac suggests patience. Kaboly suggests the homework has been done and Downs is the perfect fit. Those aren’t necessarily contradictory. A team can be “not actively looking” while still being ready to strike if the price falls into its range. And with the Colts reportedly lowering their asking price, that range might be getting closer.

THE VETERAN WHO COULD BE HEADING OUT

Now here’s the twist: Pittsburgh could be a buyer and a seller at the same time.

On November 6, according to Pro Football Rumors, Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated named linebacker Cole Holcomb as a player Pittsburgh may be open to moving. Breer described him as “a player the team looks to be willing to deal.”

Here’s the context. Holcomb is thirty years old. He’s on a two-year, five million dollar contract after Pittsburgh brought him back in March. His story is one of persistence. A serious knee injury in 2023 cost him the entire 2024 season, and he worked his way back onto the field. That’s not easy, and he deserves credit for it.

But under the new coaching staff, he’s been used, in Pro Football Rumors’ words, “strictly as a backup.” The transcript says he has four combined tackles on twenty-two defensive snaps through eight games. I couldn’t confirm those exact numbers, but they match the general picture of a limited role.

The transcript also mentions a forced fumble against New England in Week 3. I couldn’t verify that play, so I’m leaving it out.

Why would moving Holcomb matter? It wouldn’t bring back a big return on its own. But a small trade can free up a roster spot and a bit of cap flexibility, and in a deadline week, small moves can make bigger ones easier. Is it a key part of a Downs deal? Probably not directly. But it’s a sign that Omar Khan is looking at the whole roster, not just the holes.

THE PASS RUSHER WHO ISN’T GOING ANYWHERE

And now, the name Steelers fans absolutely don’t want to see in a trade headline: Alex Highsmith.

We’ve talked about the Heavy proposal sending him to Kansas City for a 2027 second-round pick. It’s a pitch, not a report. And the pushback from people who cover this team has been loud. The transcript cites Mark Kaboly, via Steelers Depot on November 2, saying he wouldn’t entertain moving Highsmith at all. I wasn’t able to open that article, so take the exact wording as reported. But it lines up with what Dulac and Fowler have said: Pittsburgh is not dismantling the pass rush that got it to six and two.

The transcript also credits Highsmith with twenty-six quarterback pressures through eight games. Again, I couldn’t confirm that number myself, but it’s a reminder that sacks don’t tell the full story. A pass rusher who consistently collapses the pocket forces bad throws and creates sacks for teammates. And with T.J. Watt, Nick Herbig and second-year edge Jack Sawyer in that same rotation, Pittsburgh has a group that can come at quarterbacks in waves.

One correction on Sawyer. The transcript calls him a rookie. He isn’t. Sawyer was a fourth-round pick in 2025 out of Ohio State, so this is his second NFL season.

THE DEFENSIVE LINE UPDATE YOU NEED TO HEAR

Speaking of Sawyer, he’s at the center of another storyline.

Sebastian Joseph-Day was placed on injured reserve on October 22, and Derrick Harmon is on IR as well. According to the transcript, citing a Steelers Wire report from November 5, Sawyer appeared on Gerry Dulac’s radio show and sounded excited about getting Harmon back, but less certain about Joseph-Day, leaving the impression that Joseph-Day might not return this season.

I want to handle that carefully. I couldn’t locate the original interview, and there’s been no official word from the team that Joseph-Day is done for the year. A teammate’s comment on a radio show is not a medical update. Until the Steelers say otherwise, the honest position is: unknown.

The good news is that the young players have held up. Rookie Gabriel Rubio and undrafted rookie Kevin Jobity Jr. have been playing real snaps, and the Steelers allowed just thirty-four rushing yards and piled up eight sacks in the Browns game. If Harmon returns as Sawyer suggested, this defensive front gets a lot stronger for the stretch run.

THE WEAPON PITTSBURGH ALREADY HAS

Now let me finish with the best news of the week, because it’s a reminder that the biggest weapon on this offense isn’t on another team’s roster.

Jaylen Warren.

Against Cleveland, he ran for one hundred forty-three yards. The transcript, citing NFL.com, adds that it came on seventeen carries with a touchdown and a fifty-one-yard run. And through eight games, he has nine hundred ninety-eight yards from scrimmage, second in the NFL. The transcript also says that’s the most by a Steelers player through eight games since James Conner had 1,085 in 2018. I couldn’t independently confirm the Conner comparison, but if it holds, that’s elite company.

And it’s not just Warren. Rico Dowdle has given Pittsburgh a second reliable runner, which means defenses can’t just key on one back.

Here’s why that matters for the Downs conversation. A great running game makes a slot receiver even more dangerous. When linebackers creep up to stop Warren, the middle of the field opens up for quick throws. That’s exactly where Downs does his best work. The two pieces would feed each other.

WHY THE BENGALS GAME RAISES THE STAKES

All of this is building toward November 15 in Cincinnati. The Bengals are five and two, right behind Pittsburgh in the AFC North. The Steelers won the first meeting in Week 3, but a road rematch is a different test.

If Pittsburgh adds Downs before Tuesday, he’d have a few practices to get up to speed before that game. Not a full playbook, but enough to run a handful of concepts and give Rodgers another target. That’s the realistic timeline for a deadline addition: limited role at first, bigger role by December.

And looking further ahead, the transcript lists Kansas City, Buffalo, Denver and Jacksonville as AFC teams the Steelers might have to beat in January. Against teams like that, you need more than one way to move the football. That’s the case for adding a weapon now.

MY TRADE BLOCK BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board, and please remember: this is opinion, not a report.

Josh Downs: yes. A fifth-round pick is an easy call. A fourth is still a yes. But here’s the wrinkle: Pittsburgh doesn’t own its 2027 fourth-round pick, since that went to Dallas in the Broderick Jones trade. So a “fourth” would likely mean a 2028 pick or a pick swap. If the Colts insist on a third, I walk away, especially for a player who will be a free agent in a few months.

Cole Holcomb: I’m fine moving him for a late-round pick swap. He’s earned respect for his comeback, but if he’s strictly a backup and another team gives him a bigger role, that’s a win for everybody.

Calvin Ridley: only as a backup plan, and only if Tennessee eats some of the money. Otherwise, pass.

Brian Thomas Jr.: pass at a Day 2 price. Too expensive for this team’s long-term plan.

Garrett Wilson: pass. The cost is too high, and the concussion is a concern.

Alex Highsmith: untouchable. Same goes for Watt, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren and Frazier.

The Colts reportedly have no leverage, Pittsburgh has reportedly already made an offer, and the local insider says the fit is perfect. At the same time, the front office appears willing to trim elsewhere on the roster to stay flexible, while keeping the core of this defense fully intact.
