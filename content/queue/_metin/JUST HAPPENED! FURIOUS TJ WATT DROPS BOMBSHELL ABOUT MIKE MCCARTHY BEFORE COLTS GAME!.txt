Back in August, at training camp, T.J. Watt stood in front of reporters and made a promise about Patrick Graham's new defense. He said the spring and the summer were about Graham introducing his system, and that "this fall is when we make that a cohesive unit." Fast forward to October 1st. Cleveland. Prime time. The defense is getting carved up, and Watt grabs a headset off an assistant coach so he can talk straight to Graham in the booth, live, in the middle of the game, with the cameras rolling. So here's the question I can't stop thinking about this Monday morning: did the fall Watt promised us ever actually arrive? Or is the relationship between the Steelers' best defender and their new defensive coordinator the most important storyline of this entire season?

What's up, Steelers Nation, welcome back to Second Floor. Last week, we broke down exactly what Watt said after the Browns loss, "way too easy," "not acceptable," all of it. Today we're doing something different. We're tracing this defense from Watt's August promise to that headset moment, and then forward to what has to happen this week against the Colts. Because a lot has changed over the weekend, including some news you're not going to love. If you're new here, hit subscribe and turn on notifications, because the next six days might decide what kind of season this is.

Let's start in August, because the context matters. When Mike McCarthy took over, he brought in Patrick Graham as defensive coordinator, a coach who'd run defenses before with the New York Giants and most recently with the Las Vegas Raiders. And Watt was openly excited. He talked about how the Steelers would stay a 3-4 base defense, but how Graham's system would move players all over the field to keep offenses guessing. He said Graham's defense would let him, Alex Highsmith and Nick Herbig line up in different spots so quarterbacks couldn't predict where pressure was coming from. Unpredictable. Aggressive. Multiple. That was the pitch.

And you know what? It made sense. This roster was built for that. You've got a former Defensive Player of the Year in Watt, a newly paid Nick Herbig, a productive Highsmith, rising young pieces like Jack Sawyer, and Cam Heyward still anchoring the middle. On paper, that's exactly the kind of personnel a creative coordinator dreams about.

And remember, this was a big change for Watt personally. For basically his entire career, he'd played in systems run by long-time Steelers coaches, first Keith Butler, then Teryl Austin. Watt won Defensive Player of the Year in 2021 and tied the single-season sack record with 22.5 in a defense built around one idea: let T.J. go get the quarterback. Graham's approach is different. It's built on disguise and movement, on making the offense guess. For a star who's used to being the focal point, that's an adjustment, even if he's fully on board. Learning a new defensive language at 31 years old, after nearly a decade in the same organization's way of doing things, is not as simple as flipping a switch.

Now let's fast forward through September, and see how that promise held up week by week.

Week 1, they beat Atlanta 20-13. Fine. Not spectacular, but a win. Week 2, they lost at New England, and the offense was the story. Week 3, they beat Cincinnati 30-27, and everybody celebrated the offense waking up. But if you looked closer, the defense had warning lights flashing. Joe Burrow threw for 282 yards and three touchdowns. Watt, according to PFF, had zero pressures. The secondary played a lot of soft zone, and Asante Samuel Jr. was benched. The only reason the defense got a happy ending was Derrick Harmon's strip-sack in the final minute.

Then came Cleveland. And that's where the cracks became impossible to ignore. Cleveland scored 21 points in the second quarter alone. Pittsburgh missed 14 tackles. The Browns piled up 183 yards after the catch. And Watt, the same guy who promised a cohesive unit by fall, was reaching for a headset to talk to his coordinator himself.

So when you put that timeline together, the story isn't just "Watt was frustrated after one loss." The story is that the defense Watt described in August, the attacking, unpredictable, multiple defense, hasn't shown up consistently yet. And the guy who sold it to us was the first one to say so out loud.

Now let me be fair to Patrick Graham, because I think a lot of fans are jumping too fast. Look at what he's been working with. His veteran safety, DeShon Elliott, has been on injured reserve all season. Joey Porter Jr., who was supposed to be his best cover corner, never played a single snap for him because of a back injury and a contract standoff, and then got traded to Dallas. Jamel Dean, the veteran corner signed this offseason, has been dealing with an ankle problem since Week 1, and aggravated it in Cleveland. Jalen Ramsey broke his wrist against Cincinnati and has been playing in a cast. Brandin Echols was in the concussion protocol. And now Derrick Harmon, his best young interior lineman, is hurt too.

An aggressive, multiple defense needs reliable pieces on the back end. You can't blitz from everywhere if you don't trust your corners to hold up in man coverage. So in fairness to Graham, some of that soft zone we've seen might not be his preference. It might be survival. When you're missing that many defensive backs, you protect the deep part of the field first. The problem is that protecting the deep part of the field turns your defense into exactly what Watt hates: a unit that sits back and waits.

Let me explain the math here, because it's the heart of the tension. Every time a defense sends an extra rusher, it takes a body out of coverage. Rush five, and you've got six defenders covering the field. Rush six, and you're down to five, and somebody is usually alone on an island. That's fine when you've got corners you trust in man coverage. It's a disaster when your corners are a rookie, a guy coming off a benching, a guy with a broken wrist and a practice-squad call-up. So when fans ask, "Why doesn't Graham just blitz more?" the answer is that every blitz is a bet on the secondary. And right now, the secondary is a bet a lot of coordinators wouldn't want to make.

And that's what makes the headset moment so fascinating. Think about it from both sides. From Watt's side, he's a 31-year-old superstar, he turns 32 this Sunday, and he knows his window is closing. Aaron Rodgers is retiring after this season. Heyward is in the late stage of his career. Watt wants to attack right now. From Graham's side, he's a first-year coordinator trying to keep a battered secondary from getting torched every week. Both of them are right about the problem. They might just have different ideas about the solution.

So what happens next? Here's the honest truth. The headset moment can go one of two directions. In the best case, it becomes a turning point. Graham hears his star, they sit down this week, and they find a middle ground that lets Watt and the pass rush be more aggressive while the secondary gets simpler assignments it can actually execute. In the worst case, it becomes the first sign of a power struggle, and every bad drive from here on out gets blamed on the coordinator. And I'll tell you right now, the national media will be watching for that second version.

And the internet, of course, had its fun with the headset clip. When the moment went viral, one fan joked online, "Send him to Dallas," a not-so-subtle shot at the way this front office has been shipping players to the Cowboys lately. Pickens. Broderick Jones. Porter. It's a joke, and Watt isn't going anywhere. But the fact that fans immediately made that connection tells you how raw the Porter trade still is, and how much this fan base is watching the relationship between the stars and the people making decisions.

My opinion? I think this is fixable, and I think the fact that Watt did it in the open is actually a good sign. Players who are checked out don't grab headsets. Players who don't care don't say "not acceptable." That's a guy who's fully invested. Now Graham has to channel that, not fight it.

And now let me hit you with the weekend news, because it changes the picture for the Colts game in a big way.

First, Derrick Harmon. According to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, Harmon is expected to miss a few weeks with his foot injury, and injured reserve is a possibility, though it's not believed to be long-term. That's a real blow. Harmon was one of the few players on this defense who'd been consistently disruptive, and his strip-sack is the reason Pittsburgh beat Cincinnati. Without him, the interior pass rush loses its most explosive young player, and that makes Watt's job on the edge even harder. When the middle of the pocket doesn't collapse, quarterbacks can step up away from the edge rushers. So losing Harmon actually makes the Watt and Graham conversation even more urgent.

Second, Jamel Dean. Rapoport reported that Dean could miss a couple of weeks as he works back from that ankle injury. So for the Colts game, and possibly beyond, the secondary will be thinner than ever.

And third, the news that's going to sting. Joey Porter Jr. made his Cowboys debut on Sunday against Houston, and he looked exactly like the corner Pittsburgh was missing. He played 24 snaps, 46 percent of the defensive plays, on a snap count. He was targeted just once in 19 coverage snaps and didn't allow a single catch. According to reports, he lined up against Houston's top receiver Nico Collins multiple times, and the Texans simply refused to throw his way. Dallas won 34-30. And afterward, Porter said, "I'm a dog at the end of the day, and I want to be out there with the guys."

Now, I've always been careful with the Porter story, and I'm going to stay careful. It's one game, on a limited snap count, against a quarterback who chose to avoid him. But you can see why Steelers fans are frustrated. The exact thing Graham's defense needed, a corner who can take away one side of the field so the pass rush can attack, just showed up in Dallas, four days after the Steelers traded him for a 2028 second-round pick and a conditional sixth. And that one-word "No" Watt gave when he was asked if anything was weighing on the defense? It looks a little different after Sunday, doesn't it?

Okay, let's look at the division, because the weekend also moved the standings.

Baltimore beat Tennessee on Sunday and moved to 3-1, though they may have a problem of their own after Lamar Jackson suffered an ankle injury in that game. Cincinnati lost to Jacksonville 22-17 and dropped to 2-2, even though Joe Burrow threw for 428 yards. So here's where the AFC North stands. Cleveland 3-1. Baltimore 3-1. Pittsburgh 2-2. Cincinnati 2-2. The Steelers aren't in last place, and they hold the head-to-head edge over the Bengals after beating them in Week 3. But they're a game behind two teams, and with Lamar's ankle being monitored, this division could get very interesting very quickly.

Here's why that matters for the Watt and Graham story. If the Steelers beat the Colts on Sunday, they're 3-2, right in the thick of the race, and the headset moment becomes a footnote. If they lose, they're 2-3, two games behind with a trip to Tampa Bay next, and every frustration, every sideline argument, every trade rumor gets ten times louder.

So let me lay out what I need to see from this defense against Indianapolis, built around the promise Watt made in August.

Number one, I need to see the "multiple" defense Watt described. Move Watt around. Line him up inside on obvious passing downs. Put Herbig and Highsmith on the same side. Use Sawyer as a fresh rusher. Make the Colts' offensive line guess. That's what Graham was hired to do, and with Harmon out, creative pressure is the only way to manufacture a pass rush.

And this is where Graham's background can actually help. In his past stops, he built a reputation for being game-plan specific, for changing his approach based on the opponent rather than running the same call sheet every week. That flexibility is exactly what this defense needs right now. Build a plan around who's healthy, not around who's on the depth chart. If that means more simulated pressures, showing blitz and dropping into coverage, or bringing a defensive back off the edge while Watt drops, fine. Give quarterbacks something confusing to look at before the snap. Watt himself said in August that this defense was supposed to keep offenses on their toes. Let's see it.

Number two, I need to see simple, sound coverage on the back end. With Dean likely out and Ramsey still in a cast, nobody expects lockdown man coverage across the field. But simple doesn't have to mean soft. Play disciplined zone, rally to the football, and tackle. Fourteen missed tackles against Cleveland was the real killer. You can live with a five-yard completion. You can't live with a five-yard completion that turns into twenty-five.

Number three, I need to see the young guys step up. Daylen Everette is getting a crash course in NFL cornerback life, and he's had a pass breakup in each of the last two games. Robert Spears-Jennings grabbed his first career interception in Cleveland off a Jack Sawyer tip. And if DeShon Elliott's practice window opens this week and he can get back on the field, that would give Graham a veteran voice in the back end at the perfect moment. These are the pieces that have to grow up fast.

Number four, and this is the big one, I need to see Watt and Graham on the same page. Not in a press release. On the field. If Watt is playing free and aggressive, if the defense is attacking instead of waiting, you'll know the conversation happened. If we're watching another week of a passive shell and Watt throwing his hands up on the sideline, you'll know it didn't.

Now let's hit our trade segment, and as always, this is opinion, not a report. The Porter debut is going to put even more pressure on Omar Khan to add a cornerback before the November 10th trade deadline. And with Dean out a couple of weeks, Ramsey in a cast, and Harmon possibly heading to injured reserve, I don't see how this front office stands pat. The Steelers have extra draft capital now, including that 2028 second from Dallas. If they believe in this season, and with Rodgers retiring, they should, then they need to use some of it on the secondary. It doesn't have to be a superstar. It has to be a reliable corner who lets Graham call the defense he actually wants to call. And honestly, adding a corner might be the single best thing the front office can do for T.J. Watt. Because a pass rusher can only get home when the coverage gives him time to get there.

And let me add one more thought on the bigger picture, because this is about more than one week. Watt is getting paid around 41 million dollars a year on the three-year, 123 million dollar extension he signed last summer. Herbig just signed a four-year, 100 million dollar deal. The Steelers have poured money into their pass rush. If the coverage behind them can't hold up, all that money is being wasted. The front office built a Ferrari of a pass rush and then gave it a flat tire in the secondary. Watt grabbing the headset was, in a way, the Ferrari honking the horn.

There's one more layer to this I don't want to skip. Watt's frustration and Rodgers' frustration came out on the same night. On offense, Rodgers was upset that plays were coming in too slowly from the sideline, and he and McCarthy had a heated exchange that McCarthy later said he regretted. On defense, Watt went around the normal chain of communication to reach Graham directly. Two of the biggest stars on the roster, on two different sides of the ball, both signaling that the communication from the coaching staff wasn't fast enough or clear enough. That's not a coincidence, Steelers Nation. That's a first-year staff still figuring out how to run a game. The good news is that operational problems are among the most fixable problems in football. The bad news is that you don't get many weeks to fix them in a season this tight.

So let's bring it home. On Sunday, T.J. Watt turns 32 years old. He'll celebrate his birthday at Acrisure Stadium against the Indianapolis Colts, in front of a home crowd that has watched him become one of the greatest defensive players in franchise history. What a perfect stage for this defense to finally become the unit he promised us back in August.

So Steelers Nation, here's where I land. The headset moment wasn't a meltdown. It was a superstar demanding the defense he was promised. Patrick Graham deserves patience, because he's been coaching a secondary held together with tape. But patience doesn't mean waiting forever. This week, with ten days of rest behind them and the Colts coming to town, I want to see the attacking, unpredictable defense Watt described in August. If we see it, this season is very much alive. If we don't, the fall Watt talked about might never come.
