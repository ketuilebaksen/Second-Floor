Steelers Nation, let’s talk about the word “save.”

The headline says the Steelers are getting star wide receivers to save their offense. And I want to be honest with you about what that means, because there are two ways to read it.

If you think it means the Steelers went out and signed a big-name free agent this week, they didn’t. There’s no new star coming in from another team. Nobody signed Brandon Aiyuk. Nobody signed Tyreek Hill. Nobody signed Darius Slayton.

But if you read it the other way, the headline is closer to the truth than you’d think. Because the Steelers are about to get two receivers back that they built this offense around in the offseason. One of them is a veteran with two 1,000-yard seasons on his résumé. The other is a young speed receiver who was just starting to click with Aaron Rodgers when he got hurt. Both practiced fully this week. Both are listed as questionable for Sunday in Philadelphia. And both are still on injured reserve, which means the Steelers have until Saturday afternoon to bring them back.

So today, I want to lay out a blueprint. Not just who’s coming back, but how they fit, what they fix, what they don’t, and what has to happen on Sunday for this to actually “save” anything.

And at the end, the board. Opinion, not a report.

Let’s get into it.

PART ONE: WHY THE OFFENSE NEEDS SAVING

Let’s start with the problem, because you can’t sell a solution without one.

In the 38-18 loss to Cincinnati, the Steelers managed 222 total yards, their lowest of the season, according to the Northeast Times. And the receivers were close to invisible. According to EssentiallySports, the wide receivers combined for five catches and 69 yards, with DK Metcalf accounting for 54 of those yards and the touchdown. Rookie Germie Bernard didn’t catch a pass on three targets.

That’s not a slump. That’s a position group that ran out of bodies.

And it wasn’t a one-game issue. Michael Pittman Jr. hasn’t played since October 1. Roman Wilson hasn’t played since October 11. For six weeks, the Steelers have been asking a rookie, a veteran coming off a calf injury and a couple of depth receivers to support Metcalf. Against a team like Cincinnati, that wasn’t enough.

PART TWO: THE METCALF PROBLEM, AND WHY IT’S NOT ALL HIS FAULT

The video I’m working from today spends some time on DK Metcalf, and I think it handles it fairly.

It says Sports Illustrated highlighted concerns about Metcalf’s drops and his ability to separate, and that Pro Football Focus has credited him with six drops this season. I haven’t seen that exact PFF number myself, so treat it as the video’s figure. But the broader concern is real. We’ve talked about Metcalf’s drops on this channel for weeks.

What I appreciate about the video is that it doesn’t stop there. It points out that the passing game has a lot of moving parts. The line has to protect. Rodgers has to have time to read the defense. The receivers have to separate and finish. And the coaches have to design something that gives all of them a chance. When several of those break down at once, the whole thing looks broken.

That’s exactly right. And it’s why the returns matter so much. Metcalf hasn’t had enough help. When you’re the only proven receiver on the field, every safety in the league knows where to look. Give him two more real threats, and the picture changes.

PART THREE: MEET THE “STARS”

So let’s talk about who’s actually coming back, and why they might be closer to “star” than you think.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: THE RÉSUMÉ

Pittman spent six seasons with the Indianapolis Colts before the Steelers acquired him this offseason. And during that time, he wasn’t just a guy. He had two 1,000-yard seasons in Indianapolis, including more than 100 catches in 2023. He was the kind of receiver quarterbacks trust on third down.

That’s why the Steelers wanted him. Not as a deep threat, but as a reliable, physical target who catches everything in the middle of the field.

One quick note on his contract. The video I’m working from says Pittman signed a new three-year contract with the Steelers. The Post-Gazette described it as a two-year, $35 million extension. I can’t reconcile those two descriptions from here, so I’ll just say he was signed to a new deal this offseason, and leave the exact terms aside.

PITTMAN IN HIS OWN WORDS

The video says Pittman has acknowledged how important it is to be physically able to help the team when he returns. Here’s what that sounded like, according to the Post-Gazette: “I don’t want to go out there and hurt this team because I can’t do something that I should be able to do.”

I keep coming back to that quote because it tells you how he’s approaching this. He’s not chasing a stat line. He’s not trying to prove anything to fans. He wants to be useful. And for a player with his résumé, coming off an injury that’s limited him all year, that’s exactly the right mindset.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.: THE LOST SEASON SO FAR

And here’s the part that’s been so frustrating. As the video says, Pittman has appeared in only three games this season, with seven catches for 89 yards.

The video makes the right point: that isn’t a reflection of his ability. It’s availability. When a player misses this much time, it’s hard to build chemistry with a quarterback and settle into a consistent role. That’s especially true with a quarterback like Rodgers, who’s notoriously demanding about timing and trust.

The good news, as we covered this week, is that he went from limited on Wednesday to full on Thursday, and was listed as questionable on Friday’s final report, according to Steelers.com. That’s the first time in weeks the arrow’s been pointing straight up.

ROMAN WILSON: THE RÉSUMÉ

Wilson’s story is different. He’s younger, faster and still writing his résumé.

He was a third-round pick in 2024 out of Michigan, and his rookie year was mostly lost to injuries. This season was supposed to be his breakout, and it was starting to look like one. Before he got hurt, he had 14 catches for 188 yards and two touchdowns in five games, according to the Post-Gazette. That includes a 38-yard touchdown catch against Cincinnati back in Week 3.

ROMAN WILSON: WHAT HE DID WITH THE TIME OFF

What I liked this week is how Wilson talked about his time away.

According to Steelers.com, he said: “The biggest advice I got from teammates and coaches was become a film junkie. Be a film rat.” And he called the time off “valuable,” saying he spent four or five weeks just watching football.

That’s not what you always hear from a player coming off injured reserve. Usually it’s all about the rehab. Wilson’s talking about getting smarter. And remember what Rodgers said about him this week, according to the Post-Gazette: “I feel like Ro’ was getting more comfortable, playing with confidence and playing fast.”

A receiver who plays fast and has spent five weeks studying defenses could be exactly what this offense needs.

DK METCALF BY THE NUMBERS

Before we move on, let’s put Metcalf’s season in context, since he’s the one constant in this room.

According to the Northeast Times, Metcalf has 35 catches for 432 yards this season. In Cincinnati, he had two catches for 54 yards and a touchdown, including the fourth-down touchdown catch that was one of the few bright moments of the night.

So he’s still making plays. He’s just doing it with almost no help. Think about it this way: Metcalf has been the only receiver on this team with a real track record for most of the season. That’s a heavy load for anybody. With Pittman and Wilson back, Metcalf goes from being the entire plan to being one part of it. That might be the best thing that’s happened to him all year.

PART FOUR: THE ROOKIE IN THE MIDDLE

The video I’m working from also brings Germie Bernard into this conversation, and I’m glad it did, because he matters to how this all fits.

The video points out that Bernard put together consecutive games with at least 80 receiving yards earlier this season. That checks out with what we covered at the time. He had 80 yards against the Colts in his first start and 87 against Tampa Bay, including the overtime game-winning touchdown.

But since then, defenses have adjusted. And in Cincinnati, he was held without a catch. That’s a normal rookie curve. The question is what happens to his role when Pittman and Wilson come back.

WHERE BERNARD FITS NOW

Here’s my view. Bernard’s role should change, but it shouldn’t shrink to nothing.

He was forced into a WR2 role because there was nobody else. With Pittman and Wilson back, he can slide into more of a rotational role, where he gets favorable matchups instead of the other team’s best cornerback. That’s often how rookies find their footing again.

And with Denver coming to Pittsburgh on Friday, November 27, on a short week, the Steelers are going to need all three of these guys. You can’t load up Pittman and Wilson with full workloads in their first game back and then ask them to do it again five days later. Bernard’s going to have plenty of snaps.

PART FIVE: THE BLUEPRINT FOR SUNDAY

So let’s say both Pittman and Wilson are activated by Saturday afternoon. What does the offense look like?

Here’s how I’d draw it up. This is my opinion, not a report on the game plan.

On early downs, the base offense leans on Jaylen Warren and the run game, with Metcalf and Pittman on the field together. Pittman’s size and blocking ability help the run game, and his presence forces a linebacker to respect the middle on play action.

On obvious passing downs, Wilson comes in. His speed stretches the field and keeps the safeties honest. That opens up the intermediate area for Pittman and the tight ends, Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington.

Bernard rotates in throughout, especially if Pittman or Wilson hits a snap limit.

And Metcalf? He becomes what he should’ve been all season: a big outside receiver who gets one-on-one chances because the defense can’t cheat toward him.

WHY THE TIGHT ENDS MATTER IN THIS BLUEPRINT

The tight ends might be the biggest winners of all.

According to the Northeast Times, Freiermuth has five touchdown catches and Washington has three. That’s more than half of Rodgers’ 15 touchdown passes. They’ve carried this passing game while the receiver room was hurting.

Now imagine defenses having to account for Pittman, Wilson and Metcalf at the same time. The linebackers and safeties who’ve been sitting on the tight ends suddenly have more to worry about. That should make Freiermuth and Washington even more dangerous in the red zone.

THREE SCENARIOS FOR SATURDAY

Since the activations haven’t happened yet, let’s walk through the three ways this could go.

Scenario one: both Pittman and Wilson are activated. That’s the best case. The receiver room is as healthy as it’s been since September, and the blueprint I just laid out becomes possible. This is what I expect, based on how both practiced this week.

Scenario two: only Wilson is activated. That would tell you the Steelers want one more week of practice for Pittman’s foot before putting him in a game, with the Denver game as the target. It wouldn’t be a bad sign. It would just be caution. Wilson would get a bigger role, and Bernard would stay in a heavier rotation.

Scenario three: neither is activated. I’d be surprised, given the full practices. But if it happens, it would most likely mean a setback we haven’t heard about, or a roster-spot problem the team couldn’t solve in time. In that case, the offense would look a lot like it did in Cincinnati, and the pressure on the run game would go way up.

PART SIX: WHAT THE RECEIVERS CAN’T FIX

Now, let’s be honest about the limits, because the word “save” sets the bar really high.

The video I’m working from makes the key point: potential isn’t production. More healthy options give the Steelers more flexibility, more combinations and a better way to spread targets around. But the players still have to execute. And returning players still have to prove they can perform at the level the team needs.

Here are three things that healthy receivers alone won’t fix.

LIMIT ONE: THE OFFENSIVE LINE

First, the line. Mason McCormick is on injured reserve with a dislocated ankle, and Spencer Anderson takes over at left guard. Anderson said this week, according to Steelers.com: “(I’m) as ready as I’m going to be.” He also said he has to take ownership of McCormick’s role as the line’s vocal leader.

If the protection breaks down, it doesn’t matter who’s running routes. The Steelers have allowed 26 sacks this season, according to the Northeast Times. Rodgers needs time.

LIMIT TWO: THE SLOW STARTS

Second, the starts. In Cincinnati, Rodgers opened 0-for-8, the first time in his career he’d started a game that way. Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio has admitted the offense struggles to find a rhythm early, especially on the road.

Receivers can help with that, but the play-calling has to help too. Easy completions early. Get the returning guys a quick touch so they settle in. Build from there.

LIMIT THREE: GAME SHAPE

Third, conditioning. Practice is not a game. Pittman hasn’t played in seven weeks. Wilson hasn’t played in about six. Even if they look sharp in practice, game speed and game hits are different. I’d expect both to be on some kind of snap count.

So don’t expect 100-yard games from either one. A realistic goal is three or four catches each, maybe one explosive play. If they give you that, they’ve done their job.

PART SEVEN: WHAT ABOUT OUTSIDE STARS?

Since the title says “star wide receivers,” let’s quickly address the outside names, because they keep coming up.

There’s no report of the Steelers pursuing anyone from outside. Brandon Aiyuk’s situation in San Francisco is coming to a head, with the 49ers’ roster exemption running through November 23, according to EssentiallySports. But the most natural fit there appears to be Washington, given his reconnection with Jayden Daniels. Darius Slayton is a free agent who Bleacher Report’s Moe Moton named as a possible fit for Pittsburgh, but that’s an opinion piece, not a report of interest.

My view: the Steelers don’t need an outside star this week. They’re about to get their own back. If one of them suffers a setback, then we can talk about other options.

HOW THE OFFENSE CAN HELP THE DEFENSE

Here’s one more angle people miss. A healthier receiver room doesn’t just help the offense. It helps the defense too.

In Cincinnati, the offense kept going three-and-out, and the defense kept getting sent back onto the field against Joe Burrow and Ja’Marr Chase. That wears any defense down. And this week, the defense is dealing with its own injury concerns. Nick Herbig is questionable with hip and rib injuries after missing practice all week, according to Steelers.com.

If Pittman and Wilson help the offense sustain even two or three more drives, that’s 15 or 20 fewer snaps for a defense that might be missing one of its best pass rushers. That’s how a receiver room can “save” more than just the offense.

PART EIGHT: THE EAGLES’ SIDE

Let’s look at what Pittman and Wilson would be lining up against.

According to the Northeast Times, Philadelphia’s defense is allowing 24.6 points per game and has forced just five turnovers all season, last in the NFL. Its defense allows 5.2 yards per play. That’s not a group that’s been taking the ball away.

And according to Steelers.com, the Eagles ruled out linebacker Zack Baun on Friday, which affects their coverage underneath, not just their run defense. That’s another reason I like the tight ends this week.

On the other hand, the Eagles have had extra time to prepare, coming off their bye. So don’t expect them to be unprepared for Pittsburgh’s new receivers. They’ll have studied what the Steelers did with Pittman and Wilson early in the season.

THE YOUTH MOVEMENT BEHIND ALL OF THIS

The video I’m working from makes a broader point I liked. This coaching staff has shown it’s willing to play young players when the situation calls for it.

Rookie right tackle Max Iheanachor, the first-round pick, has been starting since Week 3. Germie Bernard has been a regular part of the passing game. On defense, rookie corner Daylen Everette and rookie safety Robert Spears-Jennings have both been given real opportunities.

That matters for the receiver room too. It tells you the coaches won’t hesitate to lean on Bernard if Pittman or Wilson isn’t ready for a full workload. And it tells you rookie guard Gennings Dunker, who practiced fully all week and is listed as questionable, will get his chance at some point. As Dunker himself told Steelers.com, he doesn’t know where he is skill-wise yet, so I wouldn’t expect it this week. But the door is open.

PART NINE: THE ROSTER MOVES TO WATCH TODAY

Here’s what to watch for on Saturday.

As it’s generally understood, teams have until 4 p.m. Eastern the day before the game to make roster moves. So by this afternoon, we should know whether Pittman and Wilson, and maybe rookie guard Gennings Dunker, are being activated to the 53-man roster.

McCormick’s move to injured reserve opened one roster spot. If the Steelers activate more than one player, they’ll need to make room somewhere else. Watch for a corresponding move, whether that’s a release, another injured reserve placement or something else. I’m not going to guess who it’ll be.

PART TEN: THE VERDICT ON “SAVE”

So, can these receivers save the offense?

Here’s my honest answer. They can’t save it alone. But they can change it.

They give Rodgers the kind of receiver room the Steelers planned for in August. They give Metcalf help he hasn’t had in weeks. They give the tight ends more space. And they give the coaches more ways to attack a defense.

What they can’t do is block for Rodgers, call the first 15 plays, or make the Steelers start fast on the road. That’s on the line and the coaching staff.

If all of those pieces come together on Sunday, the Steelers will look like a completely different offense from the one we saw in Cincinnati. If they don’t, it won’t matter how many receivers come off injured reserve.

And there’s one more layer to this. The Steelers haven’t won a regular-season game in Philadelphia since 1965, according to Steelers.com. Streaks like that don’t mean much to the players on the field, but they do mean something about how hard this building has been for Pittsburgh. A full receiver room won’t break a 60-year drought on its own. But it gives this team its best shot in a long time.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for Sunday’s receiver plan. Opinion, not a report.

Roman Wilson: activate. Snap count around 30. Three to five targets, including one shot down the field.

Michael Pittman Jr.: activate. Snap count around 25 to 30. Use him on early downs and in the red zone.

DK Metcalf: keep him as the X receiver. Get him a quick, easy catch on the first drive to build his rhythm.

Germie Bernard: rotate in. Pick his spots against favorable matchups.

Tight ends: Freiermuth and Washington stay the red zone weapons. Target the middle with Baun out.

Outside receivers: no signings this week. Revisit only if there’s a setback.

Workload plan: manage everyone’s snaps, because Denver is five days later.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers didn’t go out and sign a star receiver this week. But they might be getting two of their own back at exactly the right time.

Pittman brings the résumé. Wilson brings the speed. Metcalf gets the help he’s needed. And the tight ends get more room to work.

That won’t save the offense on its own. But for the first time in weeks, Aaron Rodgers might walk onto the field with a full set of weapons. And with a quarterback like that, a full set of weapons can go a very long way.
