Former first-team All-Pro. Eleven interceptions in one season. Coached by Mike McCarthy for five years in Dallas. So let me tell you exactly where this stands, because I know what you saw on the thumbnail and I'm not going to waste your time. On Monday, Pittsburgh went out and signed a Super Bowl champion cornerback off another team's practice squad. And the Diggs door? It's wide open, the price is zero in trade capital, and one of the most plugged-in Steelers reporters in the business is saying out loud that it's a real option. Today we're going to break down the weapon they added, the weapon they could still add, why they needed one in the first place, and how all of it sets up Sunday against the Colts. This is James with Second Floor, and this one's a big one.

Let me be straight with you before we go any further, because that's how we do it on this channel. As of right now, Trevon Diggs has not signed with the Pittsburgh Steelers. He's still on the Seattle Seahawks' practice squad. Nobody has reported that Pittsburgh has called. What has happened is this: the Steelers made a cornerback signing on Monday, they kept a roster spot open afterward, and the Diggs conversation in Pittsburgh is louder today than it's been all season. So when you hear "huge weapon," here's what that means: one veteran already in the building, and a second, much bigger name sitting right there for the taking. Now let's get into it.

First, why did Pittsburgh need a weapon at all? To answer that, you have to go back to the film from Cleveland. I rewatched the whole game, and the secondary story is ugly. The Steelers traded Joey Porter Jr. to Dallas right before the Browns game. Then in the first half on Thursday night, Jamel Dean went down with an ankle injury and never came back. NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reported over the weekend that Dean could miss "a couple of weeks," which likely means the Colts game and maybe the trip to Tampa Bay.

And once Dean left, the tape tells you everything. Rayshawn Jenkins started at safety and had a brutal night. He missed the tackle on Jaleel McLaughlin's 28-yard touchdown run, and he was the one out of position on the busted Cover 2 that let Denzel Boston run free for 60 yards. A lot of fans pinned that play on rookie Daylen Everette, but McCarthy set the record straight on Monday: "I know there's conversation about the big play; that was not his play." Jenkins got the worst grade on the entire defense from Pro Football Focus, a 25.6, and he was eventually benched. Jalen Ramsey was playing through a broken wrist and got lost in space on the final drive. Asante Samuel Jr. had a moment where he celebrated what he thought was a pass breakup while the receiver came back with a big gain. And the missed tackles were everywhere. Patrick Queen, Payton Wilson, Jaquan Brisker, they all had plays they'd like back.

Deshaun Watson went 24 of 33 for 268 yards. The Browns scored 21 points in the second quarter alone, on just 20 plays. And on the final drive, with the game tied, Watson moved Cleveland into range for Andre Szmyt's 56-yard field goal with 10 seconds left. You don't need me to tell you the secondary was the weak spot. Cleveland told everyone.

So the front office had to do something. And on Monday, it did.

Weapon number one: Sean Murphy-Bunting.

The Steelers signed Murphy-Bunting off the Tampa Bay Buccaneers' practice squad and put him straight onto the 53-man roster. To clear room, Derrick Harmon went to injured reserve with his foot injury. Pittsburgh also waived defensive back Doneiko Slaughter and added linebacker Marte Mapu to the practice squad.

Here's what Murphy-Bunting brings. He's a seven-year veteran with 82 career games, 65 of them starts. Eleven career interceptions, 34 passes defensed, and eight forced fumbles. He came into the league with Tampa Bay in 2019, and he was part of the Buccaneers team that won Super Bowl Fifty-Five. From there, he played for Tennessee and then Arizona. He's over six feet tall, which matters, because that's the exact body type Pittsburgh lost when Porter and Dean went out. This team likes long corners who can play outside, and Murphy-Bunting fits that mold.

And there's a nice little twist coming up on the schedule. After the Colts, the Steelers go to Tampa in Week 6. Murphy-Bunting spent the first month of this season practicing against that Buccaneers offense every single day. That's a scouting report you can't buy.

But I'm not going to oversell him. Murphy-Bunting missed all of 2025. Arizona had him on the non-football injury list, the Cardinals moved on, and he landed back on Tampa's practice squad. His last full season was 2024, when he gave up 37 catches on 52 targets for 390 yards and two touchdowns, with three interceptions. He's a corner who gives up completions but takes the ball away, and he hasn't played meaningful regular-season snaps in more than a year. He's a smart, low-cost bridge. He's not a savior. Which is exactly why the second name matters so much.

Weapon number two: Trevon Diggs. The one everybody wants.

Let's start with the resume, because when Diggs is right, he's a different level of player. In 2021, he led the entire NFL with 11 interceptions, returned two of them for touchdowns, and made first-team All-Pro. He made the Pro Bowl in 2021 and again in 2022. Dallas gave him a five-year, 97 million dollar extension in 2023. That's the ceiling.

Now the connections, and this is where it gets really interesting for Pittsburgh. Mike McCarthy coached Diggs in Dallas from 2020 through 2024. And Steelers secondary coach Joe Whitt Jr. coached him in Dallas from 2021 through 2023, which covers that 11-interception All-Pro season. So you're not talking about a stranger who needs a month to learn the language. You're talking about a guy who already knows the terminology, the coaches, and what they demand on every snap.

And here's why the Diggs talk got louder this week. Longtime Steelers insider Mark Kaboly said it plainly: "Trevon Diggs is an option, especially because they know him." He added, "I do not see why that would not be an option." Steelers Depot's Dave Bryan made the case for Pittsburgh to go get him, and Sports Illustrated's Noah Strackbein wrote that the Steelers' solution to their cornerback problem is sitting in Seattle. To be clear, none of those are reports that Pittsburgh is making a move. They're informed people saying the fit makes sense. But when that many people who cover this team every day land on the same name, you pay attention.

So how would it actually work? Diggs is on Seattle's practice squad, which means any team can sign him to its active roster without giving up a draft pick or a player. No trade needed. There are two catches. First, the team that signs him has to keep him on its 53-man roster for at least three weeks. Second, Seattle can protect a small number of practice squad players each week, and if they protect Diggs, he's off the table for that week. Seattle did elevate him for Sunday's game against the Chargers, and he made his debut in a 30-23 Seahawks win. Afterward, head coach Mike Macdonald said, "Great example of doing everything we ask; he got himself a chance to play. Thought we covered well today." After that game, Diggs went right back to the practice squad. So the window is there.

And here's the part I keep coming back to. After Monday's moves, the Steelers still have one open spot on their 53-man roster. One spot. In a week when they're short at cornerback and the best available veteran on the market has five years of history with your head coach. That might be a coincidence. It might also be flexibility for exactly this kind of decision.

Now, I also have to give you the honest picture on Diggs, because the version of him you'd get in 2026 is not the 2021 version. He tore his ACL in 2023. He's 28 years old, not 29 like you might see in some videos. His 2025 season was split between Dallas and Green Bay, with 27 tackles on about 460 defensive snaps. Before Seattle, he even worked out for the Lions in August. So there's real risk here. But there's also real upside, and the price is nothing but a roster spot. When the downside is "we cut him in three weeks" and the upside is "we got a former All-Pro who knows our system for free," that's a bet a lot of front offices would take.

Now let me give you the counterargument, because Omar Khan isn't just going to sign a name because fans want it. If Dean is really back in a couple of weeks, you're about to have Dean, Samuel, Murphy-Bunting, Everette, plus Ramsey moving between the slot and safety. Adding Diggs means committing three weeks of a roster spot to what might become your fifth corner. That's a real cost on a roster that's already juggling injuries everywhere. Maybe that's why Pittsburgh went with Murphy-Bunting first. He was available immediately, there was no protection drama, and he fits the size profile they like on the outside.

So what would Diggs actually look like in this defense if he came? Think about how he won in Dallas. He was never a shutdown, follow-the-best-receiver type. He was a ball hawk, a corner who reads the quarterback's eyes, sits on routes, and jumps throws. That style fits a defense that wants to keep things in front, rush four, and take the ball away. And Joe Whitt Jr. knows exactly how to use him, because he built the plan that produced those 11 interceptions. Yes, he can give up a big play when he gambles. But a secondary that just gave up a 60-yard bust and 21 points in a quarter could use a player who changes the turnover math.

And keep the calendar in mind. After the Colts, it's Tampa Bay on the road, then the Saints in Paris on October 25th as a Steelers home game, then the Browns at Acrisure on November 1st, and then the bye. That's three straight weeks of football before any real rest. If Dean's ankle lingers, or if one more corner goes down, Pittsburgh could be patching this secondary all the way to the bye. That's the scenario where a three-week commitment to Diggs looks less like a risk and more like insurance.

But here's my opinion, and I want to be very clear this is opinion, not a report. If the Colts come into Acrisure on Sunday and pick on this secondary the way Cleveland did, I'd make the Diggs call on Monday morning. The connection is real. The cost is zero. And with Aaron Rodgers on his final ride and the trade deadline on November 10th, this is not the year to be cautious about upgrading a position that just cost you a division game.

Now, here's something that gets lost in all the Diggs talk. Some of the best weapons Pittsburgh can add for Sunday are already on the roster. They just didn't get used properly in Cleveland. And that's the other half of this video, because if you rewatch the tape, the Steelers left a lot on the field.

Start with Jaylen Warren. Seventeen carries for 93 yards, plus three catches for 33 more. Steelers Depot's grades called him the best player on the offense, and the line that stuck with me was: "How he only had 20 touches on a night in which the offense struggled... is beyond me." Twenty touches, while Rodgers threw 40 passes. And coming into that game, the Steelers were already throwing on 64.7 percent of their snaps, with only Green Bay, Carolina and Houston throwing more. Guard Mason McCormick posted a run-blocking grade above 90 from PFF that night. When your guard is winning and your back is over five yards a carry, you lean on it. Against the Colts, Warren needs 22 to 25 touches. That's a weapon. And if Rico Dowdle gets back from his toe injury this week, that's another one.

Then there's Roman Wilson. Three catches on five targets for 74 yards and a touchdown, including a 49-yard grab and the 12-yard jet pass score that opened the game. PFF graded him as the best player on the offense. And he got five targets. If a receiver is winning like that, he needs eight or nine looks. DK Metcalf had five catches for 115 yards, but there was a moment in the second half where he was wide open deep and the ball never came. Michael Pittman Jr. had two catches for 15 yards, and Steelers Depot's grader wrote that he "just doesn't have much chemistry with Aaron Rodgers." That's the receiving room Pittsburgh has to sort out, and it has nothing to do with the trade market.

And on defense, the biggest weapon might be a young safety who already plays for you. Robert Spears-Jennings replaced Jenkins in Cleveland and finished with seven tackles, two pass breakups, and an interception after rookie Jack Sawyer tipped a Watson pass. Steelers Depot summed him up in seven words: "All that guy does is make plays." Coming into that game, he was leading the entire NFL with eight special teams tackles. In my opinion, he should be starting at safety on Sunday. Pair him with a healthier back end, maybe DeShon Elliott coming off injured reserve when his window opens, and suddenly the secondary looks a lot less desperate.

There are a few other fixes from the film room that matter just as much as any signing. The protection has to be better. Rodgers was sacked five times, hit 10 times, and pressured 20 times according to Steelers Depot's charting. Rookie tackle Max Iheanachor gave up his first career sack, Troy Fautanu struggled on the edge, and Rodgers himself held the ball too long at times. More chips from the backs and tight ends, and a quicker clock in the quarterback's head, both have to happen.

Defensively, Patrick Graham has to get out of the soft zone that let Watson throw quick and easy all night. Coming into Cleveland, Pittsburgh led the NFL in sack rate when rushing four or fewer, at 11.8 percent. That's a defense that doesn't need to blitz to get home. It just needs the coverage to make the quarterback hold the ball. T.J. Watt and Cameron Heyward were outstanding in Cleveland. Watt had a sack and a forced fumble that he recovered himself near midfield in the third quarter. Heyward had seven tackles and the best PFF grade on the defense. But the rest of the rush was quiet.

And then there are the points left on the field. After Watt's forced fumble, the offense got the ball near midfield and came away with nothing. Chris Boswell missed a 48-yard field goal in the second quarter, his third miss in four games. In a three-point loss, either one of those changes the ending. Make that kick, and Szmyt's 56-yarder ties the game instead of winning it.

Now let's talk trade, because Diggs isn't the only name in the conversation before the November 10th deadline. Last week, The Athletic's Jonathan Jones put Alex Highsmith on his watch list of players to monitor before the deadline. That's a watch list, not a report of an imminent deal. And Cleveland didn't help Highsmith's case, with one tackle, one tackle for loss and a missed sack chance in the red zone. But my opinion hasn't changed. With Harmon on injured reserve, you don't sell a starting pass rusher in a win-now season based on one quiet night behind a soft coverage plan. Fix the coverage, then judge the rush. If anything, the smarter conversation is the one we just had: Pittsburgh should be adding to this defense, not subtracting from it.

So let's put it all together for Sunday. The Colts come to Acrisure Stadium on October 11th, and there's an extra storyline: Michael Pittman Jr. came to Pittsburgh from Indianapolis this offseason, so this is his first shot at his old team. Get him a couple of easy throws early. Let him feel the ball. Revenge games bring out the best in receivers, and Pittsburgh needs him to be more than a decoy.

Here's my checklist for the Colts. One: Warren gets at least 22 touches. Two: Rodgers feeds the hot hand, whether that's Wilson, Metcalf, Pittman or the tight ends. Three: the protection gets help, and the ball comes out on time. Four: Graham tightens the coverage so Watt and company can get home. Five: Spears-Jennings starts at safety, and Murphy-Bunting gets real snaps on the outside next to Samuel and Everette. And six: every takeaway becomes points, and Boswell gets back to being Boswell.

Do those things, and I believe the Steelers beat Indianapolis. And if the secondary still looks shaky? That's when the second weapon gets added. Trevon Diggs is sitting on a practice squad in Seattle, Pittsburgh has a roster spot open, and the head coach knows him better than almost anyone in the league. That's not a report. That's just a door that's open.

The Steelers are 2-2, one game behind the Browns and Ravens. This division is still very much up for grabs. But in Aaron Rodgers' final season, every Sunday counts double. Pittsburgh made its first move on Monday. The question is whether it makes the second one.
