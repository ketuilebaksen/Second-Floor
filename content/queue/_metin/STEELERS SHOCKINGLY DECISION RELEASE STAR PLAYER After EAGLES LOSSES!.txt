Steelers Nation, I'm going to start with the truth, because the headline you clicked on is getting ahead of reality.

The Steelers have not released DK Metcalf. They haven't released any star player after Sunday's loss in Philadelphia. As of Monday morning, there's no report of a release, a trade or a benching from anyone who covers this team.

But I understand why that headline exists. Because what happened on Sunday was bad. Really bad. The Steelers lost 19-16 to an Eagles team that hadn't won since Week 2. Metcalf was ejected in the third quarter for a second personal foul after going at Cooper DeJean. It was the third ejection of his career. He finished with one catch for two yards. And now his head coach is calling it unacceptable, his quarterback is calling it disappointing, and some of the loudest voices in football are saying the Steelers should move on.

So today, let's talk about the real question behind the fake headline. Not "did the Steelers release Metcalf?" They didn't. The question is: what should they do now?

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's go.

THE FINAL: EAGLES 19, STEELERS 16

Let's start with the game, because the Metcalf story doesn't happen in a vacuum.

According to the Associated Press, the Eagles beat the Steelers 19-16 on Sunday, snapping a seven-game losing streak. The Steelers are now 6-4. And the drought continues: Pittsburgh hasn't won in Philadelphia since October 24, 1965, and has now lost 12 straight games there.

The start was ugly. Philadelphia scored three times in the first quarter, which the AP says it hadn't done in 33 games. There were two Jake Elliott field goals, and a 38-yard Saquon Barkley touchdown run. It was 13-0 after one quarter.

And the Steelers' offense couldn't get out of its own way early. According to the AP, Pittsburgh had just 13 rushing yards in the first half. Rodgers was 17-of-20 for only 95 yards before halftime, averaging half a yard of air distance per attempt. Half a yard. That's not a passing game. That's handing the ball off with your arm.

WHY THIS LOSS STINGS EXTRA

Let's put the opponent in perspective, because it makes the loss harder to swallow.

According to the AP, the Eagles' seven-game losing streak was their longest since an eight-game skid in 2012. They were starting Joe Milton, making only his second straight start, with Jalen Hurts out for the season and Tanner McKee benched. And earlier this month, at the trade deadline, they'd sent defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville.

That's the team that beat the Steelers. A team in sell mode, with a backup quarterback, that hadn't won since September. Pittsburgh was favored by 4.5 points, according to CBS Sports. There's no spinning that.

HOW IT GOT CLOSE

The Steelers did fight back.

Chris Boswell hit three field goals, from 25, 50 and 42 yards, according to the AP. Jaydon Blue fumbled the opening kickoff of the second half, and Carson Bruener recovered for Pittsburgh at the Eagles' 24, but the Steelers had to settle for a field goal there too.

Then Elliott kicked two more, from 53 and 55 yards, to push the lead to 19-9.

And with 1:21 left, Rodgers found Max Hurleman for a 20-yard touchdown to make it 19-16. That's the same Max Hurleman, the undrafted receiver Rodgers had been asking for, scoring his first NFL touchdown in a one-score game in the final minutes. But it wasn't enough.

THE EJECTION: WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Now, the moment everyone's talking about. Let's walk through it carefully, because the details matter.

According to EssentiallySports, early in the third quarter, with Philadelphia up 13-6, Metcalf exchanged words with DeJean and shoved him after a play. That drew his first unsportsmanlike conduct penalty. CBS noted on the broadcast that Metcalf was frustrated by the Eagles' physical coverage downfield.

Then, with under five minutes left in the third quarter and the Eagles up 16-6, it happened again. According to Bleacher Report, video showed Metcalf putting his hand inside DeJean's face mask. DeJean appeared to complain about being poked in the eye. And because it was Metcalf's second unsportsmanlike penalty of the game, he was ejected.

DeJean himself said Metcalf "just missed" his eye, according to Yahoo Sports. And NBC Sports Philadelphia's John Clark posted that DeJean pointed two fingers toward his eyes on the sideline.

WHAT DEJEAN SAID ABOUT IT

The other side of this story deserves a mention too, because it explains what was going on in Metcalf's head.

According to Yahoo Sports, DeJean suggested that Metcalf was frustrated by not getting the ball and took it out on him. And the numbers back that up. Metcalf was targeted four times and caught one pass for two yards, according to Bleacher Report.

That frustration is understandable. A player of Metcalf's talent wants the ball. But the answer to not getting the ball is to win your route, not to go after the man covering you. The Eagles' young cornerback did exactly what good corners do. He stayed physical, stayed in Metcalf's head, and let Metcalf beat himself.

THE PRICE ON THE FIELD

The video I'm working from today makes a point that I think gets overlooked.

The ejection didn't just cost the Steelers a receiver. It cost them field position at the exact moment they were trying to get back into the game. According to the play-by-play on CBS Sports, the disqualification was enforced at the Philadelphia 19. The Steelers were in field goal range, and the 15-yard penalty pushed them back. Boswell still made the kick. But in a game decided by three points, every yard matters.

And think about the timing. The Steelers were down 10 points with a quarter and a half to play, desperately needing their passing game to wake up. And their most expensive receiver walked up the tunnel.

WHAT THE BROADCASTERS SAID

On the CBS broadcast, play-by-play man Kevin Harlan didn't hold back. According to EssentiallySports, Harlan said Metcalf has "done nothing statistically yet continues to get in these brawls."

The video I'm working from says analyst Trent Green called it a selfish decision. I couldn't find Green's exact words in a written source, so I'll treat that as the video's description. But it's consistent with the tone of everyone else.

WHAT MCCARTHY SAID

Mike McCarthy didn't sugarcoat it after the game.

According to Yahoo Sports and Pro Football Talk, he said: "We need him. We need him in those games, and that's not acceptable to get thrown out of the game." And: "We can't have players getting ejected from games."

Pro Football Talk reported that McCarthy didn't rule out internal discipline. That's a big deal. When a head coach doesn't shut that door, he's sending a message to the locker room that this isn't being swept under the rug.

WHAT RODGERS SAID

Aaron Rodgers was measured, but you could tell he was frustrated.

According to Yahoo Sports, he said: "That was disappointing." And then: "Obviously, we love DK. And I don't know what was going on out there, what was happening. But, yeah, we can't have two of those."

When asked what he'd say to Metcalf, Rodgers said: "I'm not going to tell you that." And then: "I'm going to talk to DK. We're real good friends, so."

That tells you something. Rodgers isn't throwing his receiver under the bus. But "we can't have two of those" is about as clear as Rodgers gets in public.

And according to Yahoo Sports, Metcalf left the locker room without talking to reporters.

THE LOUDEST CRITICS

The reaction outside the building was harsher.

Hall of Famer Shannon Sharpe, according to Yahoo Sports, said: "He's gone after the season." He also called Metcalf "selfish."

And former Steelers cornerback Bryant McFadden, a two-time Super Bowl champion with Pittsburgh, criticized McCarthy's handling of it on CBS. According to Yahoo Sports, McFadden said: "I didn't like how he handled it." His argument was that this is part of a pattern, and McCarthy should have addressed it more directly.

So now it's not just Metcalf under the microscope. It's how McCarthy manages him.

THE PATTERN: THIS IS NOT THE FIRST TIME

That's the part that makes this so serious.

According to the AP, Sunday was the third ejection of Metcalf's career. Bleacher Report says that ties Dre Greenlaw for the most disqualifications by any player since 2019.

The video I'm working from lists the earlier ones. In 2021, while with Seattle, Metcalf was ejected against Green Bay. And in December 2023, he was ejected against San Francisco. Both of those were reported at the time by CBS Sports and FOX Sports.

This season, he's already been fined once. According to EssentiallySports, the league fined him $11,941 for taunting after the Week 4 game in Cleveland.

And the video says that before Sunday, Metcalf had six penalties this season, the most among NFL receivers. I couldn't verify that exact number, so treat it as the video's figure. But it fits a pattern everyone can see.

WILL THE NFL SUSPEND HIM?

Here's the question for this week, and it's urgent because the Steelers play again on Friday.

According to Yahoo Sports, the NFL reviews every ejection for possible additional punishment. That means a fine is very likely, and a suspension isn't off the table. If he's suspended, he'd miss Friday's Black Friday home game against Denver.

The video I'm working from cites Benjamin Albright of KOA Colorado, who reportedly heard from a league source that Metcalf could face additional discipline. I couldn't confirm that report myself, and the video itself is careful to say nothing has been announced. So let's be precise: as of Monday morning, no suspension has been announced. It's a possibility, not a fact.

THE CONTRACT: WHY "RELEASE" ISN'T SIMPLE

Now, let's talk about the word in the headline. Release.

According to Yahoo Sports, Metcalf signed a four-year, $132 million deal with the Steelers that runs through 2029. The Steelers traded a second-round pick to Seattle to get him in March 2025.

And Pro Football Talk made a sharp point after the game. After last season, the Steelers could have voided Metcalf's guarantees and released him. PFT says that would have saved $25 million. Instead, they kept him, and it cost them about $35 million for this season. With 35 catches and 432 yards in his first nine games, PFT wrote, that decision is looking costly.

Here's the bigger problem for anyone who wants to cut him now. According to Sports Illustrated's breakdown of his contract earlier this season, Metcalf's 2027 cap hit is about $38.4 million, with about $35.8 million in dead money if he's released. That's not a number you walk away from in the middle of a playoff race.

So, in my opinion, a midseason release isn't realistic. The real decision comes in the offseason.

THE PICKENS COMPARISON

The video I'm working from also brings up George Pickens, and it's a fair comparison to raise.

Some fans are pointing out that the Steelers moved on from Pickens in part because of concerns about his behavior, only to end up with another receiver controversy. Pickens is now in Dallas.

I'd be careful with that comparison, though. Pickens was young and on his rookie deal when he left. Metcalf is a veteran with a massive contract. The decisions aren't the same. But I understand why fans see the irony.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE ARGUMENT

To be fair, not everyone is piling on.

The video points out that some fans believe Metcalf was dealing with physical coverage all game, and that the officials should have gotten control of the matchup earlier. CBS noted the frustration with the Eagles' downfield coverage on the broadcast too.

That's a reasonable point. Cornerbacks hold, grab and talk. Receivers deal with it every week. But the video also lands where I land: whatever you think of the officiating, putting your team in that spot is very hard to defend. Every receiver gets grabbed. Most don't get ejected.

SO WHAT SHOULD THE STEELERS DO?

The video I'm working from asks three questions: bench him, move on from him, or give him another chance. Let's go through each one honestly.

Option one: release him. As we just covered, the money makes that very difficult during the season. And even setting money aside, you're trying to make the playoffs. Cutting your most experienced receiver in November would be a statement, but it would also make the offense worse.

Option two: bench him, or reduce his role. This is the one I think is most realistic if McCarthy wants to send a message. Maybe Metcalf doesn't start Friday. Maybe he plays fewer snaps. With Roman Wilson back, Germie Bernard improving and Max Hurleman scoring, the Steelers have more options than they did two weeks ago.

Option three: give him another chance, with clear accountability. That's probably where this ends up. Internal discipline, a direct conversation, and an understanding that there can't be another one.

WHAT THE OTHER RECEIVERS SHOWED SUNDAY

And here's why option two is more realistic now than it would have been a month ago.

According to Bleacher Report's box score, Max Hurleman finished with six catches for 61 yards and the touchdown, on seven targets. Germie Bernard had six catches for 40 yards. And Roman Wilson, in his first game back from injured reserve, had three catches for 43 yards on six targets.

To be fair to Hurleman, it wasn't a perfect afternoon. According to the Eagles' live blog, he misjudged one kickoff and muffed another that Philadelphia recovered at the Steelers' 10. But he made the biggest play of the day on offense.

And the video I'm working from mentions something I liked. When things got heated, Wilson was seen trying to hold Metcalf back. That's a young receiver, back from a month on injured reserve, trying to protect his team. That says something about Wilson.

WHAT ABOUT MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.?

I want to be upfront here. Pittman doesn't appear in the box score I was able to find, and I couldn't confirm whether he was activated or how much he played. So I'm not going to guess. If you know his status from the game, drop it in the comments. We'll have a clearer picture once the snap counts come out.

RODGERS' STAT LINE TELLS ITS OWN STORY

Let's look at Rodgers' full day, because it shows how the offense had to work around everything.

According to Bleacher Report, he finished 30-of-44 for 227 yards with one touchdown and no interceptions. He also ran five times for 29 yards, scrambling to make things happen when nothing was open.

Thirty completions sounds like a lot, but that's only about 7.5 yards per completion. The Steelers were living on short throws, especially in the first half. And without Metcalf for the last quarter and a half, Rodgers had to spread the ball between Hurleman, Bernard, Wilson and the tight ends. To be fair, that group made the comeback possible. But it also showed how thin the margin is when your top receiver isn't on the field.

THE DEFENSE DID ITS PART, MOSTLY

Here's something that's getting lost in the Metcalf story. The defense held the Eagles to 19 points.

According to the AP, Joe Milton went 11-of-21 for 141 yards with no touchdowns. Barkley had 75 yards on 17 carries, per Bleacher Report. And Philadelphia's only touchdown came on that 38-yard Barkley run in the first quarter. After that, the Eagles' offense managed only field goals.

According to the Eagles' live blog, Alex Highsmith had two sacks of Milton, including a strip-sack, though Philadelphia recovered the fumble. That was a big performance, especially with Nick Herbig unavailable.

THE INJURY THAT COULD MATTER MORE

And that brings us to the injury that could end up being as important as anything Metcalf did.

According to the AP, Highsmith was ruled out in the second half with an ankle injury. I'm not going to guess at the severity. But with Herbig already dealing with hip and rib injuries, the Steelers could be thin on the edge heading into a short week.

That puts more on T.J. Watt and Jack Sawyer against Denver. And it's something to watch closely this week.

THE SHORT WEEK

Speaking of the short week, this is the part that makes everything harder.

The Steelers host Denver on Friday, Black Friday. That's five days after a draining loss. Practice time is limited. And the team has to deal with a possible league ruling on Metcalf, a possible internal decision on Metcalf, and injury questions on the edge, all before it can even game-plan for the Broncos.

That's a lot for a first-year head coach to manage in five days. How McCarthy handles this week will say a lot about him.

THE BIGGER PICTURE: TWO STRAIGHT LOSSES

Let's step back for a second. The Steelers were 6-2 and alone in first place in the AFC North two weeks ago. Now they've lost two straight, to Cincinnati and to a 2-7 Eagles team.

Across those two games, the offense has managed 34 points. The receiver room was supposed to be the fix this week, and the team's top receiver got ejected. The run game that carried this team ran for 13 yards in the first half on Sunday.

That's a real slide, and it's why this Metcalf moment feels bigger than one ejection. It's a symptom of a team that's frustrated and searching.

WHAT METCALF NEEDS TO DO THIS WEEK

Here's one more thing I'd like to see, and it's purely my opinion.

Metcalf left the locker room on Sunday without talking to reporters. I understand that. Emotions were high. But this week, he needs to face the cameras. Not to make excuses, and not because of the media. Because his teammates are watching.

A simple acknowledgment goes a long way in a locker room. Something like: I hurt the team, it won't happen again, and I'll earn back everyone's trust. Players respect accountability. And with a short week before Denver, the faster this is addressed, the faster everyone can move on.

The Broncos come to Acrisure Stadium on Friday afternoon, and the Steelers can't afford for this story to still be the main story by kickoff.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for this week. Opinion, not a report.

Release Metcalf: no. Not midseason. The money and the playoff race make it unrealistic. Revisit everything in the offseason.

Internal discipline: yes. Something real, and something the locker room sees. McCarthy said it's unacceptable. His actions this week have to match those words.

Friday vs. Denver: if the league doesn't suspend him, I'd still reduce his role. Not a full benching, but no starting job handed to him either. Make him earn the snaps back.

Roman Wilson: more targets. He looked like himself, and he handled the moment with maturity.

Max Hurleman: keep him involved. The touchdown was earned. Clean up the kick returns.

Germie Bernard: keep building. Ten targets is a sign Rodgers trusts him.

Edge rush: monitor Highsmith and Herbig closely. If both are limited, Watt and Sawyer carry the load, and the coaches have to scheme pressure.

Run game: 13 rushing yards in a half can't happen again. Get Jaylen Warren going early against Denver.

FINAL WORD

Here's my bottom line. The Steelers didn't release DK Metcalf, and they're not going to in the middle of a playoff race. But Sunday was a turning point in how this team, and this fan base, sees him.

Three career ejections. A fine earlier this season. One catch for two yards in a three-point loss. And a coach who publicly called it unacceptable.

What happens next is on two people. Metcalf has to show he can keep his emotions under control. And McCarthy has to show he can hold his biggest name accountable without breaking the locker room.

Get that right this week, and the Steelers can still salvage the season. Get it wrong, and Sunday in Philadelphia might be remembered as the day things started to come apart.
