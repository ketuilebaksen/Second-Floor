Steelers Nation, one week ago Omar Khan was stacking up moves like a man who knew something was coming. Two cornerbacks claimed off other teams’ practice squads. A pass rusher brought back for a third stint. Linebacker depth. And a few weeks before that, a reshuffled right side of the offensive line built from a cheap free agent and a first-round rookie. Back then, all of it was theory. Now we have a game to grade it against. Pittsburgh beat Indianapolis 34-22, and on Monday, Khan was at it again, adding a familiar veteran face. So today is a follow-up: we’ll grade every Khan move from the last month against what actually happened on Sunday, look at the brand-new receiver crisis, check in on Tyreek Hill, and answer the big question one more time. Are the Steelers unstoppable now? This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

First, the scoreboard, because it changes the whole conversation.

A week ago, the Steelers had just lost 27-24 in Cleveland, they were 2-2, and some national voices were writing them off. On Sunday, they beat a Colts team that came in hot. Aaron Rodgers threw for three touchdowns for a third straight game. Jaylen Warren put up 188 yards from scrimmage, with an 80-yard touchdown catch and a 50-yard touchdown run, the first player to do both in one game since Herschel Walker in 1986. Jonathan Taylor was held to 45 yards. The Colts settled for five field goals. And Pittsburgh is 3-2.

So the general manager deserves a closer look. Let’s grade him move by move.

Grade one: the offensive line bet. Grade: A.

Remember the decision before the Cincinnati game in Week 3? Rookie first-round pick Max Iheanachor took over at right tackle, and Brock Hoffman, the versatile interior lineman Khan signed this offseason on a one-year, 2.5 million dollar deal, stepped in at right guard. Hoffman knows Mike McCarthy’s system from their years together in Dallas. Pittsburgh won that Bengals game 30-27, and center Zach Frazier said the line looked clean.

Sunday was the next proof. Against a Colts front built around DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart, Rodgers was sacked only once. Warren ran for 101 yards on 15 carries. And Rodgers said it himself after the game: “I thought the line blocked really well.” When your 42-year-old quarterback completes his first 11 passes and your running back averages almost seven yards a carry, the line is doing its job. This is the move that’s paying the biggest dividends of Khan’s season, and it cost almost nothing.

Grade two: the secondary patches. Grade: B-plus, and rising.

This was the part everyone was nervous about. Joey Porter Jr. was traded to Dallas. Jamel Dean was hurt. DeShon Elliott was on injured reserve. Jalen Ramsey had a broken wrist. So Khan went shopping on other teams’ practice squads. On Monday of last week, he grabbed Sean Murphy-Bunting from Tampa Bay, a Super Bowl champion with the 2020 Buccaneers who has 11 career interceptions. On Tuesday, he took Terell Smith from Washington, a long, 6-foot corner who’s still working back from a torn patellar tendon that cost him 2025. Both had to go straight to the 53-man roster, and both have to stay there for at least three weeks, so these were commitments, not tryouts.

And the result on Sunday? Daniel Jones threw 44 passes and didn’t throw a touchdown pass. The Colts went 4-of-15 on third down. Asante Samuel Jr. broke up four passes on his own. Ramsey, playing through that wrist, made nine tackles. Was it perfect? No. Jones still threw for 267 yards, and Indianapolis moved the ball between the 20s. But a secondary that was supposed to be the weak link didn’t give up a single passing touchdown. That’s a passing grade, and with Dean pointing toward a return against his old team in Tampa, it can get better fast.

And here’s the bonus that makes the Murphy-Bunting signing look even smarter this week. Pittsburgh’s next opponent is Tampa Bay. Murphy-Bunting spent the first month of this season on the Buccaneers’ practice squad, going up against that offense every single day. He knows the route concepts, the timing, the little tells. Coaches can’t buy that kind of information. Khan got it for free with a practice-squad signing. Don’t be surprised if he’s in the coaches’ meetings this week explaining exactly what Tampa likes to do on third down.

There’s more help on the way, too. DeShon Elliott’s 21-day practice window is open, and McCarthy said, “It’s going to go as he goes this week,” with a decision expected Wednesday. If Elliott and Dean both return, this secondary goes from patched-up to legitimately deep in a matter of days, and the Khan claims become valuable depth instead of emergency starters.

Grade three: the defensive front depth. Grade: B.

When Derrick Harmon went on injured reserve with his foot injury, Khan brought back defensive end KJ Henry to the practice squad for his third stint in Pittsburgh. Henry was a 2023 fifth-round pick out of Clemson with 2.5 sacks in 15 career games. He’s a depth piece, not a star. But depth is exactly what you need when your interior is thinner, and the front held Taylor to three yards a carry. Cam Heyward got the only sack. Alex Highsmith said the defense “stopped him” by getting “off blocks.” The front won the war that mattered most.

And on Monday, Khan added another piece. The Steelers are signing veteran linebacker Elandon Roberts to the practice squad. McCarthy explained that timing played a big role, and he described Roberts as a veteran who “just wants to win.” He could even be elevated on game days to help the defensive front. That’s the Khan pattern again: see a need, fill it before it becomes a crisis.

Grade four: the Joey Porter Jr. trade. Grade: incomplete.

This is still the biggest decision of Khan’s season. Porter went to Dallas for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth. According to Ian Rapoport, Pittsburgh had offered him around 28 million dollars a year but wouldn’t guarantee injury money beyond the first year, and Khan wasn’t going to negotiate during the season.

Here’s what we can say after Sunday. The Steelers just held a good passing offense without a touchdown pass, without Porter and without Dean. That doesn’t settle the debate, but it’s a strong first argument for Khan’s side. And now that second-round pick sitting in Pittsburgh’s pocket has become something else: ammunition. With the trade deadline on November 10th, that pick could turn Porter into the next weapon on this roster. If that happens, this trade could look very different by Thanksgiving.

Grade five: bringing Aaron Rodgers back. Grade: A-plus.

I can’t do a Khan report card without this one. Khan brought Rodgers to Pittsburgh, and then brought him back again for one final run. Plenty of people questioned it. A 42-year-old quarterback, a new head coach, a supposed sideline feud on national TV. Now look at Sunday. Twenty-two of 31, three touchdowns, his first 11 passes complete. His 166th regular-season win as a starter, moving past Ben Roethlisberger into fifth all-time. Passing Peyton Manning for third in career touchdown passes, postseason included, with 580. And three straight games with three touchdown passes, something no Steelers quarterback had ever done in back-to-back-to-back weeks of the same season.

Whatever happens the rest of the way, Khan made a bet on an old quarterback with a young man’s arm, and right now that bet is paying off in history books.

Now, the new problem: receivers are disappearing.

Okay, here’s the part that tests Khan the most. On Monday, McCarthy confirmed that Roman Wilson and Ben Skowronek “will not be available this week” against Tampa Bay. Wilson dislocated a finger on his left hand trying to make a catch late in the first half, and he was seen in a splint. Skowronek hurt his calf when his legs were taken out from under him covering a punt. And Michael Pittman Jr. is already on injured reserve with his foot.

That leaves DK Metcalf, rookie Germie Bernard, and Kaden Wetjen as the healthy receivers on the 53-man roster, plus Brandon Smith on the practice squad, who was elevated for the Colts game.

So what’s McCarthy’s plan? He’s backing his guys. He said Smith has “definitely earned this opportunity,” and added, “We feel very good about Brandon moving forward.” When he was asked about bringing in a free agent, he said, “Those conversations are always going on.” That’s coach-speak for: we’re looking, but we’re not announcing anything.

And honestly, Bernard is the reason the Steelers can breathe a little. In his first NFL start, he caught six passes for 80 yards, both career highs. Before the season, Rodgers said on Cam Heyward’s podcast that young receivers usually take “a year or two,” and that the team had to “temper expectations.” After Sunday, Rodgers said he was “really proud of Germ the way he stepped in,” and added that “he’ll be ready to take on a bigger role this week, I’m sure.” That’s a big shift in a few weeks. Bernard isn’t waiting a year or two. He’s being thrown into the fire, and so far, he’s handling it.

And here’s the other answer, and it’s a smart one: lean on what Khan built. Last week, before the Colts game, I said this roster was built to run the ball, use two tight ends, and let Rodgers work off play action. Sunday proved it. Warren was the engine. Pat Freiermuth caught two touchdowns, including the 17-yard dagger on third-and-6 in the fourth quarter. Darnell Washington picked up a key first down. McCarthy called Warren “our feature player right now.” And Rico Dowdle is close to returning from his toe injury. When you’re missing three receivers, you build the game plan around the players you have, and this roster has a lot of them in the backfield and at tight end.

Now, the name everyone keeps asking about: Tyreek Hill.

When we talked about Hill last week, his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, had said he expected Hill to sign with a team “over the next few days.” As I record this, he still hasn’t signed anywhere. Here’s the latest. Hill hasn’t played since September 2025, when he suffered that devastating knee injury. Covers reported, citing The Athletic, that his targeted return timetable is November. And the prediction market Kalshi has had the Chiefs as the clear favorite, around 58 percent, with the Bills, Packers, and Vikings far behind. The Steelers aren’t even on that list.

And the Chiefs buzz has only grown. Hill posted on October 4th about being ready for cold weather, and after Kansas City receiver Tyquan Thornton suffered an ankle injury, he posted again, which only fueled the reunion talk. Some Chiefs players have reportedly said they’d welcome him back. Meanwhile, the Vikings, who were the betting favorite at about 45 percent a week earlier, dropped all the way to around 2 percent. Things are moving fast, and they’re moving toward Kansas City.

So let me be completely straight with you: there is no report that Pittsburgh is in on Hill. Zero.

But here’s my opinion, and I’ll label it clearly as opinion. Pittsburgh’s need just got bigger. A week ago, the Steelers were missing one receiver. Now they’re missing three. And Hill wouldn’t cost a draft pick. If he’s targeting a November return, his timing actually lines up with the stretch after Pittsburgh’s Week 9 bye. That’s when Pittman would also be eligible to come back from injured reserve. Imagine Metcalf, Pittman, Bernard, and a rested Hill in December. On paper, that’s a dangerous group.

The reality check? He’s 32, coming off a multi-ligament knee injury, and the Chiefs appear to be the frontrunner by a wide margin. If Kansas City wants him, Pittsburgh probably can’t compete with that history. I’d put the odds of Hill in black and gold very low. But if the Chiefs deal falls through, Khan should absolutely make a call. A low-risk, incentive-heavy contract would be the kind of deal he likes.

Khan’s other options, and my opinion on the best one.

Since Hill is a long shot, let’s look at the trade market. With the Marvin Harrison Jr. trade now done, Houston sent Arizona a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder for Harrison and a 2027 seventh, according to Pro Football Talk. So he’s off the table.

Here’s what I’d do, and again, this is my opinion, not a report. I’d call Tennessee about Calvin Ridley first. He’s 31, he’s barely been targeted this year in a crowded young receiver room, and Pro Football Focus listed him as a trade candidate. The price for a veteran like that should be a late-round pick, not that Dallas second-rounder. He’s the kind of player who could walk into this offense and help Rodgers immediately.

And then I’d keep that Dallas pick ready for the bigger fish. Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville is still the most intriguing young receiver who might become available. He had 1,282 yards as a rookie and has been stuck as the Jaguars’ third option. Jacksonville has said no to offers before, but things change near the deadline. If the price ever drops to that Dallas second, Khan should jump.

Last week, I said Khan’s moves gave Pittsburgh enough bodies to survive. This week, the need has moved from the secondary to the receiver room. And if there’s one thing we’ve learned about Omar Khan this season, it’s that he doesn’t wait for problems to fix themselves.

So, are the Steelers unstoppable now?

Last week, my answer was no, not yet. This week, I’m going to upgrade it. They’re not unstoppable. But for the first time this season, they look dangerous. The offensive line Khan rebuilt is protecting Rodgers. The running game Khan believed in is dominating. A secondary built from waiver-wire bargains and practice-squad claims just held a good team without a passing touchdown. And the quarterback is putting up historic numbers, passing Peyton Manning in career touchdown passes and Ben Roethlisberger in wins.

What’s standing between this team and real momentum? Health at receiver, a road losing streak that’s now at five games, and a trip to Tampa Bay on Sunday. Win there, and the Steelers go to 4-2 with a real chance to build something before the bye.

And here’s what I keep coming back to. Not one of the moves we talked about today made national headlines. No blockbuster, no huge contract. A one-year deal for a guard. A first-round tackle who wasn’t supposed to start this early. Two corners plucked off practice squads. A pass rusher on his third stint. A linebacker who “just wants to win.” That’s not a flashy GM. That’s a GM who treats a 53-man roster like a puzzle and keeps finding the missing pieces. And on Sunday, those pieces showed up.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because this is where Khan’s roster gets its next exam.

The Steelers visit Tampa Bay on October 18th at Raymond James Stadium. And there are storylines everywhere. Murphy-Bunting faces the team that had him on its practice squad a week and a half ago. Dean, if he’s cleared, faces the team where his career began. And Kenneth Gainwell, who signed with the Buccaneers this offseason after his time in Pittsburgh, will be on the other sideline.

But the biggest storyline is the road. Pittsburgh’s loss in Cleveland was its fifth straight loss away from home. This team plays with confidence at Acrisure Stadium, and now it has to prove it can carry that confidence onto the road. With three receivers out, I expect McCarthy to lean even harder into the formula that beat the Colts: Warren in space, two tight ends, play action for Rodgers, and short, high-percentage throws to Bernard. And if Dowdle is back, that backfield becomes the best weapon on the roster. Two backs who can both carry the load would give McCarthy the freedom to keep the defense fresh by controlling the clock.

On defense, the goal is simple: keep doing what worked on Sunday. Win on early downs, force third-and-long, and make the opposing quarterback beat a secondary that just proved it can hold up. If the Steelers win in Tampa with this many receivers hurt, that’s the moment people around the league will start taking this team seriously.

Let me give you the updated scorecard so you can screenshot it.

The offensive line bet: A. It’s protecting Rodgers and opening lanes for Warren.

The secondary patches: B-plus. No passing touchdowns allowed against the Colts.

The defensive front depth: B. Jonathan Taylor held to 45 yards.

The Joey Porter Jr. trade: incomplete, but the secondary held up without him on Sunday, and the pick could become a weapon.

Bringing back Aaron Rodgers: A-plus. History every week.

The receiver room: that’s Khan’s next test, and it’s urgent.
