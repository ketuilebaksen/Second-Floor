Steelers Nation, let me save you some time and some frustration. If you came here because a thumbnail told you Art Rooney II just dropped a bombshell about Omar Khan, I have to be honest with you: I searched, and I couldn’t find one. No press conference from the owner this week. No statement about the general manager. No sign of a shake-up in the front office.

What did happen this week is arguably more telling. The trade deadline passed without a single Pittsburgh move. The criticism came in hot. And when the head coach was asked about it, he didn’t just back the general manager. He named the owner too. Mike McCarthy said: “I have great confidence in Omar and Art, if there’s something there, we’ll do the right thing.”

That’s the real Rooney-and-Khan story right now. Not a bombshell. A unified front. The coach, the general manager and the owner all standing behind the same decision, at a moment when a lot of fans are questioning it.

So today, we’re going to unpack it properly. What the Steelers’ front office decided and why. What the market looked like, with real numbers on what other teams paid. The cornerback question that may matter more than receiver. What Cam Heyward said that set social media on fire. And where the Rooney-Khan relationship actually stands. My board at the end, opinion only.

THE DECISION: STAND PAT

Let’s start with the facts.

The NFL trade deadline closed Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern. The Steelers made no trades. At six and two, sitting atop the AFC North and in position to compete for a deep playoff run, that decision was always going to spark debate.

Receiver and cornerback were the two obvious needs, and receiver was the biggest. According to ESPN’s Dan Graziano, as summarized by Yardbarker, Pittsburgh discussed a trade for Titans receiver Calvin Ridley, but Tennessee stayed committed to its offense. Ridley had just six catches for 91 yards through seven games and was still recovering from a broken fibula. And a recent incident at Nashville International Airport reportedly made some teams hesitant.

And then there was Josh Downs, the Colts receiver Pittsburgh had been linked to for two weeks. Less than an hour before the deadline, according to the transcript, Kansas City sent a fourth- and a fifth-round pick to Indianapolis for Downs and a seventh-rounder. NFL.com confirmed the terms: Kansas City sent 2027 fourth- and fifth-round picks and received Downs plus a 2027 seventh.

WHY KHAN HELD BACK

The same Yardbarker piece lays out the front office’s reasoning, and it’s worth walking through.

First, draft capital. The Steelers reportedly focused on protecting what they had left, especially since they don’t own their 2027 fourth-round pick. That pick went to Dallas in the Broderick Jones trade.

Second, the quarterback future. Aaron Rodgers is expected to retire after this season. A real pursuit of his successor, whether in the draft or through a trade, would require significant draft capital and financial flexibility.

Third, the injured receivers. Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. are expected back, which gives the offense more weapons without surrendering picks.

None of those reasons are crazy. But you can disagree with how they were weighed.

WHAT THE MARKET ACTUALLY COST

Here’s the part of the transcript I found most useful, because it puts the decision in context. The going rate at this deadline was described as absurd. And when you look at the numbers, it’s hard to argue.

On Monday, the day before the deadline, Jacksonville sent two picks to Atlanta for safety Jessie Bates III. According to the transcript, it was a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth.

Then, hours later, Jacksonville made an even bigger move. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the Jaguars sent a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick to the Eagles for defensive tackle Jordan Davis.

Buffalo also went big just before the deadline, acquiring safety Amani Hooker from Tennessee. The transcript says the Bills gave up a second-round pick in a deal that also included a fifth-rounder. I couldn’t confirm the exact terms, so let’s treat them as reported.

The transcript also claims Minnesota gave up a third-round pick for Kirk Cousins. I couldn’t confirm that trade at all, and at least one Minneapolis column suggests the Vikings didn’t make a move like that, so I’m going to leave it out.

So here’s the context. A first and a second for a defensive tackle. A second for a safety. Prices like that show why a front office might hesitate. They also show that other contenders, including teams Pittsburgh could face in January, were willing to pay.

THE DOWNS DEAL: WHY IT STINGS

Of all the moves, the one that bothered Steelers fans most was Downs to Kansas City.

The transcript makes a smart point. The package the Chiefs paid looked like a reasonable gamble. Downs could have re-signed with Pittsburgh after the season. Or, if he left in free agency, the Steelers might have recouped something through the compensatory pick formula. Instead, an AFC rival got him.

But here’s the honest truth: we don’t know what Pittsburgh was asked to pay, or how the front office evaluated Downs. The transcript is right to say this deal gives fans a reason to ask questions, not enough information to declare exactly what happened behind closed doors.

And there’s a real counterpoint. Gerry Dulac of the Post-Gazette noted that Downs, at five-foot-nine and 171 pounds, “does not appear to be the type of receiver Mike McCarthy appears to favor.” If the coaching staff didn’t see him as a scheme fit, a fourth and a fifth might have been more than they wanted to pay.

THE RECEIVER CRITICISM: IS THE METCALF TRADE REALLY A DISASTER?

The transcript’s source describes the DK Metcalf trade as disastrous, a year and a half into his tenure in Pittsburgh. That’s a strong word, and I want to give it some context.

Here’s what’s true. Pittsburgh traded a second-round pick to Seattle for Metcalf in March 2025 and then signed him to a five-year, one hundred fifty million dollar extension. This season, he’s had drop issues, and according to Athlon Sports, he had just three catches over the last two games. The deep ball that went through his hands against Cleveland became an interception.

But “disastrous” might be going too far. Metcalf still draws attention from every defense he faces, and that attention opens space for Jaylen Warren, rookie Germie Bernard and the tight ends. The issue isn’t whether he’s talented. It’s whether he can be consistent enough to justify the investment.

Either way, the criticism explains why fans wanted another receiver before the deadline. When your highest-paid weapon is struggling, depth becomes even more important.

THE CORNERBACK QUESTION

Now let’s talk about the position that might matter more than receiver over the second half: cornerback.

The backstory starts with Joey Porter Jr. On October 1, the Steelers traded Porter to the Dallas Cowboys for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder, according to theScore. Porter, a 2023 second-round pick, was in the last year of his rookie deal, and the two sides couldn’t agree on an extension. McCarthy had called it an “impasse” before the season. Porter was inactive for the first three games.

That trade solved one problem and created another: depth. The transcript notes that Pittsburgh’s corners have played solidly in recent weeks, which is encouraging. But one injury, or one rough stretch, and the Steelers would have to lean on less experienced players.

WHAT THE PORTER TRADE TELLS YOU ABOUT THIS FRONT OFFICE

The Porter trade is worth a closer look, because I think it explains a lot about how Khan and Rooney operate, and it connects directly to the deadline.

According to the Associated Press, talks between Porter’s camp and Khan stalled, and the Steelers didn’t negotiate once the season began, which the AP described as a longstanding club policy. Meanwhile, several of Porter’s 2023 draft classmates, including Nick Herbig, Darnell Washington and Keeanu Benton, did receive extensions. And Porter, by the AP’s account, didn’t allow a touchdown pass in 2024 or 2025.

After the trade, Porter said: “I feel like I was an elite guy since I’ve been in this league.” And he added: “And I’m happy that I’m getting the respect that I feel I (am) owed.” McCarthy, who never coached him in a game, said: “I never really got the chance to coach Joey,” and “He obviously did some great things here.”

The AP framed Pittsburgh’s stance as principled, but possibly costly, since the team didn’t have an obvious heir at cornerback. That’s a fair critique, and it’s part of why the depth question lingered into the deadline.

But notice the pattern. With Porter, the Steelers held a line on value and took a 2028 second-round pick rather than pay above their number. At the deadline, they held a line on value again and kept their picks rather than chase Downs. Whether you agree with it or not, it’s consistent. This front office sets a price, and it doesn’t chase past it.

WHY THAT CONSISTENCY MATTERS IN THE ROONEY CONTEXT

That consistency is also probably why Rooney trusts Khan the way Kaboly described back in January. Owners tend to value general managers who stick to a plan, especially in a franchise that prides itself on stability. Pittsburgh has had only a handful of head coaches in more than half a century. It’s not an organization that changes direction every time the fan base gets loud.

So when people ask whether Rooney is upset with Khan after this deadline, the evidence points the other way. The head coach publicly named both men in the same breath. The captain backed the roster. And the general manager made a decision that fits exactly with the approach he’s taken all season.

None of that guarantees it’s the right approach. But it does mean there’s no bombshell here, no crack in the front office. If anything, this was the week the organization circled the wagons.

ASANTE SAMUEL JR. AND DAYLEN EVERETTE

Two names stand out in that conversation.

The first is Asante Samuel Jr. The transcript says he struggled early in the year and was briefly benched against the Bengals in Week 4. One correction: the Bengals game was Week 3, not Week 4. I couldn’t confirm a benching, but Yardbarker did note that physical receivers gave him trouble in tight coverage in that Week 3 game. Since then, it describes him as “serviceable” in Porter’s old role.

The second is rookie Daylen Everette, a third-round pick out of Georgia. The transcript spells his name “Dalen Everett,” but it’s Daylen Everette. According to Yardbarker in early October, citing Pro Football Focus, he had played fifty-two coverage snaps across two games of extended action, with nine targets, five catches allowed and two pass breakups. He’s had rookie moments, including a zone-coverage lapse against Cleveland in Week 4, but he’s shown the size, speed and versatility to play both man and zone.

The transcript raises a fair question about whether the stage might be too big for him. That’s not a prediction. It’s the uncertainty the Steelers have to manage if they need him in a big spot.

THE GOOD NEWS IN THE SECONDARY

Here’s where this week’s injury report helps.

According to Steelers.com, cornerback Brandin Echols and defensive back Doneiko Slaughter were both full participants in Wednesday’s practice, both coming back from hamstring injuries. Safety DeShon Elliott was limited with an ankle injury.

If Echols and Slaughter are fully healthy, the depth concern eases. And that’s important, because Sunday night’s opponent has Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. You can’t face that duo with a thin secondary.

CAM HEYWARD’S MESSAGE: “TRADES ARE FOR MADDEN”

Now let’s get to the quote of the week.

On Wednesday, Cam Heyward was asked why the Steelers didn’t add reinforcements. According to ESPN’s Brooke Pryor, as cited by Steeler Nation, Heyward said: “Trades are for Madden.” And then he added: “I make plenty of those in my franchise mode. In this game, it’s about the 11 on the field, and we feel like we have the guys to get it done.”

I love that answer. It’s funny, it’s confident, and it’s exactly what a locker-room leader should say after a quiet deadline. The players in that room aren’t sulking about who didn’t arrive. They’re focused on the guys who are already there.

And it lines up perfectly with what McCarthy said. The coach trusts the front office. The captain trusts the roster. That’s a team speaking with one voice.

WHERE ROONEY AND KHAN ACTUALLY STAND

So let’s get back to the headline. What’s the real relationship between Art Rooney II and Omar Khan?

Here’s what we know. Back in January, during the coaching search that led to McCarthy, Mark Kaboly of The Athletic suggested that Rooney “really, really trusts Omar” and might lean heavily on him in the process. I wasn’t able to open the full article, so take the details as reported, but the headline is clear.

We also know what Rooney said publicly in January about the standard. According to Pro Football Talk, he said: “The standard is try to compete to win a championship every year.” And he made it clear he has no interest in rebuilding: “I’m not sure why you waste a year of your life not trying to contend.”

So how do those two ideas fit together? On one hand, Rooney wants to compete every season. On the other, his general manager just passed on adding help at the deadline. Is that a contradiction?

I don’t think it has to be. Competing doesn’t always mean buying. Sometimes it means protecting the assets you’ll need to compete next year too, especially when your forty-two-year-old quarterback has said this is his final season. A franchise that wants to win every year has to plan for the year after Rodgers. That’s what the draft capital is for.

And McCarthy’s comment tells you the three of them are aligned. “I have great confidence in Omar and Art.” When the head coach includes the owner by name in that kind of statement, it suggests the decision had support all the way to the top.

THE CASE FOR THE FRONT OFFICE

Let me give you the strongest argument in Khan’s favor.

The prices were steep. A first and a second for a defensive tackle. A second for a safety. If Pittsburgh had matched those prices, it would have weakened its ability to find the next quarterback.

The 2024 lesson still matters. Khan bought at the deadline that year, adding Mike Williams and Preston Smith, and the team lost five straight games to finish the season. He said afterward: “If I had to do it again, I would do it again,” but also, “the result was we lost five straight games and that’s not good enough.”

The reinforcements are real. Derrick Harmon returned to practice this week. Echols and Slaughter were full participants. Pittman is getting close. Wilson is expected back.

And the team is winning. Six and two. Four straight wins. The defense hasn’t lost a game since defensive coordinator Patrick Graham moved down to the sideline before the Week 5 game against Indianapolis.

THE CASE AGAINST

And now the strongest argument against.

The AFC is wide open, and the transcript makes this point well. When a team has a legitimate chance to compete, supporters want to know whether the front office truly believes the current group is enough.

The competition got better. Buffalo, Jacksonville and Kansas City all made moves. Pittsburgh didn’t.

The window is closing. If Rodgers retires after the season, this is the last chance to win with him. Every season is one opportunity.

And the needs are real. Receiver was the biggest hole on the roster. Cornerback depth was the second. Neither got a new body.

THE TRANSCRIPT’S BALANCED TAKE

I want to give credit to the source material here, because its conclusion is fair. This deadline shouldn’t be treated as proof that Omar Khan made a mistake. But it also shouldn’t be brushed aside.

The price of quality players was high, and protecting future draft capital can be a sensible choice. At the same time, Pittsburgh had clear needs, a strong record and a real chance to challenge in the AFC. The Downs deal to Kansas City only makes the contrast more visible.

The real test is whether standing pat proves to be right. And that test starts on Sunday night.

WHAT TO WATCH FROM THE FRONT OFFICE NEXT

The deadline is closed, but Khan’s work isn’t done. Here are the next decision points to keep an eye on.

First, Michael Pittman Jr. He’s eligible to come off injured reserve, but as of Wednesday the team hadn’t opened his practice window. McCarthy said he’s “getting close.” When the Steelers designate him, that’s the front office’s first real roster move of the second half.

Second, Derrick Harmon. His twenty-one-day window opened on Wednesday. The team has to activate him within that window or he reverts to injured reserve for the season. Expect that decision soon.

Third, the practice squad. Veteran receiver Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who knows Rodgers and McCarthy from Green Bay, is there and available for elevation if the receiver room needs a body.

Fourth, the free-agent market. With trades off the table, any outside addition now has to come through free agency. Names like Brandon Aiyuk, whose status in San Francisco must be resolved by November 23, will keep coming up. But nothing has connected the Steelers to him, and as the rules are generally understood, a player released after the deadline would go through waivers first.

Those are the moves that will tell you how the front office really feels about this roster over the next month.

SUNDAY NIGHT IN CINCINNATI

The Steelers visit the Bengals on Sunday, November 15. Both teams are six and two. Both have won four straight. First place in the AFC North is on the line.

Cincinnati is coming off a 34-17 win over Atlanta in Madrid. Joe Burrow completed 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards and three touchdowns. Ja’Marr Chase had ten catches for 155 yards.

Pittsburgh won the first meeting 30-27 on September 27, sealed when Derrick Harmon strip-sacked Burrow in the final minute and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered. Now Harmon is practicing again, and McCarthy has left the door open for him to play.

If the Steelers win, the narrative around the deadline changes immediately. Heyward’s “Trades are for Madden” becomes a rallying cry. If they lose, the criticism gets louder, and the question of whether Khan did enough will follow this team for weeks.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board, and remember: this is opinion, not a report.

The front office: I’d have made one modest move at receiver, but I understand the reasoning. A first and a second for anyone right now would have been too much for this team.

Cornerback: Echols and Slaughter healthy is the best news of the week. Keep Samuel in his slot-heavy role. Give Everette situational snaps. Don’t force a rookie into a starring role on Sunday night.

Receiver: Bernard needs eight targets against Cincinnati. Metcalf needs early, easy completions. Pittman when he’s ready.

Derrick Harmon: if he’s cleared, put him on the field against Burrow.

The core: untouchable. Watt, Highsmith, Herbig, Queen, Ramsey, Warren, Frazier.
