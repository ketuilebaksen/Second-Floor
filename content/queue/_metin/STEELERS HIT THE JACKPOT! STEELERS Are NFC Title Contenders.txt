Steelers Nation, after the win in Paris, a lot of people are starting to ask a question that would have sounded crazy a month ago: are the Pittsburgh Steelers real contenders to win the AFC? They’re 5-2. They’ve won three straight. They just found a running game that can wear people down, and their defense held the Saints to 18 rushing yards. But before we start booking hotel rooms for the AFC Championship Game, I want to give you the honest picture: where the Steelers rank in the conference, what the oddsmakers think, the secret weapon that showed up in Paris, and what has to happen next for this team to go from good to great. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

Where the Steelers stand in the AFC.

Let’s start with the facts. Entering Week 8, the Steelers are the sixth seed in the AFC playoff picture. The Chiefs and Bills are both 5-1 and sit at the top. The Browns are the third seed at 5-2, holding the AFC North lead on the tiebreaker. The Jaguars are fourth at 4-2. The Broncos are fifth at 5-2. Then come the Steelers at 5-2, with the Bengals in the seventh spot at 4-2.

So yes, the Steelers are firmly in the playoff picture. But they’re also one of three AFC teams at 5-2, and they’re chasing two 5-1 teams for the top seed. The race is tight, and the next game against the Browns could move Pittsburgh from sixth all the way up to the top of the division.

What the experts and oddsmakers say.

The national view is starting to shift. The Big Lead’s AFC power rankings moved the Steelers up from ninth to sixth this week, with the note that they “went to Paris and left with an ugly win,” and that international games “always deserve an asterisk.”

The betting market is more skeptical. According to Sports Illustrated’s rankings based on Super Bowl odds, the Steelers are at +7000 to win the Super Bowl, 14th in the league. The Bills are at +700, the Chiefs at +800, and the Jaguars at +1400 among AFC teams. So the oddsmakers see Pittsburgh as a solid playoff team, not yet a favorite.

And honestly, that’s fair. A contender isn’t defined by a three-game winning streak. It’s defined by how you play against the best teams. But there are real reasons to believe this team is building toward something bigger.

A sixth seed? Steelers fans know what that can mean.

And here’s a little history for anyone who thinks the sixth seed means you can’t win it all. In the 2005 season, the Steelers were the sixth seed in the AFC. They went on the road, beat the Bengals, beat the top-seeded Colts in Indianapolis, beat the Broncos in Denver, and then won Super Bowl XL. That team was built on a physical running game and a defense that took over in the biggest moments.

I’m not saying this team is that team. But it’s a good reminder that seeding in October doesn’t decide anything. What matters is how you’re playing in January, and whether you have a formula that works on the road against good teams. Right now, the Steelers are building that formula.

How the Steelers stack up against the AFC’s best.

Let’s compare the Steelers to the teams ahead of them. The Bills and Chiefs are the class of the conference right now, both at 5-1, and the oddsmakers see them as the clear favorites. The Jaguars, at 4-2, have the best Super Bowl odds of the rest of the AFC pack at +1400. The Broncos are 5-2 with +1900 odds. The Bengals are at +1700.

Here’s the interesting part: the Steelers get chances to prove themselves against several of those teams directly. They play at Cincinnati on Sunday Night Football after the bye. They host Denver on Black Friday. And they travel to Jacksonville for Monday Night Football in December. Those are head-to-head chances to move up in the seeding and change the national conversation.

And over in the NFC, the Vikings are the league’s only undefeated team at 6-0. The Steelers won’t see them this year, but it shows you how much can change. Few people would have predicted the Vikings as the last unbeaten team before the season. Momentum in the NFL can build fast.

Reason one: they found a winning formula.

The first reason is the identity shift we saw in Paris. Coming into that game, the Steelers had one of the most pass-heavy offenses in the NFL. Against the Saints, they ran 30 times for 148 yards. And they closed the game by holding the ball for the final five minutes and 52 seconds. New Orleans never got another chance.

That’s a contender’s formula, and it’s one Steelers fans have seen win championships before. When you can protect a narrow lead by running the ball, you don’t need your 42-year-old quarterback to throw 49 passes to win. You can control the clock, keep your defense fresh, and finish games on your terms.

The secret weapon: the jumbo package.

And here’s the detail that caught my eye. In Paris, the Steelers brought back a jumbo package, sending offensive lineman Spencer Anderson onto the field as an extra blocker who reports as eligible. Add tight end Darnell Washington to that formation, and suddenly the defense is staring at a wall of big bodies.

It paid off right away. Jaylen Warren broke a 29-yard run on the opening drive, and that set the tone for the whole afternoon. Anderson summed up the mindset after the game: “We’re going to run it again, again, and again.”

Here’s why I love it. You don’t always have to fool a defense to beat it. Sometimes you can show them exactly what’s coming and still make them stop it. An extra blocker creates space, and Warren doesn’t need a huge hole to do damage. He needs a crease and a chance to get moving.

Now, it’s not magic. The Browns will have answers, and if they load the box, the Steelers will have to make them pay through the air. There’s a big difference between being patient and becoming predictable. But having a package you can lean on to protect a lead late in games? That’s exactly the kind of tool playoff teams need.

Reason two: two backs who can carry an offense.

The second reason is the backfield. Warren finished with 101 rushing yards on 18 carries, 5.6 yards per carry, and he added three catches for 36 yards. That’s 21 touches, and he made the most of them. Rodgers said the team is “almost expecting Jaylen to have 100 yards” at this point.

Rico Dowdle, who was questionable all week with a knee issue, added 41 rushing yards and made plays as a receiver too. McCarthy put it perfectly on Monday: “When we signed Rico, we knew we had two really big time backs we could lean on.”

Having two backs who can both run and catch means the Steelers don’t have to take a playmaker off the field when the situation changes. Throwing to a running back doesn’t have to feel like giving up on a play. It can be a way to keep the chains moving.

Reason three: a defense that can win in January.

The third reason is the defense. Holding a team to 18 rushing yards on 17 carries is the kind of performance that travels into the postseason. When an opponent can’t run, it has to beat you through the air, and the Steelers’ pass rush gets to tee off.

And when the Saints did threaten, someone made a play. Payton Wilson intercepted Tyler Shough with New Orleans inside the red zone, taking away a chance for the Saints to take the lead. Patrick Queen finished with 10 tackles and a sack, and his late third-down sack forced a punt that set up the game-ending drive. The Saints didn’t score a single point in the second half.

That’s what contenders do. They get stops in big moments, and they turn those stops into wins. Bend when you have to, but never break when the game is on the line.

Reason four: a quarterback who’s been there.

The fourth reason is Aaron Rodgers. He didn’t put up big numbers in Paris: 19 of 30 for 212 yards and a touchdown. But he made the right decisions, didn’t turn the ball over, and passed Peyton Manning for third place all-time in regular-season touchdown passes. On the final drive, he picked up a crucial first down with his legs on third-and-6 to keep the clock moving.

And when the Steelers needed a big play, he delivered. On third-and-14, he hit DK Metcalf for 51 yards, and that set up the only touchdown of the game. In the playoffs, you need a quarterback who can make that kind of throw when the moment calls for it. Rodgers has won a Super Bowl and four MVPs. He knows what January football looks like, and that experience can’t be bought at the trade deadline.

Reason five: they’re getting healthier.

The fifth reason is health. DeShon Elliott made his season debut in Paris, playing a limited role of fewer than 20 snaps. On Monday, McCarthy said Elliott “cleared that first hurdle” and that the plan is to keep building his workload. T.J. Watt came through the calf scare and got a sack. Dowdle played through his knee issue.

And the Steelers still have reinforcements coming later in the season, with Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve. A team that’s winning while short-handed, and then gets healthier, is a dangerous team in November and December.

About that Paris asterisk.

Let’s address the asterisk talk, because I think it cuts both ways. Yes, international games are strange. The time change, the travel, the canceled walkthrough, the unfamiliar stadium. Offenses often look sloppy in those games, and the Steelers’ 16 points weren’t pretty.

But the same conditions applied to the defense, and the defense was dominant. Holding a team to 18 rushing yards isn’t an accident of the environment. And McCarthy’s decision to keep the team in Pittsburgh until Thursday night, which a lot of people questioned, worked out. So if you’re going to put an asterisk on the offense, you have to give the defense and the coaching staff extra credit for handling everything around it.

The young core matters too.

One more thing that separates a one-year run from a real contender: young players who are getting better. The Steelers have that. First-round right tackle Max Iheanachor is starting and helping power the running game. Second-round receiver Germie Bernard has caught 17 passes in his first two starts and scored the game-winning touchdown in Tampa. Rookie corner Daylen Everette has an interception. Rookie safety Robert Spears-Jennings keeps earning snaps. When rookies are contributing in October, they’re usually even better by December, and that’s exactly when contenders separate themselves.

Now, the reasons for caution.

But I promised you the honest picture, so let’s talk about why the Steelers aren’t a favorite yet.

First, the red zone. Pittsburgh scored just one touchdown in Paris. The offense moved the ball, converted 10 of 16 third downs, and still settled for three field goals. Against a better offense, leaving those points on the field could be the difference between winning and losing. Chris Boswell bailed the Steelers out, making three field goals. And his late kick actually went through too, but the Steelers accepted an offside penalty on New Orleans instead, because the first down let them run out the clock. So that wasn’t a miss. It was a smart decision.

Second, the pass defense. Tyler Shough completed 30 of 38 passes for 278 yards. The Steelers can’t depend on a red zone interception every time an opponent puts a drive together. Against the Bills or Chiefs, that number has to come down.

And third, the schedule. According to NFL.com, the Steelers’ only win so far over a team that currently has a winning record came against the 4-2 Bengals. The three-game winning streak came against the Colts, Buccaneers, and Saints. To prove they belong with the AFC’s elite, the Steelers have to beat good teams. And that starts on Sunday.

The Browns: the first real test.

The Browns come to Acrisure Stadium on Sunday at 1 p.m. Both teams are 5-2. Cleveland beat Pittsburgh 27-24 on a Thursday night in Week 4, and the Browns are 2-0 in the division. This is the game that will tell us whether the Steelers are a contender or just a team on a nice run.

McCarthy isn’t hiding from it. On Monday, he said, “We need to beat them. They got us once already, and it’s a rivalry game for us.” He also gave the Browns their due: “They’re playing good football.”

And here’s what makes this game so important. If the Steelers win, they’ll be alone in first place in the AFC North, they’ll have split the season series, and they’ll head into the bye on a four-game winning streak. McCarthy has called this stretch a “four-game series,” and he wants to go 4-0. If they lose, Cleveland sweeps the series and the Steelers fall two games behind in the division.

Why the Browns are dangerous anyway.

Here’s something that surprised me. Even though the Browns are 5-2 and leading the division, the betting market isn’t buying them. According to Sports Illustrated’s odds-based rankings, Cleveland sits at +20000 to win the Super Bowl, 16th in the league. That’s a long way behind the Steelers at +7000.

But don’t let that fool you. The Browns just came back from 14-0 down to win in Tennessee, and they already beat the Steelers once this season. Teams that keep finding ways to win close games are dangerous, no matter what the oddsmakers say. McCarthy said it himself: “They’re playing good football.” This game will be physical, it’ll be emotional, and it’ll probably come down to a handful of plays, just like the first meeting did.

And for the Steelers, it’s a chance to show the national media that the formula from Paris works against a good team, at home, in a division game, with first place on the line. That’s the kind of win that changes how people talk about you.

What I want to see against Cleveland.

My focus for Sunday is simple. Can the Steelers keep this physical approach while finishing more drives with touchdowns? Keep the jumbo package involved. Keep feeding Warren and Dowdle. But when Cleveland crowds the line, punish them with play action and shots to Metcalf. Keep the physical identity, and let the defense’s reaction decide where the ball goes next.

On defense, I want to see the same run defense we saw in Paris, with a better plan against the pass. If the Steelers can make the Browns one-dimensional, the pass rush can take over.

Trade block: the piece that makes them a contender.

Let’s talk about the deadline, and remember, this is opinion, not a report.

If the Steelers want to go from playoff team to real AFC contender, I think the missing piece is a veteran receiver. Pittman and Wilson are on injured reserve, the red zone needs help, and Metcalf can’t be the only receiver defenses have to worry about. Josh Downs in Indianapolis is still the name I like most, with rival teams expecting the Colts to sell. He’s a young route runner on an expiring deal who could help right away.

And I would not touch the defense. Alex Highsmith’s name has popped up on a trade board, but the Steelers’ defense is the reason they’re in this race. Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” the Steelers will be active before the November 10th deadline. The right move could be the one that turns a good team into a contender.

The schedule that will decide it.

And look at what’s coming after the Browns game and the bye. A Sunday night trip to Cincinnati. A road game in Philadelphia. Denver at home on Black Friday. Houston at home on Sunday Night Football. A Monday night game in Jacksonville. The Ravens at home in December. That stretch includes several teams in the AFC playoff picture right now: the Bengals, the Broncos, and the Jaguars. If the Steelers want to be taken seriously as AFC contenders, those are the games where they’ll earn it.

The bottom line.

So are the Steelers AFC title contenders? Not yet, at least not in the eyes of the oddsmakers, who have them at +7000 to win the Super Bowl. They’re the sixth seed in the AFC, tied with two other 5-2 teams, behind the Chiefs and the Bills.

But they have the ingredients. A physical running game with two backs who can carry an offense. A jumbo package that gives them a real way to close out games. A defense that just held an opponent to 18 rushing yards. A future Hall of Fame quarterback who knows how to win in January. And a roster that’s getting healthier, with a trade deadline still ahead.

The next step is proving it against a good team. That starts Sunday against the Browns. Win that game, and the conversation changes. Lose it, and the doubts come right back. That’s what makes this one so big. Contenders aren’t crowned in October, but they’re often revealed there, and Sunday is the Steelers’ chance to reveal exactly who they are.
