Steelers Nation, Omar Khan has done it again. In the span of about 48 hours this week, while the rest of the league was still talking about Pittsburgh’s loss in Cleveland, the Steelers general manager quietly rebuilt the back end of his secondary and restocked the defense behind it. Two cornerbacks pulled off other teams’ practice squads. A pass rusher brought back for a third stint. A linebacker added for depth. No big press conference, no blockbuster, just moves. And if you go back a couple of weeks, you’ll find the same fingerprints on the offensive line change that finally gave Aaron Rodgers some breathing room. And with Michael Pittman Jr. now out for multiple weeks, there’s one more name Steelers fans can’t stop talking about, a name that could take all of this to another level: Tyreek Hill. So today, I want to look at Khan’s week, the pattern behind it, whether Tyreek Hill could be the next move, and the big question in the headline: does this make the Steelers unstoppable? I’ll give you my honest answer, because it’s a little more complicated than a yes. This is James with Second Floor, let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the moves themselves, because a lot of fans only caught one of them.

On Monday, Pittsburgh signed cornerback Sean Murphy-Bunting off Tampa Bay’s practice squad and put him straight on the 53-man roster. He’s a Super Bowl champion from the Buccaneers’ 2020 team, a veteran with 65 career starts and 11 career interceptions. To make room, Derrick Harmon went to injured reserve. The same day, the Steelers added linebacker Marte Mapu, formerly of the Patriots, to the practice squad.

And Murphy-Bunting comes with a bonus for the schedule. After the Colts, the Steelers go to Tampa Bay in Week 6, and he just spent the first month of this season practicing against that exact Buccaneers offense every day. He’s not perfect. His last full season was 2024 in Arizona, when he allowed 37 catches on 52 targets for 390 yards and two touchdowns, but he also picked off three passes. He’s a corner who gives up some completions and takes the ball away. For a secondary that needs bodies with experience, that’s a reasonable bet.

Then on Tuesday, Khan went right back to the well. He signed cornerback Terell Smith off Washington’s practice squad and put him on the 53 as well. Smith is a 2023 fifth-round pick of the Bears out of Minnesota, six feet and 204 pounds with nearly 33-inch arms and a 4.41 forty. In 2024, he had an interception against Jordan Love. He tore his patellar tendon in August of 2025 and missed that season, so he’s still working his way back, but he’s played both outside spots and he’s got the size Pittsburgh likes on the boundary. Because he came off another team’s practice squad, the Steelers have to keep him on the active roster for at least three weeks, so this isn’t a tryout. They’re committed.

And that same Tuesday, Pittsburgh signed defensive end KJ Henry to the practice squad. This is Henry’s third stint with the Steelers. He was a 2023 fifth-round pick out of Clemson, he’s bounced around the league, he was a training camp standout in San Francisco this summer before an injury settlement, and he’s got 2.5 sacks in 15 career games. With Harmon out, every bit of depth on the defensive front matters.

So that’s four moves in roughly two days, all aimed at the same problem: a defense that got exposed in Cleveland, lost Joey Porter Jr. in a trade, lost Jamel Dean to an ankle injury, and lost Harmon to a foot injury. Khan didn’t sit around waiting for things to heal. He went and found bodies.

Now, why do I say he’s done it again? Because this is a pattern with Omar Khan, and it’s worth appreciating.

Go back to the 2025 offseason. Khan traded for DK Metcalf and signed him to an extension. He traded Minkah Fitzpatrick to Miami in a deal that brought back Jalen Ramsey, who made the Pro Bowl Games in his first year in Pittsburgh. He brought in Aaron Rodgers, and then brought him back again this year for one final run. This offseason, he signed Brock Hoffman, a versatile interior lineman who played three years for Mike McCarthy in Dallas, on a one-year, 2.5 million dollar deal. And the Steelers drafted Max Iheanachor in the first round.

And here’s where those last two moves paid off in a big way. Before the Cincinnati game in Week 3, the Steelers revamped the right side of their offensive line. Iheanachor stepped in at right tackle, and Hoffman stepped in at right guard, replacing Dylan Cook and Spencer Anderson. Anderson, by the way, has been playing through a broken bone in his lower leg since Week 2. After the Bengals game, center Zach Frazier said the line looked “really clean,” and that he didn’t notice any mistakes from Iheanachor. Pittsburgh won that game 30-27. A cheap free agent and a first-round rookie, both brought in by Khan, fixed a problem that had been dragging down the offense.

That’s what Khan does. He doesn’t always make the flashy move. He makes a lot of practical moves, fast. Sometimes they look small on the transaction wire. Sometimes they end up holding the whole thing together. And in a season with this many injuries, a general manager who moves fast is worth his weight in gold.

And you can’t talk about Khan’s October without the biggest decision of all: trading Joey Porter Jr. to Dallas for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth. That one wasn’t about patching a hole. That was a philosophy call. According to Ian Rapoport, Pittsburgh offered Porter a deal worth around 28 million dollars a year but wouldn’t guarantee injury money beyond the first year, and Khan made it clear there would be no negotiations during the season. You can disagree with that philosophy. A lot of fans do. But you can’t say Khan is afraid to make hard calls. He made one, got real draft capital for a player who wasn’t going to sign on Pittsburgh’s terms, and then went to work filling the hole within days.

So let me give you my quick Khan scorecard on this stretch. The offensive line bet, Hoffman and Iheanachor on the right side: a hit, and it’s already showing up. The cornerback patches, Murphy-Bunting and Smith: incomplete, we’ll know a lot more after Sunday. The Porter trade: also incomplete, and it’ll hang over this season until the secondary proves it can hold up without him. Porter played 24 snaps in his Dallas debut and didn’t allow a catch, so the pressure is on Pittsburgh’s side of that deal. That’s an honest grade. Good, not perfect, with the biggest tests still to come.

So, does this make the Steelers unstoppable?

Here’s my honest answer. No, not yet. And I think you deserve to hear that from me before you hear it from the scoreboard. Murphy-Bunting missed all of last season. Terell Smith missed all of last season. KJ Henry is a practice squad body. These are smart patches, not superstars. Jamel Dean is expected to miss Sunday. Ramsey is playing with a broken wrist. DeShon Elliott just had his practice window opened, and The Athletic’s Mike DeFabo reported the team is targeting a Week 7 return, so he likely misses this game and the next one. And on Thursday, Mike McCarthy said Michael Pittman Jr. will miss “multiple weeks” after re-injuring his foot. The team is still deciding whether he goes on injured reserve.

So no, this roster isn’t unstoppable right now. But here’s what Khan’s moves do. They give Pittsburgh enough bodies to survive the next three weeks without panicking. They give Patrick Graham options. And they set up a path where, if the coaches use the roster the right way, this team can get on a run. And that’s where the offense comes in, because the most important pieces Khan has added aren’t on defense. They’re up front on offense, and they’re being underused.

Let’s talk about the offense, because this is where the “unstoppable” part actually lives or dies.

Coming into Cleveland, the Steelers were throwing the ball on 64.7 percent of their snaps, the fourth-highest rate in the league. They’ve also been living in 11 personnel, three wide receivers, on something like two-thirds of their snaps, and spending a lot of time in shotgun and spread looks. Now look at the roster Khan built. Two tight ends who can actually do things: Darnell Washington, a massive blocker who also caught a touchdown in Cleveland, and Pat Freiermuth, who caught one too. A running back, Jaylen Warren, who ran 17 times for 93 yards against the Browns and only got 20 touches all night. And a right side of the line that finally looks stable with Hoffman and Iheanachor.

That’s not a spread roster. That’s a roster built to run the ball, use two tight ends, get under center, and let Rodgers work off play action. And this is a quarterback who has made a career out of punishing defenses with play action, so asking him to sit in the shotgun and beat tight coverage on every snap doesn’t play to his strengths. McCarthy himself listed struggles against Cover 1, man coverage, among the problems from the Cleveland self-scout. Defenses are daring Pittsburgh’s receivers to win one-on-one. And outside of DK Metcalf, there isn’t a receiver on this roster that consistently scares people vertically.

And now, with Pittman out for multiple weeks, the receiver room is even thinner. That’s the moment to lean into what this roster is built for. More Warren. More Washington blocking. More Freiermuth in the passing game. More play action. And if Rico Dowdle can come back from his toe injury, he was limited in practice for a second straight day and was seen working in drills, then suddenly you’ve got a real one-two punch in the backfield. That’s how you protect a 42-year-old quarterback. Not by asking him to throw 40 times.

The receiver room without Pittman.

Let’s be specific about who steps up. Roman Wilson should get a bigger role, and honestly, he’s earned it. In Cleveland, he caught three of his five targets for 74 yards and a touchdown, including a 49-yarder, and Pro Football Focus graded him as the best player on the offense that night. Metcalf had five catches for 115 yards and made big plays late. Those two have to be the foundation.

And then there’s rookie Germie Bernard. I want to see this kid get a real opportunity, and by real I mean actual routes designed for him, not just a handful of snaps here and there. He showed real route-running and some ability after the catch in training camp, and he caught a touchdown in the preseason finale. But Rodgers also put it plainly before the season on Cam Heyward’s podcast: “Anytime you bring in a young player, unless they’re Calvin Johnson, you have to temper your expectations.” He said, “It usually takes guys a year or two,” and added, “you need to temper expectations for sure with Germie. But he’s a great kid.”

I get that. Rodgers has seen a lot of young receivers come through, and he knows the learning curve. But here’s the thing. With Pittman out, the Steelers don’t have the luxury of waiting a year or two. If Bernard makes a mistake, and he will, the coaches and Rodgers have to keep looking his way. That’s how young receivers get better. You can’t develop a player without giving him meaningful snaps.

The defensive front against the Colts.

Now, the other side of the ball has its own test on Sunday. With Harmon on injured reserve, the interior is thinner, with Keeanu Benton and Yahya Black taking on bigger roles and Sebastian Joseph-Day rotating in next to Cameron Heyward. And the Colts are going to test that, because Jonathan Taylor is still Jonathan Taylor. In Indianapolis’s 30-13 win over Washington last weekend, Taylor ran 20 times for 95 yards and two touchdowns.

The good news? Indianapolis didn’t exactly light it up through the air in that game. Daniel Jones went 19 of 34 for just 143 yards with an interception, and two sacks. So the formula for the Colts is pretty clear: lean on Taylor, stay ahead of the chains, and keep the ball out of trouble. If Pittsburgh can win on early downs and force Jones into third-and-long, a patched-up secondary suddenly looks a lot more capable. That’s the matchup within the matchup on Sunday.

That’s why the offense has to help. If Pittsburgh keeps going three-and-out and sending the defense right back out against Taylor, that front is going to wear down in the second half. Long drives, manageable third downs and time of possession aren’t just offensive goals on Sunday. They’re how you protect a defense that’s missing pieces.

And in the secondary, here’s how I expect it to shake out. Asante Samuel Jr. on one side. Daylen Everette and Murphy-Bunting competing for the other side, with Terell Smith as new depth. Ramsey moving between the slot and safety, cast and all. Robert Spears-Jennings, who made seven tackles with two pass breakups and an interception after replacing Rayshawn Jenkins in Cleveland, should get every chance at more snaps. And Brandin Echols deserves some consideration outside, too. That’s a lot of new faces, and communication will be everything.

Now let’s get to the trade and free agency segment, because this is the one everybody’s asking me about. Tyreek Hill, and whether he could be coming to Pittsburgh.

First, let me give you the facts, because I’m not going to sell you a fake signing. Hill is a free agent right now. Miami released him in February after a major knee injury that included multiple torn ligaments. He’s 32 years old. On October 5th, his agent Drew Rosenhaus said, “I would anticipate Tyreek potentially signing with a team over the next few days.” ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that Hill has been talking with multiple teams, and that Kansas City has stayed in contact. As of the last update I could find, he hadn’t signed anywhere. And to be clear, there is no report that the Steelers have called. Even a Steelers-focused writer at Sports Illustrated called a Pittsburgh signing “extremely unlikely,” mostly because Kansas City looks like the natural landing spot. So this is not a done deal, and anybody telling you it is, is making it up.

But here’s why I think the Steelers should be in on it, and this is my opinion, not a report.

Reason number one: the need just changed overnight. When Sports Illustrated wrote that, Pittman was still healthy. Now McCarthy says he’s out for “multiple weeks,” and the team is deciding whether to put him on injured reserve. That leaves DK Metcalf, Roman Wilson, rookie Germie Bernard and depth pieces. Outside of Metcalf, there isn’t a receiver on this roster that consistently scares defenses vertically, and defenses have been daring Pittsburgh to beat them one-on-one. Tyreek Hill, even at 32, is the kind of player who changes how a defense lines up. Put him on one side and Metcalf on the other, and that man coverage gets a lot harder to play.

Reason number two: it costs Khan no draft picks. Hill is a free agent. There’s no trade, no second-round pick, no young player going out the door. It’s money and a roster spot. And if Pittman goes to injured reserve, the roster spot opens up on its own. That’s exactly the kind of practical, no-picks-required move Khan has made all week with Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith, just at a much higher profile.

Reason number three: it fits the window. This is Aaron Rodgers’ final season. Rodgers has made a career out of throwing to receivers who win quickly and create after the catch. Hill has been one of the most explosive players in football for a decade. And Khan has already shown he’s willing to bet on star veterans, trading for Metcalf and Ramsey in 2025 and bringing Rodgers back for one more run. If you’re going to go for it with a 42-year-old quarterback, this is the kind of swing that matches the moment.

Reason number four: it would take pressure off everybody. With Hill and Metcalf stretching the field, safeties have to stay deep. That opens up the run game for Warren and maybe Dowdle. It opens up the middle for Freiermuth and Washington. And it gives Bernard and Wilson easier matchups to develop against. One receiver can’t fix everything, but the right receiver can make everything around him easier.

Now, the honest risks, because you deserve both sides. Hill is coming off torn knee ligaments, and nobody knows yet what he looks like on the field at 32. He’d need time to get into football shape and learn the offense, so even if he signed tomorrow, he probably wouldn’t help against the Colts. Kansas City knows him and may have the inside track. And the Steelers’ biggest weakness is still the secondary, which is why I’ve said all week that a cornerback like Trevon Diggs, who played five years under McCarthy, should also be on Khan’s list.

So here’s where I land. If Hill’s knee checks out and the price is reasonable, a one-year, prove-it style deal, Pittsburgh should absolutely make the call. Pittman’s injury opened the door, Rodgers’ final season is the reason, and Khan’s track record says he won’t be shy. Would it make the Steelers unstoppable? No single signing does that. But Metcalf, Hill, Warren, two tight ends and a fixed offensive line, with Rodgers throwing to them, would make this offense a lot scarier than anything we saw in Cleveland. And that’s worth a phone call.

The bigger picture, and where this is going.

Some people are already throwing around the word rebuild. I don’t see it. When you look at Art Rooney II, Omar Khan and Mike McCarthy, you don’t see a group that wants to tear everything down in October. Khan’s moves this week tell you that. Teams that are rebuilding don’t sign two cornerbacks off practice squads in 48 hours. They let the young guys play and take the losses. Pittsburgh is trying to win now, with a 42-year-old quarterback on his final ride and T.J. Watt in his prime.

The stretch ahead is the whole season. Colts at home on Sunday, T.J. Watt’s birthday. Then at Tampa Bay. Then the Saints in Paris on October 25th, a Steelers home game. Then the Browns at Acrisure on November 1st, and then the bye. Go 3-1 through that stretch, and Pittsburgh is 5-3 at the bye with Dean, Elliott and maybe Dowdle back and healthy. That’s breathing room. That’s a team that can be a buyer at the November 10th deadline. Go 1-3, and every rebuild conversation gets a lot louder.

Here’s what I’ll be watching on Sunday to judge whether Khan’s moves are working. First, which corner starts opposite Samuel, and how the Colts test him. Second, how many snaps Spears-Jennings gets at safety. Third, Warren’s touch count, I want to see 22 or more. Fourth, how often the Steelers line up with two tight ends and work from under center. And fifth, whether Germie Bernard gets real, designed targets with Pittman out. If those five things go the right way, you’ll know the roster Khan built is finally being used the way it was meant to be.

So let me bring it home. Did Omar Khan do it again? Yes. Fast, practical moves, the same way he’s built this roster for two years. Do those moves make the Steelers unstoppable? Not by themselves. The final step is on Mike McCarthy and his staff: use the line Khan built, lean on the tight ends and the running game, give the young receivers real chances, and keep Rodgers upright. Do that, and the “unstoppable” talk might not sound so crazy by Halloween. And if Khan’s next call goes to Tyreek Hill’s agent, you’ll know he isn’t done yet.
