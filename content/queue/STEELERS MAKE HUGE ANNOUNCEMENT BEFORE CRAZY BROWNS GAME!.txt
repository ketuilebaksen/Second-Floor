Steelers Nation, let’s fix one thing right away. Some headlines are still calling this game “upcoming.” It’s not. The Browns game is over, and it wasn’t crazy close. It was a 27-6 statement at Acrisure Stadium. Pittsburgh is 6-2, alone in first place in the AFC North, and headed into the bye on a four-game winning streak.

And the “huge announcement”? There wasn’t a press release or a blockbuster trade this morning. The announcement came on the field, and Mike McCarthy delivered the follow-up himself after the game: “This bye was going to happen regardless. It’s not like you’re going to change direction, so, and our team needs a bye.” Then the line that matters for the next two weeks: “I look to come out of this bye really healthy.”

So today I’m doing something different. I’m handing out a full report card from the Browns game, unit by unit. Then I’ll give you the health picture coming out of the bye, the trade market heading into deadline week, and my opinion on what Omar Khan should do. I’m James, this is Second Floor. Grades are in.

RUNNING BACKS: A-PLUS

Let’s start at the top of the class.

Jaylen Warren ran for 143 yards on 17 carries, tying his career high, on his 28th birthday. That’s more than eight yards a carry. His 51-yard run in the first quarter set the tone. And Rico Dowdle added 56 yards and the game’s first touchdown on a 9-yard run. As a team, Pittsburgh ran for 200 yards, its best total of the season.

The context makes it even better. According to Steelers.com’s Bob Labriola, Warren now has four 100-yard games since the offensive line changes back in Week 3, and 848 yards from scrimmage on the season. And McCarthy couldn’t stop praising the duo: “We’ve got two dynamic backs. Jaylen is on fire. You can’t get him the ball enough.”

Warren, of course, deflected the credit. “Just playing with the boys I play with,” he said. “I wouldn’t want to be part of any other team.”

Grade: A-plus. No notes.

OFFENSIVE LINE: A

You don’t run for 200 yards without a line that’s mauling people, and this group did exactly that.

Rodgers raved about them: “Definitely love those boys up front. They dominated the line of scrimmage.” He also said, “The line is playing with a lot of confidence.” And the best example of that confidence came on a third-and-20, when the Steelers called a trap run and still picked up the first down. Rodgers’ reaction: “pretty wild.”

The only reason this isn’t an A-plus is the penalty that got everyone talking. After the first touchdown, Mason McCormick lifted Jared Verse and slammed him during the extra point. It drew a 15-yard flag. McCormick said he didn’t know it was Verse. Considering Verse spent the week calling this line dirty, the optics were funny, but a penalty is a penalty.

Grade: A.

TIGHT ENDS: A

This might be the most underrated story of the game.

Darnell Washington caught three passes for a career-high 94 yards, including a 58-yard catch-and-run to the goal line that set up Warren’s touchdown and made it 27-6. Washington has always been known as a blocker. Sunday, he was a weapon.

And Pat Freiermuth scored the touchdown that broke Cleveland’s spirit. With seconds left before halftime, no timeouts, and the ball at the goal line, McCarthy skipped the field goal. Freiermuth pushed it in on a tush push. Troy Fautanu’s take: “Everyone knows what’s going on. See if you can stop it.”

Grade: A.

QUARTERBACK: B-MINUS

Here’s where the grades come down a bit.

Rodgers finished 13 of 25 for 172 yards, with no touchdown passes and one interception, which came off a DK Metcalf drop, according to the Tribune-Review. He managed the game well, protected the football for the most part, and hit the big play to Washington. He also handled the checks at the line that let the run game feast.

And he clearly enjoyed the way his team won. “I feel like from the first go we had a good mindset, we protected well, we opened up some holes,” he said. On the trash talk that filled the week, he added, “A lot of attitude. A lot of trash talking. Good trash talking. Love being out there with those guys.”

But this wasn’t a vintage Rodgers day through the air, and it didn’t need to be. Mason Rudolph finished the game once it was out of reach. I’m giving a B-minus because the job was managing a blowout, and he did that, but the passing game still has room to grow.

Grade: B-minus.

WIDE RECEIVERS: C

This is the unit that explains the trade talk.

Germie Bernard had a 15-yard catch that set up the touchdown before halftime. But overall, the receivers weren’t a factor in the passing game. Rodgers’ praise for them was about blocking: “the receivers blocked well on the edge.” That’s important, and it’s part of why the run game worked. But a playoff team needs its receivers to win in the passing game too.

The Metcalf drop that turned into an interception is the low point. And with Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson on injured reserve and Ben Skowronek still out, the depth is thin. Brandon Smith, who was signed to the active roster on Saturday, and Brandon Johnson, elevated for the game, gave the team bodies, but not much production that showed up in the recaps.

Grade: C. And that grade is exactly why Khan’s phone is busy this week.

PASS RUSH: A-PLUS

Now the defense, and wow.

Eight sacks, a season high, spread across seven players, according to the team. T.J. Watt had two. Patrick Queen and Cam Heyward had a sack and a half each. Nick Herbig had one. Kevin Jobity Jr. got the first sack of his career. Keeanu Benton and Alex Highsmith split one.

Watt also now holds the record for the most career sacks against the Cleveland Browns by any player in NFL history. His reaction: “Means I’ve been playing a long time.” And he explained the approach: “We were able to pin our ears back and get after them.”

Grade: A-plus.

RUN DEFENSE AND LINEBACKERS: A-PLUS

Cleveland ran for 34 yards. Thirty-four. And in the first quarter, the Browns had minus-1 yard of total offense.

Queen led the team with 12 tackles, according to the team, plus a sack and a half, a forced fumble, and a pass defended. A week after ESPN put his name on a trade candidate list, he played like a cornerstone. His quote: “We just played ball.”

McCarthy summed up the run defense perfectly: “Our run defense has really set the bar really high these last two weeks.” Two weeks, two opponents held under 40 rushing yards: the Saints managed 18 in Paris, and the Browns managed 34 on Sunday.

Grade: A-plus.

SECONDARY: B-PLUS

The secondary deserves credit for surviving without Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both out with hamstrings. Deshaun Watson threw for 324 yards, according to ESPN, which looks big on paper, but a lot of that came after the game was decided. Jaquan Brisker delivered a hit that briefly knocked Watson out in the first quarter, and Cleveland went 0-for-3 in the red zone.

Brisker’s quote might be the line of the day: “It’s Pittsburgh football.”

I’m giving a B-plus because the yardage allowed is still a little high, even in garbage time. But when it mattered, they held.

Grade: B-plus.

SPECIAL TEAMS: B

Chris Boswell made field goals from 20 and 37 yards and all three extra points, but missed from 57, according to the Tribune-Review. No disasters, no game-changers.

Grade: B.

COACHING: A

And finally, the coaching staff. McCarthy’s decision to go for the touchdown before halftime was the call of the game. The game plan was clear: run the ball, rush the passer, avoid mistakes. And it worked. McCarthy’s own words: “We wanted to run the ball. It was a focal point today.”

He also kept everyone grounded: “We still have things to work on,” and, “I think we’re exactly what our record says we are.”

Grade: A.

OVERALL GPA: A-MINUS

Add it up, and this was an A-minus performance. Dominant on the ground, dominant on defense, smart coaching. The only real weak spot was the passing game, and specifically the receivers. Which brings us to the next two weeks.

FOR THE HISTORY BOOKS

A few marks from Sunday that deserve their own moment.

Pittsburgh has now won 23 straight regular-season home games against Cleveland, according to ESPN. That ties the NFL record for consecutive home wins over a single opponent. The Browns arrived talking about making Pittsburgh theirs and left adding another chapter to one of the most one-sided home streaks in football.

Watt’s record against Cleveland now stands alone as the most sacks by any player against the Browns. Warren tied his career high on his birthday. And according to Labriola, Pittsburgh’s defense has allowed only one second-half touchdown all season. One. In eight games.

Over the four-game winning streak, the Steelers have allowed just 14.3 points per game, per the Tribune-Review. That’s the profile of a defense that can carry a team deep into January.

THE VIEW FROM CLEVELAND

It’s worth looking at the other sideline for a second, because it shows just how lopsided this was.

Browns head coach Todd Monken kept it short: “we didn’t play well enough today.” Deshaun Watson left briefly in the first quarter after the Brisker hit, and Shedeur Sanders came in for a few plays. That stint ended with a fumble by tight end Harold Fannin Jr., and Cleveland fumbled again on its very next possession, according to ESPN.

The Browns also came out of the game banged up. Defensive tackle Maliek Collins suffered an elbow injury in the first half, and cornerback Tyson Campbell was evaluated for a possible concussion. Cleveland visits New Orleans next week.

And remember, all week the Browns’ talk centered on the Steelers’ line. Fautanu revealed afterward how his group handled it: “We started not talking with their players, just to tick them off.” Heyward’s response to all the noise: “Bigger fish to fry.”

THE MOOD IN THE LOCKER ROOM

What I liked most after the game was that nobody sounded satisfied.

McCarthy, asked whether the win raised the team’s stock, said, “You say stock, I don’t know what to apply stock to, but it feels great and it’s important.” Keeanu Benton warned against complacency: “No matter how many games we win, we’ve got to stay level-headed.” Heyward credited the group: “It’s not just one person. It’s a group of individuals doing the job for the team.” And he added, “It says that we can execute at a high level.”

And Watt, who just set a record, sounded like a guy who thinks the best is still ahead: “Our identity is still being formed as the season goes on. A lot of football left.”

That’s the right mindset for a team that just took over first place with half a season left.

WHAT TO FIX DURING THE BYE

Even an A-minus game leaves homework. Here’s my bye-week to-do list for the coaching staff.

One. The passing game on third down. When the run game is rolling, it doesn’t matter as much. But when Pittsburgh falls behind the sticks against a top defense, it needs reliable answers through the air. That starts with getting the receivers more involved.

Two. Ball security at receiver. The Metcalf drop that turned into an interception can’t happen in a one-score game in December.

Three. The red zone. Pittsburgh ranked 27th in red-zone offense heading into Sunday, according to ClutchPoints. The goal-line call before halftime worked, and Warren’s short touchdown worked. That’s progress. Keep building on it.

Four. Health. McCarthy already said it. Come out of the bye “really healthy.” That means getting Echols, Slaughter, and Skowronek back, and seeing whether Wilson and Pittman can return.

THE HEALTH PICTURE COMING OUT OF THE BYE

McCarthy said he wants to come out of the bye “really healthy,” so let’s look at who could be back.

Before the game, McCarthy didn’t rule anybody out for the season. He said, “I think all those guys have a chance to come back,” according to Heavy.

Here’s the breakdown, based on the latest reporting.

Roman Wilson, the receiver with the finger injury, was in the rehab group at practice for the first time last week, according to Ray Fittipaldo of the Post-Gazette. He has 14 catches for 188 yards and two touchdowns in five games this season. One report says he could be eligible as soon as the Cincinnati game on November 15. Other counts of his IR timeline point to the following week in Philadelphia. Either way, he’s trending in the right direction.

Michael Pittman Jr. is the bigger question. He’s on IR with plantar fasciitis, and Mark Kaboly said the team is “not ruling out, I guess, surgery at this point,” and that “if he needs surgery he could be out for the year.”

On the defensive line, Derrick Harmon was walking around the locker room without support, according to Kaboly, though Fittipaldo said he isn’t working with the rehab group yet. And Sebastian Joseph-Day, placed on IR the week before the Browns game, will miss at least two more games after the bye.

And Echols and Slaughter, who missed Sunday with hamstrings, get two weeks to heal.

WHERE THE STANDINGS SIT

Quick standings check, because this is what the win bought. Pittsburgh is 6-2. Cleveland fell to 5-3. The season series is tied at one game apiece, and both teams are now 2-1 inside the division. That means the Browns no longer hold the head-to-head edge, and Pittsburgh owns the better overall record going into the second half.

And the road ahead is real. Right after the bye, it’s Sunday night in Cincinnati. Then Philadelphia, Denver on Black Friday, Houston on Sunday night, Monday night in Jacksonville, and Baltimore at home. Labriola noted that after the bye, the Steelers play nine games, five on the road, against five teams that made the playoffs in 2025. The A-minus team from Sunday will need to be an A team in December.

THE TRADE MARKET HEADING INTO DEADLINE WEEK

Now, the receiver hunt. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

The leading name is still Josh Downs of the Colts. He requested a trade last week. Evan Sidery reported that the Steelers and Chiefs both offered Day 3 picks and that a deal was “expected to go down after this weekend,” likely for “a 4th or 5th-round pick.”

On Monday, Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk, as relayed by Gridiron Heroics, said the Steelers have greater interest in Downs, while Kansas City prefers Jacksonville’s Brian Thomas Jr. In Florio’s words: “Downs, however, is Kansas City’s Plan B, if they can’t get Thomas away from the Jaguars.”

And the San Francisco 49ers have been floated as another possible Downs landing spot, though without a reported offer. One write-up cited Fantasy Points Data, which ranked Downs third in the NFL in average separation score. That’s exactly the skill Pittsburgh’s receiver room is missing.

Downs played on Sunday in a 42-7 Colts loss at Jacksonville, catching four of five targets for 23 yards and a touchdown, according to Athlon.

WHAT THE REPORT CARD SAYS ABOUT THE TRADE

Here’s how I connect the dots. When your grades are A-plus at running back, A-plus on the pass rush, A-plus against the run, and a C at receiver, you know where to spend your extra credit.

The Steelers don’t need to change who they are. They need to fix the one unit holding the offense back in big moments. A quick-separating slot receiver would turn third-and-seven from a coin flip into a manageable throw. And with the schedule after the bye featuring nine games, five on the road, against five 2025 playoff teams, according to Steelers.com, there’s no room for a weak link.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Get Downs now. Not on deadline day. Now. He’d get the full bye to learn the playbook, and he’d be ready for Sunday night in Cincinnati. A fourth-round pick is my ceiling, with maybe a late-round sweetener. Don’t touch the 2028 second-rounder from the Joey Porter Jr. trade.

If Kansas City goes all in on Thomas, as Florio suggests, the path to Downs should be clear. If somebody else, like San Francisco or the Rams, makes a bigger offer, let them. The worst move would be overpaying out of panic. This team is 6-2 and winning without him.

And if no deal happens, the bye still helps. Wilson is trending toward a return. Skowronek gets two weeks for his calf. Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who hasn’t been elevated yet, gets a full practice period to earn a role. That’s not a dream scenario, but it’s not a disaster either.

WHAT’S NEXT

The bye is now. The deadline is November 10. The next game is Sunday night, November 15, in Cincinnati.

T.J. Watt put it best: “Get our bodies back from bye week, come back blazing.”
