Picture this, Steelers Nation. Third-and-seven, Black Friday, Acrisure Stadium. Denver walks a safety down because they have been sitting on Jaylen Warren all afternoon. Aaron Rodgers takes the snap, looks left, and lets it fly to a guy who is already five yards behind everybody. Tyreek Hill. Touchdown. The building shakes. That is the dream a lot of you have been sending me all week, and today I am going to give it to you straight. Because this offense has a real problem making explosive plays, DK Metcalf just got thrown out of another game, and an 8-2 Denver team is coming to town on a short week. So let's talk about what the Cheetah could do in black and gold, what is standing in the way, and what this team has to do on Friday whether he ever shows up or not.

Let me set the scene first, because everything starts with Sunday in Philadelphia.

The Steelers lost 19-16 to the Eagles. They are 6-4. They have still not won in Philadelphia since 1965, and that was a game they were favored to win against a team that came in on a seven-game losing streak. The Eagles jumped out 13-0 in the first quarter, the Steelers fought back, Max Hurleman caught his first NFL touchdown with 1:21 left, and it was not enough. And the defense was not the problem. The Eagles finished with only a little over 200 yards of offense. One report I saw said it was the fewest yards the Steelers have allowed in a loss in about 14 years. I could not confirm that exact stat on my own, so take it with a grain of salt, but the point stands. The defense did its job. The offense did not.

And then there is the play everybody is still talking about.

Early in the third quarter, DK Metcalf got flagged for an unsportsmanlike penalty after shoving Cooper DeJean. Then a little later, with the Steelers down 16-6, the cameras caught Metcalf's hand up at DeJean's face mask, near the eyes, and he got his second unsportsmanlike. That is an automatic ejection. The penalty was enforced from the Philadelphia 19, and the Eagles still turned it into points. One analyst on national TV said Metcalf looked like he heard the result, nodded, and walked off. And for a team fighting for its playoff life, that body language stung almost as much as the play itself.

Here is the good news, if you want to call it that. The league did not suspend him. According to Adam Schefter, the NFL is not suspending Metcalf, but his conduct will still be reviewed for discipline in the form of fines. So he can play on Friday against Denver.

What did Metcalf say? He told reporters, "I was just telling him, stop pushing me. That's all I said." He said he did not try to poke him in the eye. He said teams have been coming after him physically his whole career, going back to San Francisco when he was in Seattle. And when he was asked about leaving the locker room early on Sunday, he admitted, "I was avoiding y'all." He also said, "I just wish I would've been there for my team." That last one is the right answer. The rest of it, I will let you judge.

Mike McCarthy did not hide from it. He said, "We need him in those games, and that's not acceptable to get thrown out of the game." He also said, "We can't have players getting ejected from games." And then on Monday he said something that tells you where his head is. "Life is about learning situations. I do believe in second chances." And he said he appreciated the way Metcalf communicated that day. So the team is handling it internally. No release, no benching announcement, no public punishment.

Aaron Rodgers? "That was disappointing." Then, "Obviously, we love DK." And then the line that matters: "We can't have two of those." Rodgers said he was going to talk to DK, and that they are real good friends. That is a quarterback protecting his receiver in public while drawing a very clear line.

And this is not the first time. According to the AP, this was the third ejection of Metcalf's career, after one against Green Bay in 2021 and one against San Francisco in December 2023. And last November, he was fined $12,172 after an attempt to get at Quay Walker's eyes. Earlier this season, he was also fined $11,941 for taunting in the Cleveland game. So when Rodgers says "we can't have two of those," he is not just talking about Sunday. He is talking about a pattern that this team cannot afford in a playoff race.

Let me walk you through the Eagles game quickly too, because the details matter for Friday. Jake Elliott hit field goals from 41 and 29 yards, and Saquon Barkley ran in a 38-yard touchdown, so it was 13-0 after one quarter. Chris Boswell answered with field goals from 25, 50 and 42. Elliott added kicks from 53 and 55 to push it to 19-9. Then the Steelers finally found the end zone with that 20-yard touchdown from Rodgers to Hurleman with 1:21 left. Rodgers finished 30 of 44 for 227 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions, plus 29 yards on the ground. The Steelers actually won time of possession, almost 35 minutes. But they went 4 of 16 on third down, and you are not going to beat anybody like that. And a second-half kickoff fumble set up more Philadelphia points. Ball security, third downs, and big plays. That is the to-do list.

Now here is why all of this ties into the Tyreek Hill conversation.

Look at the production. Metcalf caught one pass for two yards on four targets in Philadelphia before he was thrown out. On the season, he is around 36 catches for about 434 yards and two touchdowns. One national show pointed out he has only a handful of catches over the last four games, and that the old Seattle version of Metcalf, the guy who used to rip off 20-plus-yard catches every week, has not walked through the door in Pittsburgh. He is catching less than half of his targets this year. That is not what the Steelers paid for when they gave him a massive extension.

And the offense as a whole? Explosive plays are the biggest missing piece. In Philadelphia, the first half was a mess of short throws. One breakdown had Rodgers averaging basically half a yard of air distance per attempt in the first half. Half a yard. The run game got stuffed early. And against Cincinnati the week before, the wide receivers combined for barely 69 yards. When you do not have a receiver who can take the top off a defense, safeties walk down, linebackers sit on the run, and everything gets harder. Rodgers said it himself after the Bengals game. They played a lot of man coverage, press coverage, "and we didn't make them pay at all."

So when Steelers fans say, "Go get Tyreek Hill," I understand exactly where that is coming from.

Now let me be very clear before I go any further, because this channel does not sell you fake news. Tyreek Hill has not signed with the Steelers. There is no report that the Steelers are close to signing him, or that they have even had talks with him this month. As of the latest reports I could find, Hill is still a free agent, still working his way back from a major knee injury, and his agent Drew Rosenhaus has said he wants to sign with a contender once he is healthy. Everything I am about to say is my opinion, not a report. It is a what-if. But it is a fun what-if, and it is a real conversation in this fan base, so let's have it.

Here is what I think Tyreek Hill could do for this Steelers team if it ever happened.

Number one: he fixes the explosive-play problem overnight. That is the whole point of Tyreek Hill. At his best, he was the most dangerous deep threat in football. In 2022 he had 119 catches for 1,710 yards. In 2023 he had 119 catches for 1,799 yards and 13 touchdowns and led the league in receiving. Even if he is not that guy anymore, even if he is 80 percent of that guy, he is still faster than almost anyone the Steelers have on the roster. One deep shot a quarter changes how a defense plays you for 60 minutes.

Number two: he makes Jaylen Warren even more dangerous. Teams have figured out that if you stop Warren, you stop this offense. Cincinnati did it. Philadelphia did it early. Warren had nine carries for 28 yards against the Eagles. With Hill on the field, a defense cannot put an extra man in the box without worrying about a 60-yard touchdown over the top. That is how you open the run game back up.

Number three: he takes pressure off Metcalf. Right now Metcalf is facing a lot of press coverage and a lot of physical play, and it is clearly getting in his head. Put Hill on the other side, and a defense has to choose. Roll the coverage toward the Cheetah, and Metcalf gets one-on-one with no help. That is the matchup Metcalf used to destroy in Seattle.

Number four: he beats man coverage. That is exactly the coverage teams have been throwing at Rodgers. When you have a receiver who wins at the line in a blink and runs away from cornerbacks, man coverage becomes a death wish. Rodgers' quote about not making the defense pay? Hill makes the defense pay.

Number five: he gives Rodgers one more weapon for his last ride. Rodgers said back in May that this is it, that 2026 is his final season. He turns 43 in a few days. You want to give a legend everything he needs for one last run? A Hall of Fame-level speed receiver pairs with a Hall of Fame quarterback. In my opinion, that is the kind of move that could turn a 6-4 team into a team nobody wants to see in January.

And here is the incredible part, the part that gets me fired up. Picture this offense in December. Warren in the backfield. Pittman back from injured reserve. Germie Bernard growing every week. Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington at tight end. Max Hurleman turning into a feel-good story. Metcalf on one side, Hill on the other. That is a scary group, Steelers Nation. That is a group that can score with anybody.

And remember who we are talking about here. Eight Pro Bowls. Five-time first-team All-Pro. A Super Bowl ring from his Kansas City days. This is not a role player. This is one of the most decorated receivers of his generation. When healthy, defenses build their entire game plan around him. Even a slower Hill still demands a safety over the top, and that alone would change everything for Warren and the tight ends.

Let me also give you a realistic timeline, because this is where a lot of fans get carried away. If Hill signed this week, I do not see him helping on Friday against Denver. Maybe not against Houston on Sunday night the week after either. He would need practices, conditioning, and time to learn the playbook and build timing with Rodgers. The more realistic window is the back end of December. Look at the schedule. Monday night in Jacksonville on December 14. Baltimore at home. Carolina at home. Then Tennessee on the road and Baltimore on the road to close the season. If Hill could be ready for that final stretch, he could be a difference-maker in the division games and in January. That is the window where he would matter most.

But now the reality check. Because I promised you honesty here on Second Floor.

Hurdle number one: the knee. Hill dislocated his left knee and tore multiple ligaments, including the ACL, against the Jets in September 2025. He had surgery the next day. Miami released him in February. His agent has said publicly that this was not a run-of-the-mill injury. He has not played a real NFL snap in over a year. Speed is his whole game, and coming back from that kind of injury at 32 is a big ask. Nobody knows what version of Hill shows up.

Hurdle number two: availability. Even if he signed tomorrow, he would not be ready to play on Friday. He would need time to get into football shape and learn the offense. And there is also an open league review connected to off-field matters, which means some form of discipline is possible. That is all I will say about that part, but it is a real factor for any team.

Hurdle number three: the competition. Kansas City has been linked to him for months, and that is his old home. Other contenders have kicked the tires too. If Hill wants to win, he has options, and the Steelers would have to convince him Pittsburgh is the best place to do it.

Hurdle number four: the money and the roster spot. The Steelers just traded for nobody at the deadline. The front office made it clear they did not want short-term moves that would not help beyond 2026. Hill at 32, coming off that injury, is the definition of a short-term move. Would Omar Khan suddenly change his whole approach for one player? I have my doubts.

Hurdle number five: the fit with McCarthy. Mike McCarthy likes big receivers built for cold weather. That is not just me saying it. Gerry Dulac pointed that out during the Josh Downs talk. Hill is listed around 5-foot-10, and his best years came in warm or dome cities. Can he play through December in Pittsburgh at full speed? That is a fair question.

So where do I land? My honest take, and this is opinion: I love the idea. I would kick the tires if the knee checks out, the price is right, and he can be ready for the last month of the season. A one-year, low-risk deal with incentives, the kind of deal that does not block anything for 2027. But I would not bet on it happening, and I would not let it distract this team from the game in front of it. If it happens, it could be incredible. If it does not, the Steelers have to find their explosive plays with the guys already in the building.

And speaking of the guys in the building, let's talk Denver, because the Broncos are no joke.

Denver comes into Acrisure at 8-2. They lead the AFC West. They just beat the Raiders 38-24. Bo Nix does not take many sacks, and he does not throw many interceptions. That makes him a tough quarterback to rattle. But one analyst on national TV had a really interesting take. He said if he had to pick one team to win on Friday, he is choosing the backs-against-the-wall Steelers. His argument was simple. This Steelers defense can make it a long day for Bo Nix. They can get after him. And I agree with that.

Here is why. Denver's run game is banged up. RJ Harvey has been hurt, and nobody seems sure if he is back on Friday. J.K. Dobbins has basically disappeared from the conversation. So the main back you have to stop might be the rookie, Jonah Coleman. Now, to be fair, Denver just beat the Raiders with Coleman carrying a big load, so you cannot take him lightly. But if the Steelers can stop the run on first and second down and make Nix throw on third-and-long, this front can win the game.

The problem is health on that front. Alex Highsmith hurt his ankle in Philadelphia after a strong start, with two sacks including a strip-sack. McCarthy said it is going to be tough for him to make it this week. Nick Herbig was limited on Tuesday after not practicing last week, so he could be back but maybe not at full speed. That means T.J. Watt, Jack Sawyer and the rest of the edge group have to carry the pass rush on a short week. Sawyer said it best: "It's the next man up mentality."

And keep an eye on this. Denver leads the NFL in fourth-quarter sacks. The Steelers have had real problems protecting Rodgers late, and in the red zone they have been one of the worst teams in football at finishing drives with touchdowns. Against a team that closes like Denver does, you cannot leave points on the field.

The betting market sees a close one. Denver came in as a slight favorite, around two and a half points, with a total near 40. That tells you nobody expects a shootout. This is a game that will be decided by a few plays, a turnover here, a red zone stop there. And it is on a short week, with the Steelers coming off a road loss and the Broncos coming off a big home win. Short weeks usually favor the team that can keep things simple. That is the Steelers' path. Run the football, play defense, and avoid the big mistake.

Now the good news on offense. McCarthy said, "I'm very confident that Michael Pittman should be out there this week." Pittman has been out since the Cleveland game on October 1 with a foot injury, and he still has to be activated from injured reserve. If he plays, that is a big body Rodgers trusts. Roman Wilson came back in Philadelphia and caught three passes for 43 yards. Germie Bernard keeps growing. And Hurleman, who McCarthy said "earned it," just caught six passes for 61 yards and a touchdown. Ben Skowronek missed practice with that calf again, so he is a question mark.

Let me give you my trade-and-free-agency board for this week, because I always do this. And again, these are my opinions, not reports.

Tyreek Hill: kick the tires, one-year deal, only if the knee checks out and the price is low. Do not block 2027 for him.

Brandon Aiyuk: still sitting out there in his own situation with the 49ers. I would not chase him unless he becomes a free agent at a low price.

Darius Slayton: a cheap veteran with deep speed who has been on and off the market. A reasonable low-cost option if the Steelers want a vertical threat without the risk.

DK Metcalf: he stays. A release this season makes no financial sense with his contract, and the Steelers need his size. But the next few weeks are a test. Make plays, keep your cool, and the noise goes away.

The core of this team: untouchable. Watt, Heyward, Warren, Frazier, Ramsey, Queen. This is a playoff team. Act like it.

And there is one bigger-picture question hanging over all of this. Rodgers is in his final season. The Steelers drafted Drew Allar out of Penn State, and they have Will Howard from Ohio State, the quarterback who won a national title. One national voice said it plainly: you are not going to find out about those young quarterbacks on the field this year. This season is about getting Rodgers to January with the best possible group around him. That is why fans want Hill. That is why fans want any explosive weapon they can get. This window is now.

So here are my five keys for Friday against Denver.

One: get Warren going early. Not seven carries. Not nine carries. Give him the ball 20 times and make Denver prove they can stop him for four quarters.

Two: take one deep shot every quarter, even if it is incomplete. Make the safeties respect the deep ball. Pittman, Wilson, Metcalf, Bernard, whoever is open. Just take it.

Three: protect Rodgers in the fourth quarter. Denver lives on late sacks. Chip the edge rushers, use Freiermuth and Washington in protection, get the ball out fast.

Four: win the red zone. Field goals will not beat an 8-2 team. The Steelers need touchdowns.

Five: discipline. No personal fouls, no ejections, no free first downs. Metcalf, that one is especially for you. Go out there and let your play do the talking.

My prediction for Friday: Steelers 20, Broncos 17. The defense makes Bo Nix uncomfortable, Pittman comes back and makes a big third-down catch, Warren finally gets his touches, and the Steelers get back on track at home. If I am wrong, I will be right back here owning it, like I always do.

And as for Tyreek Hill, here is my bottom line. Dreaming is free. And this dream would be incredible. Hill and Metcalf on the outside, Warren behind Rodgers, a defense that can take over a game. If the Cheetah ever runs out of that tunnel in black and gold, the whole AFC will notice. But until it is real, this team has to win with the guys it has. And Friday is the perfect time to start.
