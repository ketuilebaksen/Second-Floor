Steelers Nation, the first practice week after the bye is always revealing, and this one delivered. Wednesday’s injury report came out, a key defensive lineman returned to the practice field, the cornerback room got healthier, and the head coach said something every Steelers fan has been waiting to hear for weeks: this team is about to be healthier than it’s been in a long time.

That’s the wave. And it’s coming at the perfect time, because Sunday night in Cincinnati is a six-and-two versus six-and-two showdown for first place in the AFC North.

Now, I want to be clear about what this wave is and what it isn’t. Nobody new joined the roster. The trade deadline came and went on Tuesday without a Steelers move. So these aren’t additions from the outside. They’re reinforcements from inside the building, plus some important context on why the deadline went the way it did and what options could still open up in the weeks ahead.

Here’s the plan. We’ll start with the injury report, player by player. Then we’ll break down the deadline postmortem, including exactly what Kansas City paid for Josh Downs. We’ll look at the free-agent names being floated now that trades are off the table. And we’ll finish with what all of it means for Sunday night. My board at the end, opinion only.

UPDATE NUMBER ONE: DERRICK HARMON IS BACK ON THE PRACTICE FIELD

The biggest news of the day: Derrick Harmon returned to practice.

The Steelers opened his twenty-one-day practice window on Wednesday, designating him to return from injured reserve. According to Steelers.com, he was a limited participant with the foot injury that has kept him out since early October. He remains on injured reserve for now. The team has twenty-one days to activate him, or he stays on the list for the rest of the season.

And here’s the part that matters for Sunday. According to Heavy, Mike McCarthy left open the possibility that Harmon could play against Cincinnati.

Why is that such a big deal? Because the last time the Steelers saw Joe Burrow, Harmon ended the game. In the 30-27 win on September 27, he strip-sacked Burrow in the final minute, and Sebastian Joseph-Day recovered the fumble. That’s the play that sealed Pittsburgh’s first win over the Bengals.

There’s a small discrepancy in the reports about exactly when Harmon went on injured reserve. Steelers.com lists October 10. Another report had October 5. Either way, he’s been out since the Week 4 loss to Cleveland, and his return would give the defensive line a major boost.

UPDATE NUMBER TWO: THE CORNERBACK ROOM GETS HEALTHY

Here’s an update that flew under the radar but could matter a lot against Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins.

According to Wednesday’s report on Steelers.com, cornerback Brandin Echols was a full participant with his hamstring injury. Defensive back Doneiko Slaughter was also a full participant with a hamstring injury.

That’s huge. During the deadline conversation, cornerback depth was one of the positions fans worried about most. If Echols and Slaughter are both healthy for Sunday night, Pittsburgh’s secondary suddenly has the bodies it needs to match up with one of the best receiving duos in football.

Safety DeShon Elliott, who came off injured reserve in late October, was limited with an ankle injury. That’s one to keep an eye on as the week goes on.

UPDATE NUMBER THREE: SKOWRONEK FULL, VETERANS RESTING

Receiver Ben Skowronek was a full participant with a calf injury. Steelers.com’s Bob Labriola had already said Skowronek could be in the mix for the Cincinnati game, and a full practice on Wednesday supports that.

And three key veterans didn’t practice, but not because of injuries. T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward and tight end Darnell Washington were all listed as did not participate for rest. That’s a standard Wednesday approach for veterans, and it’s nothing to worry about. If anything, it’s a sign the staff is managing workloads ahead of a big game.

UPDATE NUMBER FOUR: McCARTHY’S HEALTH MESSAGE

On Monday, McCarthy summed up the week perfectly. “We’re going to be obviously healthier than we’ve been in a while,” he said, according to Steelers.com. He also said: “We just want to get our guys, get them healthy.”

That’s not coach-speak. Look at the report. Harmon practicing. Echols and Slaughter full. Skowronek full. For a team that spent October patching holes all over the roster, this is the healthiest Pittsburgh has looked in weeks.

HOW McCARTHY MIGHT USE HARMON IF HE PLAYS

Let’s think about what a Harmon return would actually look like on Sunday night, because “back” doesn’t mean “every snap.”

A defensive lineman coming off a foot injury after more than a month away usually starts on a snap count. My guess is that, if he’s activated, the staff would use him in specific packages: obvious passing downs, third-and-long, the two-minute drill. That’s where his disruption matters most against a quarterback like Burrow, who gets the ball out fast and punishes you when the pocket stays clean.

And it helps the young players too. Rookie Gabriel Rubio and undrafted rookie Kevin Jobity Jr. have been carrying a heavy load since Harmon and Joseph-Day went down. Jobity got his first career sack against Cleveland. If Harmon can take fifteen or twenty snaps, those two stay fresher for the fourth quarter, which is exactly when the last game against Cincinnati was decided.

There’s also the leadership angle. McCarthy and defensive line coach Domata Peko have both been high on Harmon. Peko said in September: “He’s a guy that, man, has a very, very high ceiling.” Getting that kind of talent back in the rotation before a divisional showdown is the closest thing to a deadline addition the Steelers could have asked for.

UPDATE NUMBER FIVE: WHERE PITTMAN STANDS

Now, the one name fans are most curious about: receiver Michael Pittman Jr.

As of Wednesday, Pittman is eligible to return from injured reserve, but the team hasn’t opened his practice window yet. According to Steelers.com, McCarthy said, “I think Michael is getting close,” and that the staff is watching his progress in workouts. Pittman has said he wants to return “sooner rather than later.”

So he’s not on the practice report yet. That means Sunday night looks unlikely unless the Steelers designate him in the next day or two and he practices fully. But the direction is positive. If he returns after Cincinnati, he could help for the final stretch.

THE DEADLINE POSTMORTEM: WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Now let’s step back and look at the deadline, because there’s new detail on why the Steelers didn’t make a move.

According to the transcript this video is built on, Pittsburgh had interest in three receivers: Calvin Ridley, Josh Downs and Brian Thomas Jr. Let’s go through each one.

JOSH DOWNS: THE ONE THAT GOT AWAY

Downs was the name Steelers fans wanted most, and now we know exactly what it took to get him.

According to NFL.com, the Chiefs sent 2027 fourth- and fifth-round picks to Indianapolis for Downs and a 2027 seventh-round pick. Downs said goodbye on social media: “I am beyond grateful and blessed for my time in Indy.” And he added: “Nothing but love for the Shoe.”

The transcript says Downs wanted to go to Kansas City, and that Pittsburgh wasn’t willing to offer anything higher than a fifth-round pick, which basically took the Steelers out of serious contention. I couldn’t confirm Pittsburgh’s exact offer, so let’s treat that as reported. But think about the math. Kansas City gave a fourth and a fifth. Pittsburgh’s own 2027 fourth-round pick already belonged to Dallas from the Broderick Jones trade. To beat the Chiefs, the Steelers probably would have needed to go to a third, or package future picks.

Would Downs have helped? Absolutely. He gets separation against man coverage, which is exactly what this offense has struggled with. But a third-round pick for a player on an expiring contract is a steep price for a team that will need every premium pick when it’s time to replace Aaron Rodgers.

BRIAN THOMAS JR.: NEVER REALLY AVAILABLE

The second name was Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jaguars. According to the transcript, Jacksonville simply wasn’t interested in moving him. And that turned out to be a theme across the receiver market. The transcript names DeVonta Smith, Garrett Wilson and Ladd McConkey as receivers who all stayed put, plus one more name that’s garbled in the transcript badly enough that I won’t guess who it is.

Those names sound exciting when people link them to Pittsburgh. But if the teams aren’t negotiating, there’s no deal to make.

CALVIN RIDLEY: THE STEELERS WALKED AWAY

The third name was Calvin Ridley. According to the transcript, the Steelers had real interest in the Titans receiver but backed away without submitting an offer after news of an incident at the Nashville airport over the weekend. I’m not going to get into the details of that incident. Titans coach Robert Saleh called it “a very minor miscommunication” and said law enforcement is handling it. Beyond that, the facts aren’t established.

On the football side, Ridley was always a questionable fit anyway. He has just six catches for ninety-one yards this season, he’s in his thirties, and the transcript notes he’s coming back from a broken fibula and has had issues with drops. Pittsburgh already has that concern with DK Metcalf. Adding another receiver with the same question mark wasn’t going to solve the problem.

THE PART THAT’S HARD TO DEFEND

Here’s the honest truth, and the transcript says it too. When you look around the AFC, the competition got better.

Kansas City added Downs. Jacksonville added safety Jessie Bates III, along with defensive tackles Davon Godchaux and Jordan Davis. Denver added linebacker Daiyan Henley. The transcript also says Baltimore brought in a center, but the name is garbled and I couldn’t confirm the move, so I’ll leave it there. What I can confirm is that Baltimore added quarterback Jacoby Brissett from Arizona with Lamar Jackson sidelined.

Meanwhile, Pittsburgh added no one through a trade. That’s the part even defenders of the decision have trouble with. When your playoff rivals keep getting stronger and your roster stays the same, it’s fair to wonder if something was left on the table.

But the transcript also makes a smart point about identity. This isn’t an offense built to win shootouts. The Steelers are built to run the football, play physical defense and let Rodgers manage the game without forcing throws into dangerous spots. A receiver at the deadline wasn’t going to change that identity. The question is whether the identity is enough in January.

THE FREE-AGENT ANGLE: OPTIONS THAT COST NO PICKS

Now here’s where things get interesting. The trade deadline is over, but free agency isn’t. Teams can still sign players who aren’t under contract, and those moves cost no draft picks.

The transcript floats two names. I want to be very clear: there is no report connecting Pittsburgh to either one. These are possibilities, not news.

TYREEK HILL

The first is Tyreek Hill.

Hill was released by the Dolphins this offseason after a serious knee injury. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, described it back in March as “a multiple ligament injury,” adding: “This wasn’t your run-of-the-mill ACLs.” Hill later revealed he needed a second surgery. In July, Rosenhaus said “There really isn’t a set timetable on when he’s going to be ready to go.”

More recently, in mid-October, a FOX Sports report summarized by Fantasy Nerds said Hill was nearing full recovery and that his readiness would be fully assessed after he practices with a prospective team. Rosenhaus has also said Hill wants to play for a contender. The transcript adds that Rosenhaus has been reminding teams they might not need a trade if Hill becomes available to sign, but I couldn’t find that exact comment, so treat it as reported.

Hill also remains under an open NFL personal-conduct review, which any team would have to factor in.

So would a Hill signing make sense for Pittsburgh? On the field, if he’s healthy, he’d be a dangerous weapon. But there are a lot of ifs: medical clearance, the condition of the knee, the timeline and the contract. It’s an idea worth monitoring, not something to expect.

BRANDON AIYUK

The second name is Brandon Aiyuk. And here there’s an update.

According to The Fantasy Footballers, the 49ers reinstated Aiyuk on Monday, November 9, from the reserve/left squad list, and the team was granted a two-week roster exemption. That means San Francisco has until November 23 to add him to the active roster, release him or trade him.

The transcript says Aiyuk is expected to be released. I’d be careful with that. The same Fantasy Footballers report notes that Aiyuk owes roughly thirteen million dollars in bonus forfeiture, which complicates a release. And it’s unclear whether any team, including San Francisco, wants to roster him. The transcript also says Aiyuk would prefer Washington, but that’s not something I could confirm.

There’s another wrinkle worth remembering. As the league’s rules are generally understood, a player released after the trade deadline goes through waivers before becoming a free agent. And Pittsburgh, at six and two, would sit low in the waiver order. That makes an Aiyuk signing even less likely for the Steelers.

Still, the transcript’s suggestion is reasonable: if Aiyuk somehow hits the open market, a workout wouldn’t hurt. He hasn’t played since October 2024, so nobody should expect him to walk in and contribute right away. But a look costs nothing.

THE REAL PLAN: THE RECEIVERS ALREADY HERE

Here’s the reality, though. Neither Hill nor Aiyuk is likely to change the Steelers’ season. The players who will decide it are already in the building.

Let’s go through them.

DK METCALF

The biggest factor in whether this passing game improves is Metcalf. He’s supposed to be the explosive weapon who stretches defenses and wins contested catches. Instead, he’s had too many drops, and he caught just three passes over the last two games, according to Athlon Sports.

The good news, as the transcript points out, is that Metcalf doesn’t need a massive transformation. He just needs to catch the routine passes and finish the plays he’s capable of making. A more consistent Metcalf might be worth more to this offense than any veteran the Steelers could have added.

GERMIE BERNARD

Next is Germie Bernard. The transcript calls him “Jeremy,” but it’s Germie, the rookie second-rounder out of Alabama. According to the transcript, he’s gotten only five targets in each of the last two games. The suggestion is to push that number closer to eight or ten, and I agree.

He’s shown he can create after the catch. He caught the overtime game-winning touchdown against Tampa Bay. And Rodgers has said the rookie’s confidence is starting to match his potential. With no trade, Bernard is the number two receiver. Give him the volume and find out what he can be.

ROMAN WILSON

Then there’s Roman Wilson. The transcript says he’s expected back around Week 11 against Philadelphia, after a finger injury. I couldn’t confirm that exact timeline, so let’s call it reported. Before the injury, he was creating separation downfield and helping on some key third downs. If he returns healthy, he adds a different dimension without the Steelers bringing anyone in. The concern, as the transcript notes, is consistency. He’s had stretches where he’s struggled to stay on the field and stay productive.

MICHAEL PITTMAN JR.

And finally, Pittman. His first stretch in Pittsburgh hasn’t gone the way anyone hoped, with the foot injury limiting him to three games. The transcript says his best performance came in Week 1 against Atlanta. If he gets back to full strength, he’s the reliable chain-mover the Steelers expected when they traded for him.

But the transcript raises a fair question: what if he comes back and still can’t create separation? Health might not fix everything.

OMAR KHAN’S GAMBLE

Put it all together, and you can see the bet Khan made.

He’s betting that Metcalf becomes more consistent. That Bernard keeps developing. That Wilson returns and contributes. That Pittman gets healthy enough to be dependable. And that Harmon, Echols and Slaughter give the defense the depth it needs.

If even two or three of those things happen, Pittsburgh has enough to complement its run game and its defense. If they don’t, the passing game becomes the weakness that keeps this team from reaching its ceiling.

The transcript puts the Steelers somewhere around fifteenth in Super Bowl odds. I couldn’t confirm that number, but it fits the general view: good team, real questions, not a favorite.

THE BENGALS’ INJURY REPORT

Before we wrap up, a quick look at the other side.

Cincinnati’s Wednesday report, according to Steelers.com, listed cornerback DJ Turner as limited with an ankle injury, guard Dylan Fairchild as limited with a pectoral injury, and defensive tackle Jonathan Allen as a rest day.

And here’s a fun twist: safety Kyle Dugger did not practice with a quad injury. Yes, that Kyle Dugger. The same player Pittsburgh traded for from New England before last year’s deadline. He’s now in Cincinnati, and his status is worth watching for Sunday night.

WHAT ALL OF THIS MEANS FOR SUNDAY NIGHT

So let’s bring it back to the game.

The Bengals are rolling. They beat Atlanta 34-17 in Madrid, with Burrow completing 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards and three touchdowns. They’ve won four straight. And they’ve had this game circled since September.

But the Steelers are getting healthier at exactly the right moment. If Harmon plays, the pass rush gets another weapon that’s already beaten Burrow once this season. If Echols and Slaughter are full go, the secondary can match up with Chase and Higgins. And Watt and Heyward are fresh after a rest day.

On offense, the plan is the same. Lean on Jaylen Warren, who’s second in the NFL with 998 scrimmage yards. Give Bernard more targets. Get Metcalf involved early. And use the tight ends in the red zone.
