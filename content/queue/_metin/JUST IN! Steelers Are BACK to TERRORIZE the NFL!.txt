Steelers Nation, the pass rush in Pittsburgh is back to terrorizing quarterbacks, and now there’s a wild idea floating around the internet that would take it to another level. Some analysts are asking a simple question: what if Omar Khan went out and got Nick Bosa? A former Defensive Player of the Year lining up across from T.J. Watt. It sounds like a video game. So today, we’re going to do two things. First, we’re going to look at why this Steelers defense is suddenly scaring every offense on the schedule, from Watt to Nick Herbig to Jalen Ramsey. And second, we’re going to put the Bosa idea under the microscope and give you an honest answer on whether it’s real, whether it makes sense, and where Khan should actually be shopping before the deadline. And let me be upfront: there is no report that the Steelers are talking to the 49ers about Bosa. This is a hypothetical, and we’re going to treat it like one. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The new Steelers identity.

To understand why this conversation is even happening, you have to understand what this team looks like in 2026. This is a brand-new era. Mike McCarthy is the head coach. Patrick Graham runs the defense. Brian Angelichio is the offensive coordinator, and Danny Crossman handles special teams. Aaron Rodgers is the quarterback, with Mason Rudolph, Will Howard, and Drew Allar behind him.

And six games in, the identity is clear. The Steelers are 4-2, they’ve won two in a row, and the engine of this team is a defense built to disrupt. Graham’s unit wants to hit the quarterback, take the ball away, and make offenses play behind the sticks. When it works, it’s suffocating. And lately, it’s been working.

The terror is back: what the pass rush just did.

Look at the last two weeks. Against the Colts, Pittsburgh held Indianapolis to 4-of-15 on third down and didn’t allow a passing touchdown in a 34-22 win. Then in Tampa, the Steelers sacked Jalon Daniels five times, picked him off twice, and held the Buccaneers to 224 total yards in a 26-19 overtime win.

The sack sheet in Tampa tells you how many ways this defense can hurt you. Jalen Ramsey had two, including the blitz on fourth-and-8 that ended the game. Nick Herbig had two. Yahya Black had one. That’s a veteran defensive back, an edge rusher, and a big man in the middle all getting home in the same game. Offensive coordinators can’t just slide the protection toward one player and hope for the best.

And then there’s the tone-setter up front. Cam Heyward is still the steadying force on the interior, with Keeanu Benton and Yahya Black growing alongside him. Heyward’s pressure forced the throw that rookie Daylen Everette intercepted in the fourth quarter. That’s the kind of play that doesn’t show up as a sack, but it changes games.

How Graham turns pressure into terror.

What makes this rush so dangerous isn’t just talent. It’s how Patrick Graham deploys it. He doesn’t line up the same four guys and send them every snap. He moves people around. Ramsey can show up in the slot one play and come screaming off the edge the next. Herbig and Watt can be on the field together, forcing the offense to pick which side to help. Heyward can line up over the center or kick outside. The quarterback looks at the defense before the snap and honestly can’t be sure where the pressure is coming from.

And the numbers back it up. Add up the last two games and opponents converted just 10 of 34 third downs against this defense. That’s under 30 percent. When you get offenses into third-and-long and then disguise where the rush is coming from, you get exactly what Pittsburgh got in Tampa: sacks, hurried throws, and interceptions.

The young pieces matter too. Jack Sawyer, the top backup on the edge, gives Graham a rotation that keeps Watt and Herbig fresh into the fourth quarter. That’s not a small thing. The Tampa game went to overtime, and the Steelers’ rush was still getting home on the final snap. Fresh legs in the fourth quarter are a weapon.

T.J. Watt: still the standard.

Now, any conversation about the Steelers’ pass rush starts and ends with T.J. Watt. Early this season, he reminded everybody why he’s one of the most feared defenders in football. In the win over Atlanta, he had two sacks and a 35-yard pick-six. Against Cleveland, he picked up another sack. Through the first month, he had 4.5 sacks, an interception, and a forced fumble.

Then came the scary moment against the Colts. On his 32nd birthday, Watt got poked in the eye in the second quarter, went to the medical tent, and sat on the bench with ice over his eye while he tested his vision. A lot of players would have called it a day. Watt came back out for the second half. That’s who he is.

And here’s the part people forget: Watt doesn’t have to have a sack to wreck a game plan. Offenses spend the whole week figuring out how to slow him down. They chip him with tight ends, keep backs in to help, and roll protection his way. Every extra blocker dedicated to Watt is one less blocker for Herbig, Heyward, Black, or a blitzing Ramsey. That’s why this rush is so dangerous right now.

Nick Herbig: the other half of the problem.

And then there’s Herbig, who might be the most underrated pass rusher in the league. In Tampa, he finished with two sacks, three tackles for loss, and two quarterback hits. Last season, according to Pro Football Focus, he had 47 total pressures, including eight sacks, and graded seventh out of 115 qualified edge defenders.

Here’s an interesting twist. Back in April, FOX Sports’ Ralph Vacchiano actually pitched the 49ers trading for Herbig, arguing San Francisco’s pass rush had been “completely punchless” the year before. So while some fans are dreaming about Bosa coming to Pittsburgh, there were people in the offseason suggesting the opposite: a Steelers edge rusher going to San Francisco. Khan kept Herbig. And right now, that looks like a great decision. Herbig is in the final year of his contract, which is something to keep an eye on, but on the field, he’s playing like a player who’s going to get paid.

The Bosa idea: what’s being pitched.

Okay, let’s get to the big one. The argument goes like this. The Steelers are contending. Their identity is defense. If you want to push this team deeper into December and January, why not add another elite edge rusher? Put Bosa on one side and Watt on the other, and suddenly opposing quarterbacks have nowhere to step. Rodgers gets more possessions, short fields, and easier games.

On paper, the résumé is undeniable. Bosa was the 2022 Defensive Player of the Year, has multiple Pro Bowls, and has been one of the most disruptive defenders of his generation. In September of 2023, he signed a five-year, $170 million extension with San Francisco that made him the highest-paid defensive player in the league at the time.

But here’s where we have to be honest, because the full picture matters.

Why the Bosa dream runs into reality.

First, the health. Bosa tore his ACL in 2020. He tore his ACL again in late September of 2025, which ended his season almost before it started. This year, he dealt with knee irritation and swelling in training camp, returned on a limited basis in Week 1, and then hurt his calf before a practice in Week 3. As of last week, he had missed three straight games, and the 49ers ruled him out again for their Monday night game against Washington. Kyle Shanahan has said he’ll be back this season, and Bosa himself said returning in Week 7 against Atlanta was likely. But that’s a lot of time on the sidelines for a player you’d be paying superstar money.

Second, the contract. Bosa’s deal is one of the biggest in the sport, and Watt’s extension from 2025 made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in football at the time. Putting two contracts like that on the same defensive front would force Khan to make painful choices elsewhere, especially with Herbig heading toward a new deal of his own.

Third, and this is the biggest one: the 49ers aren’t sellers. San Francisco came into Week 6 at 5-0. Contending teams don’t trade their franchise pass rusher in October, especially when they expect him back within weeks. There’s simply no sign that Bosa is available.

So would Bosa make the Steelers’ defense scarier? Of course. Is it realistic? Not right now. This one is a mirage, and I’d rather tell you that than sell you a dream.

The interior is the secret weapon.

Everybody talks about the edges, but the interior is where this rush really starts. When Heyward, Benton, and Black collapse the middle of the pocket, the quarterback can’t step up to escape the edge rushers. He has to drift, throw early, or take off running. That’s why Tampa’s Bucky Irving managed only 46 yards on 19 carries, and that’s why Watt and Herbig keep finding clean shots at the quarterback. A great pass rush is built from the inside out, and Pittsburgh has the bodies to do it.

What a dominant defense means for Rodgers.

And there’s a hidden benefit for the offense too. Every takeaway and every three-and-out gives Rodgers more possessions and shorter fields. In Tampa, the two fourth-quarter interceptions turned directly into six points, and Dean’s return set the offense up at the 3-yard line. When the defense is playing like this, the offense doesn’t need to be explosive every week. It just needs to be efficient and finish drives.

What a hypothetical Bosa trade would actually cost.

Let’s play it out anyway, because I know you’re curious. If San Francisco ever did make Bosa available, what would it take? This is pure opinion. A player with his résumé would almost certainly cost a first-round pick, likely more, plus taking on the remaining money in his contract. For a Steelers team that’s trying to build around a 42-year-old quarterback right now while also planning for the future, giving up premium picks for a pass rusher coming off multiple injuries would be a huge gamble.

There’s also the scheme question. In San Francisco, Bosa has played as a hand-in-the-dirt defensive end in a four-man front. Watt and Herbig are outside linebackers who stand up, drop into coverage at times, and rush from different angles. Graham’s system is flexible enough that he could find ways to use Bosa, but you’d also be taking snaps away from Herbig, who’s playing as well as anyone on the roster. That’s an awkward fit when you think about it, not a seamless one.

And remember, the Steelers have made bold midseason moves before. In September of 2019, they sent a first-round pick to Miami for Minkah Fitzpatrick, and he became an All-Pro almost immediately. So I’m not saying Khan would never take a swing. I’m saying a swing should fix your biggest problem. Right now, Pittsburgh’s biggest problem isn’t getting to the quarterback. It’s protecting its own.

Trade block: where Khan should really be looking.

Let’s talk about the deadline, and remember, this is my opinion, not a report.

The trade deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday after the Week 9 bye. And when I look at this roster, edge rusher isn’t the biggest need. Watt, Herbig, and the rotation behind them, including Jack Sawyer, give Graham plenty of firepower. If Khan spends big, I think it should be somewhere else.

Number one is wide receiver. Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson are both on injured reserve, and Pittman’s earliest return is after the bye. DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard each caught 11 passes in Tampa, but the offense needs a third reliable option. Jauan Jennings of the Vikings is the name I like, after Pro Football Talk reported Minnesota could move him, though nothing ties him to Pittsburgh. Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland has also been floated by NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala, though a trade within the division would be rare.

Number two is cornerback depth. Brandin Echols is dealing with a hamstring injury, and the Steelers have no timeline for his return. Khan already claimed Sean Murphy-Bunting and Terell Smith off practice squads earlier this month, so a big move here isn’t necessary, but a veteran depth piece wouldn’t hurt.

Number three is the offensive line. Rodgers was sacked five times in Tampa. If Khan finds a reliable veteran tackle or interior lineman at a reasonable price, that would protect his 42-year-old quarterback and help the offense finally keep pace with the defense.

And Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the deadline. So something could be coming. My bet is it’s on offense, not a blockbuster for a pass rusher.

The offense has to catch up.

Speaking of offense, that’s the real story behind the defense’s dominance. A team with a defense like this shouldn’t need a 13-point fourth-quarter comeback against a Buccaneers team that’s now 1-5. The Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters in Tampa and didn’t score a touchdown until early in the fourth.

Mike McCarthy didn’t hide from it on Monday. He said the Steelers left about four or five sets of downs out there and called third downs and the run game priorities this week. Jaylen Warren carried 14 times for 50 yards, and Rico Dowdle had seven carries for 40. McCarthy called Warren “one of the hottest players in the National Football League as we stand here.” The goal is more balance, so Rodgers isn’t throwing it 49 times behind a struggling line.

And when the offense did get going, it looked good. Metcalf had 11 catches for 93 yards, including a clutch third-and-13 grab in overtime. Bernard had 11 catches for 87 yards and the game-winning touchdown. And Rodgers’ touchdown in overtime was his 539th in the regular season, tying Peyton Manning for third all-time. If that version of the offense shows up from the first drive, the defense won’t have to be perfect.

Reinforcements on the way.

There’s more good news on the defensive side. DeShon Elliott is in his 21-day practice window off injured reserve, and McCarthy said Monday he hopes Elliott can return, adding, “I thought he had a great week last week.” Getting a physical, vocal safety back would add even more bite to this defense.

And Jamel Dean is back and playing at a high level. In his first game since his ankle injury, against the team that drafted him, he defended six passes, a franchise record according to the Steelers’ official site, and returned an interception 40 yards to set up the go-ahead field goal. McCarthy gave him a game ball. So even without a blockbuster trade, this defense is getting healthier and better.

Why the defense can carry this team for now.

Here’s the encouraging part. Even if the offense needs a few more weeks to find its rhythm, this defense has shown it can keep the Steelers in any game. Tampa scored only one touchdown on Sunday. The Colts didn’t score a passing touchdown the week before. That’s the kind of floor that wins in October and November, when the weather turns and games get tighter.

And for a rookie like Daylen Everette, every week of experience matters. He was targeted early in Tampa, drew a pass interference flag, and still came back to make the interception of his young career. Young defenders who can respond to adversity like that are exactly what turns a good defense into a feared one over the course of a season.

What’s next: Paris and the Browns.

Now the Steelers head to Paris to face the Saints on Sunday, October 25th, at the Stade de France, with a 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh-time kickoff. They’ll fly out after Thursday’s practice. Despite protests in France, the NFL has said preparations are continuing as scheduled.

Then, seven days later, the Browns come to Acrisure Stadium. Cleveland, Baltimore, and Pittsburgh are all 4-2, with the Browns holding first place on their division record. If this pass rush can keep terrorizing quarterbacks for the next two weeks, the Steelers could take control of the AFC North heading into the bye.

The bottom line.

So, are the Steelers back to terrorizing the NFL? On defense, yes. Watt is still the standard, Herbig is playing like a star in a contract year, Ramsey is doing things no defensive back should be able to do, and Heyward and Black are winning inside. That’s the terror.

As for Nick Bosa, it’s a fun idea, but it’s not happening right now. He’s injured, he’s expensive, and a 49ers team that entered Week 6 undefeated isn’t selling. If Khan makes a move before November 10th, I expect it to help Rodgers and the offense, not add another pass rusher to a group that’s already doing its job.
