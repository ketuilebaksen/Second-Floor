Steelers Nation, after a 38-18 beating in Cincinnati, everybody wants to know what the Steelers are going to do about it. And I’ve seen the headlines claiming the team already announced some shocking decision.

Here’s the honest answer. As of early Monday, the Steelers haven’t announced any major move since the loss. No firing. No blockbuster signing. No benching.

But there are real decisions on the table this week, and one of them has a deadline. A veteran receiver that Pittsburgh was linked to before the trade deadline is now sitting on the waiver wire, and the Steelers have to decide whether to put in a claim. On top of that, they have injured receivers whose practice windows could open, a hole on the offensive line, and a safety with a knee question.

And there’s one more thing a lot of fans missed: the kickoff time for Sunday’s game in Philadelphia has changed.

So today: the Hollywood Brown decision, with the case for and against. The receivers who could return. The injuries from Sunday night. What Mike Tomlin said about his old team on NBC. Why Philadelphia might be exactly what this team needs. And my board, opinion only.

Let’s get into it.

FIRST, WHAT THE TRANSCRIPT GOT RIGHT AND WHAT CHANGED

Quick context. The source for today’s video was recorded before Sunday night’s game. It talked about the chance to sweep the Bengals, secure the head-to-head tiebreaker and strengthen Pittsburgh’s grip on first place.

That’s all gone now. Cincinnati won 38-18, moved to 7-2, and took sole possession of first place in the AFC North. The Steelers fell to 6-3, one game back, with the season series split.

But the transcript’s main topic, whether the Steelers should add a receiver, is even more relevant after that game. Aaron Rodgers finished 15 of 29 for under 150 yards. The offense didn’t score a touchdown until the fourth quarter. So let’s talk about it.

DECISION NUMBER ONE: HOLLYWOOD BROWN

Here are the facts.

On Friday, November 13, the Eagles waived receiver Marquise “Hollywood” Brown, along with offensive tackle Fred Johnson, according to The Liberty Line. Insider Jordan Schultz reported that the split was mutual and that “both sides agreed it would be beneficial for all involved.”

Brown’s time in Philadelphia didn’t go well. According to Yahoo Sports, he had just five catches for 63 yards on eight targets in four games, dealing with injuries and spending some game days inactive. In his final game, a 19-11 loss to the Giants, he played 35 snaps and caught his one target for 16 yards, according to The Liberty Line.

But last year in Kansas City, he was productive. According to Yahoo Sports, Brown had 49 catches for 587 yards and five touchdowns in 2025. He’s also a former first-round pick of the Baltimore Ravens.

The transcript says Pittsburgh was linked to Brown in trade talk before the deadline, and Steeler Nation ran a headline calling this a “surprise second chance” for the Steelers. The trade never happened. Now he’s available for nothing but a contract.

HOW THE WAIVER PROCESS WORKS

This is important, so let me explain it.

Because the trade deadline has passed, any team can put in a waiver claim on Brown. As the process is generally understood, if more than one team claims a player, the team with the highest waiver priority gets him, and priority usually goes to teams with worse records.

That’s a problem for Pittsburgh. Even at 6-3, the Steelers would sit low in the waiver order. If a struggling team wants Brown, it gets first crack.

And there’s a catch, which the transcript points out. Claiming a player off waivers means taking on his current contract. The Liberty Line reports Brown signed a one-year deal with the Eagles in March, though the salary wasn’t listed.

If nobody claims him, Brown becomes a free agent and can sign anywhere. Schultz expects a playoff contender to grab him.

THE CASE FOR CLAIMING BROWN

The transcript lays out the argument fairly, so let’s start with the positives.

Brown can still run. He can accelerate, threaten a defense deep and make plays after the catch. The transcript also mentions some difficult catches he made in Kansas City. He’s getting older, but in the transcript’s view, the speed hasn’t disappeared.

And look at what Pittsburgh’s offense needs right now. According to Athlon’s midseason review, Rodgers’ average depth of target ranked 31st in the league through eight games. This offense struggles to stretch the field. A receiver with real speed could change how defenses play the Steelers.

There’s also a precedent. According to Yahoo Sports, the Steelers added Adam Thielen off waivers last season. So this front office has shown it’s willing to add veterans that way.

Analyst Jacob Punturi, cited by Yahoo Sports, called Brown a possible “low-risk, high-reward” fit. That’s the case in a nutshell.

THE CASE AGAINST

Now the other side, and this is where the transcript lands.

Brown is undersized. He’s had issues with drops. And the transcript doesn’t see him as a polished route runner. On a team that already has 15 dropped passes this season, according to Athlon, drops are a real concern.

But the biggest argument against is overlap. The transcript compares Brown to Roman Wilson: both smaller, fast receivers who can stretch the field. The transcript trusts Wilson’s hands more, while giving Brown the edge after the catch. They’re not identical, but they fill a similar role.

And Wilson has something Brown doesn’t: time with Rodgers. As the transcript points out, he’s been building trust and taking reps with Rodgers over the last two seasons. Wilson also caught a 38-yard touchdown from Rodgers in the Week 3 win over Cincinnati.

So the transcript’s question is simple: what job would you ask Brown to do that Wilson couldn’t? Its conclusion is to wait, evaluate the returning players and see whether the existing group can come together.

WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM THE DEADLINE

It’s worth remembering why the Steelers stood pat on November 10.

According to Yardbarker, the front office focused on protecting its draft capital, especially with Rodgers expected to retire after the season and a quarterback succession plan to think about. McCarthy backed that approach publicly, saying: “I have great confidence in Omar and Art, if there’s something there, we’ll do the right thing.”

Here’s the key difference now. Claiming Brown off waivers doesn’t cost a single draft pick. The only cost is his contract and a roster spot. So the main reason the Steelers gave for passing on receivers before the deadline doesn’t apply here.

That doesn’t mean they’ll claim him. They may simply not see him as an upgrade. But it does mean the decision is now purely about football fit, not about draft capital.

WHAT BROWN WOULD ACTUALLY DO IN THIS OFFENSE

If the Steelers did land Brown, what would his job be?

I’d see it as a specialized role. Line him up outside on early downs to run vertical routes and force safeties to stay deep. Use him in motion and on quick screens to get the ball in space, where the transcript says he’s still dangerous after the catch. And use his speed to open the middle of the field for Pat Freiermuth, Darnell Washington and Jaylen Warren.

There’s also an obvious storyline. If Brown signed with Pittsburgh this week, his first game could be in Philadelphia, against the team that just let him go. Yahoo Sports flagged that possibility too. Players don’t need extra motivation, but that wouldn’t hurt.

WHERE I LAND ON BROWN

Here’s my take. Before Sunday, I’d have agreed with the transcript. Wait for Wilson. Wait for Pittman.

After Sunday, I’m not as sure. The passing game looked broken for three quarters. Rodgers opened with six or seven straight incompletions. And the receivers who are supposed to help are still on injured reserve.

That said, Brown’s Philadelphia numbers aren’t encouraging, and the waiver order works against Pittsburgh. So I’d put in a claim if the contract is reasonable, knowing there’s a good chance another team gets him first. And if he clears to free agency, I’d at least make the call.

But let’s be clear: that’s my opinion, not a report. Nothing I’ve seen says the Steelers have made a decision either way.

DECISION NUMBER TWO: THE RECEIVERS ON INJURED RESERVE

Now let’s talk about the guys already in the building.

Roman Wilson is officially eligible to come off injured reserve now that the Bengals game is in the books. According to SI, he’d missed four straight weeks with a finger injury as of November 12. The transcript hopes he can be back for Philadelphia, but notes that eligibility is only the first step. The team still has to open his practice window.

Then there’s Michael Pittman Jr. He’s missed the last four games with a foot injury, and his practice window still hadn’t opened as of last week. According to Steelers Now, he’s been running on a treadmill and outside in cleats. He said he hoped to return after the bye, but added that he “can’t go out there if I’m not effective.”

The transcript hopes Pittman’s window opens this week with Philadelphia as a possible return game. I’d be more cautious. He’d need to practice before anyone can say he’s close.

If both get back, the transcript sees a clear structure: Wilson stretching the field, Pittman as a chain mover, and rookie Germie Bernard as the most dependable option right now. One correction: the transcript calls him “Jeremy” Bernard. It’s Germie.

THE RECEIVER ELEVATION PUZZLE

In the meantime, the Steelers have to fill out the receiver room with practice squad players.

The transcript lays out the options. Brandon Smith is already on the 53-man roster. Brandon Johnson, according to the transcript, has used all three of his standard practice squad elevations, which means the team would have to sign him to the 53 to use him again. I couldn’t confirm his elevation count, so take that as the transcript’s report.

Marquez Valdes-Scantling, Rodgers’ old Green Bay teammate, is on the practice squad and hasn’t been elevated yet.

And Max Hurleman, the practice squad receiver Rodgers and McCarthy praised last week, still has to come off the practice squad injured list before he’s an option.

That’s a lot of moving parts. And it’s another reason the Brown decision isn’t simple. Every roster spot matters.

DECISION NUMBER THREE: THE OFFENSIVE LINE

The receiver question isn’t the only one. Sunday’s loss created a new problem up front.

Left guard Mason McCormick was carted off late in the first half with a right lower-leg injury, which the team announced as an ankle injury. According to Steeler Nation, he had played every offensive snap this season. After the game, McCarthy said: “Just the love for him is very evident and it continues in the training room right now. We will speak on that when the time is right.”

Spencer Anderson is the likely replacement, according to Steeler Nation. But if McCormick is out for an extended period, the Steelers may also need to add depth on the line. That could mean a practice squad elevation, a signing, or both.

That’s another roster spot the front office has to account for before it thinks about adding a receiver.

THE ELLIOTT QUESTION

And then there’s DeShon Elliott.

The veteran safety hurt his knee in pregame warmups and was ruled out. McCarthy offered only this: “During his warm-up, he had something that showed up,” according to ClutchPoints.

Without him, Joe Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns, and Ja’Marr Chase caught 11 passes for 231 yards. The transcript was excited about getting the dime package back, with Brandin Echols in the slot. That plan never got a real chance with Elliott out.

The team says it’s waiting for more clarity on the knee before Philadelphia.

THE LONGER-TERM RECEIVER OPTIONS

The transcript also looks further down the road. If Wilson returns and is inconsistent, if DK Metcalf keeps struggling, or if Pittman doesn’t look like himself, the transcript says it would revisit outside options.

Two names come up. First, Brandon Aiyuk. The 49ers have a roster exemption through November 23 to decide his future after the NFL reinstated him on November 9. He hasn’t been released, and nothing connects him to Pittsburgh.

Second, Tyreek Hill, who the transcript says is a free agent. The transcript says he’d need to be medically cleared first. Hill also has a pending off-field legal matter, which I won’t speculate on.

The transcript’s reasoning is that those two would offer something different from what Wilson brings. That’s a fair point. But both are hypothetical for now.

MCCARTHY AND THE METCALF QUESTION

The transcript makes one more point I agree with completely: adding a receiver doesn’t take the responsibility off the coaches.

According to the transcript, McCarthy and his offensive staff have talked about trying different things to get Metcalf more involved. Metcalf did score on Sunday, a fourth-down touchdown in the fourth quarter, plus the two-point conversion. But that came with the game long decided.

What the Steelers need is Metcalf making plays when the game is still close. That’s a scheme question as much as a personnel question.

THE BENGALS INJURIES DIDN’T MATTER

One more note from the pregame transcript, because it’s a useful lesson.

Before the game, the transcript pointed to Cincinnati’s injuries: safety Kyle Dugger out, receiver Andrei Iosivas and cornerback Jalen Davis doubtful. The transcript also mentioned a rotational pass rusher on injured reserve, though the name was garbled in the audio, so I’ll leave it out.

But the transcript was careful, and right, to warn that Cincinnati still had its biggest weapons: Burrow, Chase, Tee Higgins, Chase Brown and defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence. And that’s exactly how it played out. The Bengals’ stars carried them, and the missing depth didn’t matter at all.

That’s a lesson for Philadelphia, too. The Eagles are missing their starting quarterback, but they still have talented players. Pittsburgh can’t assume an injury list wins the game for them.

WHAT TOMLIN SAID ON NBC

Now, here’s a part of the story I didn’t expect.

Mike Tomlin, now a studio analyst on NBC’s “Football Night in America,” broke down his former team after the game. According to Steeler Nation, he didn’t hold back.

He said the Steelers had “breakdowns in so many areas” and needed to acknowledge that “they got smashed.” He said Pittsburgh “didn’t get enough done in any of the phases” for any storyline to take hold. And he summed it up by saying the Steelers “ran into a buzzsaw.”

He also said there was something to learn, but only if the team first admitted what happened. Coming from the man who coached most of that roster for years, that’s a pretty powerful message. It’s also consistent with what McCarthy and Rodgers said: own it, learn from it, move on.

A PIECE OF HISTORY, FOR WHAT IT’S WORTH

Here’s a fun note to lighten things up, sort of.

According to Yahoo Sports, Sunday’s 38-18 final was a Scorigami, a final score that had never happened before in NFL history. It was the 1,100th unique final score in league history, and the second Scorigami of that day. The Steelers have now been part of 72 Scorigami games.

Not the kind of history Pittsburgh wanted to make. But it’s a reminder of just how unusual that game was.

THE EAGLES: STRUGGLING, BUT IT’S PHILADELPHIA

Now, here’s why this week might be the right week for a bounce-back.

The transcript says the Eagles have struggled, and that’s an understatement. According to Yahoo Sports, Philadelphia has lost seven straight after a 2-0 start and is the first Eagles team since 1999 to start 2-7.

Jalen Hurts is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, according to Steelers Now. Tanner McKee lost all three of his starts, and the Eagles turned to Joe Milton, whom they signed off the Cowboys’ practice squad on October 16. Milton started the 19-11 loss to the Giants. Philadelphia also has its bye week before hosting Pittsburgh, so it’ll be rested.

But as the transcript points out, Philadelphia has been a house of horrors for the Steelers. According to Steelers.com, Pittsburgh hasn’t won a regular-season game there since 1965, going 0-11 since. The last trip was a 27-13 loss in 2024.

So the matchup looks manageable on paper. History says nothing is automatic.

WHAT A WIN IN PHILADELPHIA WOULD MEAN

Let’s think about what’s at stake next Sunday.

A win would get the Steelers to 7-3 and keep the pressure on Cincinnati. It would end a streak in Philadelphia that dates back to 1965. And it would give Rodgers a personal milestone. According to Yahoo Sports, before Sunday’s game he was one win away from tying Drew Brees for the fourth-most wins by a starting quarterback in NFL history, including the postseason.

A loss, on the other hand, would mean back-to-back defeats and a much louder conversation about this team. That’s why this game matters more than the Eagles’ record suggests.

THE STRETCH AFTER PHILADELPHIA

And the schedule doesn’t let up.

After Philadelphia, the Steelers host Denver on Black Friday, November 27, at 3 p.m. Eastern, according to CBS Pittsburgh. Then it’s Houston at home on Sunday Night Football on December 6, and a Monday night trip to Jacksonville on December 14.

There’s some help on the way for that stretch. According to Steelers Now, defensive lineman Sebastian Joseph-Day is eligible to return for the Denver game. If Wilson and Pittman are back by then too, the Steelers could look very different in December than they did Sunday night.

KICKOFF TIME CORRECTION

One more important detail. The game has been flexed.

According to Steelers Now, the NFL moved Steelers at Eagles from 4:25 p.m. to 1 p.m. Eastern on Sunday, November 22. It’s still on CBS. The change was announced on November 9.

I listed the old 4:25 time in a couple of recent videos, so consider this a correction. It’s a 1 p.m. kickoff.

WHAT ABOUT THE TRAVEL SCARE?

The transcript also mentioned a travel concern before the Bengals game, after a nationwide ground stop on Friday. It said a bus trip to Cincinnati was a possibility if flights weren’t available.

Whatever the travel plan ended up being, the Steelers made it to Cincinnati and the game was played on schedule Sunday night. So that’s no longer an issue.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board for the week. Remember, this is opinion, not a report.

Hollywood Brown: put in a claim if the contract works. If another team gets him, move on without regret.

Roman Wilson: open his practice window on Wednesday. Target Philadelphia if he looks sharp, Denver on Black Friday if he needs more time.

Michael Pittman Jr.: open his window only when he’s ready. No rush.

Offensive line: sign or elevate a veteran guard for depth behind Spencer Anderson, especially if McCormick’s injury is long-term.

Max Hurleman: activate him from the practice squad injured list this week if he’s healthy.

Game plan for Philadelphia: start fast. Give Warren the ball on the first drive. Get Rodgers easy completions early. Don’t let a struggling team believe it can win.

Here’s my bottom line. The Steelers haven’t announced a shocking decision after the Bengals loss, at least not yet. But they’re facing real ones this week: whether to claim Hollywood Brown, when to bring back Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr., how to fix the left side of the line, and what to do at safety.

Mike Tomlin said they ran into a buzzsaw. Mike McCarthy said it wasn’t up to the standard. Aaron Rodgers said they’ll deal with it appropriately. Now it’s up to the front office to give this team the pieces it needs, and up to the players to show Sunday night was just one bad night.
