Steelers Nation, Patrick Queen’s name is all over your timeline this week, and some of the headlines make it sound like something explosive just happened. A bombshell. A shocking decision. Maybe even a trade.

So let me give you the truth right at the top. Patrick Queen has not been traded. He has not asked to be traded. The Steelers have not announced any decision about his future. And the most dramatic claim floating around this week, that the Dallas Cowboys just called Pittsburgh again about him, was shot down on Friday by sources who told a Cowboys reporter it was, quote, “totally fake.”

What did happen is still worth talking about, because it gets right to the heart of what kind of season this is. ESPN put Queen on its list of the top 25 trade candidates in the league. He’s in the final year of his contract. He’s had a rollercoaster season, from “business decision” accusations to what one writer called maybe his best game as a Steeler. And it’s all landing on the eve of the biggest game of the year against Cleveland.

I’m James, this is Second Floor, and today we’re opening the full Patrick Queen file. Where the rumor came from, what the numbers say, what Queen himself has said, and what I think the Steelers should do. Let’s go.

WHERE THIS ALL STARTED: ESPN’S TOP 25 LIST

On Wednesday morning, ESPN’s Matt Bowen and Jeremy Fowler published their ranking of the top 25 trade deadline candidates across the NFL. Queen landed at number 10.

But you have to read what they actually wrote, not just the ranking. Fowler’s note: “Rumors of a Queen trade persisted in the spring, with the Cowboys among teams that checked on his availability.” And then the sentence most headlines skipped: “But the Steelers’ status as a 5-2 team likely keeps him in Pittsburgh.”

ESPN gave a Queen trade a 20 percent chance. One in five. That’s not a prediction that he’s leaving. That’s a reporter telling you why his name is on the list, and why it probably won’t happen. The team fits ESPN listed were Dallas and Buffalo.

Bowen’s scouting report was balanced. He called Queen “a run-and-hit defender with lateral pursuit speed” who can “track the ball on the perimeter and close quickly on underneath throws as a zone defender.” He also said Queen “struggles at times as a run defender in tight quarters, and his route identification can improve.” But then he added the résumé: “he has had at least 115 tackles in each of his four previous seasons,” with “26 pass breakups over the past five years.”

That’s not a guy the league views as washed. That’s a productive starter on an expiring deal. Those always land on these lists.

THE “DALLAS CALLED AGAIN” STORY IS NOT TRUE

Here’s where things got twisted. On Thursday, some outlets aggregated the ESPN item under headlines suggesting the Steelers had received trade calls on Queen, and people started reading that as fresh calls this week.

Then on Friday, Mike Fisher of Cowboys Roundtable reported that, according to NFL sources, Dallas hasn’t contacted Pittsburgh about Queen since May. When asked about the idea of a recent call, one source told Fisher, “That’s totally fake.”

So the timeline is simple. In the spring, Dallas checked in. Pittsburgh kept Queen and paid him his $2.5 million roster bonus on March 13. Nothing new has been reported since. The “bombshell” is a six-month-old phone call.

THE CONTRACT: WHY HIS NAME KEEPS COMING UP

Let’s talk money, because that’s the real engine of these rumors.

Queen signed a three-year, $41 million deal with Pittsburgh in 2024. This is the final year. His 2026 cap hit is around $17 million. He’s set to become an unrestricted free agent in 2027, and the Steelers did not extend him this past offseason.

When we looked at this earlier in the season, the rough math on a trade was about $10.8 million in savings against roughly $6.4 million in dead money, because the roster bonus has already been paid. Those numbers make a deal possible on paper. But possible isn’t the same as smart.

And remember what Queen said back in May, when the first wave of rumors hit. He called the noise a “charade” and said, “If they do wanna extend, cool. If not, cool,” and then, “The better I play, the better we both can be.” That’s a player who knew exactly where he stood and decided to bet on himself.

THE NUMBERS THIS SEASON

So how has that bet gone? Let’s look at the production.

Through seven games, per Heavy, Queen has 48 combined tackles, two tackles for loss, two quarterback hits, three pass breakups, one sack, and one interception. That’s real involvement in every phase: run defense, pass rush, and coverage.

It hasn’t been a smooth ride. The Cleveland game in Week 4 was rough for him, with too many missed tackles. And Tampa in Week 6 brought criticism again. But the trend line in the last two weeks has pointed up.

THE “BUSINESS DECISION” SAGA

You can’t tell the Queen story this season without this chapter.

After the Week 4 loss in Cleveland, former Steelers linebacker Vince Williams accused Queen of “making business decisions,” meaning, essentially, protecting himself in a contract year instead of sacrificing his body. Williams apologized about 40 minutes later, according to Heavy, but the phrase stuck. Podcasts and fans kept repeating it.

After the Tampa win, Steelers Depot analyst Josh Carney criticized Queen for not shooting a gap aggressively enough on one run. And this time Queen answered directly on X. He wrote that it “Wasn’t a business decision. It was me knowing I wasn’t making it so I didn’t wanna let the running back split our defense.” He added, “Something called doing your job,” and told his critic to “Come coach us since you know so much.”

Now, you can argue about whether a player should fire back at analysts online. But his explanation is actually a real football answer. Linebackers are taught gap integrity. If you can’t win the gap cleanly, you don’t run yourself out of the play and leave a highway for the back. Whether he made the right read on that snap is a film debate. Calling it cowardice is a different claim entirely.

THEN CAME PARIS

And then Queen did the thing that ends arguments. He played.

Against New Orleans at the Stade de France, Queen was everywhere. Steeler Nation’s Parker Abate called it “maybe Patrick Queen’s best game in a Steelers uniform,” describing him as “all over the field and attacking gaps on every run play.” Heavy noted it earned his highest Pro Football Focus grade of the season. The Saints finished with 18 rushing yards on 17 carries. And in the fourth quarter, after the Saints missed a 52-yard field goal, Queen’s third-down sack forced a New Orleans punt that set up the game-sealing, all-run drive.

One note on the tackle count, because sources differ. Heavy credits Queen with eight combined tackles in Paris. Other game recaps have him at ten. Either way, it was his most complete performance of the year.

And there’s a nice backstory. After that game, Queen talked about advice he’d gotten from veteran Elandon Roberts, the linebacker who rejoined the Steelers’ practice squad this month. The message was simple: “Be who you are.”

WHAT QUEEN IS SAYING THIS WEEK

Queen hasn’t addressed the ESPN list publicly in anything I could find. But he has talked about football, and his words this week tell you a lot.

On Monday, asked about the defense’s recent play, he pointed to “just having full control of what’s going on on the defensive side the last two weeks.” And on Thursday, talking about defensive coordinator Patrick Graham, he said, “At the end of the day a ‘D.C.’ is your ‘D.C.’ for a reason.”

That’s a player who sounds bought in. Not a guy angling for a way out.

WHY TRADING HIM NOW WOULD MAKE NO SENSE

Let me lay out the football case, because it’s stronger than the contract case.

One, the record. Pittsburgh is 5-2 and playing for sole possession of first place on Sunday. You don’t sell starters when you’re tied for the division lead.

Two, the defensive depth chart is already thin. The Steelers’ final injury report on Friday ruled out two defensive backs, Brandin Echols and Doneiko Slaughter, both with hamstrings. According to SI, that leaves Jalen Ramsey without a true backup at nickel. When your secondary is short, your linebackers have to be smarter and faster in coverage, not newer.

Three, the opponent. Cleveland has a physical run game with Quinshon Judkins, and they ran for 112 yards against Pittsburgh in Week 4. You want your most experienced off-ball linebacker on the field, alongside Payton Wilson, who’s been a tackling machine and grabbed the interception that saved points in Paris.

Four, the price. What would Queen bring back? A mid-round pick, maybe. ESPN’s own analysis frames the interest as old. Trading a starting linebacker for a Day 3 pick in the middle of a division race would send exactly the wrong message to a locker room that’s won three straight.

WHY QUEEN IS NOT JOEY PORTER JR.

Some fans see the Queen headlines and think of the last time a Steelers starter in a contract year got moved in October. Joey Porter Jr. went to Dallas for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth. So why wouldn’t Queen be next?

Because the situations are completely different. With Porter, there was a real contract standoff. Pittsburgh offered a deal reported at around $28 million a year, but wouldn’t guarantee injury protection after the first season, and Porter wanted long-term security. He’d missed time with a back injury, the two sides never got close, and Omar Khan made clear the team doesn’t negotiate during the season. The relationship had reached an impasse, and Dallas was the one serious bidder.

With Queen, there’s no standoff. He’s not holding out, not demanding a new deal, not missing time. He said back in May he’s fine either way. He’s playing. That’s a different animal. Porter’s trade was about a relationship that couldn’t be fixed. Queen’s situation is just a contract with a clock on it, and those clocks run out on dozens of starters every year without anyone getting traded.

THE WEEK 4 TAPE: QUEEN’S REVENGE GAME

There’s another reason Sunday matters so much for Queen personally. The first Cleveland game was the low point of his season, and the start of the whole “business decision” narrative.

Look back at that night. Cleveland ran for 112 yards. In the second quarter alone, the Browns piled up 191 yards and three touchdowns on 20 plays, including a 28-yard touchdown run and a two-yard Judkins score. Queen finished with five tackles and too many misses, and the defense as a whole looked a step slow until T.J. Watt grabbed the headset and things changed in the second half.

Now flip to Paris. The Saints managed 18 rushing yards. The linebackers were downhill, physical, and on time. If that version of Queen shows up against Cleveland, the narrative flips completely. This is his chance to answer the critics in the only place that really counts, on film against the team that started it all.

And he won’t be alone. Payton Wilson has been the most consistent tackler on the defense all season, with double-digit tackle games against the Colts and the Bucs, and his interception in Paris ended a 13-play Saints drive at the Pittsburgh 11. The Queen-Wilson pairing in the middle is the engine of this run defense. Break it up now, and you’re asking someone new to learn Graham’s calls in the middle of a playoff push.

WHAT WOULD A QUEEN TRADE EVEN RETURN?

Let’s play it out, just to see how little sense it makes. This week’s market gave us real price tags. Indianapolis traded Quenton Nelson, an eight-time Pro Bowl guard, to Seattle for a 2027 third and a 2028 fifth. DeForest Buckner, a former first-team All-Pro, went to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth. And the expected price for Josh Downs, according to Evan Sidery, is a fourth or a fifth.

So for an off-ball linebacker on an expiring contract, with ESPN saying the interest dates back to the spring, you’re realistically looking at Day 3 compensation. A fourth-round pick, maybe a fifth. In exchange for a starter on a 5-2 team, in the middle of a division race, with the secondary already banged up. That’s not a trade. That’s a giveaway.

THE BIGGER PICTURE: 2027 AND BEYOND

Now, the transcript I worked from raised one fair point, and I want to give it its due. This roster is at a crossroads. Aaron Rodgers is 42 and has said this is his final season. Several of the defense’s biggest names are over 30. When Rodgers walks away, Pittsburgh could be heading into a transition, and some veterans might not be part of the next chapter.

But that’s a March conversation, not a November one. And it’s one where Queen’s play right now matters. If he keeps playing like he did in Paris, the Steelers have options. They could try to extend him. They could let him walk and potentially collect a compensatory pick. Either path is better than selling low at the deadline.

The ESPN list didn’t change any of that. It just reminded everyone that the decision is coming eventually.

BROWNS WEEK: THE FINAL INJURY PICTURE

Let’s turn to Sunday, because that’s what Queen’s focused on, and so are we.

The good news first. Jalen Ramsey was a full participant Friday and has no game designation. Alex Highsmith, limited earlier in the week with his groin, practiced fully on Friday and is good to go. T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward, and Darnell Washington all practiced fully after rest days.

Ben Skowronek is questionable after three straight limited practices with his calf. If he plays, it’ll be his first action since he got hurt covering a punt against the Colts.

And the bad news: Echols and Slaughter are out. SI says Sean Murphy-Bunting is next up behind Asante Samuel Jr., with Daylen Everette and Terell Smith behind Jamel Dean. Pittsburgh could also lean on a practice-squad elevation, with Sebastian Castro, Darius Rush, and Car’lin Vigers listed as options.

On the Cleveland side, the Browns listed three players as questionable, including offensive tackle Tytus Howard, who spent the week in the concussion protocol. Head coach Todd Monken indicated Howard was on track to potentially clear by Sunday. Cornerback Myles Harden and safety Daniel Thomas are also questionable. Jared Verse, who missed Wednesday’s practice with a knee, wasn’t among the players listed as questionable in that final report I saw, so plan on seeing him across from the Steelers’ tackles.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Alright, here’s the part where I give you my opinion. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

On Queen: keep him. Not just through Sunday, through the deadline. Revisit in March. If he finishes the season like Paris, make him a fair offer. If the price is too high, let him walk and take the compensatory pick. Selling him now saves some cap space and tanks the vibe of a team that’s finally found an identity.

On buying: ESPN’s same list named Pittsburgh as a fit for several players. Brian Thomas Jr. at number 2. Eagles cornerback Riq Woolen at number 8. Josh Downs at number 13. Chargers receiver Quentin Johnston at 20. And Titans corner Alontae Taylor at 25.

Downs remains my top target. Evan Sidery reported that Pittsburgh and Kansas City have both offered Day 3 compensation, with a deal expected after this weekend at roughly a fourth or fifth-round price. At that cost, I’d make the move.

And here’s a new one I’d at least investigate, given Friday’s report: cornerback depth. With Echols and Slaughter both hurt, this secondary is one injury away from real trouble. ESPN listed Alontae Taylor as a possible fit. I’m not saying go get him. I’m saying a cheap, veteran nickel corner might be more urgent than anyone thought a week ago.

What I wouldn’t do: trade Queen to fund any of it. You don’t patch one hole by digging another.

THREE THINGS TO WATCH FROM QUEEN ON SUNDAY

One. Gap discipline against Judkins. The “business decision” criticism came from run plays. If Queen fills hard and finishes tackles, that narrative dies for good.

Two. Coverage underneath. With the secondary thin, Queen and Payton Wilson will be asked to carry tight ends and backs, including Harold Fannin. Watch for Queen closing on those short throws, the strength Bowen highlighted.

Three. Third down. His sack in Paris came on third down. Don’t be surprised if Graham sends his linebackers in those moments. One more big third-down play could swing the game.

MY CALL AND YOUR TURN

My pick stays the same: Steelers 23, Browns 17. The run game leads the way, the defense makes a key stop late, and Queen has a game that makes the ESPN list look silly.
