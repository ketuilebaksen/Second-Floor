Steelers Nation, the Steelers just signed a veteran receiver, and this one comes with a Super Bowl ring and a long history with Aaron Rodgers. But I know what a lot of you were hoping when you saw the word “signed.” You were hoping it was Tyreek Hill. So let me be straight with you right at the top. Hill has not signed with Pittsburgh, and there is no report linking him to the Steelers. The player who actually signed is Marquez Valdes-Scantling, and today we’re going to talk about both. First, what MVS brings to a receiver room that’s been hit hard by injuries. Then, the dream so many of you keep sending me in the comments: Tyreek Hill in black and gold, and how he could help bring a seventh Lombardi Trophy to Pittsburgh. The Steelers are 4-2, they’ve won two straight, and the championship talk is getting louder, so it’s the perfect time to ask the question. Just remember, everything about Hill as a Steelers player is my opinion, not a report. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go.

The signing: Marquez Valdes-Scantling is back.

Here’s the news. According to The Athletic’s Mike DeFabo, the Steelers are signing veteran wide receiver Marquez Valdes-Scantling to their practice squad. The Pittsburgh Post-Gazette confirmed the move on Tuesday. The Chargers had released him over the weekend after he appeared in three games for them this season, catching two passes for 22 yards.

This is a reunion on two levels. First, MVS has been in Pittsburgh before. The Steelers signed him to the practice squad in November of 2025, he worked his way onto the active roster, and he played in five regular-season games, catching 10 passes for 80 yards and a touchdown. He also caught three passes for 26 yards in last season’s playoff game.

Second, and this is the part that should get your attention, he’s a Rodgers guy. Green Bay drafted him in the fifth round in 2018, which was Mike McCarthy’s final season as the Packers’ head coach. He then spent four seasons catching passes from Rodgers, totaling 123 receptions, 2,153 yards, and 13 touchdowns. So he knows the quarterback, and he knows the head coach.

Why “star” fits, and why it doesn’t.

Now, is MVS a superstar? No, and I’m not going to pretend he is. He’s 31, he’s played nine seasons with seven different teams, and his career totals are 221 catches, 3,708 yards, and 21 touchdowns. But he has a Super Bowl ring from his time in Kansas City, and he’s made big plays on the biggest stage. In the AFC Championship Game after the 2022 season, he caught six passes for 116 yards and a touchdown against the Bengals, and the Chiefs went on to win the Super Bowl.

What he brings is speed and trust. He’s always been a vertical receiver, a guy who can run past a cornerback and make defenses respect the deep ball. And on a team that’s missing three of its top five receivers, a veteran who already knows the quarterback’s timing is exactly the kind of low-cost insurance Omar Khan likes. Because he’s on the practice squad, the Steelers can elevate him on game days without committing a full roster spot. Don’t be surprised if he’s in uniform in Paris.

And honestly, in a way, he’s a small taste of what Hill would bring: a receiver who stretches the field. MVS won’t change the offense the way Hill could, but he’s the realistic version of that idea, available right now.

Why this team suddenly looks like a contender.

Before we talk about Hill, let’s talk about why this conversation makes sense right now. A month ago, the Steelers were 2-2 after a loss in Cleveland, and some people were ready to write them off. Since then, they beat the Colts 34-22 and then went into Tampa and came back from 16-3 down to win 26-19 in overtime. That was their first road win of the season.

The defense is the foundation. Over those two games, opponents converted just 10 of 34 third downs. Pittsburgh sacked Jalon Daniels five times and picked him off twice in Tampa. Jalen Ramsey is playing through a broken wrist and still making game-winning plays. Nick Herbig is getting home. Jamel Dean just set a franchise record with six passes defended in one game.

And the quarterback is making history. Rodgers’ overtime touchdown in Tampa was his 539th regular-season touchdown pass, tying Peyton Manning for third all-time. Before that, against the Colts, he completed his first 11 passes and threw three touchdowns for the third straight game.

So when you put an elite defense together with a future Hall of Fame quarterback, you start to see the outline of a contender. What’s missing is the explosive play on offense.

The hole Hill would fill.

And that’s the part of this roster that worries me. Michael Pittman Jr. is on injured reserve with a foot injury. Roman Wilson went on injured reserve last weekend with a finger injury, which means his earliest possible return is Week 11. Ben Skowronek has been out with a calf injury.

That has left DK Metcalf and rookie Germie Bernard carrying the passing game. And to their credit, they delivered in Tampa. Metcalf caught 11 passes for 93 yards, including a clutch third-and-13 in overtime. Bernard also had 11 catches, for 87 yards and the game-winning touchdown. He’s now caught 17 passes in his first two career starts.

But look at how that game unfolded. The Steelers had five three-and-outs in the first three quarters and didn’t score a touchdown until early in the fourth. Mike McCarthy admitted on Monday that the team “left some out there.” When defenses can sit on the short stuff, this offense has to grind for everything. What it doesn’t have is a receiver who can take the top off a defense on any snap. And that is exactly what Tyreek Hill has done his whole career.

Who Tyreek Hill is at his peak.

For the younger fans, let me remind you what we’re talking about. With the Chiefs, Hill made six Pro Bowls, was named first-team All-Pro three times, and won a Super Bowl. Then Kansas City traded him to Miami before the 2022 season, and somehow he got even better. He was a Pro Bowler and first-team All-Pro in each of his first two seasons with the Dolphins. In 2022, he caught 119 passes for 1,710 yards. In 2023, he caught 119 more for 1,799 yards and 13 touchdowns, leading the entire league in receiving yards.

That’s not just a good receiver. That’s one of the most dangerous offensive weapons of his generation. Defenses had to build entire game plans around where he was lined up. Safeties couldn’t creep forward. Linebackers couldn’t sit in the short zones. One missed tackle or one late safety, and the play was gone for 70 yards.

The championship blueprint, my opinion.

So here’s how I see Hill turning this team into a champion, and remember, this is my opinion, not a report.

Step one: he changes how defenses play Metcalf. Right now, teams can roll coverage toward DK and dare someone else to beat them. Put Hill on the other side, and that’s no longer possible. You can’t double both. Metcalf gets more one-on-one matchups, and he’s one of the best in the league at winning those with his size.

Step two: he opens up the run game. Jaylen Warren ran for 101 yards against the Colts and McCarthy called him “one of the hottest players in the National Football League as we stand here.” When the safeties have to stay deep because of Hill’s speed, there are fewer bodies in the box. That means more room for Warren and Rico Dowdle, who averaged almost six yards a carry in Tampa.

Step three: he gives Bernard room to grow. The rookie is thriving as a chain-mover. With Hill drawing attention, Bernard would see softer coverage in the middle of the field, which is exactly where young receivers build confidence.

Step four: he plays with a quarterback who throws a beautiful deep ball. Rodgers and Hill have never played together. But think about Rodgers’ career. He’s at his best when he has a receiver who can win quickly and win deep. Hill is the definition of that. Put the two of them together in December, and suddenly the Steelers have a big-play threat to match their dominant defense.

And step five: he brings championship experience. Hill has won a Super Bowl. Rodgers has won a Super Bowl. McCarthy has won a Super Bowl, and if you’re old enough, you remember who they beat: the Steelers, in Super Bowl XLV. Imagine those three chasing one more ring together, this time in Pittsburgh. That’s the dream, and I get why it’s so powerful.

How the timing could work.

Here’s another reason the idea makes sense on paper. The Steelers’ bye comes in Week 9. If Hill were to sign somewhere in the next few weeks, he’d need time to get back into football shape anyway. The second half of the season, after the bye, is when Pittman would also be eligible to come back from injured reserve, and when Wilson could return. Picture Metcalf, Pittman, Bernard, Wilson, and Hill all healthy in December. That’s a receiver room that could match up with anybody in the AFC.

And a playoff run is built in December and January, not October. Steelers fans know that better than anyone. The 2008 team that won the franchise’s sixth Lombardi Trophy was built on a dominant defense and an offense that made enough big plays at the right time. That’s the formula this team is trying to follow.

The reality check: where things really stand.

Now, I promised you the full truth, and this is where the dream gets complicated.

First, Hill still hasn’t signed with anyone. He’s 32 years old. He dislocated his left knee and tore multiple ligaments, including his ACL, at the end of September 2025, and he played only four games that season. Miami released him in February. During his rehab this summer, he admitted he had “no power” in his left leg, although later videos showed him looking more explosive. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said on The Pat McAfee Show that a return could come “perhaps some time in October.” So the knee is still a question mark, and for a receiver whose whole game is built on speed, that matters.

Second, the contract. According to The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones, Hill doesn’t want to sign a veteran minimum deal. Rosenhaus has called the situation “very fluid” and said a deal “could happen any time.” But a player coming off a major knee injury who wants more than the minimum is a harder sell for any team.

Third, there’s an off-field issue. Hill is involved in a civil case, and reporting indicates there’s an open review under the league’s personal conduct policy that could lead to discipline. That makes his availability for this season uncertain no matter where he signs.

Fourth, the Chiefs. Kansas City is the only team that’s been specifically linked to Hill. The Chiefs have injuries at receiver, with Tyquan Thornton likely out for the rest of the regular season and Rashee Rice dealing with a hamstring injury. But Jones reported that he doesn’t “get the sense” the Chiefs are “in a rush” to sign Hill, and Kansas City has been looking at other options, including hosting former receiver Justin Watson for a visit.

And fifth, the Steelers. There is no report that Pittsburgh has interest in Hill, has spoken with his camp, or has looked into signing him. None. That’s the honest truth.

So what would it take for Pittsburgh?

If the Steelers ever did want to get involved, here’s what I think it would look like. Again, opinion. A short-term, incentive-heavy contract. A base salary low enough to protect the team if the knee doesn’t hold up, with bonuses tied to games played and production. That’s the kind of structure Omar Khan likes, because it limits the risk.

The question is whether Hill would accept that, given that he doesn’t want the minimum. And the bigger question is whether the off-field situation and the knee make it worth the headache at all. Khan has built this roster with a lot of quiet, low-risk moves. A high-profile signing with this much uncertainty would be very different from what we’ve seen from him this year.

So my honest odds? Low. Very low. But not zero, because receiver injuries keep piling up, and the NFL changes fast.

Trade block: the realistic paths to a championship receiver.

Since Hill is a long shot, let’s look at what Khan could realistically do before the November 10th trade deadline. This is my opinion, not a report.

The most realistic move is a veteran receiver through a trade. Jauan Jennings of the Vikings is the name I keep circling after Pro Football Talk reported Minnesota could move him, though there’s nothing tying him to Pittsburgh. Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland has been floated by NFL Network’s Aditi Kinkhabwala, but a deal within the AFC North would be rare. And Mark Kaboly has reported “strong indications” that the Steelers will be active before the deadline.

Khan also has ammunition. The Steelers got a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick from Dallas in the Joey Porter Jr. trade. That second-rounder could turn into a receiver who helps right away. That’s the kind of move that doesn’t make as much noise as a Hill signing, but it could matter just as much in January.

Why the Steelers can win without Hill.

And here’s the thing I want every Steelers fan to remember. Even without Hill, this team has a path. The defense is playing at a championship level. Rodgers is still producing. Warren and Dowdle give the offense balance. Metcalf and Bernard just combined for 22 catches in a road win. And now Valdes-Scantling gives Rodgers a familiar face who can run deep, which helps the offense stretch the field until the injured receivers return. And reinforcements are coming, with DeShon Elliott’s activation decision due this week, Pittman and Wilson eligible later in the season, and the trade deadline still ahead.

Championships aren’t won by one signing. They’re won by a team that gets better as the season goes on. If the offense can start faster, convert more third downs, and finish drives, this team doesn’t need a superstar addition to be dangerous in January.

What Hill would mean for the Steelers’ identity.

I also think it’s worth asking a bigger question. What kind of team do the Steelers want to be? Under McCarthy and Patrick Graham, the identity is clear: run the ball, protect the quarterback, play aggressive defense, and win the turnover battle. Adding Hill wouldn’t change that identity. It would sharpen it. Every great defensive team in Steelers history had an offense that could hit a big play when the game was on the line. Lynn Swann and John Stallworth did it in the 1970s. Hines Ward and Santonio Holmes did it in the 2000s. Holmes caught the game-winning touchdown in Super Bowl XLIII. That’s the role a player like Hill could fill, a closer on offense to match a closer on defense.

But it also means being smart about risk. The Steelers have built a culture this year that McCarthy keeps talking about. A signing that brings distractions, uncertain availability, or a knee that isn’t ready could hurt more than it helps. That’s why any move, for Hill or anyone else, has to fit what’s already working.

The next test: Paris.

Before any of that, there’s a game to win. And it’ll be interesting to see whether MVS gets elevated for that game, because the receiver room needs bodies. The Steelers face the New Orleans Saints in Paris on Sunday at the Stade de France, with a 9:30 a.m. Pittsburgh-time kickoff. They fly out after Thursday’s practice. Then the Browns come to Pittsburgh seven days later. Right now, the Steelers, Browns, and Ravens are all 4-2, with Cleveland in first place on its division record.

Win those two games, and the Steelers head into the bye with momentum, a healthy roster coming back, and a real shot at the AFC North crown. That’s when the championship conversation stops being a dream and starts being a plan.

The bottom line.

Could Tyreek Hill make the Steelers champions? On paper, the fit is incredible. His speed would change everything about this offense, open up Metcalf and the running game, and give Rodgers the deep threat he’s been missing. With this defense, that combination would scare anybody in the league.

But the reality is different. Hill hasn’t signed, his knee is still a question, he wants more than the minimum, there’s an off-field review hanging over him, and Kansas City is the only team linked to him. The Steelers aren’t in the picture right now. So keep dreaming, but notice what Khan actually did this week: he brought back a veteran with speed and a Rodgers connection, at almost no cost. And keep watching the trade deadline, because that’s where his bigger move is likely to come from.
