Steelers Nation, the Colts game is in the books, and now the real work starts upstairs. With Michael Pittman Jr. on injured reserve, Pittsburgh played Indianapolis without one of its top two receivers, and the question coming out of that game is simple: which weapon does Omar Khan add next? Because let’s be clear about one thing right away. As I record this, the Steelers have not signed or traded for a new receiver. There’s no report of a deal. But the window to add one is wide open, the trade deadline is November 10th, and the biggest receiver trade of the season just went down without Pittsburgh. So today we’re doing something different: five ways Khan could handle it, ranked from the boldest to the smartest, and I’ll tell you exactly which one I’d go get. Everything you’re about to hear is my opinion unless I tell you it’s a report, and I’ll be clear every single time. This is James with Second Floor. Let’s go to work.

Before we start the countdown, let’s talk about the trade that changed the market this weekend: Marvin Harrison Jr. Arizona was finalizing a deal for him on Saturday, and the Cardinals kept him inactive for their Sunday game against Detroit. Reports said the destination would be held until Sunday’s games were over, so by the time you’re watching this, it may already be official. And every sign pointed to the same place. Pro Football Talk reported that Houston was the likely home, with the Texans offering a 2028 second-round pick while Arizona wanted a 2027 second.

And when you look at Houston’s roster, it makes total sense. Back in August, second-year receiver Jayden Higgins tore his ACL, ending his 2026 season. Higgins was supposed to be the number two receiver next to Nico Collins. So the Texans had a hole, a quarterback in C.J. Stroud who played with Harrison at Ohio State, and a GM in Nick Caserio who needed an answer. That’s a perfect storm for a deal. It’s also a reminder for Steelers fans: when a team has a clear need and a clean connection, it usually wins these races. Pittsburgh had the need, but no report ever put the Steelers in the room.

And one number explains why Khan might not be heartbroken. Pro Football Focus noted this week that Harrison’s receiving grade has dropped every season of his career, down to 51.9 this year, with a drop rate of 16.7 percent. PFF also said he has “immense potential.” Both things are true. But a declining grade and a high drop rate are exactly the kind of red flags that make a disciplined GM say, let somebody else take that bet.

Before we get to the options, there’s one piece of the Pittman situation that changes the math, and a lot of people are missing it. When a player goes on injured reserve during the season, he has to miss at least four games. Pittman went on IR the day before the Colts game, so that game counts as the first of the four, and he’ll miss the next three as well. Then comes the Week 9 bye. That means the earliest he could realistically be back is right after the bye, which is the same week as the trade deadline. Think about that. Khan isn’t looking for a receiver for the rest of the season. He’s looking for one to get through about a month. That’s a completely different shopping list. It’s a lot easier to justify a cheap veteran than a second-round pick when the starter you’re replacing could be back in early November.

So with Harrison likely gone, which weapon should Pittsburgh add? Here’s the countdown.

Option number five: the blockbuster swing, Brian Thomas Jr.

I’m starting with the biggest name, because it’s the one Steelers Nation keeps asking about, and because it’s the move with the most upside on the board.

Thomas is the receiver who, on paper, should be the most attractive young wideout available to anybody. His 2024 rookie season was outstanding: 87 catches, 1,282 yards, 10 touchdowns. Since then, the role has shrunk. In 2025, he had 48 catches for 707 yards and two touchdowns. This year, he has nine catches in four games as Jacksonville’s third receiver. He’s 24 years old, and his cap hit is under 4 million dollars.

Here’s why he’s at number five and not number one. Jacksonville doesn’t want to sell. Jaguars GM James Gladstone called the trade talk “fraudulent,” and The Athletic’s Jeff Howe reported the Jaguars have already said no to significant offers. When a team turns down real offers, the price is not going down. If Khan wants Thomas, he’s probably talking about a first-round-level conversation, and that’s a lot of capital for a player who’s been a third option for a year and a half.

But let me play the other side for a second. With Harrison likely headed to Houston, Thomas just became the most interesting young receiver left in this conversation. Teams that lost out on Harrison might turn to Jacksonville next. That could raise the price even more, or it could force the Jaguars to finally decide what Thomas is worth to them. Either way, Pittsburgh would be bidding against other teams, and Khan has never loved bidding wars.

There’s also a calendar wrinkle here. The Steelers and Jaguars meet on Monday Night Football on December 14th. Imagine Thomas suiting up in black and gold against his old team in prime time. That’s a storyline the league would love. But storylines don’t win trades. My grade on this option: highest ceiling, lowest probability.

Option number four: the free-agent rental, Tyreek Hill.

This one doesn’t cost a single draft pick, which is exactly why some fans love it.

Hill is a free agent. He’s 32, and he’s coming back from knee ligament tears. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said he expected Hill to sign within days, and the Chiefs have been in touch with him. There is no report linking him to Pittsburgh, and Sports Illustrated called a Steelers signing “extremely unlikely.”

So why is he even on this list? Because Pittsburgh’s need is short-term and urgent. Pittman is out for at least four games. Rodgers is 42. If Hill is healthy enough to be even 70 percent of the player he used to be, he gives defenses something they have to account for on every snap. And it would cost nothing but cap space.

The problem is the knee and the roster spot. Pittsburgh’s 53-man roster is full. Every free-agent signing means someone has to go. And if you’re bringing in a receiver who has to work his way back from a major knee injury, you’re betting that he’ll be ready by November and December. If the Chiefs are already talking to him, he may never even reach the open market in a real way. My grade on this one: cheap, exciting, and very unlikely.

Option number three: the Chargers two-for-one.

Now this is my own idea, and I want to be extremely clear: this is a fan’s trade proposal, not a report, not a rumor, nothing anybody has said is being discussed.

Pittsburgh has two needs: an outside receiver and depth at cornerback. And one team has interesting pieces at both spots: the Los Angeles Chargers.

At receiver, there’s Quentin Johnston. He’s a former first-round pick, and PFF named him one of its trade candidates this week. His career receiving grade is 65.2, with 1.28 yards per route run. But this year has been rough: 0.93 yards per route run and a drop rate of 22.2 percent. His contract runs through 2027, so he wouldn’t be a rental.

At cornerback, there’s a name you already know: Donte Jackson. He had five interceptions for the Steelers in 2024 before signing with the Chargers, then had four more interceptions and 12 passes defended in Los Angeles last year. This is the final year of his deal, with a 6 million dollar base salary. He’s dealt with a concussion and missed Week 5 with a hamstring injury, so health is a real question.

So here’s the idea. Instead of trying to pay a second-round pick and more for one player, you call the Chargers and ask about both, using mid-round picks. Johnston is a big-bodied receiver who needs a fresh start. Jackson already knows the building and the defense. In a perfect world, Khan fills two holes in one phone call.

Now, the risks. Johnston’s drop numbers this year are honestly worse than Harrison’s. Jackson is banged up. And the Chargers have to want to do it, which nobody has suggested they do. It’s creative, but creative trades fall apart all the time. My grade: the most interesting idea on the list, but a long shot.

And why does Donte Jackson’s name keep coming up with Steelers fans? Because his 2024 season in Pittsburgh was legit: five interceptions and eight passes defended. He played with confidence, he made plays on the ball, and he fit what the defense wanted from its outside corners. Bringing back a player who already knows the coaches, the terminology, and the locker room is worth something in the middle of a season. There’s no learning curve. That’s the kind of hidden value that never shows up in a trade grade on the internet.

Option number two: no trade, trust the room.

This is the safe option, and plenty of smart people in Pittsburgh will argue for it. It doesn’t add a weapon from outside. It bets that the weapons are already in the building.

Let’s be honest about what Pittsburgh has. DK Metcalf is the top receiver and a matchup nightmare. Roman Wilson has taken a real step forward. Germie Bernard is a rookie who’s earned trust from Rodgers. Ben Skowronek is a reliable blocker and special-teamer. And the Steelers elevated Brandon Smith from the practice squad for the Colts game. That’s not a great receiver room, but it’s not a broken one.

The deadline is November 10th, the Tuesday after Pittsburgh’s Week 9 bye. That gives Khan four more games to see what this group looks like without Pittman. If Wilson keeps growing and Bernard becomes a real third option, the Steelers can save their draft picks for the spots that matter more. If the offense stalls, Khan will have four games of tape to justify a bigger move, and he’ll still have that 2028 second-round pick from the Dallas trade in his pocket.

And remember what Khan did when Pittman went on IR. The open roster spot didn’t go to a receiver. It went to defensive back Doneiko Slaughter. That tells you the front office believes in its receivers enough for now, and is more worried about the secondary with Jamel Dean out and DeShon Elliott still on IR.

That’s the case for patience: wait through October, then be aggressive by November 10th if the tape says you need to be. It’s a fair plan. But here’s why it’s my number two and not my number one: four games is a quarter of the season, and every week without a reliable second outside receiver puts more weight on Metcalf and on a 42-year-old quarterback. A cheap veteran now protects those four games without touching the future.

Option number one: the veteran weapon, Calvin Ridley.

And here’s my top pick. It’s not the flashiest name, but I think it’s the smartest weapon on the board, and that’s exactly why I like it more than most people would.

Ridley is with the Tennessee Titans. He’s 31. PFF listed him as a trade candidate, pointing out that he has only five targets through four games and 0.74 yards per route run. He only played four games in 2025. And as PFF put it, “The Titans have a young and deep receiver room.” Translation: he’s not in their plans the way he used to be.

Why does that make him interesting for Pittsburgh? Because the price should be low. You’re not paying for potential here. You’re paying for a veteran who has been a number one receiver before, who knows how to run routes against NFL defenses, and who might be thrilled to go from five targets in a month to playing with Aaron Rodgers and DK Metcalf on a team fighting for the playoffs.

The Steelers’ offense is built around veterans right now. Rodgers is in his final chapter. Metcalf is the top target. A savvy route runner who can win on third down fits that timeline better than a 24-year-old project who needs two years to develop. If Ridley can still separate, he’s the kind of player Rodgers trusts quickly.

The risk? Age and production. A receiver who’s been barely targeted for two seasons might not have much left. And his contract matters. Before any deal, Pittsburgh would have to know exactly how much money is coming with him, and whether Tennessee would eat some of it. My grade: realistic, low-cost, and the move I’d make. If I’m Khan, this is the call I’m making this week, while the Dallas second-round pick stays right where it is.

And let’s talk about that Dallas pick for a second, because it’s the most important asset in this whole conversation. When Pittsburgh traded Joey Porter Jr. to the Cowboys, it got a 2028 second-round pick back. That pick gives Khan flexibility. He can use it at the deadline, he can hold it, or he can package it with a later pick for a bigger player. What he shouldn’t do is spend it out of panic after one bad Sunday. A second-round pick two years from now is a cheap, controllable starter in 2028, when Rodgers will be gone and the roster will need young talent. That’s why, in my opinion, the bar for using it should be high. A rental receiver for a month doesn’t clear that bar. A young, cost-controlled starter for three or four years might.

Now, let’s talk about the trade block itself, because here’s one more name to keep in mind.

This one is purely my opinion, not a report. If Pittsburgh does decide to make a move later this month, the player I’d watch most closely is the one already circled on the schedule: Brian Thomas Jr. Not because Jacksonville wants to deal him. They clearly don’t, at least not right now. But because the trade market tends to change once the deadline gets close. A team that’s out of the race in early November can look at the same player very differently than it did in early October. If the Jaguars are struggling a month from now and Thomas is still the third option, the conversation could change. That’s when Khan’s patience could pay off.

And one more thought on the whole Harrison saga. A lot of fans are going to look at Houston and say Pittsburgh missed out. I get it. When a former fourth overall pick moves, it feels like a big deal. But look at the numbers again: a falling PFF grade every year, a high drop rate, and one AFC executive describing him as “a third receiver with upside.” If the price really was a second-round pick and more, I’m not sure Pittsburgh lost anything by staying out of it. Sometimes the best trade is the one you don’t make.

Now, let’s connect it to the Colts game, because that’s where this decision really starts.

Both teams came into that game at 2-2. Indianapolis had won two in a row, including a 30-13 win over Washington, and the Colts hadn’t won in Pittsburgh since November 9th, 2008, almost 18 years.

That game was the first look at the Steelers’ offense without Pittman, and it’s the first piece of evidence for everything we just talked about. Whatever the scoreboard said, the tape is what Khan will study this week. If Wilson and Bernard made plays, the case for patience gets a little stronger. If Rodgers kept forcing the ball to Metcalf into double coverage and nobody else could get open, the phones in Khan’s office are going to get a lot busier.

Here’s what I’d go back and look at in the film on offense. First, how many targets go to receivers not named Metcalf. Second, whether the Steelers use more two-tight-end sets with Pat Freiermuth to make up for the missing receiver. Third, whether the running game, with Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle, who was questionable coming into the weekend, took pressure off the passing game. The Colts’ interior, with DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart, is strong, so the plan was always to get the runs outside.

On defense, the job was Jonathan Taylor. The week before, he ran for 95 yards and two touchdowns on 20 carries against Washington. And Daniel Jones came in with seven turnovers in four games, so the chances were there for a defense that lives on takeaways. The Colts were also without pass rusher Arden Key, with Keenan Allen and Charvarius Ward listed as questionable. But here’s the point for today’s video: no matter how the defense played, the receiver question doesn’t go away. One game doesn’t fill a four-week hole.

And think about how wild the timing of this Sunday was. Harrison’s new home was set to be revealed right as the games wrapped up. The trade-market story and the on-field story landed on the same afternoon. So now Khan has both pieces of information: where the best young receiver on the market went, and what his own offense looks like without Pittman. That’s exactly when a GM starts making calls.

Let me rank the options one more time so you can screenshot it.

Number five, Brian Thomas Jr.: the biggest upside, the highest price, and a team that doesn’t want to sell.

Number four, Tyreek Hill: no draft pick, but a 32-year-old coming off knee ligament tears, with the Chiefs already in contact.

Number three, a Chargers deal for Quentin Johnston and Donte Jackson: my own creative idea, two needs filled at once, but plenty of risk.

Number two, no trade: trust the room for four weeks, then decide by November 10th.

And number one, Calvin Ridley: the low-cost veteran weapon I’d add right now, without touching the Dallas pick.
