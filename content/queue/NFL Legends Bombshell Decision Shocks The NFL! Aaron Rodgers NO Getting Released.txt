Steelers Nation, let's untangle that headline first, because it's mixing up a few different stories.

There was no new "bombshell decision" by an NFL legend. What actually happened is this. The Steelers released Aaron Rodgers on Wednesday. According to Yahoo Sports, he cleared waivers on Thursday, which makes him a free agent. And then several NFL legends, including Steelers great Terry Bradshaw, publicly criticized the way Rodgers left.

And then, on Friday, the Steelers did the most important thing of all. They went out with Mason Rudolph at quarterback and beat the 8-2 Denver Broncos 27-5.

So today we've got a lot to cover. What the legends said. Where Rodgers stands now. How the Steelers responded on the field. And then a bigger question that the video I'm working from today raises: after everything that's happened, can this team finally break its playoff curse?

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let's go.

FIRST: STEELERS 27, BRONCOS 5

Let's start with the good news, because after the week this team had, it deserves to go first.

According to ESPN, the Steelers beat the Broncos 27-5 on Black Friday at Acrisure Stadium. They led wire-to-wire and were up 24-5 at halftime. The Steelers are now 7-4, and the two-game losing streak is over.

According to NFL.com, ESPN Analytics gave them a 74 percent chance to make the playoffs after the win. ESPN's own recap put it at 92 percent. Either way, that's a big jump from where they were a week ago.

THE DEFENSE SCORED TWICE

This game was won by the defense, and it wasn't close.

According to Sky Sports, T.J. Watt recovered a Bo Nix fumble in the end zone for a touchdown in the first quarter. That was the Steelers' first score.

Then, with 1:33 left in the first half, Jalen Ramsey intercepted Nix and returned it 36 yards for a touchdown, according to Sky Sports. According to ESPN, that was Ramsey's second career pick-six. His first came as a rookie in 2016.

And to finish it off, rookie cornerback Daylen Everette intercepted Nix at the Pittsburgh 7-yard line to end the game, according to Sky Sports.

Nix finished 22-of-40 for 169 yards with two interceptions, according to ESPN. And according to NFL.com, only four of his completions came on passes thrown more than 10 yards downfield.

THE RUN DEFENSE SHUT DENVER DOWN

Denver's run game didn't go anywhere either.

According to NFL.com, the Steelers held rookie Jonah Coleman to 26 yards on 15 carries. ESPN noted that Denver averaged under three yards per carry for just the third time this season.

And that came without Alex Highsmith, and with Nick Herbig still working his way back. According to Bleacher Report, Herbig had a key sack that knocked Denver out of field goal range. So the edge rotation held up, even short-handed.

WHAT THE WIN MEANS IN THE STANDINGS

This wasn't just a feel-good win. It mattered in the AFC race.

According to ESPN, the Steelers entered the game holding the final wild-card spot in the AFC. Now they're 7-4. And Denver fell to 8-3, two games behind Buffalo for the AFC's top seed, according to ESPN.

Beating a team that good, by that much, also matters if tiebreakers come into play later. Head-to-head wins against other playoff teams are exactly the kind of thing that decides seeding in January.

MASON RUDOLPH'S DAY

Now, the quarterback everyone was watching.

According to NFL.com, Rudolph made his 20th career start, and his first since Week 12 of last season. He completed 9 of 15 passes for 142 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions.

According to ESPN, he completed his first five passes for 94 yards. He found rookie Germie Bernard for a 58-yard catch-and-run, which set up a 22-yard touchdown pass to DK Metcalf over Pat Surtain II.

And in the fourth quarter, he hit Roman Wilson for 37 yards, setting up Chris Boswell's 33-yard field goal, according to Sky Sports.

Nine completions isn't a lot. But Rudolph didn't need to do a lot. He took care of the football, made the big throws when they were there, and let the defense win the game. That's exactly what I said he needed to do.

METCALF RESPONDS

And let's give DK Metcalf credit.

A week after his ejection in Philadelphia, he caught a touchdown pass over one of the best cornerbacks in football. According to Bleacher Report, he finished with three catches for 36 yards and the score on four targets. And no flags.

That's how you answer criticism. Not with words. With a clean, productive game.

THE RUN GAME

According to NFL.com, Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle combined for 117 yards on 31 carries. Bleacher Report has Dowdle with 71 yards on 11 carries, and Warren with 20 carries.

There was one blemish. According to Sky Sports, linebacker Alex Singleton dropped Warren in the end zone for a safety in the second quarter. And Bleacher Report notes Dowdle fumbled late in the third quarter. But overall, the run game did its job, especially in the second half, when the Steelers needed to run the clock.

WHAT RUDOLPH SAID ABOUT THE WEEK

After the game, Rudolph talked about how the team handled everything that happened this week.

According to NFL.com, he said: "Neither of us had been through that kind of week." And about McCarthy: "Unbelievable week. He balanced a lot."

He also said he was "Proud of the way our team rallied around him." And he described the team's message as: "Let's block out the noise and go win a game."

According to Sky Sports, Rudolph also said: "It was a lot. Sort of unprecedented, certainly like nothing I've ever been through."

WHAT MCCARTHY SAID

McCarthy, for his part, kept it simple.

According to NFL.com, when he was asked about his reaction to Rodgers' comments, he said: "I really don't have one." And: "I have to put all my energy into winning."

He also said: "It was definitely a hard-travel week for us as a football team."

That's the tone you want from a head coach after a week like that. No back-and-forth in the media. Just football.

THE VETERANS' TAKE

Cam Heyward said, according to NFL.com: "Can't say enough about the men just locking back in." And: "A situation happens and it can go one of two ways."

T.J. Watt was more measured. When asked whether the week had brought the team closer, he said, according to NFL.com: "Time will tell."

I like Watt's answer, honestly. One win doesn't fix everything. But it's a start.

THE RODGERS SIDE OF THE STORY

Now let's go back to the headline, and to Rodgers.

According to Yahoo Sports, Rodgers was released on Wednesday and cleared waivers on Thursday. That means no team claimed him, and he's now a free agent who can sign anywhere.

And he wasn't quiet. According to NFL.com and Sky Sports, during the broadcast on Black Friday, Al Michaels read a message attributed to Rodgers in which he claimed the team had "barred" him from the facility.

According to NFL.com, Steelers president Art Rooney II criticized Rodgers for what was described as insubordination. I haven't seen Rooney's full statement, so I'll leave it at that.

So the relationship is clearly over. And it ended about as badly as a relationship between a team and a future Hall of Famer can end.

WHAT TERRY BRADSHAW SAID

Now, the legends. Let's start with the one closest to the Steelers.

Terry Bradshaw, the four-time Super Bowl champion, spoke about it on Fox on Thursday, according to Yahoo Sports via Steelers Now and Mediaite. Here's what he said:

"I'm sorry that Aaron Rodgers had to do this. I'm sorry that he had to go public with all his gripes and complaints."

"To my opinion, he's a strange guy anyway."

And the line that's been quoted everywhere: "We don't turn on our coaching staff. We don't turn on our ownership. We show class on the way out."

Bradshaw and Rodgers have history. Back in 2021, Bradshaw criticized Rodgers on Fox NFL Sunday over his comments about being "immunized," according to Yahoo Sports. So this isn't new between them. But the message about how a Steeler leaves the organization clearly hit home for a lot of fans.

WHAT SHANNON SHARPE SAID

Hall of Fame tight end Shannon Sharpe also weighed in, on his Nightcap podcast, according to Yahoo Sports.

He said: "He's always been selfish. If you notice, he's never accepted responsibility."

And he offered a challenge: "Go to a team and lead the team to the Super Bowl like Peyton Manning did."

Sharpe has been critical of Rodgers for a long time, so that's not surprising. But it adds to the chorus.

WHAT RUSSELL WILSON SAID

The most interesting voice might be Russell Wilson's, because he's played quarterback in Pittsburgh himself.

On CBS's Thanksgiving pregame show, according to Yahoo Sports, he said: "The reality is Aaron Rodgers wanted to call the plays, right? And that's the difference here."

And then: "It's about Aaron Rodgers's legacy. After all the success he's had, is this how you want to go out?"

And finally: "This is a disappointment."

I think Wilson summed it up best. It's not about whether Rodgers had valid frustrations. It's about how it ended.

IS THERE ANOTHER SIDE?

To be fair, there's another side to this, and I want to acknowledge it.

Rodgers raised real football concerns about play-calling and the offense. And the Steelers' passing game really did struggle in November. His numbers dropped, the protection broke down, and the offense couldn't get going early in games.

But there's a difference between raising concerns inside the building and taking them to national television. That's the line Bradshaw, Sharpe and Wilson all seem to be drawing. And after Friday's win, the Steelers look like a team that's chosen to move forward.

WHERE DOES RODGERS GO NOW?

Honestly, I don't know, and I'm not going to guess.

He's a free agent. He's said this is his final season. He turns 43 next week. Whether a contender calls him, or whether this is the end of his career, is a question for the coming days. I'll update you when we know more.

NOW, THE BIG QUESTION: THE PLAYOFF CURSE

Okay, let's shift gears to the video I'm working from today, because it asks the question that really matters for the rest of this season.

This video was recorded during the bye week, when the Steelers were 6-2 and Rodgers was still the quarterback. So some of it is outdated now. But the question it asks is more relevant than ever: did this front office finally build a team that wins in January?

THE CURSE, BY THE NUMBERS

Here's the history, and it's ugly.

The Steelers' last playoff win came on January 15, 2017, at Kansas City. The video says the score was 18-6. It was actually 18-16, and Chris Boswell kicked six field goals that day.

Since then, according to the video, the Steelers have lost playoff games to New England, Jacksonville, Cleveland, Kansas City, Buffalo, Baltimore and Houston. That's seven straight playoff losses.

The video also says the last five losses all came by double digits, and that no team in NFL history had done that before. I couldn't verify that specific claim, so I'll leave it as the video's figure.

And the last two were brutal. The video says Baltimore won 28-4 in the Wild Card round two years ago. That score isn't right. It was 28-14. And last January, Houston won 30-6 in Pittsburgh.

THE PATTERN: SLOW STARTS

The video cites the Tribune-Review, which went through those playoff losses and found a common thread: slow starts.

Pittsburgh would fall behind early, and then ask its quarterback to throw his way out of a hole against a defense that knew the pass was coming. That's a recipe for disaster in January.

And honestly, that's exactly what we saw in Cincinnati and Philadelphia this month. Slow starts, early deficits, and an offense that couldn't catch up. Which is why Friday's game was so encouraging. The Steelers scored first, built a big lead, and never let Denver back in.

WHAT'S DIFFERENT ABOUT THIS TEAM

The video argues that a few things about this roster look nothing like the teams that kept folding in January. Let's go through them, updated for this week.

First, the coaching change. Mike Tomlin stepped down after last season, and McCarthy replaced him. And Patrick Graham replaced the previous defensive coordinator, who the video says was on the staff for each of the last four playoff losses.

Second, the defense. The video says the Steelers led the NFL in sacks going into the bye. And they just held one of the AFC's best teams to five points.

Third, the run game. Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle give this team something it hasn't had in years: a ground game that can carry the offense in cold weather.

THE SEASON'S ARC

The video also walks through how this season unfolded, and it's worth revisiting now that we know more.

The Steelers opened with a 20-13 win over Atlanta, with T.J. Watt returning an interception for a touchdown. Then came a rough loss in New England. The video says the Patriots blitzed Rodgers on 39 percent of his dropbacks that day, his highest rate as a Steeler. I couldn't confirm that figure, so treat it as the video's number.

Then the offense woke up in a 30-27 win over Cincinnati. Then a 27-24 Thursday night loss in Cleveland, decided by a 56-yard field goal with 10 seconds left. Then the four-game winning streak: Indianapolis, the overtime comeback in Tampa, the win in Paris, and the 27-6 rematch over Cleveland.

Then the bye. Then two losses, to Cincinnati and Philadelphia. And now, a 27-5 win over Denver.

That's a season with real peaks and real valleys. But the peaks have come when the defense and the run game lead the way. That's the pattern I'd bet on for December.

THE PORTER TRADE, REVISITED

The video brings up one more decision that shaped this season: the Joey Porter Jr. trade.

One correction first. The video calls Porter a "starting quarterback." He's a cornerback. According to the video, the Steelers sent him to Dallas for a 2027 sixth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick, after contract talks went nowhere. That matches what we reported at the time.

The video says Sports Illustrated gave Pittsburgh a D for the deal, though I couldn't confirm that grade myself. The video's own take is kinder: the defense didn't collapse without him.

After Friday, with Ramsey scoring a touchdown and rookie Daylen Everette sealing the game with an interception, it's hard to argue the secondary is falling apart. Everette, in particular, looks like a player who's growing into the role Porter used to fill.

THE ROOKIE CLASS IS SHOWING UP

And that brings me to the rookies, because the video has a great point about them.

The Steelers held the 2026 draft in Pittsburgh, and according to the video, they walked in with 12 picks, more than any other team, and came out with 10 rookies. The video says one major grading site gave the class a C-plus, and that grade already looks low.

Look at Friday. Germie Bernard, the second-round pick out of Alabama, had the 58-yard catch-and-run that set up the first offensive touchdown. Daylen Everette, the third-round corner out of Georgia, had the game-sealing interception. Max Iheanachor, the first-round right tackle, has been starting since Week 3. Robert Spears-Jennings, the seventh-round safety, has been making plays all season. And Drew Allar, the third-round quarterback, is now one injury away from being the backup.

The video says Iheanachor never played football until college, and that the Steelers landed him after Philadelphia jumped one spot ahead of them. I couldn't confirm those details, so I'll leave them as the video's account. But the bigger point stands. This rookie class is already contributing to wins.

WHAT'S DIFFERENT NOW, THAT THE VIDEO COULDN'T KNOW

And here's the twist the video couldn't have seen coming. The quarterback.

The video talked about a 42-year-old quarterback in his final season who'd learned to stop winning games by himself. That quarterback is gone now. Mason Rudolph is the starter.

Is that better or worse for the playoff curse? Honestly, I don't know yet. Rudolph has led a playoff push before, in 2023. But he's never won a playoff game. And on Friday, he only needed to throw 15 passes.

What I do know is this: the formula the video describes, a great defense and a strong run game, doesn't depend as much on the quarterback. If the Steelers can play the way they did on Friday, they can beat anyone.

THE DATE CIRCLED ON THE CALENDAR

The video mentions one game on the schedule where the January ghost shows up early. It was talking about the Bengals game at the time. But now there's an even bigger one.

According to ESPN, the Steelers host the Houston Texans next, on Sunday, December 6, at 8:20 p.m. Eastern. That's the team that beat them 30-6 at home in the playoffs last January.

That's the ghost. And now the Steelers get to face it with a new coach, a new quarterback and a defense that's playing as well as any in the league.

WHY THE EXTRA REST MATTERS

There's one more detail that could help the Steelers against Houston: time.

After playing on Sunday and Friday, the Steelers don't play again until Sunday night, December 6. That's more than a week off. For a team dealing with Alex Highsmith's ankle, Nick Herbig's hip and ribs, Ben Skowronek's calf and Cole Holcomb's heel, that extra rest could be huge.

And it gives Mason Rudolph a full week of practice as the starter, something he didn't have before the Denver game. That means more reps with the first-team receivers and a game plan built around what he does best.

THE HOUSTON REMATCH

Let's talk about that Houston game a little more, because it's personal for this team.

Last January's 30-6 loss was, according to the video, the worst home playoff loss in franchise history. The video says the Texans returned a fumble and an interception for touchdowns in the fourth quarter. And Mike Tomlin stepped down the next day.

Almost everything about this Steelers team has changed since then. A new head coach. A new defensive coordinator. A new quarterback. A new running back tandem. And a defense that just scored two touchdowns against Denver.

If the Steelers want to prove they've changed, there's no better opponent to do it against.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here's my board for the week. Opinion, not a report.

Rudolph: he's the starter. Give him the job for the rest of the season, unless he plays his way out of it.

The Rodgers chapter: it's closed. Don't engage with the noise. Let the results talk.

The defense: keep doing what you did on Friday. Pressure without heavy blitzing, takeaways, and tackle well.

Highsmith: get him healthy for Houston. With more than a week off, there's time.

Metcalf: Friday was the right response. More of that.

Run game: keep feeding Warren and Dowdle. That's the January formula.

Houston: treat it like a playoff game. It's the best test of whether this team has changed.
