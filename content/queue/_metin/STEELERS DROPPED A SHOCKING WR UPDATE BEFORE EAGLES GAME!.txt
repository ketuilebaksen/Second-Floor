Steelers Nation, two days ago this was a hopeful story. Today, it’s starting to look like a plan.

On Thursday, Michael Pittman Jr. went from limited to full in practice. Roman Wilson stacked a second straight full day. And all of a sudden, the receiver room that gave Aaron Rodgers five catches for 69 yards in Cincinnati could look completely different on Sunday in Philadelphia.

That’s the update. It isn’t shocking in the scary sense. It’s shocking in the sense that nobody expected both of these guys to look this ready this fast, especially Pittman, whose foot has been a problem since the summer.

But there’s a catch, and it’s a real one. Both receivers are still on injured reserve. The Steelers have to activate them by Saturday afternoon, and that takes roster spots they don’t fully have yet. So today we’re breaking down the receiver news, the roster math behind it, the rookie guard who’s also knocking on the door, and the injury that still hangs over this defense.

And at the end, my board. Opinion, not a report.

Let’s go.

THE THURSDAY REPORT, LINE BY LINE

Let’s start with the facts, because that’s what this whole story rests on.

According to the Thursday injury report, as compiled by Still Curtain and PhillyVoice:

Michael Pittman Jr., foot: limited Wednesday, full Thursday.

Roman Wilson, hand: full Wednesday, full Thursday.

Gennings Dunker, knee: full Wednesday, full Thursday.

DeShon Elliott, knee: limited Wednesday, full Thursday.

Jalen Ramsey, personal: did not practice Wednesday, full Thursday.

Patrick Queen, hand: limited both days.

Nick Herbig, hip and ribs: did not practice either day.

And T.J. Watt, Cam Heyward and Darnell Washington, who rested on Wednesday, were all full on Thursday.

That’s a lot of green on one report. Almost everybody is either full or trending up, Queen is holding steady, and Herbig is the one real red flag. For a team that just got beaten by 20 points, that’s about as good a Thursday as you could ask for.

REMEMBER WHERE THIS STARTED

Before we get too excited, let’s remember the baseline, because that’s what makes this update matter.

In Cincinnati, according to EssentiallySports, Steelers receivers combined for five catches and 69 yards. DK Metcalf had 54 of those yards and the touchdown. Rodgers opened the game 0-for-8, the first time in his career he’d started a game that way. And the offense finished with 222 total yards, its lowest of the season, according to the Northeast Times.

That’s the room Pittman and Wilson would be walking back into. Not a room that needs a little help. A room that needs a jolt.

WHY PITTMAN’S UPGRADE IS THE BIG ONE

Wilson being full again was expected. He was full on day one. The real news is Pittman.

Here’s why it matters. According to Yahoo Sports, Pittman hasn’t played since the October 1 loss in Cleveland. The Post-Gazette’s Ray Fittipaldo counted it at 48 days since his last game when he wrote his story Wednesday. He went on injured reserve with the foot injury in October, and PhillyVoice describes it as plantar fasciitis. That’s the kind of injury that doesn’t follow a neat schedule.

So when he was limited on Wednesday, the natural assumption was that the Steelers would ease him in and look at Denver. Instead, he went full on Thursday. And PhillyVoice’s Jimmy Kempski wrote that it’s “a good bet he’ll be activated this week and play on Sunday.”

That’s a big change in two days. And I’ll be honest, earlier this week I was in the “be patient, aim for Black Friday” camp. Thursday changes my thinking. Not completely. But it changes it.

WHAT PITTMAN SAID, AND WHY IT MATTERS

What I like most about this story is how Pittman himself is talking about it.

According to the Post-Gazette, he said: “I don’t want to go out there and hurt this team because I can’t do something that I should be able to do.” And he added: “If the coaches decide I can’t then we’ll see where that goes.”

That’s a veteran talking. He isn’t campaigning to play. He’s saying he only wants to be out there if he can actually do the job. That tells you he’s not going to hide a setback just to get on the field, which is exactly what you want from a player coming off a foot injury.

And remember the context. According to the Post-Gazette, Pittman signed a two-year, $35 million extension this offseason. He’s played three games, with seven catches for 89 yards. He came here to be a core piece of this offense, and he’s barely gotten the chance. You can feel how badly he wants to fix that, but he’s being smart about it.

WHAT RODGERS SAID ABOUT BOTH OF THEM

Rodgers weighed in too, and his tone was measured.

On Pittman, according to the Post-Gazette: “We’ll see if he’s healthy.” And then: “If he feels good to go, it will help our offense.”

On Wilson, he said: “I feel like Ro’ was getting more comfortable, playing with confidence and playing fast.”

That second quote is the one I keep coming back to. “Playing fast.” That’s what this offense lacked in Cincinnati. Receivers who play fast, win early in the route and get open before the pass rush gets home. Rodgers is 42. He needs receivers who are where they’re supposed to be on time.

WHAT MCCARTHY IS WAITING FOR

Mike McCarthy hasn’t made the call yet, and he’s been clear about that.

According to the Post-Gazette, McCarthy planned to watch a full week of practice before deciding whether to add any of these players to the 53-man roster for Sunday. His view on the returns: “Any time you have players come back, it’s definitely a boost.”

So Friday’s practice matters. If Pittman and Wilson both stay full and come out of Friday feeling good, I’d expect both activations to come Saturday. If either one has a setback, the plan changes.

THE CATCH: THE ROSTER MATH

Now, here’s the part of this story that doesn’t get enough attention.

Activating a player off injured reserve means putting him on the 53-man roster. And to put a player on the 53, you need an open spot. When McCormick went to injured reserve on Wednesday, that opened one. Before that, as I understand it, the roster was full, which is why the team waived cornerback Terell Smith last week to make room for Derrick Harmon.

So here’s the math as I see it. If the Steelers want to activate both Pittman and Wilson, they likely need at least one more move. And if they want Dunker activated too, that could mean another.

That means somebody’s going to lose a roster spot, or go on injured reserve, or get waived with the hope of sliding him to the practice squad. I don’t know who, and I’m not going to guess at a specific name. But it’s coming. And the team has until 4 p.m. Eastern on Saturday, as it’s generally understood, to sort it out.

WHO LOSES SNAPS IN THE RECEIVER ROOM

Even if the roster math works, there’s a second question. Who gives up playing time?

Think about how this room has looked without Pittman and Wilson. DK Metcalf on the outside. Rookie Germie Bernard taking a big share of the snaps. Ben Skowronek back from his calf injury. Brandon Smith and Kaden Wetjen filling in. That’s been the group for weeks.

If Pittman and Wilson are both active, the snap count shifts. My guess is Bernard’s role changes the most. He’s been a real bright spot this season, but he was held without a catch on three targets in Cincinnati, according to EssentiallySports. A rookie who’s been forced into a big role might actually benefit from sliding back a bit and getting more favorable matchups.

Skowronek’s value is special teams and blocking, so he probably keeps a role. And the depth guys likely become game-day inactives.

WHAT PITTMAN BRINGS THAT THIS OFFENSE LACKS

Let’s talk football for a second, because this is about more than names.

Pittman is a big, physical possession receiver. His game is winning contested catches, working the middle of the field and moving the chains on third down. That’s exactly what McCarthy talked about this week when he said the fastest way to the end zone is through the middle of the field, and admitted the numbers haven’t backed that up so far.

Pittman is built for the middle of the field. If he’s healthy, he gives Rodgers a target who can catch in traffic, take a hit and hold on. That’s different from Metcalf, who’s been at his best on the outside, and different from Wilson, whose value is speed.

WHAT WILSON BRINGS

Wilson is the other side of the coin.

According to the Post-Gazette, Wilson played the first five games, with 14 catches for 188 yards and two touchdowns, before going on injured reserve with the hand injury after the October 11 game against the Colts. One of those touchdowns was a 38-yard catch against Cincinnati back in Week 3.

His value is speed and route running. He can line up inside or outside. And against the man coverage that gave this offense so much trouble in Cincinnati, a receiver who can separate quickly is worth his weight in gold.

So picture the room on Sunday if everything breaks right. Metcalf on one side. Pittman working the middle. Wilson stretching the field. Bernard rotating in. Freiermuth and Washington at tight end. That’s a real passing attack. That’s the version of this offense people imagined back in August.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR METCALF

There’s one more player who benefits from all this, and it’s DK Metcalf.

According to the Northeast Times, Metcalf has 35 catches for 432 yards this season. It’s been a frustrating year for him, with drops and a lot of criticism. But part of the problem is that defenses have been able to roll coverage toward him every week, because there wasn’t enough around him to punish them.

Put Pittman in the middle and Wilson on the other side, and that changes. Safeties can’t cheat toward Metcalf if Wilson is running past the corner on the opposite side. Linebackers can’t sit underneath if Pittman is working the seams. Metcalf doesn’t need to be the focal point. He just needs more one-on-one chances, and a healthier room gives him exactly that.

DON’T EXPECT EVERYTHING ON DAY ONE

But let’s keep our feet on the ground.

The video I’m working from today makes this point well. Returning to practice is not the same as being cleared to play. And even being cleared isn’t the same as being in game shape.

Pittman hasn’t played in seven weeks. Wilson hasn’t played in more than five. Practice reps are one thing. Getting hit in a real game, at full speed, for 40 or 50 snaps, is another. I’d expect both to be on some kind of snap count on Sunday, especially with Denver coming to town on Friday, November 27, on a short week.

So even if both play, the realistic goal is a handful of meaningful targets each. Not a full workload.

MAX HURLEMAN: A CORRECTION AND AN UPDATE

Quick correction from me before we move on. Earlier this week, I described Max Hurleman as having a 21-day practice window opened along with the others. According to the Post-Gazette, the more accurate description is that Hurleman was activated back to the practice squad after 10 weeks on the practice squad’s injured list. Pittman and Wilson are the ones with the 21-day windows.

It’s a small detail, but I’d rather get it right. The practical upshot is the same: Hurleman is healthy, practicing and available for a game-day elevation if the coaches want him. With Pittman and Wilson possibly back, though, I’d expect him to be more of a special teams option than a receiver option this week.

NOW, THE LEFT GUARD SIDE OF THE STORY

The video I’m working from today spends a lot of time on the offensive line, and it frames Dunker as the possible solution very few fans expected so soon.

Let’s get the facts straight first. Mason McCormick dislocated his ankle in Cincinnati, and he’s on injured reserve. The video cites Sports Illustrated in saying there’s still a chance he returns in the postseason, which lines up with what NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported earlier this week. The video also says McCormick had started all nine games this season.

And the video is right that McCarthy has praised McCormick’s leadership, and that he’s expected to stay involved with the offensive line room while he recovers. That matters. Losing a starter is one thing. Keeping his voice in the meeting room softens the blow.

ANDERSON STARTS, BUT THE CLOCK IS RUNNING

For Sunday, all signs point to Spencer Anderson. Still Curtain says Anderson is expected to start at left guard.

The video raises a fair point about him: Anderson has already lost a starting job once this season. That happened at right guard, where Brock Hoffman took over after the first couple of games. The video’s take is that it doesn’t mean Anderson can’t succeed this time, but it does give the Steelers a reason to keep evaluating alternatives. I think that’s a balanced way to put it.

DUNKER’S PROFILE

Now Dunker. According to the video, he played 47 games at Iowa, including 38 starts. I couldn’t confirm those exact college numbers myself, so treat them as the video’s figures. What’s confirmed is that he was a third-round pick in 2026, he played mostly tackle in college, and he hasn’t played a regular-season NFL snap.

The video also says he played right guard in the preseason. The opening now is at left guard. And the video does a nice job explaining why that matters: switching sides changes your footwork, your stance, your blocking angles. It’s not like flipping a switch, especially for a rookie coming back from a knee injury.

But the encouraging part is the practice report. Dunker was full on Wednesday and full again on Thursday. Two full days in a row is exactly what you want to see.

THE 21-DAY WINDOW, EXPLAINED

The video makes a point that’s worth repeating, because a lot of fans miss it.

Opening a 21-day practice window doesn’t put a player back on the active roster. It just lets him practice while the team evaluates him. Under the rules as generally understood, the team can activate him at any point during those 21 days. If it doesn’t, he stays on injured reserve for the rest of the season.

So the Steelers don’t have to rush Dunker. They have three weeks. That’s the key point. Activating him this week isn’t a requirement. It’s an option.

HOW I’D HANDLE IT

Here’s my take on left guard, and it’s built around that window.

Anderson starts Sunday. If the Steelers have the roster room, maybe Dunker gets activated as a backup. If not, he keeps practicing and they revisit it next week. With a short week before Denver, I’d lean toward keeping things stable up front and not throwing a rookie into his first NFL game in a hostile building.

The video says it well: the Steelers need the best available player at left guard, not the youngest or most exciting one. Right now, that’s Anderson. In two weeks, it might be Dunker. Let the practice tape decide.

THE DEFENSE: HERBIG IS THE STORY

Now the less encouraging part of the report.

Nick Herbig missed a second straight practice with his hip and ribs injury, according to Still Curtain, Steel Curtain Network and Steelers Depot. Steelers Depot’s headline put it simply: two players made progress, Herbig didn’t. I couldn’t open that article, but the headline matches the official report.

I’m not going to guess at how serious it is. What I can tell you is how the Steelers would cover for him. According to Still Curtain, if Herbig can’t play, T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith and Jack Sawyer would absorb his snaps.

That’s not a bad trio. Watt has 7.5 sacks and a pick-six this season, according to the Northeast Times. Highsmith has been one of the team’s best run defenders on the edge. And Sawyer, in his second year, has earned more trust as the season’s gone on.

QUEEN, ELLIOTT AND RAMSEY

Patrick Queen stayed limited with his hand injury on Thursday. That’s not a red flag on its own. Linebackers often stay limited all week with hand issues and still play on Sunday. Friday’s final report will give us his game status.

DeShon Elliott moving up to full is good news. He was hurt in warmups in Cincinnati and never got on the field. Getting him back for Philadelphia restores some veteran communication to a secondary that clearly needed it.

And Jalen Ramsey was back on Thursday after missing Wednesday for personal reasons. That’s all we need to know there.

WHAT THE EAGLES’ INJURY REPORT SHOWS

Let’s look across the field, because Philadelphia’s report matters too.

According to PhillyVoice, Eagles receiver DeVonta Smith was limited Wednesday and full Thursday with a hamstring injury, after missing the last three games. Kempski wrote: “It’s pretty safe to say that he’ll be good to go for Sunday.”

That matters for the Steelers’ secondary. Smith is Philadelphia’s best receiver, and after what Chase did in Cincinnati, a sharp route runner is the last thing this defense wants to see.

PhillyVoice also says linebacker Zack Baun hurt his back against the Giants and missed both practices. Kempski wrote, “I wouldn’t count on him playing this week.” That’s a real loss for Philadelphia’s defense, and it could help the Steelers’ run game.

And at quarterback, PhillyVoice confirms Joe Milton will start, with Jalen Hurts out for the season.

THE REST OF PHILADELPHIA’S LIST

A few more Eagles notes from PhillyVoice. Receiver Dontayvion Wicks was limited both days with an ankle injury, after missing time alongside Smith. Running back Will Shipley is on injured reserve with a Lisfranc injury, but his 21-day window opened on November 16. And the Eagles’ offensive line has been without right tackle Lane Johnson since he retired abruptly after Week 4.

That last one matters for the Steelers’ pass rush, with or without Herbig. An offensive line that’s been reshuffled for weeks, protecting a quarterback who’s been sacked seven times in his only start, is the kind of matchup Watt was built for.

THE MATCHUP THAT MATTERS MOST

Put the two reports together and here’s the game I see.

The Steelers’ run game against a Philadelphia defense that may be missing Baun. According to the Northeast Times, the Eagles allow 126.1 rushing yards per game, 25th in the league. Pittsburgh averages 4.8 yards per carry. That’s the edge.

And then the Steelers’ secondary against DeVonta Smith. That’s the risk. If Pittsburgh’s defense gives up big plays again, Philadelphia stays in this game no matter who’s at quarterback.

The good news for the offense is that a healthy receiver room makes the run game better too. When defenses have to respect Pittman, Wilson and Metcalf, they can’t load the box against Jaylen Warren. That’s how these pieces connect.

MY BOARD (OPINION, NOT A REPORT)

Here’s my board after Thursday. Opinion, not a report.

Roman Wilson: activate. Two full practices, no setbacks. Give him four or five targets, including one deep shot.

Michael Pittman Jr.: activate if Friday goes clean. I’ve changed my view from earlier in the week. But snap count, maybe 25 to 30 plays, with Denver five days later.

Roster room: expect at least one more move to make space. I’m not naming names, but somebody at the bottom of the roster is going to feel it.

Max Hurleman: special teams elevation only if needed. The receiver spots are filling up.

Left guard: Spencer Anderson starts. Dunker only gets activated as depth if there’s room. No rush with a full 21-day window.

Pass rush: if Herbig is out, Watt, Highsmith and Sawyer share the load, and Graham dials up pressure against Joe Milton.

Secondary: Elliott back, Ramsey back. Priority number one is keeping DeVonta Smith in front of you.

FINAL WORD

Here’s my bottom line. A week ago, the Steelers’ receiver room looked like the biggest problem on the roster. Now there’s a real chance Pittman and Wilson both suit up in Philadelphia, giving Rodgers more weapons than he’s had since September.

That doesn’t fix everything. Herbig’s status is still a concern. Left guard is still a question. And the roster math still has to work by Saturday. But for the first time since the Cincinnati game, the momentum is pointing in the right direction.

Watch Friday’s report. Watch Saturday’s moves. And then watch whether this offense finally looks like the one we were promised.
