Steelers Nation, there’s a moment from Sunday’s 27-6 win over Cleveland that most people watched once and moved on from. They shouldn’t have. Because it tells you everything about who Aaron Rodgers is right now, and it might be the most important decision he’s made all season.

Here’s the situation. End of the first half. Steelers on the doorstep, seconds on the clock, no timeouts. The safe move is obvious. Kick the short field goal, take a 16-6 lead into the locker room, and move on.

Rodgers said no.

And I don’t mean that as a figure of speech. Mike McCarthy confirmed it after the game: “Frankly, actually Aaron called that play, the Nittany.” That’s the short-yardage package with Pat Freiermuth taking the snap under center and pushing it in. Freiermuth scored with time expiring, Pittsburgh led 20-6, and the game was basically over.

Rodgers’ explanation was six words long: “I wasn’t coming off the field.”

So when you see a headline about an Aaron Rodgers “bombshell decision,” that’s the real one. Not a retirement announcement, not a trade demand, not a contract drama. A 42-year-old quarterback in his final season, telling his coach, in effect, that they were going for it, and being right.

I’m James, this is Second Floor. Today: Rodgers’ decision and what it says about this team, the DK Metcalf problem that’s suddenly front and center, Jon Gruden coming to Pittsburgh’s defense, the Garrett Wilson rumor that sparked an insider war, and where the Josh Downs chase stands with the deadline one week away. Let’s go.

WHY THE NITTANY CALL MATTERED

Let’s put that decision in context. Pittsburgh’s biggest weakness on offense all year has been the red zone. Heading into Sunday, the Steelers ranked 27th in red-zone offense, according to ClutchPoints. In Paris, they settled for field goals again and again.

And even in the Browns game, there was a moment that showed the problem. According to Steeler Nation, Pittsburgh reached the 2-yard line on one drive, threw twice, didn’t score, and settled for a field goal. Some fans have even speculated that Rodgers has been checking out of runs into passes near the goal line. That’s speculation, not reporting, but it shows how frustrated people have been.

So when Rodgers stood at the goal line before halftime and said “Nittany,” he was doing two things. He was taking the field goal off the table, and he was trusting the most physical part of this offense, the push game, to finish the job. It worked. And it’s exactly the kind of mindset this team needs in December.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio said the staff will make the red zone a bye-week focus: “We’ve got to see if we have the right plays down there.” Rodgers already gave them one.

“RIGHT WHERE WE WANT TO BE”

Rodgers also set the tone for the bye week. After the win, he said, “We’d like to play our best and still win. We also like to win.” And then: “We’re 6-2 going into the bye, first in the division. We’re right where we want to be.”

He’s also already thinking about what’s next: “Winning is the most important thing — 2-1 in the division now. Either way, that game in two weeks is going to be really important.”

That game is Sunday night in Cincinnati on November 15. And it got a lot more interesting on Sunday, which we’ll get to in a minute.

Here’s where Rodgers stands statistically through eight games, according to Heavy: 60.8 percent completions, 1,871 yards, 13 touchdowns, and six interceptions. Not eye-popping. But he’s 6-2, and his team is in first place. And on Sunday, he made the call that broke Cleveland.

FROM SHOUTING MATCH TO FULL TRUST

To understand why that call matters, think about how far the Rodgers-McCarthy relationship has come in one month.

Back in Week 4 in Cleveland, cameras caught Rodgers yelling about a late play call, and McCarthy appeared to snap back. Afterward, McCarthy said, “Any negative communication on my part, I wish I wouldn’t use those words.” Rodgers explained, “It was loud. So some of the calls, it was hard to hear, for sure.” It was the low point of the season.

Then came Tampa. Down 16-3 heading into the fourth quarter, Rodgers told the huddle, according to Germie Bernard on Cam Heyward’s podcast, “Listen guys, I’m going to take a little bit of control. I’m going to start calling some of the plays.” Pittsburgh came back and won in overtime.

Then Heyward went on The Rich Eisen Show and explained why the team trusts Rodgers at the line: “Well, if you had a younger quarterback, and Aaron is not a younger quarterback, you’d be very worried.” But: “When he’s checking plays and he’s making sure everybody’s accountable, you feel as comfortable as you can.”

And now, against Cleveland, the head coach publicly credits his quarterback with the call that decided the game. “Frankly, actually Aaron called that play.” That’s not a coach losing control. That’s a coach who trusts his quarterback enough to let him own a moment. These two won a Super Bowl together in Green Bay. Right now, they look like they remember how.

THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE STATEMENT

Quick reminder of what that decision was part of, because this win was dominant across the board.

Jaylen Warren ran for 143 yards on 17 carries, tying his career high on his 28th birthday. Rico Dowdle added 56 yards and a touchdown. The team ran for 200 yards, a season high. Darnell Washington caught three passes for a career-high 94 yards, including a 58-yard catch-and-run. And the defense sacked Watson eight times, held Cleveland to 34 rushing yards, and allowed minus-1 yard of offense in the first quarter.

Pittsburgh has now won 23 straight regular-season home games against Cleveland, according to ESPN, tying the NFL record for consecutive home wins over one opponent. And T.J. Watt now has the most career sacks of any player against the Browns.

THE METCALF PROBLEM

Now the uncomfortable part. Because while Rodgers was making the right call at the goal line, his number one receiver had a day to forget.

DK Metcalf caught one pass for four yards on six targets against the Browns. And on a deep ball down the middle where he was wide open, he dropped it, and the ball deflected right to Browns cornerback Denzel Ward for an interception, according to Heavy.

On Monday, Angelichio addressed it, via PennLive. He said the Ward interception “is one he’d want back.” And then he made the team’s position clear: “We haven’t lost confidence in DK Metcalf whatsoever.”

But the numbers are hard to ignore. According to Heavy, Metcalf has been held under 50 receiving yards in five of eight games. He’s averaging 11.5 yards per catch and 40.6 yards per game, both career lows. His only touchdown came in Week 3 against Cincinnati. He’s on pace for about 59 catches but only around 803 yards, which would be a career worst. Before last season, he’d never finished under 900. And he’s the seventh-highest-paid receiver in the league by average annual value.

ALEX SMITH’S WARNING

ESPN analyst and former quarterback Alex Smith went even further on NFL Countdown on Sunday. His words were blunt.

“DK Metcalf, I’m sorry, he looks like a shell of himself.” He added, “This guy cannot separate anymore,” and, “He can’t pull away on a go route. Can’t pull away on a slant.”

And then the line that connects it all to Rodgers: “The problem is who is he throwing the ball to?” And: “At some point, I think Aaron’s going to stop looking that way.”

That’s harsh, and I’m not ready to say Metcalf is done. The talent is still there. He had 115 yards against these same Browns in Week 4, and a big catch in Paris that set up a touchdown. But when a former quarterback says the trust between a quarterback and his top receiver is at risk, you pay attention. Because Rodgers decides where the ball goes. And on Sunday, the biggest passing play of the day went to a tight end, not a receiver.

GRUDEN DEFENDS THE STEELERS

Not everyone is down on Pittsburgh. On Monday morning on Wake Up Barstool, Dave Portnoy floated the idea that the Steelers are a fraud in the division, pointing to what he called an easy schedule. Jon Gruden wasn’t having it.

“You don’t win four straight games unless you’re playing good football,” Gruden said. He praised the defense: “This defense is way better than advertised.” And his conclusion: “I think Pittsburgh is a little better than people realize.”

Gruden has a point about the defense. According to Heavy, Pittsburgh ranks sixth in the NFL in fewest yards allowed per game. Against Cleveland, the Steelers sacked Deshaun Watson eight times and held the Browns to 34 rushing yards. And over the four-game winning streak, the defense has allowed just 14.3 points per game, per the Tribune-Review.

Portnoy’s schedule argument isn’t crazy. The Browns, Colts, Bucs, Saints, and Browns again isn’t a murderers’ row. But you can only beat who’s on the schedule. And the second half of the season will answer the question for everyone.

THE AFC NORTH JUST GOT SCRAMBLED

Speaking of that second half, the division looks different after Sunday.

The Bengals beat Tennessee in overtime to improve to 5-2, according to Yahoo Sports. Joe Burrow put up huge numbers, 472 yards and six touchdowns from scrimmage, but also threw three interceptions, and Cincinnati’s defense gave up a season-high 36 points.

And then the big one. The Ravens fell to 4-4, and Lamar Jackson hurt his throwing hand on the first offensive snap against Buffalo. According to the report, he was placed on injured reserve, is having thumb surgery, and is expected to miss at least four weeks, possibly the rest of the season. Backup Tyler Huntley also hurt his toe.

So here’s the division right now. Steelers, 6-2. Bengals, 5-2. Browns, 5-3. Ravens, 4-4 without their MVP quarterback. That makes November 15 in Cincinnati a showdown between the top two teams in the North.

THE GARRETT WILSON RUMOR AND THE INSIDER WAR

Now let’s get to the trade market, where things got spicy this weekend.

National insider Jason La Canfora, in a video for Casino Guru News, said, “I’d keep an eye on the Pittsburgh Steelers, who are being pretty ambitious,” and that they “would love to add another playmaker.” He pointed specifically at Jets receiver Garrett Wilson.

Wilson is legit. He was the 10th overall pick in 2022 and is a three-time 1,000-yard receiver. This season, he has 43 catches for 527 yards and three touchdowns in eight games, a career-best 65.9 yards per game, according to Heavy. And he played with Rodgers on the 2024 Jets.

But then came the pushback. ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that a well-placed source said there’s “no way” the Jets trade Wilson before the November 10 deadline.

La Canfora fired back on X, writing, “The more the corrupt access merchants launder the idea that Garrett Wilson is ‘untouchable,’” followed by a word I won’t repeat here. So you’ve got two national insiders openly disagreeing in public.

WHY I’M SKEPTICAL OF THE WILSON IDEA

Let me give you the obstacles, because they’re big.

First, the contract. Wilson signed a four-year, $130 million extension with the Jets in July 2025, averaging over $32 million a year, according to Heavy. Adding that to Metcalf’s deal would mean two receivers at roughly the same top-of-market price. That’s a massive commitment for a team whose quarterback is retiring after this season.

Second, the price. Heavy reported the Steelers might have to give up as much as their 2027 second-round pick. And that’s assuming the Jets would even talk.

Third, the Rodgers relationship. ESPN’s Rich Cimini previously reported that Wilson said he wasn’t keeping in touch with Rodgers after Rodgers left New York, and the relationship was described as icy, at least back then. If the main selling point is a reunion, that’s a problem.

So La Canfora may be right that Pittsburgh is ambitious. But Schefter’s “no way” plus the contract plus the history makes Wilson a long shot in my book.

THE DOWNS MARKET: STILL THE MOST REALISTIC

Meanwhile, the more realistic target is still on the board.

Colts receiver Josh Downs requested a trade last week. Pro Football Rumors reported Monday that a Colts deal before the deadline appears likely. Insider Jordan Schultz, on Ryan Clark’s The Pivot, said Indianapolis wants a third-round pick, but he expects it to end up at a fourth.

The field of suitors, per that report, includes the Chiefs, the Commanders, and the Steelers. Washington is a new name, motivated by losing Terry McLaurin for the season. But according to Florio, Kansas City’s top target is Jacksonville’s Brian Thomas Jr., with Downs as its Plan B. And Florio also reported that the Steelers are more interested in Downs than in Thomas.

That’s why I keep coming back to Downs. He’s a rental at a Day 3 price, he’s a quick-separating slot receiver, and Pittsburgh reportedly has an offer on the table already, according to Evan Sidery’s report last week.

HOW A RECEIVER ADD HELPS METCALF TOO

Here’s an angle that matters. Adding a receiver isn’t necessarily a vote of no confidence in Metcalf. It might be the best thing that could happen to him.

Right now, Metcalf is being asked to be the number one option against every top corner, every week. If Pittsburgh adds a separator underneath, defenses can’t roll coverage to Metcalf as easily. That could free him up to do what he does best: run vertical routes and win contested catches. La Canfora himself described a version of this, with Metcalf running go routes on the outside.

And with Germie Bernard developing, Darnell Washington emerging after his 94-yard day, and Freiermuth reliable inside, a slot receiver could be the piece that makes everything else work.

RODGERS’ LAST RIDE CHANGES THE DEADLINE MATH

There’s one more reason the trade decision matters so much: this is it for Rodgers. Back in May, he made it clear 2026 would be his final season. He’s 42. He’s in his 22nd year. He passed Peyton Manning for sole possession of third place in career regular-season touchdown passes in Paris.

When your quarterback has one season left, every decision becomes about now. That’s why rentals make sense. A player like Downs, who can become a free agent in March, fits perfectly. You’re not building for 2028. You’re building for January 2027.

And it’s also why I’d be careful with long-term contracts like Wilson’s. When Rodgers walks away, Pittsburgh will be retooling at quarterback. Committing two massive receiver deals right before that transition would limit the next chapter.

WHAT ABOUT MVS?

One internal option still deserves a mention. Marquez Valdes-Scantling has been on the practice squad since October 20 and hasn’t been elevated yet. Rodgers has lobbied for him openly, calling him “one of my favorite teammates I’ve ever been with” and saying, “It would be nice to get him up.”

MVS is a vertical threat with two Super Bowl rings and four years of history with Rodgers in Green Bay. During the bye, he gets a full stretch of practice to make his case. If Metcalf’s struggles continue, MVS could give Rodgers a trusted deep option, even if the front office also adds a slot receiver through a trade.

TRADE BLOCK: OPINION, NOT A REPORT

Here’s where I give you my take. Everything in this segment is my opinion, not a report.

Downs, yes. Make the deal before the deadline, ideally this week so he gets the bye to learn the offense. A fourth-round pick, maybe with a late-round sweetener, is fair. Don’t touch the 2028 second-rounder from the Joey Porter Jr. trade.

Garrett Wilson, no. Not because he’s not good. He is. But the contract, the second-round price, the icy history with Rodgers, and Schefter’s “no way” add up to too many obstacles. And spending big on a long-term receiver doesn’t fit a team whose quarterback is in his final season.

And Metcalf? Keep him, and keep believing in him. Angelichio said the team hasn’t lost confidence. I agree. But give him help.

THE BYE WEEK GAME PLAN

So here’s what I’d want the Steelers to accomplish over the next twelve days.

One. Fix the red zone. Rodgers’ Nittany call was a start. Angelichio says they’re evaluating the plays. Turning field goals into touchdowns is the single biggest upgrade available.

Two. Get Metcalf right. Whether that’s a confidence reset, scheme tweaks, or a new receiver taking pressure off him, the Steelers need their highest-paid receiver producing in November.

Three. Get healthy. McCarthy said, “I look to come out of this bye really healthy.” Brandin Echols, Doneiko Slaughter, and Ben Skowronek all have time to heal, and Roman Wilson is in the rehab group.

Four. Make the trade. If the right deal is there, do it this week.
